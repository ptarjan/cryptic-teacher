# Nightly jobs

`tools/daily_update.sh` is a queue of small units (`tools/unit_queue.py`), not
one long run. The scheduler fires a tick every ten minutes; the tick chooses the
puzzles to annotate under the usage gates, asks `tools/daily_units.py` what is
due, and starts each due unit as a detached process with its own lock, time
limit, ledger row and worktree, then exits. The units are:

1. `fetch:<fetcher>`, hourly: the newest puzzle of each series; the
   fortnightly TLS (`fetch:tls_puzzles`, read off the issue's image) every
   six hours. Two
   series are not fetched, and `tools/daily_units.py` `SLOW_FETCHERS` says
   why: the Listener's clues are printed only behind the Times' paywall, and
   the Nation's weekly cryptic ended in April 2020. `tools/coverage_report.py`
   `STALE_AFTER_DAYS` alarms on a series silent past its cadence;
2. `blog:times`, `blog:telegraph`, `bucket:telegraph`, `xval:globe`, `ft`,
   `azed`: the puzzles rebuilt from blogs and other copies, each on its own
   cadence; each filer cross-checks and commits its own filings. The
   backfills (`bucket:telegraph`, `ft`, `azed`) have no per-run cap: each
   works to a wall-clock budget at its source's own request pace, and one
   that stops with backlog left runs again at the next tick
   (`unit_queue.backlog_left`) until it is drained, two at most at once so
   the fetchers and annotations keep their trees. Their commits name what
   they filed, e.g. "File azed-1738, azed-1739, azed-1740 and 14 more (azed
   backfill)";
3. `solutions`, every three hours: re-fetches any puzzle still waiting for
   published solutions (`--refresh-unsolved`) and grades our own fills;
4. `annotate:<id>`: Claude annotates one puzzle. Every new arrival (dated
   within the last two days, or given its official key since) gets one, plus
   up to `ANNOTATE_MAX` older un-annotated puzzles a day, newest first. Each is
   validated, committed and pushed on its own by `tools/puzzle_worker.sh`;
5. `miss:<id>:<entry>`, `reports`: Claude diagnoses a graded miss, and fixes
   the bad hints solvers reported from the site;
6. `blog-facts`, `ratings`, `checks`, `minute`: the derived tables and the
   alerts about a series gone quiet or a corpus defect.

A slow or failing unit holds up only itself: a failure is retried after ten
minutes, doubling each time, and a unit past its limit is killed and alerted.
A puzzle published mid-morning is fetched within the hour and annotated at the
next tick. `python3 tools/unit_queue.py status daily` shows what ran, how it
ended and what is running; `UNIT_QUEUE_DRY=1 tools/daily_update.sh` is a tick
that only says what it would start.

It works newest first because that is where readers are: a puzzle gets most of
its visits in the week it comes out. Working oldest first would leave the top of
the site without hints forever. One puzzle a day would never catch up, because
the tracked series publish several puzzles a day between them.

## A dropped job loses minutes, not the run

Every long job (the full OCR pass, the book reads, the daily run, the
pre-reset burn) can be stopped, OOM-killed or rebooted at any moment, and what
it filed before then must still reach `origin/master`. A scan filer marks a
source read in its ledger outside the repo, so a puzzle filed off it but never
committed is never filed again. Three mechanisms, one shared helper
(`tools/durable.sh`):

- **Commit as it goes.** While a job files, what it filed is committed and
  pushed every `DURABLE_EVERY` seconds by `tools/push_puzzle_commit.sh`,
  which does not touch the working tree the job is still writing into.
- **Commit on stop.** SIGTERM, SIGINT or SIGHUP ends the job's child, commits
  and pushes, then exits; `tools/corpus_queue.py stop` sends the pass TERM and
  waits up to `STOP_GRACE` seconds before it kills anything.
- **Salvage on start.** A SIGKILL runs no trap. A job that sets
  `CT_SALVAGE_PATHS` before sourcing `tools/nightly_worktree.sh` has its last
  run's uncommitted files under those paths, and any commit a failed push
  left, pushed before the worktree is reset.
- **Never held on old code.** The full pass moves its tree to `origin/master`
  after every checkpoint, and its units start as fresh processes off the tree.
  A commit `origin`'s pre-push refuses (written by older code than `origin`'s)
  can't block that move: it is kept under `refs/durable/refused/`, the tree
  moves anyway, and then its sources are read again with the new code from
  their cached readings, so nothing is OCR'd twice.

Puzzle files are written whole (a temp file renamed into place), and a `.json`
that does not parse is never committed. Downloads live under one root (see
"Downloaded source material" in [LAYOUT.md](LAYOUT.md)) and derived caches under `~/.cache`, never
`/tmp`, and every fetcher skips what it already has, so a restart resumes. `tools/test_durable.sh` kills a fake job each way
and fails if a scheduled job with no timeout, or one over 15 minutes, does not
use these mechanisms.

## Waiting for solutions: cold solving

The Guardian withholds solutions to its Saturday *Prize* crossword for about a
week. `--refresh-unsolved` re-checks every night until they appear.

So that the newest puzzle is not left without hints for that week, a puzzle
with no answers is *solved* from the clues alone, but only as the first half of
annotating it: the nightly and the burn place it in their annotation queue, and
`tools/puzzle_worker.sh` has one run solve it and then annotate it. The script
then applies that fill again from the committed puzzle; if the applier refuses
it, nothing the run wrote ships and the solve ledger records why. Nothing is
solved that the run will not annotate.

- `tools/solve_packet.py` gives the model the clues and the grid's crossing map.
- `tools/solve_prompt.md` gives it the method.
- `tools/apply_solution.py` is the only way the answers reach the puzzle file.
  It writes nothing unless every entry is answered, every answer has the length
  the grid needs, and all crossing letters agree (around 60 on a 15x15 grid).
- The answers are marked with a `solutions` detail (`model`, `date`, `check`)
  in the file and `solutionsUnofficial` in the index. The site says so
  wherever it shows them.
- The puzzle keeps being re-fetched. When the official key lands, it replaces
  the model's answers and prints a `BLIND SOLVE GRADED` line with the score. The
  annotation of any clue the model got wrong is thrown away, so the queue
  rewrites it.

## Scheduling

The household bridge runs every scheduled job. Each one's cron line, script,
log and timeout are in `household-plugins/<name>/plugin.toml`. **There must be
exactly one schedule.** A second scheduler is not a fallback: both copies would
annotate the same backlog out of the same weekly quota.

The two annotating jobs are `cryptic-daily` (`*/10 * * * *`, the tick of
`tools/daily_update.sh`) and `cryptic-prereset` (`5 * * * *`,
`tools/prereset_backfill.sh`). While the burn runs, the daily units leave the
backlog to it and annotate only new arrivals, so the two never buy the same
puzzle. `cryptic-books` (`*/10 * * * *`, `tools/acquire_books.sh`) is the same
shape: one loan or one book's read a unit (`tools/book_units.py`).

- Output (stdout and stderr) goes to `.update.log` and `.prereset.log`. Both are
  untracked on purpose.
- Each script takes its own lock, so a fire while the last run is still going
  exits at once: `tools/nightly_worktree.sh` holds a `flock` on the job's
  worktree, and `tools/prereset_backfill.sh` a `.prereset.lock` directory.
  Two overlapping backfills would spend the same quota window. A unit of a
  queue holds its own lock and one of the queue's trees (`<tree>-1`,
  `<tree>-2` ..., named to `tools/nightly_worktree.sh` by `CT_JOB`).

## The `claude` credential

A scheduled run needs the `claude` CLI already logged in, as the user the
scheduler runs as.

- This works wherever the credential is a readable file:
  `~/.claude/.credentials.json`, or a directory named by `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`.
- It does not work where the credential is in a desktop keyring that only a GUI
  login session can unlock. **On macOS**, the CLI keeps its OAuth token in the
  login keychain. cron runs outside that session, so every run fails with
  `Not logged in · Please run /login` and the backlog never drains. On macOS,
  schedule the jobs with a per-user LaunchAgent in `gui/<uid>` instead of cron,
  and remove the plugin's schedule when you add it; do not run both.
- A scheduler gives a job a bare `PATH` that does not include the `claude`
  CLI. The scripts source `tools/claude_path.sh` to find it.
