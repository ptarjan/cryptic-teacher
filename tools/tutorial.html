Every clue has two parts

A cryptic clue is two clues joined into one sentence:

  • The definition. This is a plain clue for the answer, like one in an ordinary crossword. It can be one word or several. It always sits at the start or the end of the clue, never in the middle.
  • The wordplay. This is a set of instructions for building the answer out of letters. It fills the rest of the clue.

The number in brackets at the end is the letter count: how many letters the answer has. (3,4) means two words, of 3 and then 4 letters.

Read as a normal sentence, the clue is about something else entirely. That normal reading is called the surface, and it is there to mislead you. Ignore it. Your job is to find where the definition stops and the wordplay starts.

Here is how you know you are right. The definition means the answer, and the wordplay builds the same answer. When both work, you have it.

Indicators and link words

Most wordplay contains an indicator: a word that tells you what to do with the words right next to it. "Broken" might mean "rearrange these letters". "Back" might mean "write this word backwards". The words an indicator works on are called the fodder. An indicator can come just before its fodder or just after it.

Each clue type below lists some common indicators. The lists are examples, not complete. Any word with a similar meaning can do the same job.

A few clues also have a link word between the definition and the wordplay, such as for, is, 's, gives, makes or from. It joins the two parts, like an equals sign. It adds no letters.

Punctuation is mostly there for the surface, so you can usually ignore it. Two exceptions: a question mark often warns you that a word is being used in an odd sense, and an exclamation mark can mean an &lit (see the end of the list below).

In the examples below, the definition is green and the indicator is pink. Here is one clue with every part labelled:

Stale, broken, is smallest (5)
The definition is "smallest", at the end. The rest is the wordplay. "Broken" is the indicator: it says "rearrange". STALE is the fodder. "Is" is a link word. Rearrange STALE to get LEAST, which means "smallest", and has 5 letters.

The main clue types

Anagram

Destroying climate, sun reaches highest point (10)
Rearrange the letters of some words in the clue. "Destroying" is the indicator. CLIMATE SUN is the fodder: 10 letters, the same as the letter count. Rearranged, they spell CULMINATES, which means "reaches highest point".
Anagram indicators suggest mess or change: broken, wild, drunk, confused, cooked, at sea. Always count the fodder's letters. They must match the letter count exactly.

Charade

Give authority? Those people might (7)
Build the answer from small pieces placed one after another, like beads on a string. "Those people" is 'EM (as in "give 'em"). "Might" is POWER. Together: EMPOWER, which means "give authority".
A charade often has no indicator. The pieces simply follow each other in the order the clue gives them. Small words like and, with or by often just join the pieces, and add no letters.

Container

Stylish attempt to cover butt (6)
Put one piece inside another. "Attempt" is TRY. "Butt" is END (the end of something). TRY covers END, so END goes inside TRY: TR + END + Y = TRENDY, which means "stylish". The outer word can split at any point. Try each gap until you get a real word of the right length.
Container indicators: holding, swallowing, wearing, about, in, covering.

Hidden word

Unyielding squad a man triumphantly arrests (7)
The answer is written inside the clue, in consecutive letters that run across the gaps between words: squAD A MAN Triumphantly gives ADAMANT, which means "unyielding". The words "arrest" (hold) the answer inside them.
Hidden word indicators: in, found in, some, part of, held by, arrests, conceals.

Homophone

Deer, we hear, is precious (4)
The answer sounds like another word but is spelled differently. Say "deer" out loud: it sounds like DEAR, which means "precious". "We hear" is the indicator. "Is" is a link word.
Homophone indicators: we hear, reportedly, on the radio, said, called out.

Reversal

Apple device set up date feature on smartphone (6)
Write a word backwards. "Apple device" is MAC. This is a down clue, where the answer is written from top to bottom, so "set up" means "read it from the bottom up": MAC becomes CAM. "Date" is ERA. CAM + ERA = CAMERA, which is a "feature on smartphone".
Reversal indicators in across clues: back, returned, reflected. In down clues: up, rising, climbing, set up. Some, like over or turned, work in either.

Deletion

Sexpot stripped for show (4)
Take letters away from a word. "Stripped" means remove the outside letters, so SEXPOT loses its first and last letters: (S)EXPO(T) gives EXPO, which is a "show". "For" is a link word.
Deletion indicators: headless (lose the first letter), endless (lose the last), gutted (lose all the middle letters), almost, short, without.

First letters

Taxi initially carrying a businessman (3)
Take the first letter of each word. "Initially" is the indicator, so take the first letters of "carrying a businessman": C, A, B gives CAB, which means "taxi". Take letters only from the words the indicator points at, and stop when you have the letter count. The rest of the clue is the definition.
First letter indicators: initially, at first, leaders of, heads of. Last letter indicators: finally, ultimately, at last, ends of.

Double definition

Lousy payment for cartoon (7)
The clue is two definitions of the same word, and there is no wordplay. Each definition can be one word or several. A very small wage is called "peanuts", and Peanuts is also a cartoon strip, so the answer is PEANUTS. "For" is a link word joining the two definitions.
These are often the shortest clues in the puzzle.

Cryptic definition

Flower of London (6)
The whole clue is one definition with a trick in it, and there is no wordplay. The trick is that "flower" here means "something that flows". The river that flows through London is the THAMES. Clues like this often end with a question mark, to warn you that a word means something unusual.

&lit

Terribly angered! (7)
The whole clue is the wordplay and the whole clue is the definition, so all of it is green, and "terribly" is pink with a green underline because it is also the indicator. As wordplay: "terribly" is an anagram indicator, and ANGERED rearranged spells ENRAGED. As a definition: "terribly angered" means enraged. The name is short for "and literally so". These clues are rare, and often end with an exclamation mark.

How to solve a clue, step by step

  1. Read the clue once. Then ignore what it seems to be about.
  2. Test the first word or two as the definition, then the last word or two. Whatever is left is the wordplay. If both ends could work, pick the one where the rest of the clue builds a word with the right letter count.
  3. Look for indicators. They tell you which clue type you have.
  4. Count letters. If "confused" sits next to words with 9 letters in total, and the letter count is (9), it is probably an anagram.
  5. Use the letters you already have from answers that cross this one.
  6. Use the hints when you are stuck. Each hint teaches you one step, without giving away the answer. The first three are "Spot the indicator words", "Where is the definition?" and "What kind of clue is this?". You can take them in any order. "The building blocks" opens after those, and "Full walkthrough" opens last, because they give more away.

Reading solving blogs

Solvers write free blogs that explain every clue in the big daily cryptics, the morning after each puzzle. Fifteensquared covers the Guardian and the Independent. Big Dave's Crossword Blog covers the Telegraph. Times for the Times covers the Times. They are the best place to look up a puzzle this site does not have.

The bloggers use a shorthand that they rarely explain. Here is what it means:

You'll seeIt means
underlinedthe definition
CAPITALSthe fodder, or the letters that go into the answer
(round brackets)the indicator, copied from the clue
[square brackets]letters that are removed, or the words that remove them
*an anagram: RETAIL* means "rearrange RETAIL"
<, << or reva reversal: write it backwards
dda double definition
cda cryptic definition
&litthe whole clue is both the definition and the wordplay
lift and separatesplit what looks like one phrase into separate words: read "flowerbed" as "flower" and "bed"
Ximenean / libertariana setter who follows the rules strictly, or one who bends them

For example, a blogger might write: Chaperone (shredded) CORSET* = ESCORT. That means: the definition is "Chaperone", the indicator is "shredded", and rearranging CORSET gives ESCORT, which means "chaperone". The hints on this site tell you the same things, one at a time.
