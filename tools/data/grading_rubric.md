# What makes a cryptic clue good

A rubric for blind head-to-head scoring. Five axes, each 1–5. They are deliberately
separable: a clue can be flawlessly fair and completely lifeless, and the whole point
of scoring them apart is to find out *which* kind of clue you are writing.

## 1. Surface sense

Does the clue read as a sentence a person might actually write, with a meaning of its
own that has nothing to do with the answer?

- **5** — reads like a line from a novel, a headline or overheard speech. You would not
  guess it was assembled from parts.
- **3** — grammatical and coherent, but slightly odd; the kind of sentence that exists
  only in crosswords.
- **1** — word salad, or a bare list of instructions with no surface meaning at all.

Watch for the tell of machine-made clues: technically grammatical, semantically inert.
"Cold heap is inexpensive" parses fine and means nothing.

## 2. Misdirection

Does the surface actively point *away* from the mechanism?

- **5** — the definition hides inside the surface's own scenery, the indicator looks
  like ordinary description, or a word is doing a completely different job than it
  appears to (a name that is a verb, a capital letter that means nothing).
- **3** — mild disguise; the mechanism is not signposted but not concealed either.
- **1** — the wordplay announces itself. "Rebuilt", "twisted" and "rearranged" sitting
  in an obviously artificial phrase.

Note especially whether the *definition* is disguised, not just the wordplay.

## 3. Penny-drop

When it resolves, is there a moment of pleasure — wit, aptness, a pun that lands, a
definition that turns out to mean something other than what you read?

- **5** — you smile, or want to show someone.
- **3** — satisfying to solve, but mechanical; nothing you would quote.
- **1** — resolves with a shrug.

This is the axis that separates competent setting from good setting, and the hardest
one to fake.

## 4. Economy

Does every word earn its place?

- **5** — nothing removable; padding is doing surface work that could not be done in
  fewer words.
- **3** — a word or two of filler that buys nothing.
- **1** — bloated, or padded to disguise a thin idea.

Economy is *not* shortness. A four-word clue that reads like a telegram scores worse
than an eight-word clue where the extra four words build a picture. Do not reward
brevity as such.

## 5. Fairness

Could a solver reach the answer from the clue alone, by rules they already know?

- **5** — definition at one end, every letter accounted for, indicators doing jobs they
  genuinely do, no unsignalled liberties.
- **3** — a stretched indicator or a loose definition, still solvable.
- **1** — the wordplay does not compute, or the definition is not a real synonym.

Libertarian setters bend agreement and part of speech on purpose; that is a 4, not a 2.
Reserve 1–2 for clues that are actually broken.

## Two extra judgements per group

- **Favourite**: which single clue you would put in a puzzle, and one sentence why.
- **Machine guess**: which clue, if any, you suspect was written by a program, and what
  gave it away. Say "none" if nothing stands out. This is a check on whether the
  comparison was genuinely blind — a judge who can pick the machine every time is
  telling us something the five axes are not.
