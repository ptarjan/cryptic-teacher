Every puzzle with full hints carries a difficulty badge: gentle, moderate, tough or brutal. This page explains where the badge comes from and how we check it.

What the badge means

The badge compares a puzzle with the other puzzles on this site, not with crosswords in general. No paper publishes a difficulty rating, so there is nothing absolute to measure against. "Tough" means tougher than most puzzles here.

The rating is a score in standard deviations: 0 is an average puzzle on this site, +1 is one standard deviation harder. The bands are cut at fixed points on that score:

The SNITCH rates every Times puzzle by how long about a hundred regular solvers took, each compared with their own usual time. Its rating is called the NITCH: 100 is a normal day for them, and 130 means solving took 30% longer than usual. On Times puzzles the badge's tooltip quotes the NITCH range scored by the middle half of our puzzles in that band, shown in the table above.

What goes into it

We measure the {{component_count}} things below. Each one is compared with the rest of the site, so a puzzle scores above or below the usual. The rating is a weighted average of those scores. The weights are set by hand and never fitted to solve times. If a puzzle is missing a measure, it is rated on the rest. The "on its own" figure is how well that measure alone tracks Times solve times, using the check described below.

{{components}}

The rating comes only from the puzzle: its grid, its clues and its answers. It never uses the day of the week. The Times gets harder from Monday to Friday on purpose, so the weekday alone would predict a Times puzzle well. But it would say nothing about the clues, and most papers have no weekly pattern for it to carry to.

Solvers' posted times, on Times puzzles

The Times for the Times blog writes up every Times puzzle, and its commenters often say how long they took, or that they gave up. Where a post has at least {{comment_min_times}} stated times, the badge averages the clue rating with those times and with the share of commenters who did not finish. Each is compared only with other puzzles from the same series. A series needs at least {{comment_series_min}} such puzzles before any of them is blended. Today that covers {{blended_series}}. A puzzle can change band as its post gathers comments.

No other blog posts solve times, so every other series is rated from the clues alone.

How we check it

The SNITCH is the only independent rating of puzzles we hold, so it is the test. Because the Times ramps up through the week by design, and the rating leaves the weekday out, we test against the NITCH minus the average NITCH for that weekday. The question is not "was this hard?" but "was this hard for a Tuesday?". The Sunday Times appears only one day a week, so it is tested against the plain NITCH.

The puzzles are split into three periods by date. Each period is tested against weekday averages taken from the other two, so no puzzle helps set its own target. The figure is a rank correlation: 0 means no relation, and 1 means the rating puts the puzzles in exactly the order the solvers' times do.

{{checks}}

A second check needs no solve times. The Guardian Quiptic and the Observer Everyman are gentle puzzles by their own papers' design. {{margin}}

A new measure is added only if the rating with it beats the rating without it on both Times sets, in at least two of the three periods and on average, without making the Sunday Times or the gentle-puzzle check worse. Whether a measure should make a puzzle harder or easier is fixed before it is tested.

Limits

  • Only puzzles with our hints get a badge, because the wordplay measures need them. The unannotated check above uses only {{portable_measures}}, and those track solve times less well.
  • Only the Times has a SNITCH. For every other paper there are no solve times at all. The same formula rates those puzzles, and the gentle-puzzle check is the only test it gets there.
  • Posted times and the NITCH are both solve times for the same puzzle, so part of what the blended rows gain is solvers agreeing with solvers.
  • Clue features are nearly exhausted. The recent rounds of candidate measures, from clue length to where the definition sits, all failed the test. The biggest recent gain came from solvers' own times, not from reading the clues better.

The share of each answer's letters that no crossing answer gives you. An unchecked letter has to come from the clue alone, so a grid with many of them gives less help.

How rare the puzzle's three rarest answers are, from a word list ordered by how often words appear. A word you seldom meet is slower to think of, even once the clue has told you what it means.

Which kinds of wordplay the clues use, and how much work each is. A hidden word gives itself up. A cryptic definition offers nothing to confirm your guess. A clue built from many pieces, especially one- and two-letter abbreviations, takes longer to assemble. This needs our hints.

How many operations the clues ask for: indicators per clue, plus extra for a clue that stacks three or more kinds of wordplay. Each step is one more thing to spot and do. This needs our hints.

How seldom the answers appeared in earlier puzzles, from any paper. A solver who has met an answer before gets to it sooner. Only puzzles published before this one count, so a rating never changes because later puzzles arrived.

How seldom each answer was clued with the same kind of definition in earlier puzzles. A stock pairing, like IDE clued as "fish", is recognised on sight. A new one has to be worked out.

The share of clues ending in a question mark. The mark usually means the definition is a pun, an example or a cryptic definition rather than a plain synonym. Commenters name these clues as hard, and as their last one in, more often than others.

The share of clues whose definition a dictionary of word senses (WordNet) does not link to the answer. An indirect definition cannot be reached by running through synonyms.

How many clues the puzzle has. Every clue is one more thing to solve, so a Jumbo takes longer than a daily however easy each clue is.
