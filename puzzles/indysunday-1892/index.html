Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,892

Across

1 across SWITCH

Substitute second one of three Shakespearean sisters (6)

Definition: Substitute · charade

  • second → S s = second, the unit of time as abbreviated on a stopwatch
  • one of three Shakespearean sisters → WITCH Macbeth opens with three of them stirring a cauldron, the weird sisters

'Second one of three' sounds like an instruction to pick the middle sister out of the trio. It is nothing of the sort: the abbreviation comes first and the sister arrives whole.

5 across DEMISTER

Outside in deluge, fellow gets drier in the car (8)

Definition: drier in the car · charade + outer letters

  • Outside in deluge → DE the first and last letters of DELUGE, the middle of the word washed away
  • fellow → MISTER a fellow is a mister, the everyday title for a man

'Outside' looks like part of the rainy picture rather than an instruction, and that is the whole disguise. Every word of the surface is weather; the answer is the gadget that fights it.

9 across FIRETRAP

Dangerous building's engineers caught by appropriate criminal charge (8)

Definition: Dangerous building · container + charade

  • appropriate → FIT appropriate = fit, as in 'fit for purpose'; it opens after two letters
  • engineers → RE RE = Royal Engineers, the British Army's engineering corps and crosswords' standard engineers
  • criminal charge → RAP a rap is a criminal charge — 'taking the rap', 'a murder rap'

The surface is all inspectors and courtrooms, which is why 'appropriate' reads as an adjective describing the charge rather than as a word waiting to be prised open.

10 across WEALTH

Plenty breaking the law (6)

Definition: Plenty · anagram

  • the law → THELAW anagram fodder, six letters

Four words, and three of them are machinery: the whole clue reads as a headline about crime while the definition sits in the very first word.

11 across PLASTIC SURGEON

Doctor photos including bottom, prick and leg (7,7)

Definition: Doctor · container + charade

  • photos → PICS photos are pics, and this word opens after its first letter
  • bottom → LAST the bottom of a league table is last
  • prick → URGE to prick someone on is to urge or spur them into action
  • leg → ON in cricket the leg side of the field is also the on side, so 'leg' regularly means ON

'Doctor' at the head of a clue nearly always means shuffle these letters, and here it is the definition. The crudest-looking words in the surface turn out to be the honest ones.

12 across GREW

Rose planted in west-facing flower garden (4)

Definition: Rose · hidden word + reversal

  • flower garden → GREW four letters in a row straddle the gap between these two words, and the entry travels back through them

The clue is gardening from end to end, so every word looks like scenery. The definition is the one word that also names a flower, which is precisely why it leads.

14 across AUTOSTRADA

Gold fried tortilla filled by Romeo the Italian way (10)

Definition: the Italian way · charade + container

  • Gold → AU AU is the chemical symbol for gold
  • fried tortilla → TOSTADA a tostada is a flat fried tortilla in Mexican cooking; it splits open after four letters
  • Romeo → R R is Romeo in the radio alphabet

Two of the three pieces are conventions rather than words, a chemical symbol and a radio-alphabet letter, and the surface's Mexican kitchen has nothing to do with the answer.

17 across DELIBERATE

Slow and careful French composer reduced tempo (10)

Definition: Slow and careful · deletion + charade

  • French composer → DELIBE Léo Delibes wrote Coppélia and Lakmé; cut short, his name loses its final S
  • tempo → RATE the tempo of a thing is the rate it goes at

The definition wants the unhurried sense of the word rather than the 'done on purpose' one, and Delibes is the sort of nineteenth-century name crosswords keep in circulation.

19 across WOOL

Strand unwound from ball left on court (4)

Definition: Strand unwound from ball · charade

  • court → WOO to court someone is to woo them
  • left → L L = left, as marked in stage directions and on a pair of boots

Tennis is the picture the surface wants — a ball, a court, a line call — and the answer is knitting. 'On' is doing positional work in a clue full of sporting prepositions.

20 across SHERLOCK HOLMES

Doctor's cleverer friend treated more shell shock (8,6)

Definition: Doctor's cleverer friend · anagram

  • more shell shock → MORESHELLSHOCK anagram fodder, fourteen letters

Shell shock and a doctor build a first-world-war surface with nothing to do with Baker Street, and that distance is what keeps a fourteen-letter anagram out of sight.

23 across MANIOC

Mother with 50p, say, brought back root vegetable (6)

Definition: root vegetable · charade + reversal

  • Mother → MA ma = mother, the nursery word
  • 50p, say → NIOC a 50p piece, the British fifty-pence, is one example of a coin — and here it is turned round

'Say' is the setter flagging an example rather than a synonym: the value is irrelevant, all that matters is that it names a coin.

24 across INNOCENT

Old pope inside rather than outside church, and disrobed (8)

Definition: Old pope · charade + container + deletion

  • inside → IN inside is simply in — the word itself, not an abbreviation
  • rather than → NOT 'walk rather than drive' is 'walk and not drive', so the phrase hands over not; this word splits open after two letters
  • church → CE CE = Church of England, the church in a crossword unless another is named
  • and → N the word 'and' with its outer letters taken off leaves N

Read as English it is a scene: a pope indoors rather than out at church, and stripped of his robes. Every one of those words is an instruction instead. 'Rather than' is the hardest — it is standing in for NOT — and 'and' is a word to be worked on here, not a joiner.

25 across POSEIDON

Attitude I assume is Olympian (8)

Definition: Olympian · charade

  • Attitude → POSE a pose is an attitude struck for effect
  • I → I the setter's own I, taken simply as the letter
  • assume → DON to don a coat is to assume it — to put it on

The surface is a boast about one's own bearing, and the joke is that the Olympian turns out to be a god of the sea rather than a lofty manner.

26 across SALUTE

US et al organised simultaneous gunfire, say (6)

Definition: simultaneous gunfire, say · anagram

  • US et al → USETAL anagram fodder, six letters

'US et al' looks like a list of allies in a news report, and the enumeration is the only hint that it is six letters waiting to be shuffled.

2 down WRIT LARGE

Obvious trail grew blurred (4,5)

Definition: Obvious · anagram

  • trail grew → TRAILGREW anagram fodder, nine letters

The surface is a path fading into the distance, which is why 'blurred' reads as description rather than instruction. Nothing looks like fodder until you count those two words against the enumeration.

3 down TEENS

Children anxious when putting electronics together (5)

Definition: Children · anagram

  • anxious → TENSE an anxious person is tense, and those five letters are the fodder

Most anagram indicators just say 'these letters move'. This one names exactly which letters travel, and the surface — a family wrestling with a gadget — makes 'putting together' read as construction.

4 down HERMITAGE

Huge museum valued old stuff, bagging millions (9)

Definition: Huge museum · container

  • valued old stuff → HERITAGE the buildings and customs a country keeps because they are old and valued are its heritage; this word splits after three letters
  • millions → M m = million, as written on a salary or a budget

A museum valuing old stuff and taking in millions is exactly what museums do, so the second half of the clue reads as more description and never as an instruction.

5 down DEPOSIT

500 pies to supply store (7)

Definition: store · charade + anagram

  • 500 → D D is 500 in Roman numerals
  • pies to → EPOSIT the six letters of PIES TO, bent into a new order

The surface is a bakery order, and the misdirection is grammatical: the one word carrying the instruction looks like the plain verb it is pronounced nothing like.

6 down MOWER

Our team runs after flash vehicle driven on the pitch? (5)

Definition: vehicle driven on the pitch? · charade

  • flash → MO a flash is a moment, and a moment is a mo — 'back in a mo'
  • Our team → WE the team we belong to is 'we', first person plural
  • runs → R r = runs on a cricket scorecard, where a batter's runs are totted up

Read as English it is a chase — our lot running after a flash motor — and the answer is groundsman's kit. A pitch here is the playing surface, not a sales pitch.

7 down SCARECROW

Welfare services in Worcestershire going up, an alarming figure (9)

Definition: an alarming figure · container + reversal

  • Worcestershire → SCROW Worcestershire is an English county and WORCS is its standard short form, seen on envelopes and road signs; here it climbs the grid
  • Welfare services → CARE welfare services are care — social care, care in the community

The surface reads as a news item about council funding, so not one word of it looks like an instruction. The county abbreviation is the piece a solver outside Britain has to be told.

8 down EATEN

What some pappardelle at Enzo's might be? (5)

Definition: What some pappardelle · &lit + hidden word

  • pappardelle at Enzo's → EATEN five letters run consecutively through these words, straddling both gaps

Pappardelle is a broad ribbon pasta and Enzo's is any Italian restaurant you care to name. The clue is a plain question that also spells its own answer out.

13 down WHITE LINE

Spooner's low-alcohol champagne perhaps driver's limit (5,4)

Definition: driver's limit · spoonerism

  • low-alcohol → WHITE a drink low in alcohol is a light one, and it hands its opening sound over to its neighbour
  • champagne perhaps → LINE champagne is one example of wine, and it collects the sound the other word gave up

Say the plain version aloud first — a light wine is exactly what the surface is offering — and the swap does the rest. What comes out is painted on a road.

15 down SEE THINGS

Boiling southern trip (3,6)

Definition: trip · charade

  • Boiling → SEETHING water boiling is seething, and so is a person in a temper
  • southern → S S = southern, as on a compass or a railway's name

Three words, and the join between them is invisible: the whole clue reads as a description of a bad-tempered journey to the south coast.

16 down DO ONE'S NUT

Flip deep-fried pastry, pinching runny nose (2,4,3)

Definition: Flip · container + anagram

  • deep-fried pastry → DONUT a deep-fried ring of pastry is a donut, spelled the short American way; it opens after two letters
  • nose → ONES the four letters of NOSE, run together in a different order

A runny nose and a doughnut make one greasy picture, which is why neither word looks like an instruction until the enumeration refuses to fit.

18 down AUCTION

Sell uranium which military operation has seized (7)

Definition: Sell · container

  • military operation → ACTION a military operation is an action — troops are said to see action
  • uranium → U U is the chemical symbol for uranium

The surface is an arms-trade news item, which makes 'seized' read as confiscation by the authorities rather than as an instruction to swallow a letter.

20 down SPADO

Hot-tub party being made sexless (5)

Definition: being made sexless · charade

  • Hot-tub → SPA a hot tub is the centrepiece of a spa
  • party → DO a do is a party in everyday British usage — 'a bit of a do'

The wordplay is far kinder than the answer: a solver who has never met this word can still build it from two of the friendliest short words in crosswords.

21 down LUCID

Sensible group of investigators has uncovered clue first (5)

Definition: Sensible · charade + middle letters

  • clue → LU cLUe with the letters at both ends stripped away
  • group of investigators → CID the CID is the Criminal Investigation Department, the detective branch of a British police force

The surface is a police procedural, which is exactly why 'clue' reads as part of the plot rather than as four letters waiting to be trimmed.

22 down LOCAL

Anaesthetic that's laughter-inducing, about to be inhaled (5)

Definition: Anaesthetic · container

  • that's laughter-inducing → LOL LOL is what you type for laughing out loud; it splits open after two letters
  • about → CA ca. is short for circa, Latin for about, used before an approximate date

Laughing gas is the surface's joke — nitrous oxide really is an inhaled anaesthetic that leaves patients giggling — and the definition sits in the very first word.
