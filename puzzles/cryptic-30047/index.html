Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,047

Across

1 across TO COME

Expected firm book binding (2,4)

Definition: Expected · container

  • firm → CO Co. = company, firm
  • book → TOME a tome is a large book; the container instruction opens it around its middle to take the firm's abbreviation inside

'Binding' is what books get, which is why it hides so well as the container instruction — the TOME simply closes around the firm.

4 across IMPERIAL

Risk touring America in August (8)

Definition: August · container

  • Risk → IMPERIL to risk = to imperil; it 'tours' the A
  • America → A A = America

August sits at the end of the clue so its capital letter passes as the month; read it as the adjective — august, majestic, imperial.

9 across OVERT

Up first in The Open (5)

Definition: Open · charade + first letter

  • Up → OVER up = over, finished — as in 'time's up'
  • The → T its first letter

'The Open' reads as the golf championship, which is the disguise: 'Open' alone is the definition, and 'The' only donates its first letter.

10 across POSTDATES

Comes after Poles gathered in fruit (9)

Definition: Comes after · container

  • Poles → POSTS poles = fence posts, not North and South
  • fruit → DATE a date, taken in by the POSTS

'Poles' are fence posts, not the North and South, and that misdirection is the whole clue. 'Gathered in' is harvest language, and it points the right way round — the container takes the fruit inside.

11 across PUNCH LINE

Judy’s husband having job say, to get to the other side … (5,4)

Definition: to get to the other side · charade

  • Judy’s husband → PUNCH Mr Punch, of Punch and Judy
  • job say → LINE a line = a job — 'what line are you in?'; 'say' marks it as one example

Fed hides the oldest joke in the language by never telling it: the definition is simply that joke's punch line, quoted.

12 across RURAL

… of the country. Largely regret going by rail – without one … (5)

Definition: of the country · charade + deletion

  • Largely regret → RU most of RUE (regret)
  • rail → RAL after losing its I
  • without one → the instruction: I (one) comes out of RAIL

Two subtractions in five words: 'largely' keeps most of RUE, and 'one' is the I that leaves RAIL. The ellipses just sew this surface to its neighbours.

13 across RACKETEERING

… track engineer working close to train shed – it’s extortion (12)

Definition: extortion · anagram + deletion

  • track engineer → TRACKENGINEER anagram fodder — thirteen letters, one too many
  • close to train shed → shed the N, the close (last letter) of traiN
  • working → anagram indicator

A railway surface built to hide railway-free wordplay: 'shed' is the verb, not the building, and 'close to train' names the N it throws away.

17 across TAX INSPECTOR

Revenue man in quiet zone checks in on duty at the earliest opportunity (3,9)

Definition: Revenue man · charade + container

  • duty → TAX duty = tax; 'on duty' hangs the rest after it
  • in → IN spelled straight into the answer
  • quiet → P p = piano, quiet, from sheet music
  • zone → SECTOR the P checks in as early as it can: S-P-ECTOR

'At the earliest opportunity' does precise work: P could lodge anywhere in SECTOR, and Fed tells you to take the first berth, right after the S.

20 across SAY-SO

Rumour is, many Ascot odds must be cut (3-2)

Definition: Rumour · alternate letters

  • is, many Ascot → SAYSO cut the odd letters of iS mAnY aScOt and read what is left

'Odds' points at the racecourse, but they are the odd-numbered letters — cut them and the evens spell the answer. Note the fodder starts at 'is': even link-looking words can be letters.

21 across INNKEEPER

Cool name – probably number one part in nativity play? (9)

Definition: part in nativity play? · charade

  • Cool → IN cool = in, fashionable
  • name → N n = name
  • probably number one → KEEPER the goalkeeper — the player who probably wears shirt number 1

'Probably' is fair play: keepers usually wear the number 1 shirt, not always. And the question mark concedes the innkeeper never appears in the gospels — only in school nativity plays.

23 across ASPIRATES

Supplies air to a buccaneer on board ship (9)

Definition: Supplies air to · charade + container

  • a → A the A from the clue — not joinery here
  • buccaneer → PIRATE a pirate, going aboard
  • ship → SS SS = steamship; 'on board' puts the rest between its letters

'On board ship' is a fixed convention: inside the letters SS. To aspirate is to supply air — what the h does in 'hat'.

24 across PHASE

Worry in audition for stage (5)

Definition: stage · homophone

  • Worry → PHASE to faze is to worry

FAZE and PHASE are exact sound-twins, and 'in audition' keeps the hearing signal inside the theatre. A phase is a stage — of the moon, of a project, of a teenager.

25 across STANDARD

Day after Laurel and Hardy discovered model (8)

Definition: model · charade + middle letters

  • Laurel → STAN Stan Laurel
  • Day → D d = day, placed after STAN
  • Hardy discovered → ARD HARDY dis-covered: its outer letters stripped off

'Discovered' read as dis-covered — the covers taken off — is a classic worth banking. The famous duo split unevenly: Laurel gives his first name, Hardy only his middle.

26 across ATTEST

Every now and then say, Tati is in French show (6)

Definition: show · charade + alternate letters

  • say, Tati → ATT every second letter of sAy TaTi
  • is in French → EST French for 'is'

Jacques Tati, the French film-maker, keeps the surface in France — where 'is' translates to EST. His name only lends its alternate letters.

Down

1 down TROUPERS

Small story about taking posh cast on tour … (8)

Definition: cast on tour · charade + reversal + container

  • Small → S s = small, as on clothing labels
  • story → REPORT 'taking' the U it reads REP-U-ORT
  • posh → U U = upper-class, posh

'About' turns the whole assembly — S plus REP-U-ORT — end for end, and the ellipsis just runs the surface on into 2 down.

2 down CREW NECK

… band drink meaning Jersey’s cut? (4,4)

Definition: Jersey’s cut? · charade

  • band → CREW a band of people = a crew
  • drink → NECK to neck a drink = to down it

The capital J sends you to the Channel Islands; the jersey in question is the woollen one, and its cut is the neckline.

3 down MATCH

Spill double (5)

Definition: Spill · double definition

  • Spill → MATCH a spill is a thin strip of wood or paper used to carry a flame from a fire to light something else
  • double → MATCH your double is your exact counterpart, someone who looks just like you

Two words, no indicator: nearly always two definitions. 'Spill' meaning a fire-lighting taper is old household vocabulary that crosswords keep alive.

5 down MISREPRESENTS

Theatre feel bitter about lines girl delivers inaccurately (13)

Definition: delivers inaccurately · charade + container

  • Theatre → REP rep = repertory theatre
  • feel bitter about → RESENT to resent is to feel bitter about
  • girl → MISS a girl can be addressed as Miss; the 'lines' instruction opens that word around REPRESENT, which slots inside it

'Lines' is the container word — a lining goes inside — and the theatre surface makes you hear an actor's lines instead. Note that 'about' belongs to 'feel bitter about', not to any reversal.

6 down ENDURANCE

Doctor: cure can – having killed cold – end patient suffering (9)

Definition: patient suffering · anagram + deletion

  • cure can → CURECAN anagram fodder — one letter too many
  • having killed cold → kill a C (c = cold, as on taps) before mixing
  • end → END joins the fodder
  • Doctor → anagram indicator

'Doctor' at the head of a clue is the oldest anagram signal there is, and the surface's grim prescription — suffer to the end — is exactly what the answer means.

7 down INTERN

Trainee from Bury on reception, ultimately (6)

Definition: Trainee · charade + last letter

  • Bury → INTER to bury = to inter; the capital B plays at the Lancashire town
  • reception, ultimately → N the last letter of receptioN

'On' is positional and only works because this is a down entry: INTER sits on the N in the grid.

8 down LUSHLY

Sumptuously drunk, sitting on lavatory, exhausted (6)

Definition: Sumptuously · charade + outer letters

  • drunk → LUSH a drunk = a lush
  • lavatory, exhausted → LY LAVATORY 'exhausted' — emptied down to its outer letters

'Sitting on' is positional in a down entry — LUSH above LY — and 'exhausted' empties a word the way 'on vacation' does at 22 down.

10 down PRIME MINISTER

Ready before clergyman describes Eden? (5,8)

Definition: Eden? · charade

  • Ready → PRIME to ready = to prime
  • clergyman → MINISTER a minister

Eden is a definition by example — Anthony Eden held the office in the 1950s — and the question mark admits it. The surface wants the other Eden, the paradise a clergyman might describe.

14 down ENAMOURED

Charmed by change of demeanour (9)

Definition: Charmed · anagram

  • demeanour → DEMEANOUR anagram fodder — nine letters
  • change of → anagram indicator

'Change of demeanour' is such an everyday phrase that the instruction hides in plain sight — the change is literal, letter by letter.

15 down STOPPAGE

Check warehouse facility using powers to knock out door at the back (8)

Definition: Check · container + deletion + last letter

  • warehouse facility → STORAGE about to lose a letter
  • powers → PP p = power, twice
  • door at the back → the R (back of dooR) that the PP knocks out of STORAGE

A substitution: the PP evicts the R from STORAGE and squats in its place. 'Check' is the noun — a stoppage in play.

16 down PRURIENT

Juicy gossip for you – Salvador Dali Tate Modern exhibit finally showing (8)

Definition: Juicy · last letters

  • gossip for you – Salvador Dali Tate Modern exhibit → PRURIENT the last letters of all eight words in a row

Eight consecutive final letters is a marathon — most setters stop at four. 'Juicy' means salacious, which is what the gossip surface was priming you for all along.

18 down OSCARS

Very upset about Mark getting awards (6)

Definition: awards · reversal + container

  • Very → OS SO (very), 'upset' in this down entry
  • Mark → SCAR a mark = a scar; O…S closes 'about' it

'Upset' can only mean read-upwards in a down clue, and the capital M on Mark is the disguise — no saint or gospel, just a blemish.

19 down MYOPIA

Viewer’s complaint leads to Musical Youth’s opening piano number being cancelled (6)

Definition: Viewer’s complaint · charade + first letters + deletion

  • Musical Youth’s opening → MYO the leads (first letters) of those three words
  • piano number being cancelled → PIA PIANO with its No. (number) cancelled

Musical Youth were a real 1980s band, which is what sells the surface. The trick to file away: 'number' is the abbreviation No., and PIANO cancels those two letters.

22 down EXPAT

Cross old man in Egypt on vacation – he’s far from home (5)

Definition: he’s far from home · container + outer letters

  • Cross → X a cross = an X
  • old man → PA pa = father
  • Egypt → ET 'on vacation' — vacated, leaving only the outer letters

'On vacation' means vacated — emptied — a pun worth keeping: Egypt on holiday loses everything inside. The definition is a little sentence standing for the person it describes.
