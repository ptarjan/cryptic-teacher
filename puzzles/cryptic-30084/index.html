Across

1 across NASALLY

Before 5050, space explorers ultimately pay through the nose (7)

Definition: through the nose · charade + last letter

  • space explorers → NASA the American space agency — the crossword's standard explorers
  • 5050 → LL fifty is L in Roman numerals, so fifty-fifty is LL
  • ultimately pay → Y the final letter of paY

The surface is a complaint about prices and the answer is about anatomy. Two conventions to bank here: L is fifty in Roman numerals, and 'ultimately' asks for a word's last letter.

5 across STAND-BY

Substitute case of stout with case of brandy (5-2)

Definition: Substitute · charade + outer letters

  • case of stout → ST the case of a word is its outside: STouT
  • with → AND with = and, the joiner taken at face value as letters
  • case of brandy → BY BrandY, outside only

Both drinks come in cases in the packing sense — keep the outside, drop the contents — and the little word between them is not joinery at all but three letters of the answer.

10 across OIL RIG

Locale for Derrick’s young lady? ‘I love travelling west’ (3,3)

Definition: Locale for Derrick · charade + reversal

  • young lady → GIRL Derrick's girl, in the surface story at least
  • I → I the clue's own I
  • love → O love is nothing on a tennis scoreboard — a zero

Two derricks in one clue: the man in the surface and the drilling tower in the answer. 'Travelling west' reverses an across entry, reading it right to left.

11 across ACCURATE

A Catholic member of the clergy is faithful (8)

Definition: faithful · charade

  • A → A the clue's own A
  • Catholic → C C is the standard abbreviation, as in RC
  • member of the clergy → CURATE a curate is a junior parish priest

Three pieces laid end to end with no indicator at all, which is the hardest charade to spot: nothing in the sentence admits that anything is being built.

12 across BAR

Prevent Shakespeare forgetting Bottom (3)

Definition: Prevent · deletion + last letter

  • Shakespeare → BARD the Bard of Avon
  • Bottom → D the bottom letter of BARD is the one forgotten

Bottom is a Shakespeare character and a position in a word at the same time, and the clue means the second while pointing at the first.

13 across MISERY

Woe is me! Reckless and extremely risky (6)

Definition: Woe · anagram + outer letters

  • is me! → MISE four letters, recklessly rearranged
  • extremely risky → RY the extremes of the word — its first and last letters

'Woe is me' is one exclamation in the surface and two separate jobs in the parse: the definition stops dead after the first word.

14 across TROPHIES

Degenerates wanting Academy Awards (8)

Definition: Awards · deletion

  • Degenerates → ATROPHIES to atrophy is to waste away, to degenerate
  • Academy → A A = Academy, as in the RA

The Oscars are all over the surface and nowhere in the parse: 'Academy' is worth exactly one letter, and 'Awards' is the definition.

15 across METER

Measure English trout initially found in French sea (5)

Definition: Measure · container + first letter

  • French sea → MER la mer — the wrapper
  • English → E E is the usual abbreviation for English
  • trout initially → T the first letter of trout

MER for the French sea is worth banking: it turns up constantly, and almost always wrapped around something. The angler is here only to supply one initial.

16 across MAKE SENSE

Man seeks new drug to hold water (4,5)

Definition: hold water · anagram

  • Man seeks → MANSEEKS eight letters of fodder, straight out of the lonely-hearts column
  • drug → E E for ecstasy, the crossword's default drug
  • new → anagram indicator

'New' sits between the fodder and the extra letter, shuffling both together. The surface reads as a small ad taking a dark turn, and the answer is an idiom about arguments.

19 across SPINAL TAP

Personal number key during father’s rejected US medical procedure (6,3)

Definition: US medical procedure · reversal + charade + container

  • father’s rejected → SAP PA'S — father's — thrown back to make the wrapper
  • Personal number → PIN a PIN is literally a personal identification number
  • key → ALT the Alt key, on every keyboard

Two pieces go inside at once, and the wrapper is a possessive turned round: PA'S becomes SAP. Nothing here is about the band, or about the spine.

21 across HARRY

Prince arrived in Hungary on vacation (5)

Definition: Prince · container + outer letters

  • Hungary on vacation → HY vacated — emptied out — so only the outer letters stay
  • arrived → ARR arr. is the timetable's abbreviation for arrived

'On vacation' means vacated rather than sunbathing, a pun worth keeping: a country on holiday loses everything inside it.

24 across ALLEY CAT

Stray lately wandering around California (5,3)

Definition: Stray · anagram + container

  • lately → ALLEYT six letters rearranged, and they then close around the state
  • California → CA the two-letter postal abbreviation

The rearranged letters split around CA, which is why the answer's two words do not line up with the clue's — the seam falls inside the anagram.

26 across RHOMBI

Greek character married bachelor, one with diamonds? (6)

Definition: diamonds · charade

  • Greek character → RHO the seventeenth letter of the Greek alphabet
  • married → M M, as on any form
  • bachelor → B B is the crossword's bachelor, from the degree
  • one → I the Roman numeral

Three of the four pieces come out of the abbreviation drawer: married, bachelor and one are all doing letter duty rather than describing anybody.

27 across ICI

British company once part of public infrastructure (3)

Definition: British company once · hidden word

  • public infrastructure → ICI the letters straddle the gap: publIC Infrastructure

'Part of' is the signal that something is buried in the words that follow, and three letters across a word break is where the eye refuses to look.

28 across PRINCESS

Royal papers describing metal crown missing (8)

Definition: Royal · container + deletion

  • papers → PRESS the press is the papers, collectively — the wrapper
  • metal → ZINC one of the commoner metals
  • crown missing → Z the crown of ZINC is its top letter, and it goes, so only INC is described

'Describing' means drawing around, as you describe a circle — a container signal, not a definition one. The crown of a word is the letter on top of it.

29 across DEALER

Trader: ‘Expensive coats, Levi’s – 60% off!’ (6)

Definition: Trader · container + deletion

  • Expensive → DEAR dear = expensive, and it coats what comes next
  • Levi’s → LEVIS the jeans, five letters
  • 60% off → VIS three of those five letters — sixty per cent — are knocked off the price

A sale sign doing arithmetic: sixty per cent off five letters leaves two, and they are the two at the front.

30 across ASSERTS

American to highlight revolting states (7)

Definition: states · charade + reversal

  • American → A A = American, the dictionary's own label
  • highlight → SSERTS STRESS, to highlight, turned round

The surface promises rebellious states and delivers none: 'revolting' is a reversal, and the states are the definition rather than the scenery.

31 across ASPERSE

‘Snake’ – language of slander (7)

Definition: slander · charade

  • Snake → ASP the asp is the crossword's snake of choice
  • language → ERSE Erse is Irish Gaelic, a regular in these grids

The quotation marks warn that 'Snake' is not being used for its meaning. Erse is worth banking: four letters, a language, and endlessly useful to setters.

Down

2 down ASININE

Foolish like 1 + 9? (7)

Definition: Foolish · charade

  • like → AS 'like' = as, the plainest comparison in the language
  • 1 → I the Roman numeral for one
  • 9 → NINE the digit taken as its own name

It looks like arithmetic and it is spelling: a numeral can stand for its Roman letter or for the word we say out loud, and this clue uses one of each.

3 down A FREE REIN

I earn change importing without charge or latitude (1,4,4)

Definition: latitude · anagram + container

  • I earn → AREIN five letters rearranged into A REIN, which then opens up
  • without charge → FREE free = at no charge, and it is imported into the middle

'Change' shuffles the two words in front of it and 'importing' brings something in, so the surface's customs declaration is doing two mechanical jobs at once.

4 down LEG BYE

Extra line to say farewell (3,3)

Definition: Extra · charade

  • line → LEG a leg is one straight stretch of a route or a journey
  • to say farewell → BYE bye is the shortest goodbye there is

Cricket hides in plain sight: the definition is the single word 'Extra', and the two everyday words after it are simply laid end to end.

6 down TICK OVER

Heart admits nothing against being idle (4,4)

Definition: idle · container + charade

  • Heart → TICKER the heart is the body's ticker, and it opens to let two letters in
  • nothing → O the round letter is the crossword's zero
  • against → V v for versus, as on any fixture list

'Admits' is the container signal — something is let inside — and the surface's medical hush is hiding an engine turning over in a car park.

7 down NORTH

Point revealed by 14 or 13? (5)

Definition: Point · charade + last letter + first letters

  • 14 → N spell the number out — fourteeN — and keep its last letter
  • or → OR the clue's own 'or', taken as two letters
  • 13 → TH THirteen, spelled out, leads with these two

Write the numerals out as words and the answer is lying across the join: fourtee | n or th | irteen. Nothing else in the clue does any work, which is what makes it hard.

8 down BOTHERS

Worries relatives when first king abdicates (7)

Definition: Worries · deletion

  • relatives → BROTHERS brothers are relatives
  • first king → R R is Rex, the king — and the clue says it is the first of the word's two Rs

Abdication is a neat deletion signal: the king steps down and leaves the word. Note that the clue has to say which R, because there are two of them.

9 down MARTIAL ARTIST

Dan perhaps mixing art material – it’s missing base (7,6)

Definition: Dan perhaps · anagram + deletion

  • art material → ARTMATERIAL eleven letters of fodder
  • it’s → ITS three more, the apostrophe ignored
  • missing base → E e is the base of natural logarithms, and it is the letter taken out

Fourteen letters go in and thirteen come out, so something has to leave before the shuffle, and 'base' is the mathematician's e. The art student is pure misdirection.

17 down ELABORATE

Add more detail to complex (9)

Definition: Add more detail · double definition

  • Add more detail → ELABORATE the verb: asked to elaborate, you say more
  • complex → ELABORATE the adjective: an elaborate scheme has many worked-up parts

One spelling, two pronunciations: the verb ends like 'rate', the adjective swallows the ending. The split falls after 'detail', with 'to' as the only joinery.

18 down SLICKEST

Celts ski, going downhill with the most smooth manner (8)

Definition: most smooth · anagram

  • Celts ski → CELTSSKI eight letters of fodder
  • going downhill → anagram indicator
  • manner → surface only: it gives the skiers something to be smooth in, and no letters

'Going downhill' does two jobs at once — it is what skiers do, and deterioration is the standard image behind an anagram indicator.

20 down POLARIS

7 star submarine (7)

Definition: 7 star · double definition

  • 7 star → POLARIS solve 7 down first: the star that marks that compass point
  • submarine → POLARIS the Royal Navy's Polaris boats took their name from the missile they carried

A cross-reference: 7 down has to fall first. Both halves then point at one word from opposite worlds, celestial navigation and the cold war.

22 down ROBBERS

They steal over 2 billion euros, not even starting with rupees (7)

Definition: They steal · charade + first letter + alternate letters

  • rupees → R the letter it starts with
  • over → O O is the cricket scorer's abbreviation for an over
  • 2 billion → BB billion is B, and the clue asks for two of them
  • euros → ERS 'not even' keeps the odd letters only: EuRoS

'Not even' is the alternate-letter instruction and 'starting with' says where the R goes rather than what it is. Three currencies, none of them worth any money here.

23 down BRIDES

Second-tier attractions, those with trains? (6)

Definition: those with trains · charade

  • Second-tier → B the second tier of any grading is the B list
  • attractions → RIDES the attractions at a funfair are its rides

The question mark is the setter admitting that not every one of them wears a train, and the railway sense of the word is the trap.

25 down ERNIE

Wise one in Munich on retreat drinking last of beer? (5)

Definition: Wise · reversal + container + last letter

  • one in Munich → EINE 'one' in the German of Munich, before it turns round
  • last of beer → R the final letter of beeR

Munich sets the language rather than the map: the clue wants the German for one. The beer belongs to the surface and supplies a single letter.
