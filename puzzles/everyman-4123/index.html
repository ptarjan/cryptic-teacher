Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,123

Setter: Everyman · Published: Saturday 25 October 2025 · Difficulty: Tough (harder than 85% of the puzzles here) · Grid: 15×15

Across

1 across BEAR HUGS

Embraces giving you headlice, according to Spooner (4,4)

Definition: Embraces · spoonerism

  • headlice → BEAR head lice live in hair, and that word hands its H over to the word beside it, taking a B in return
  • headlice → HUGS lice are bugs; this word gives up its B and takes the H coming the other way

Say the plain version first — headlice are hair bugs — and then trade the two opening sounds. The surface keeps both halves in the same unpleasant scene so neither gives itself away.

5 across WARBLE

Sing happily as blue uniform's ditched following combat (6)

Definition: Sing happily · charade + deletion

  • combat → WAR combat between nations is war
  • uniform → U U is the signaller's word for that letter, and this is the letter being thrown out
  • blue → BLE the colour with the letter just named taken out of it

The military surface does all the misdirection: 'blue uniform' looks like one thing to picture, when the second word is only the signaller's name for a letter.

9 across AGNUS DEI

Prayer that's sung, unfortunately - bad idea all round (5,3)

Definition: Prayer · anagram

  • sung → SUNG four letters offered up for rearrangement, not a description of the prayer
  • idea → IDEA the other four letters, to be mixed in with the first lot

'Sung' wants to belong to the definition — a prayer that is sung — and belongs to the fodder instead, which is why the letters come up short if you leave it there.

10 across BIGOTS

Reactionaries: large (outsize); internally tense (6)

Definition: Reactionaries · charade + container

  • large → BIG the everyday three-letter word for large
  • outsize → OS OS is the size printed on a clothing label for outsize
  • tense → T T is the grammarian's abbreviation for tense

The colon, brackets and semicolon are punctuation for the surface only — both asides are wordplay. 'Tense' is the grammar word here, not the mood.

12 across YANKEE-DOODLE

Fancifully, one's drawn scribble portraying American icon (6-6)

Definition: American icon · charade

  • one's drawn → YANKEE to yank is to draw something with a sharp pull, and the -ee ending names the one it is done to, as in payee or employee
  • scribble → DOODLE the idle scribble made while the mind is somewhere else

'Drawn' looks like a job for a pencil, matching the scribble beside it, and means pulled instead. That switch is the whole difficulty of the clue.

15 across CURLY FRIES

Questionable furry slice as may be served with burger (5,5)

Definition: may be served with burger · anagram

  • furry slice → FURRYSLICE ten letters from a pairing no menu would ever print, ready to be reordered

The surface is an unappetising plateful and every word of it is working: the odd pairing supplies the letters and the doubt cast on it is the instruction.

17 across BALL

Dance and sob audibly (4)

Definition: Dance · homophone

  • sob → BALL to bawl is to sob noisily, and said aloud it can be written the other way

Four letters and three words of clue, so nothing is being assembled: one word is simply spoken and respelt. The definition is the formal dance, not the thing you kick.

19 across ARCH

Knowing what may mark a triumph (4)

Definition: Knowing · double definition

  • Knowing → the sense of sly, teasing amusement — the look that lets you see it is playing with you
  • what may mark a triumph → Rome raised one for a general to ride through on his return, and cities still put them up over a victory

Two definitions and no wordplay, and the join is hard to see because the first word could easily be the start of a longer phrase.

20 across SCRAP METAL

Recalled former MP (Bow) beginning to sing Any Old Iron (5,5)

Definition: Any Old Iron · charade + reversal + first letter

  • former → LATE the late chairman is the former one, no longer in post
  • MP → MP a Member of Parliament, taken at face value from the clue's own letters
  • (Bow) → ARC a bow is a curve, and an arc is the geometer's word for one
  • sing → S the first letter of this word

'Any Old Iron' is a music-hall song about a rag-and-bone man's haul, italicised as a title while defining the haul itself. Bow is an East End constituency, so the politics reads straight.

21 across FRENCH POODLE

Preposterously: Force getting no help with dog, principally a dainty one (6,6)

Definition: a dainty one · anagram + first letter

  • Force getting no help → FORCENOHELP eleven letters from these words, to be dealt out again
  • dog → D the letter at the head of this word, and the twelfth the enumeration asks for

The colon makes the first word look like a heading, and it is the shuffle instruction. 'Dog' sits next to the definition and belongs to the wordplay instead.

26 across OUTFIT

Firm clothing (6)

Definition: Firm · double definition

  • Firm → a business, especially a small one — the crew who do the work
  • clothing → a complete set of clothes worn together and chosen to go with each other

A two-word clue is all definition, so the only question is where the first meaning stops. 'Firm' looks like an adjective describing the second word and is a noun.

27 across HIJACKER

Greeting sailor with hesitant expression: perhaps a pirate? (8)

Definition: perhaps a pirate? · charade

  • Greeting → HI the shortest greeting there is
  • sailor → JACK jack is an old word for a sailor, as in jack tar
  • hesitant expression → ER er is the noise made while the next word is being found

The surface is one polite exchange on a quayside; the parse is three unrelated scraps laid end to end, which is what a charade always looks like once seen.

28 across TIDIER

In better order, more like 13? (6)

Definition: In better order · double definition

  • In better order → the plain sense: things put straight, with less mess about them than before
  • more like 13 → 13 down is a shore washed twice a day, so this wants a comparative built on the word for that movement of the sea

The second half is the setter's own invention rather than a dictionary word: a shore where the sea comes and goes is, in this joke, more tide-y. The question mark owns it.

29 across ONE-SIDED

Being pretentious, Everyman's identified as unbalanced (3-5)

Definition: unbalanced · charade

  • Everyman's → ONES the setter's own by-line, and the lofty way to say 'mine' is 'one's'
  • identified → IDED ID'd is how a police report writes 'identified'

Everyman signs every one of these puzzles, so the name in a clue almost always points back at the setter rather than at anybody in the surface story.

Down

1 down BEAU

Boy essaying amorous undertakings, primarily? (4)

Definition: Boy essaying amorous undertakings, primarily? · &lit + first letters

  • Boy essaying amorous undertakings → BEAU the opening letters of these four words, read straight off in the order they stand

An &lit: one sentence doing both jobs at once. Read for sense it describes a young man trying his luck; read for letters it spells him out.

2 down ACNE

What teenagers are loth be seen spotted in? (4)

Definition: What teenagers are loth be seen spotted in? · cryptic definition

  • loth be seen → the reading the surface pushes: an outfit so embarrassing a self-conscious teenager would hate to be caught wearing it
  • spotted in → the reading the setter meant: not noticed but covered in spots, worn on the face rather than the body

Nothing here comes apart into letters. One phrase does two jobs, because 'be seen in' belongs to clothes and the skin complaint is worn like an outfit nobody chose. The question mark owns the joke.

3 down HUSH-A-BYE

Yeah! Bush composed lullaby (4-1-3)

Definition: lullaby · anagram

  • Yeah! Bush → YEAHBUSH eight letters from an exclamation and a surname, to be dealt out again

The exclamation mark makes the first word look like a stray cry of agreement, and the capital B like a singer or a president. Both are only a letter supply.

4 down GREEK

Like some salad? I'm angry - not quite; I'm alarmed (5)

Definition: Like some salad? · charade + deletion

  • I'm angry → GR an angry growl written down is grr, and it is cut off before its final letter
  • I'm alarmed → EEK eek is the squeak of alarm printed in comic strips

Two noises, not two words. The surface reads as one person's mood swinging about, while the parse wants only the sounds people write down for those moods.

6 down AMIGO

Lammy is goof at regular intervals, pal (5)

Definition: pal · alternate letters

  • Lammy is goof → AMIGO the second letter of this run and then every other letter after it, straight across the word breaks

David Lammy is a British Labour politician, and the surface is a mild insult aimed at him. The parse cares about none of that, only about where his letters fall.

7 down BROADCASTS

Fat collections of actors in TV shows (10)

Definition: TV shows · charade

  • Fat → BROAD fat in the sense of wide — broad in the beam
  • collections of actors → CASTS a cast is the whole company appearing in one production, and several productions have several

'Fat collections of actors' reads as one idea and is really two pieces meeting: the definition is the last two words, not the first.

8 down EASTER LILY

Bloomer as 51 overwhelmed by wind (6,4)

Definition: Bloomer · container

  • wind → EASTERLY a wind is named for where it blows from, so one out of the east is an easterly
  • 51 → LI Roman numerals: L is fifty and I is one

'Bloomer' is a blunder in the surface and a plant in the parse, which is one of the oldest puns in the crossword vocabulary. The wind opens up to let the number in.

11 down GENEVA

Info on electric vehicle's acceleration in European city (6)

Definition: European city · charade

  • Info → GEN gen is British slang for information, shortened from wartime 'general information'
  • electric vehicle → EV EV is the standard shorthand on any car dealer's forecourt
  • acceleration → A physics writes a for acceleration in its equations

In a down clue 'on' means sitting on top of, which is exactly where the first piece goes. The surface pretends to be a news item about the motor trade.

13 down OCEAN-FRONT

None factor in disarray where waves break onto the shore (5-5)

Definition: where waves break onto the shore · anagram

  • None factor → NONEFACTOR ten letters from a pairing that means nothing on its own, waiting to be redealt

'None factor' is not a phrase anybody says, and that is the tell: two words dropped in together with no job except to supply letters.

14 down PROCREATED

Started a family, as seen in ghastly tape-record (10)

Definition: Started a family · anagram

  • tape-record → TAPERECORD the ten letters of this hyphenated verb, handed over to be rearranged

'As seen in' is the shape of a hidden-word signal, and the letters really are all present in that last word — scattered, though, rather than consecutive.

16 down RECIPE

Orders for chef that is leaving flipping épicerie (6)

Definition: Orders for chef · reversal + deletion

  • that is → IE i.e. is the everyday abbreviation of the Latin id est, which translates as 'that is'
  • épicerie → EPICER French for a grocer's shop, with the two letters just named taken off its end

'Flipping' does double duty: mild British swearing in the surface, an instruction to turn the word round in the parse. The italics mark the last word as French rather than English.

18 down SMALL ADS

Newspaper feature showing a duke enthralled by underwear (5,3)

Definition: Newspaper feature · container

  • underwear → SMALLS smalls is the British informal word for underwear, the small items on a washing line
  • a duke → AD A stands for itself and D is the standard abbreviation for duke

The aristocratic scandal of the surface is pure decoration. What matters is the domestic British word for underwear, which opens up at the last letter.

22 down CHIME

Measure of Archimedes: ring a bell? (5)

Definition: ring a bell? · hidden word

  • Archimedes → CHIME five consecutive letters, beginning at the second letter of the name

'Measure of' reads as a quantity and is really asking for a slice of the word beside it. The Greek mathematician is present for his spelling alone.

23 down ORION

Looking up: black ring that shines in the sky (5)

Definition: that shines in the sky · charade + reversal

  • black → NOIR noir is French for black, borrowed by English for bleak films
  • ring → O a ring drawn on paper is a circle, which is the letter O

'Looking up' does two jobs at once: stargazing in the surface, and in the parse an instruction to a down answer to be read from the bottom.

24 down SKID

Small child to lose grip (4)

Definition: to lose grip · charade

  • Small → S S is the size marked on clothing for small
  • child → KID a kid is a child, and was a young goat first

The definition is the last three words, and they read as part of the same sentence about a child holding on to something. It is a tyre that lets go.

25 down BRAD

Pitt the Elder's last character to be overcome by evil? (4)

Definition: Pitt · container

  • evil → BAD evil in its plainest three-letter form
  • the Elder's last character → R the final letter of Elder, a character meaning a single letter here rather than a person

Pitt the Elder was an eighteenth-century British prime minister, which is the surface story. The question mark admits that the definition wants the Hollywood Pitt.
