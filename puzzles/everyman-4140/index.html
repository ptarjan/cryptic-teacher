Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,140

Setter: Everyman · Published: Sunday 22 February 2026 · Difficulty: Moderate (harder than 41% of the puzzles here) · Grid: 15×15

Across

1 across TAKES A TURN

Rolls dice in game, perhaps Occupy Ringed Planet (5,1,4)

Definition: Rolls dice in game, perhaps · charade

  • Occupy → TAKE an army that takes a city occupies it — the military sense of the verb
  • Ringed Planet → SATURN the sixth planet is the one famous for its rings, and the capitals dress the pair up as a title

The capitals and italics make those two words look like the name of a board game. They are two ordinary wordplay pieces, and the spaces in the answer fall nowhere near the spaces in the wordplay.

6 across BLOC

Group using sample of Pablo Casals (4)

Definition: Group · hidden word

  • Pablo Casals → BLOC the four letters run on unbroken from the middle of the first name into the start of the second

Pablo Casals was a Catalan cellist, and a real name planted in a clue is far more often a hiding place than a fact you are expected to know.

9 across WINE O'CLOCK

Drinks time: company kicked back during lock-in we arranged (4,1'5)

Definition: Drinks time · anagram + reversal + container

  • lock-in we → WINELOCK eight letters re-ordered; a lock-in is the British pub custom of drinking on behind bolted doors after closing time
  • company → CO CO is the abbreviation that follows a firm's name, and it turns round before it goes in

The apostrophe in the enumeration (4,1'5) tells you a set phrase is coming. The eight jumbled letters then split in two, with the turned-round pair dropped into the gap.

10 across ODIN

Mythical figure: sculptor's forgotten head (4)

Definition: Mythical figure · deletion

  • sculptor's → RODIN Auguste Rodin, who made The Thinker, is the sculptor crosswords reach for
  • forgotten head → R the R at the front is the letter that goes

The colon is doing the job of an equals sign, and the surface — a sculptor mislaying a head — describes exactly what happens to the letters.

11 across CLOTHES HORSE

Fashionable type puts on blanket, nosebag etc? (7,5)

Definition: Fashionable type · charade

  • puts on → CLOTHES to clothe somebody is to put garments on them; here the verb takes its -s
  • blanket, nosebag etc? → HORSE stable kit: the list names what the animal is dressed in, and 'etc?' admits it is pointing by example

Two halves that share no world at all — a wardrobe on one side, a stable yard on the other — and only the enumeration hints that the phrase is a single compound.

15 across ROOKERY

Pooh's friend Kanga (to begin with) and Eeyore regularly muddled in bird's home (7)

Definition: bird's home · charade + first letter + alternate letters + anagram

  • Pooh's friend → ROO Roo is the young kangaroo of the Hundred Acre Wood
  • Kanga (to begin with) → K Roo's mother supplies her initial and nothing more
  • Eeyore → ERY the first, third and fifth letters of the donkey's name are E, Y and R, and they are then re-ordered

Three of Pooh's neighbours in one line, and only the first is taken whole: the others give up a single letter and a scattering. 'Muddled' governs those three letters alone.

16 across SUCCOUR

Comfort from a mug, reportedly (7)

Definition: Comfort · homophone

  • a mug → SUCCOUR a mug is British slang for somebody easily fooled — a sucker — and the two spellings are said the same way

The surface offers a hot drink in a cup, which is the wrong sort of mug entirely, and the spelling that comes out shares almost nothing with the one that went in.

17 across ACERBIC

Grimacer bickering, somewhat bitter (7)

Definition: bitter · hidden word

  • Grimacer bickering → ACERBIC the run starts four letters into the first word and stops three letters into the second, crossing the space between them

'Grimacer' is barely a word, and a setter reaching for an awkward noun like that is usually building a hiding place rather than a picture.

19 across PRESENT

Quietly take exception to gift (7)

Definition: gift · charade

  • Quietly → P p is piano in a score, the composer's mark for playing softly
  • take exception to → RESENT to resent something is to take exception to it and go on doing so

Musical marks are worth learning as a set — p for quiet, f for loud — because a lone letter has to come from somewhere, and this is where setters fetch it.

20 across CHANGE COURSE

Not much dough: part of meal to take different direction (6,6)

Definition: take different direction · charade

  • Not much dough → CHANGE dough is slang for money, and change is the small stuff — coins rather than notes
  • part of meal → COURSE a meal arrives in courses, starter then main then pudding, one part at a time

The colon looks like it separates definition from wordplay and does not: everything before 'to' is wordplay, and the definition is the last three words.

23 across UGLY

Unattractive on the outside, underling and lackey (4)

Definition: Unattractive · outer letters

  • underling → UG the letters at each end of the word, in the order they stand
  • lackey → LY the same trick on the second word, its outer pair

Underling and lackey mean the same thing, and that redundancy is the tell: they are in the clue for their shapes, not their sense.

24 across INTOXICATE

Supply excitation? (10)

Definition: Supply excitation? · &lit + anagram

  • excitation → INTOXICATE ten letters go in and ten come out, with none left over

An &lit two words long: the whole clue is the definition and the whole clue is the wordplay. The trap is reading 'supply' as the verb, to provide.

25 across BUST

Shatter sculpture (4)

Definition: Shatter · double definition

  • Shatter → BUST the informal verb for breaking something outright, the way a dropped mug goes
  • sculpture → BUST the carved head and shoulders that stands on a plinth

Two words, no wordplay and nothing to move: one verb, one noun, and the clue never says which of them is the definition.

26 across STREAKIEST

See skirt at resort that's most in need of a clean (10)

Definition: most in need of a clean · anagram

  • See skirt at → STREAKIEST ten letters, the little word 'at' among them as fodder rather than as joinery

'At resort' reads as one phrase about a seaside holiday, which is how the clue hides the join between the end of the fodder and the instruction after it.

Down

1 down TOWS

Pulls studious type up (4)

Definition: Pulls · reversal

  • studious type → SWOT British school slang for the pupil who is always working, usually said with a sneer

'Up' contributes nothing to the picture of a hard-working student, and a word that earns no keep on the surface is nearly always the instruction.

2 down KIND

Family with daughter that's a strain (4)

Definition: a strain · charade

  • Family → KIN your kin are the family you are related to — next of kin
  • daughter → D D is the standard abbreviation for daughter on a family tree

'Strain' does quiet work here: not the effort or the pulled muscle the surface suggests, but a variety, as in a strain of flu.

3 down SHOULDER BAG

In the event that old monarch given black and silver luggage item ... (11)

Definition: luggage item · charade

  • In the event that → SHOULD English uses this word for a supposition: should it rain, we'll eat inside
  • old monarch → ER ER is Elizabeth Regina, the late Queen's cipher, printed on postboxes across Britain
  • black → B B is black on a pencil and in chess notation
  • silver → AG Ag is silver on the periodic table, from the Latin argentum

Four pieces in a row, and the seam is invisible: the answer breaks after SHOULDER while the wordplay breaks after SHOULD. The trailing dots only run the surface on into the next clue.

4 down TOLSTOY

Tales ordinarily long, scribe telling of yore, primarily? (7)

Definition: Tales ordinarily long, scribe telling of yore, primarily? · &lit + first letters

  • Tales ordinarily long → TOL the opening letter of each of the three words, in the order they are printed
  • scribe telling of yore → STOY four more openings, and the little word 'of' earns its letter like the rest

An &lit: read once, the sentence describes a long-winded old storyteller; read again, every word hands over its first letter. Nothing is left for a separate definition.

5 down ROCKETS

Leaves sun in spaceships (7)

Definition: spaceships · charade

  • Leaves → ROCKET the peppery salad leaf, sold as arugula outside Britain
  • sun → S S is the almanac's single letter for the sun

'Leaves' wants to be a verb about departing, which is exactly what a spaceship does. It is the salad instead, and the plural belongs to the definition rather than to it.

7 down LEDERHOSEN

Seen her old fancy man's attire (10)

Definition: man's attire · anagram

  • Seen her old → LEDERHOSEN ten letters go in and every one of them is used once

The surface is an ex spotted in his finery, so 'old fancy man' reads as one phrase — and that is precisely where the instruction is buried.

8 down CONSECRATE

Dupe taking wings off used car that's seen better days: bless! (10)

Definition: bless · charade + deletion

  • Dupe → CON to con somebody is to dupe them out of their money
  • used → SE the U at the front and the D at the back fly off, and the middle pair is what stays
  • car that's seen better days → CRATE a crate is a rattling old vehicle, the same joke as calling a car a heap

'Dupe' is the con and not the shabby car, and 'bless!' reads as an aside about something decrepit rather than as the definition it turns out to be.

12 down HOCKEY STICK

Spooner's spoken of thickset yokel's sporting equipment (6,5)

Definition: sporting equipment · spoonerism

  • thickset → HOCKEY a thickset build is a stocky one, and it gives up the ST at its front
  • yokel → STICK a hick is a country bumpkin, and it collects the ST the first word let go

Say the plain pair aloud first and the swap does the rest. The stray 's' on 'yokel's' belongs to the surface possessive, not to the wordplay.

13 down BREADCRUMB

Apply gratin to crab? Er, dumb move (10)

Definition: Apply gratin to · anagram

  • crab? Er, dumb → BREADCRUMB the question mark and the comma are punctuation rather than letters: ten in, ten out

'Dumb move' reads as one insult, which is where the split hides: 'dumb' is fodder and only 'move' is the instruction. 'Er' is hesitation on the surface and two more letters in the parse.

14 down POLE VAULTS

E European crypts in which people get high (4,6)

Definition: in which people get high · charade

  • E European → POLE E is the usual shortening for East, and a native of Poland is one Eastern European
  • crypts → VAULTS a vault is the stone chamber under a church where the dead are laid, which is what a crypt is

'Get high' is athletic rather than chemical, so the drug den the surface builds in a crypt is the reading to drop.

18 down COCONUT

You can get butter from one person that's mad on hot drink, did you say? (7)

Definition: You can get butter from one · charade + homophone

  • hot drink → COCO cocoa is the hot chocolate drink, and said aloud its final A is not heard
  • person that's mad on → NUT a nut for something is an enthusiast — a jazz nut, a football nut — and 'mad on' is the phrase that makes one

'Butter' points at dairy and 'mad' at insanity, and neither is in play: one is what the fruit yields, the other is plain enthusiasm.

19 down PLUS-ONE

Date, such as 31st is to 30th (4-3)

Definition: Date · double definition

  • Date → PLUSONE the guest you are allowed to bring to a wedding, named for the way the invitation counts you
  • such as 31st is to 30th → PLUSONE one number stands to the other exactly as the phrase says: an increase of a single unit

Both halves are the same phrase read two ways, a social one and an arithmetical one, and a comma is all that separates them.

21 down FADE

A short-lived pleasure, Everyman's beginning to decline (4)

Definition: decline · charade + first letter

  • A short-lived pleasure → FAD a fad is a craze everybody drops again within a season
  • Everyman's → E the first letter of the setter's own pen name, which he likes to sign into his puzzles

Everyman writing himself into the grid is a habit of his worth remembering: the name is there to be cut down to a letter, not to be thought about.

22 down MEET

Run into rain that's upside-down (4)

Definition: Run into · reversal

  • rain → TEEM to teem is to rain hard: 'teeming down' is the British phrase for a downpour

'Run into' is the accidental sense — bumping into somebody in the street — while the surface's picture of running out into the rain is the reading to let go.
