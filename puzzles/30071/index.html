Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,071

Setter: Brendan · Published: 29 July 2026 · Difficulty: Brutal (harder than 100% of the puzzles here) · Grid: 15×15

Across

4 across NEWBIE

Pay attention about opposing points, namely for novice (6)

Definition: novice · container + charade

  • Pay attention → NB NB — nota bene, 'note well' — splits around the insertion
  • opposing points → EW east and west, opposite compass points
  • namely → IE i.e. = namely

The surface sounds like a debating tip. 'Opposing points' is a classic signal for a compass pair, and NB and IE are both Latin tags worth memorising.

6 across DOCUSOAP

Medic and aged Brit traversing America for TV series (8)

Definition: TV series · charade + container

  • Medic → DOC doc = medic
  • aged Brit → OAP old-age pensioner, the standard aged Brit
  • America → US US, straddled ('traversing') by DOC and OAP

A docusoap — documentary crossed with soap opera — is another of this grid's blended words. OAP, old-age pensioner, is the crossword's standard Brit of a certain age.

9 across WEIRDO

Current controller leading party is eccentric (6)

Definition: eccentric · charade

  • Current controller → WEIR a weir controls a river's current — not electricity
  • party → DO a do = a party, a bit of a do

The 'current' is a river's, not a wire's — a weir holds it back. DO for 'party' is core crossword vocabulary, and here 'eccentric' is a noun.

10 across INFOTECH

Foci then shifted to computer applications etc` (8)

Definition: computer applications etc · anagram

  • Foci then → FOCITHEN anagram fodder, 8 letters
  • shifted → anagram indicator

Infotech squeezes information and technology into one word, like so many answers in this grid. The stray mark after 'etc' is the Guardian's typo, not a clue.

11 across PORTMANTEAU

Piece of luggage left on board by guy you heard consuming drink (11)

Definition: Piece of luggage · charade + homophone + container

  • left on board → PORT port is the left side when you're on board ship
  • guy → MAN man = guy
  • you heard → U U sounds like 'you'
  • drink → TEA TEA is swallowed ('consuming') by MAN + U

Portmanteau — Lewis Carroll's word for two meanings packed into one case — is this puzzle's theme named in a single answer. On board ship, left is always PORT.

15 across ASSEGAI

Weapon, for instance, deployed in unending attack (7)

Definition: Weapon · container + deletion

  • for instance → EG e.g. = for instance
  • unending attack → ASSAI ASSAIL (attack) 'unending' — its final L is dropped

An assegai is a slender African throwing spear — an obscure answer, so the wordplay is generous. 'Unending' clips the tail off ASSAIL before EG is deployed inside.

17 across TUSSORE

Coarse fabric making us hurt after short time (7)

Definition: Coarse fabric · charade

  • short time → T time cut short: T
  • us → US the word itself
  • hurt → SORE sore = hurt

Tussore, a coarse wild silk, may be unfamiliar — the kindly charade makes up for it. 'Short time' cutting TIME down to T is a move you'll meet constantly.

18 across LABRADOODLE

Awfully adorable old pet (11)

Definition: pet · anagram

  • adorable old → ADORABLEOLD anagram fodder, 11 letters
  • Awfully → anagram indicator

An anagram that behaves like a definition too: a labradoodle — labrador crossed with poodle, another blend — genuinely is an awfully adorable old pet. Brendan picks fodder that tells the truth.

22 across TEAMMATE

Fellow in squad raving at me in first half, otherwise in second (8)

Definition: Fellow in squad · anagram

  • at me → TEAM 'raving' scrambles AT ME into the first half
  • at me → MATE 'otherwise' scrambles the same four letters differently for the second half

One four-letter scrap, AT ME, is shuffled twice over — 'raving' one way for the front half, 'otherwise' another for the back. Economical fodder, spent twice.

23 across ORWELL

One English author or another abridged (6)

Definition: One English author · charade + deletion

  • or → OR the word itself, lifted straight into the answer
  • another abridged → WELL H G WELLS, another English author, abridged — the final S is cut

George Orwell is the 'one', H G Wells the 'another', losing his final S. The pair also anchor the grid's theme: Orwell coined NEWSPEAK, sitting at 4 down.

24 across ELEPHANT

Owner of trunk that must be repacked for the plane (8)

Definition: Owner of trunk · anagram

  • the plane → THEPLANE anagram fodder, 8 letters
  • must be repacked → anagram indicator

The trunk belongs to the animal, not the luggage rack — and by lovely economy the fodder is 'the plane' all that repacking was for.

25 across KNOTTY

Like bad deal, difficult to undo? (6)

Definition: Like bad deal · double definition

  • Like bad deal → KNOTTY deal is cheap softwood — a bad plank is knotty, full of knots
  • difficult to undo → KNOTTY a knotty problem is hard to untangle

'Deal' is timber — pine or fir — not a bargain, and a poor plank is full of knots. The second sense, a knotty problem, shares the same tangled picture.

Down

1 down LIB-DEM

Line from the same source about British political type (3-3)

Definition: political type · charade + container

  • Line → L l = line, as in a line of verse
  • from the same source → IDEM idem is the Latin citation tag meaning 'the same source as before'
  • British → B B = British, wrapped inside IDEM ('about')

IDEM is Latin for 'the same' — footnotes use it to repeat a source just cited. Wrap it round B and out comes a compressed political label, the first of this grid's many squashed-together words.

2 down POINSETTIA

Popular houseplant I set in a pot adapted (10)

Definition: Popular houseplant · anagram

  • I set in a pot → ISETINAPOT anagram fodder, 10 letters
  • adapted → anagram indicator

The fodder hides in plain sight as potting instructions — the surface is a gardener repotting, which is exactly where a houseplant answer hides best.

3 down AUTOCUES

They help people with deliveries after car gets in line, we hear (8)

Definition: They help people with deliveries · charade + homophone

  • car → AUTO auto = car
  • line, we hear → CUES sounds like 'queues' — lines of people

Autocues help speakers with the delivery of their lines — nothing to do with parcels. The homophone touches only 'line': queues become CUES.

4 down NEWSPEAK

Quartet playing best performance? That’s made-up propaganda (8)

Definition: made-up propaganda · charade

  • Quartet playing → NEWS the quartet is the four compass points N, E, W, S, 'playing' together in that order
  • best performance → PEAK a peak is a best performance — being at your peak

Newspeak is the invented propaganda language of Orwell's Nineteen Eighty-Four — and its author is waiting at 23 across. 'Quartet' for the four compass points is a trick worth filing away.

5 down WAITRESS

Female serving as half of ward sister’s replacement (8)

Definition: Female serving · charade + deletion + anagram

  • half of ward → WA the first half of WArd — the RD is dropped
  • sister → ITRESS 'replacement' redeals the letters of SISTER

The surface stays on the hospital ward, but 'female serving' means waiting tables. A word's 'replacement' — its letters redealt — is a gentler anagram signal than the usual chaos words.

7 down OP-ED

Newspaper article began in old-fashioned way (2-2)

Definition: Newspaper article · double definition

  • Newspaper article → OPED an op-ed, the opinion piece opposite the editorial page
  • began in old-fashioned way → OPED 'ope' is archaic for open, and what oped, opened — began

An op-ed sits opposite the editorial page — that's where the name comes from. 'Ope' is poetic old English for open, and a meeting that opens has begun.

8 down PAHS

Negative reactions from pedant and his sort, primarily (4)

Definition: Negative reactions · first letters

  • pedant and his sort → PAHS first letters ('primarily') of Pedant And His Sort

'Primarily' points at first letters, and the pedant's own initials spell out his little snorts of contempt — pah! The fodder suits the answer beautifully.

12 down NAIL-BITING

Suspenseful part in capture (4-6)

Definition: Suspenseful · container

  • part → BIT a bit = a part
  • capture → NAILING nailing = capturing; it opens up around BIT

To nail a culprit is to capture them, and the -ING form opens up to swallow the BIT. 'Suspenseful' is the whole definition — one word doing honest work.

13 down WORDFEST

Concluding pieces from head of state in last literary event (8)

Definition: literary event · last letters + container

  • head of state → DFE the concluding pieces — last letters — of heaD, oF and statE
  • last → WORST coming last is doing worst; DFE slots inside

'Concluding pieces from' harvests the last letters of all three words that follow, even little 'of'. A wordfest — word plus festival — keeps the grid's blended-word theme running.

14 down TELEPLAY

Drama as seen from the box (8)

Definition: Drama as seen from the box · charade + &lit

  • the box → TELE the box = the telly, TELE for short
  • Drama → PLAY a play = a drama, following TELE

The whole clue is the definition — a teleplay is exactly drama seen on the box — while TELE and PLAY rebuild it, so every word earns its keep twice.

16 down GALUMPHS

Proceeds clumsily with talk about growth and height (8)

Definition: Proceeds clumsily · container + charade

  • talk → GAS gas = idle talk; it wraps 'about' the rest
  • growth → LUMP a lump = a growth
  • height → H h = height, as on measurements

To galumph — Lewis Carroll's blend of gallop and triumph, from Jabberwocky — keeps the portmanteau theme going. GAS for 'talk' is standard crossword currency.

19 down OARING

Bellowing, losing head, and having a row (6)

Definition: having a row · deletion

  • Bellowing, losing head → OARING ROARING (bellowing) loses its head — the R goes

Two rows: the definition's row rhymes with 'go' — pulling oars — while the surface's shouting match rhymes with 'cow'. Beheading ROARING settles which one you need.

20 down STYE

Pound’s gone out of fashion – that could affect one’s view of things (4)

Definition: that could affect one’s view of things · deletion

  • Pound → L £ is written L, from Latin libra — this is the letter that goes
  • fashion → STYLE style = fashion, left as STYE once the L has gone

'Pound' giving L, from the Latin libra behind the £ sign, is everywhere in crosswords. A stye is the eyelid swelling that genuinely does affect your view of things.

21 down CASE

Legal action that may be taken on by boarder (4)

Definition: Legal action · double definition

  • Legal action → CASE the case a lawyer takes on
  • taken on by boarder → CASE the suitcase a passenger takes on board

Two cases: the one a lawyer takes on, and the one a passenger takes on board. 'Boarder' does the misleading — it sounds like a lodger, not a traveller.
