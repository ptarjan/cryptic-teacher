Across

7 across MANOEUVRE

Island getting upset over EU operation (9)

Definition: operation · charade + anagram

A manoeuvre is a planned movement of troops or vehicles, which is 'operation' in the sense of an exercise carried out rather than surgery.

  • Island → MAN the Isle of Man, the crossword's default island
  • over EU → OVEREU anagram fodder, six letters
  • getting upset — Something upset has been tipped over and its contents land in no particular order, which is what happens to the letters after it.

A bare 'island' is worth trying as MAN before anything else — the Isle of Man, Iona and Elba are the three the setters reach for.

8 across CRANE

Bird in bamboo across river (5)

Definition: Bird · container

A crane is a tall long-legged wading bird; the lifting machine took its name from the bird's outstretched neck, not the other way round.

  • bamboo → CANE bamboo is a giant cane
  • river → R R is the mapmaker's abbreviation for river
  • across — A thing laid across another lies over it from one side to the other, so the letter named next is covered rather than followed.

'Bird in bamboo' sounds like a hiding place and isn't: 'in' is only joining the definition to the wordplay, and the real instruction is the word after it.

9 across REGIMENTS

I’m welcomed by chaps following soldiers in big groups (9)

Definition: big groups · charade + container

A regiment is a permanent army unit made up of several battalions, so 'big groups' is the plain sense — large bodies of people organised together.

  • soldiers → RE the Royal Engineers, the two letters a clue's soldiers nearly always want
  • chaps → GENTS gents is the everyday word for chaps, and it is what splits open
  • I’m → IM the clue's own two words, taken as two letters
  • welcomed by — To be welcomed by someone is to be taken into their house, so these letters end up inside the word that does the welcoming.
  • following — Following a body of men means walking along behind them, so this piece is written after them rather than in front.

RE for Royal Engineers is worth learning by heart: soldiers, sappers and engineers all point at the same pair of letters, and one of them turns up most weeks.

10 across INANE

Senseless, not sharp in the head, unbalanced (5)

Definition: Senseless · deletion + first letter

Inane means empty of meaning — an inane remark carries nothing at all, which is what calling it senseless says.

  • unbalanced → INSANE someone unbalanced is out of their mind
  • sharp → S the letter at the head of Sharp — this is the one that goes
  • not — A plain negative is enough to take something away: what comes after it is what the longer word has to do without.
  • in the head — The head of a word is its front, so all that is meant by it is the single letter the word begins with.

Three comma-separated insults, and each is doing a different job: the first defines, the middle pair name a letter to lose, and the last supplies the word to lose it from.

12 across DUTIES

Suited to perform services (6)

Definition: services · anagram

Duties are the tasks a post obliges you to carry out, and services covers the same ground: work owed rather than a favour freely given.

  • Suited → SUITED anagram fodder, six letters
  • to perform → anagram indicator
  • to perform — A piece is performed in whatever arrangement the players work it up into — a loose signal, but the only word here asking for anything to be done.

When the first word of a three-word clue has exactly as many letters as the enumeration asks for, try shuffling it before you try anything else.

13 across FRETSAWS

Worries, as wife’s picked up tools (8)

Definition: tools · charade + container

A fretsaw is the fine-bladed saw used for cutting curves in thin wood, so 'tools' defines by category rather than by synonym.

  • Worries → FRETS to fret is to worry away at something
  • as → AS the clue's own word, taken as two letters and then prised apart
  • wife → W W is the standard abbreviation for wife
  • picked up — A car that picks you up takes you inside it, so the letter collected here sits within the short word beside it, not after it.

The apostrophe-s here is 'has', pure grammar, and belongs to the DIY scene on the surface rather than to the letters — not every 's in a clue is an S.

14 across ABANDON

Give up wearing a ring? (7)

Definition: Give up · charade

To abandon something is to give it up for good and walk away from it, rather than merely handing it over to someone else.

  • a → A the clue's own article, taken as a letter
  • ring → BAND a wedding band is a ring
  • wearing → ON the clothes you are wearing are the ones you have on

The wordplay is the phrase 'a band on', which is all that wearing a ring amounts to — and the question mark is there because the whole sentence can be read that way.

17 across DAFTEST

Most stupid to try bucking trend first (7)

Definition: Most stupid · charade + reversal

Daft is the friendly British word for silly, so its superlative covers 'most stupid' without the sting the plainer word carries.

  • trend → DAF a passing trend is a fad, written backwards
  • to try → TEST to try something out is to test it
  • bucking — A bucking horse throws its rider over its own head, and the word it is attached to lands the other way round.
  • first — First means at the front of the answer, so the piece just turned round goes ahead of the other one.

'Bucking trend' is one idea on the surface — going against the fashion — and two separate instructions underneath, which is why the seam is so hard to see.

20 across DOCTRINE

Teaching medic to withhold oxygen in first sign of emergency (8)

Definition: Teaching · deletion + charade + first letter

A doctrine is a body of teaching held and handed on by a church or a party — teaching as the thing taught, not the act of doing it.

  • medic → DOCTOR the everyday word for a medic, before it is made to give something up
  • oxygen → O O is oxygen's symbol on the periodic table — this is what is held back
  • in → IN the clue's own word, taken as two letters
  • emergency → E the first letter of Emergency
  • to withhold — To withhold something is to keep it back, so the letter named after it never reaches the answer.
  • first sign of — The first sign of anything is whatever shows at the very start, which here is one opening letter.

A first-aid scene on the surface, and it is the second O the medic loses. Watch 'in': here it is two letters of the answer, not the link word it usually is.

22 across SETTER

Someone like me puts tester to use (6)

Definition: Someone like me · anagram

The setter is the compiler who writes the puzzle, so 'someone like me' is that person pointing at themselves: a convention worth learning early.

  • tester → TESTER anagram fodder, six letters
  • to use → anagram indicator
  • to use — Putting something to use means finding a fresh job for it, and letters given a fresh job turn up in a fresh order.

'Me' is the person who wrote the clue — a standing crossword joke — and 'tester' describes what they are doing to you while you read it.

24 across SERUM

‘Kentish Spirit’ is thin, watery liquid (5)

Definition: thin, watery liquid · charade

Serum is the pale watery part of blood that is left once the clot is taken out, which the definition describes exactly.

  • Kentish → SE Kent sits in the south-eastern corner of England
  • Spirit → RUM rum is the sailor's spirit, distilled from sugar cane

The quotation marks dress the first two words up as the name of a pub or a boat, which is exactly why they do not look like a compass point and a drink.

25 across RACEGOERS

Of course, they are spectators! (9)

Definition: Of course, they are spectators! · cryptic definition

The crowd at a meeting are spectators of a course in the most literal sense the phrase allows, which is what the pun turns on.

  • Of course → the throwaway agreement — naturally, obviously — which is how the sentence asks to be read
  • they are spectators → the second reading takes the two words before it literally: these are the people of the course, out at the rails watching

Two words carry the whole joke: 'of course' is an idiom on the surface and a possessive underneath, and the exclamation mark is the setter's nudge that something is up.

26 across VEINS

Some soave in small vessels (5)

Definition: vessels · hidden word

Veins are the vessels that carry blood back to the heart, so 'vessels' is the anatomical sense of the word rather than ships or jars.

  • soave in small → VEINS five consecutive letters, running across the ends of three words in a row
  • Some — Some of a thing is a part of it and not the whole, so what is wanted is a stretch of letters cut out of the words that follow.

An Italian white and a tasting note make a smooth surface, and the definition waiting at the end of it turns out to be anatomical rather than nautical.

27 across REINVENTS

Puts a new spin on horse-based air conditioning? (9)

Definition: Puts a new spin on · charade

To reinvent something is to make it over into a new form, which is what putting a new spin on a thing means: presenting it afresh.

  • horse-based → REIN the strap a rider steers with, and it is based on the horse
  • air conditioning → VENTS the slatted openings air conditioning blows through

The surface invents an absurd product and the question mark apologises for it, which is enough to hide the fact that the first three words are a plain definition.

Down

1 down GATEAU

Sweet treat dunked in senega tea, unusually (6)

Definition: Sweet treat · hidden word

A gateau is a rich iced cake, the French word English keeps for the fancy end of the trolley rather than everyday baking.

  • senega tea, unusually → GATEAU six consecutive letters, starting in the middle of the first word and finishing in the third
  • dunked in — Dunking pushes something down below the surface of what surrounds it, so the letters wanted are submerged in the words that follow.

'Unusually' looks like an anagram signal and is really just more hiding place: the letters need no shuffling, and a comma is no barrier to a concealed run.

2 down LOGICIAN

Following write-up, I am able to take on one person skilled at thought (8)

Definition: person skilled at thought · charade + container

A logician works professionally at reasoning, so the definition is thought as a discipline with rules rather than anyone who happens to think hard.

  • write-up → LOG a log is the written-up record of what happened
  • I → I the clue's own pronoun, taken as a letter
  • am able → CAN 'I can' is 'I am able' — and this is the word that opens up
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one
  • Following — To follow is to walk behind, so everything named afterwards is written after the first piece rather than before it.
  • to take on — A firm that takes someone on brings them inside it, so the piece named next is absorbed rather than added at the end.

The surface is a job application, which is why the break after 'write-up' is invisible: 'I am able' stops being a boast and becomes three separate scraps of spelling.

3 down RULERS

Those in charge crushing a surreal rebellion (6)

Definition: Those in charge · anagram + deletion

Rulers are the people holding power over a country, which is what being in charge amounts to; the thing in a pencil case is a different sense.

  • surreal → SURREAL anagram fodder, before the deletion
  • crushing a → the article is squashed out, taking one A with it
  • rebellion → anagram indicator
  • crushing — To crush something is to squash it out of existence, so the little word it lands on never reaches the answer.
  • rebellion — A rebellion overthrows the established order, and the letters left behind end up in a new order too.

On the surface a coup is being put down; underneath, the rebellion is what does the damage, and it only starts once a single letter has been crushed.

4 down GRATIFY

Leader of group to sign and approve, please (7)

Definition: please · charade + first letter

To gratify someone is to give them pleasure or satisfaction, which is 'please' in its verb sense rather than the polite formula.

  • group → G the first letter of Group
  • to sign and approve → RATIFY to ratify a treaty is to sign it formally into force
  • Leader of — A leader walks at the front of the party, so what is handed over is the letter standing at the front of the word.

Two verbs sit at either end of this clue and the quiet one is the definition: 'please' as something you do to a person, not the word you add to a request.

5 down FRONDS

Leaves off career to retrain, and succeeds in the end (6)

Definition: Leaves · last letters

Fronds are the long divided leaves of a fern or a palm, so the definition is the botanical sense — a particular kind of leaf, not foliage in general.

  • off → F ofF — last letter
  • career → R careeR — last letter
  • to → O tO — last letter
  • retrain → N retraiN — last letter
  • and → D anD — last letter
  • succeeds → S succeedS — last letter
  • in the end — What comes at a word's finish is its final letter, and every word in this run hands over whichever letter it stops at.

The surface tells a complete story about a mid-life change of job, so the instruction sitting at the end of it reads as the happy ending rather than as directions.

6 down UNKNOWNS

Sunk in snow-drifts – is getting out what might cause difficulty? (8)

Definition: what might cause difficulty? · anagram + deletion

The unknowns in a plan are the things nobody can put a value on yet, and it is exactly those that make a job hard to finish.

  • Sunk in snow → SUNKINSNOW anagram fodder, before the deletion
  • is → these two letters are the ones that leave
  • drifts → anagram indicator
  • drifts — Snow that drifts has been blown out of whatever shape it was lying in, and the letters go the same way.
  • getting out — Getting out is leaving, so the little word named just before it walks away instead of joining the mix.

The dash does the work of a full stop. Everything before it is a car buried in a drift; everything after it is a two-letter word climbing out before the shake-up.

11 down MEGA

Really big game to play (4)

Definition: Really big · anagram

Mega is the prefix meaning a million, borrowed as slang: a mega party is an enormous one, so it stands in for 'really big'.

  • game → GAME anagram fodder, four letters
  • to play → anagram indicator
  • to play — Play is the slack in a rope or a hinge, and letters with that much give in them will not stay where they were put.

The definition is playground slang, which is fair but never the first sense you meet: the word is really the scientific prefix for a million, let loose as an adjective.

15 down BROKERED

Facilitated purchase of very poor chianti? (8)

Definition: Facilitated purchase of · charade

To broker a deal is to arrange it between buyer and seller, so the past tense answers 'facilitated purchase of' — the middleman's verb.

  • very poor → BROKE someone broke has run out of money altogether
  • chianti → RED chianti is a Tuscan wine, and the question mark admits it is only one example of the colour

The definition runs to three words and the wordplay to two, and the join is invisible because 'purchase of very poor chianti' reads as one miserable shopping trip.

16 down OHIO

State how you might answer phone within two rings (4)

Definition: State · container

Ohio is a midwestern American state, so 'State' defines by example: one of fifty, chosen because its spelling happens to suit the wordplay.

  • two rings → OO a ring drawn on the page is an O, and the clue asks for a pair of them
  • how you might answer phone → HI the two-letter greeting you say when you pick up
  • within — Within means inside the boundary of a thing, so the greeting goes between the pair named next rather than beside them.

'Within two rings' is telephone etiquette on the surface and an instruction underneath — the same three words doing both jobs without changing a letter.

18 down TATTOOED

Having a depiction that’s only skin-deep? (8)

Definition: Having a depiction that’s only skin-deep? · &lit

Someone tattooed carries a design inked into the skin, so 'only skin-deep' is meant literally here rather than as the usual dismissal of something superficial.

  • a depiction that’s only skin-deep → TATTOO a picture that goes no further than the layer of skin it is inked into
  • Having → ED the ending English adds to say someone owns one: moneyed, bearded, talented
  • Having — This is the word that turns a thing into an adjective about its owner — a man with money is moneyed, a man with a beard bearded.

There is no second half to hunt for: the sentence defines the answer and builds it at the same time, which is what an all-in-one clue does.

19 down DEBATER

Angrily berated one making an argument (7)

Definition: one making an argument · anagram

A debater argues a case formally, for or against a motion, so the definition is 'argument' in the reasoned sense rather than a row.

  • berated → BERATED anagram fodder, seven letters
  • Angrily → anagram indicator
  • Angrily — Anger throws things about without caring where they land, so the word beside it comes apart and is put back differently.

Berating someone angrily is precisely what the answer is not meant to do: a debate is an argument conducted with rules and a chairperson.

21 down TRUANT

School leaver? (6)

Definition: School leaver? · cryptic definition

A truant leaves school without permission, so the answer takes 'leaver' at its word, while the phrase as a whole normally means someone who has finished school.

  • School leaver → the phrase everybody knows: a sixteen-year-old who has finished their education and is looking for a job
  • ? → the question mark is the whole warning — take 'leaver' literally, as somebody walking out during the day rather than at the end of the years

Nothing here to take apart: no indicator, no fodder, no letters to move. When a two-word clue offers none of those, it is one phrase with two readings and you want the second.

22 down SIENNA

Ms Hathaway is on the way up, Ms Miller (6)

Definition: Ms Miller · charade + reversal

Ms Miller is the actor Sienna Miller, so this is a definition by example — her first name doubles as the earthy red-brown pigment.

  • Hathaway → ANNE the first name of the actor who starred in The Devil Wears Prada
  • is → IS the clue's own word, taken as two letters
  • on the way up — This is a down entry, so anything travelling upwards is read from the bottom of the column back to the top.

Two actors in one short clue: one supplies letters and the other is the definition, and the join between them is the little word 'is' doing no grammatical work at all.

23 down ERRATA

Slips on slips? (6)

Definition: Slips · double definition

Errata are an author's slips in the sense of mistakes, and they reach the reader on a slip of paper bound into the book: both senses at once.

  • Slips → the everyday word for small mistakes, the kind that get through proofreading
  • on slips → in publishing these are printed on a loose leaf of paper tipped into the front of the book

The same word twice, in two senses — the setter's whole joke, and the question mark is the only sign that a pun rather than a repetition is intended.
