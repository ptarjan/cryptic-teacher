Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,899

Across

1 across DIABETES

Awful telephone company's reported two types of complaint (8)

Definition: two types of complaint · charade + homophone

  • Awful → DIRE dire straits are awful ones
  • telephone company's → BT'S BT is Britain's best-known telephone company
  • Awful telephone company's → DIABETES run the two pieces together and the break between them moves, leaving one medical word

The setter is counting on you hearing an advert for a phone network. Everything depends on saying the pieces quickly rather than looking at them.

6 across SHRIMP

Little'un runs into quiet kid causing trouble (6)

Definition: Little'un · container + charade

  • quiet → SH the noise you make to hush somebody
  • runs → R R is the cricket scorer's abbreviation for runs
  • kid causing trouble → IMP a mischievous child is an imp

Cricket supplies the single letter: on a scorecard runs are abbreviated to R, and that convention turns up in clue after clue once you know it.

9 across STURDY

Robust seat at the front, like a stool? (6)

Definition: Robust · charade + first letter

  • seat → S the opening letter of the word
  • like a stool? → TURDY in the medical sense a stool is a turd, so turd-like is turdy

The question mark is warning you twice over: 'turdy' is the setter's own coinage, and 'stool' is the word a doctor uses rather than the furniture the surface offers.

10 across READJUST

Interpret right tune another time (8)

Definition: tune another time · charade

  • Interpret → READ to read a situation is to interpret it
  • right → JUST a just decision is a right one

Both pieces are ordinary words wearing their less obvious senses - reading as interpretation, just as fairness - which is what keeps the surface looking like a note about music.

11 across AS THE CROW FLIES

Straight bananas for sale with EC regulators' backing (2,3,4,5)

Definition: Straight · anagram + last letter

  • for sale with EC → FORSALEWITHEC thirteen letters taken exactly as they stand
  • regulators' → S the letter at the back of the word, and the fourteenth for the mix

The surface is the old tabloid myth about Brussels banning bendy bananas, which is why 'bananas' reads as part of the story rather than as the instruction it is.

13 across HITMAN

Greeting leather-clad male who might take you out? (6)

Definition: who might take you out? · container + charade

  • Greeting → HI the shortest greeting there is
  • leather → TAN tan is leather, named for the tanning that makes it
  • male → M M is the standard abbreviation for male, as on any form

Everything hinges on reading 'leather-clad male' as an instruction rather than a description: it is not somebody in leathers, it is one piece wrapped around another.

14 across SOLVENCY

Having bucks to spare, doctor loves NYC (8)

Definition: Having bucks to spare · anagram

  • loves → LOVES five letters taken exactly as they stand
  • NYC → NYC New York City by the three initials everyone uses

'Doctor' is the only word here that is neither definition nor material, and it hides by looking like the person in the story rather than the instruction.

16 across DECADENT

Depression ten times, perhaps self-indulgent? (8)

Definition: self-indulgent · charade

  • ten times, perhaps → DECA deca- is the prefix that multiplies by ten, as in decathlon or decalitre
  • Depression → DENT a depression in a car door is a dent

The pieces come in the opposite order to the clue, which is the whole difficulty here: the depression is the tail of the answer even though it opens the sentence.

19 across IN VAIN

Where plumber has spare part, hopefully installing one for nothing (2,4)

Definition: for nothing · container

  • Where plumber has spare part, hopefully → IN VAN a plumber's spare parts live in the van outside
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one

'Hopefully' earns its place: it is the setter admitting the part may not be there after all, which is exactly what makes the whole trip pointless.

21 across STAMPING GROUND

Bit of glue inside sealing circular patch (8,6)

Definition: patch · container + charade + first letter

  • sealing → STAMPING an official seal is stamped onto the document
  • glue → G the opening letter of the word
  • circular → ROUND circular means round

The surface is somebody gluing a patch on. 'Sealing' has to be heard as the stamping of a document before the fourteen letters will come apart.

22 across DOWNFALL

Ruin unearthed entirely by Spooner (8)

Definition: Ruin · spoonerism

  • unearthed entirely → FOUND ALL to unearth something is to find it, and entirely is all
  • by Spooner → DOWNFALL trade the opening sounds of those two words and the same run of sound re-divides into one

Say 'found all' and the answer one after the other: it is the same string of sounds cut in a different place, which is what Spooner's trick always comes down to.

23 across CHALET

Wooden hut guy occupied for period without power (6)

Definition: Wooden hut · charade + deletion

  • guy → CHAP a guy is a chap
  • occupied for period → LET a flat let for a fortnight is occupied by whoever took it
  • power → P P is the physics symbol for power, and it is the letter that goes

The trick is seeing that the two pieces make a word of their own before anything is removed - a chaplet is a wreath or a rosary - and that is where the letter hides.

24 across BLITHE

Happy using high explosive for bombing campaign's finale (6)

Definition: Happy · substitution

  • bombing campaign → BLITZ the Blitz was Germany's bombing campaign against Britain in 1940
  • campaign's finale → Z the last letter of that word, and the one being displaced
  • high explosive → HE HE is the services' abbreviation for high explosive

A swap like this is rarer than a charade and reads like one until you notice 'for'. The surface never leaves the war, which is what hides the substitution.

25 across DISHEVEL

Mess left after meal the day before (8)

Definition: Mess · charade

  • meal → DISH a dish is a meal, or the plate it arrives on
  • the day before → EVE the eve of an event is the day before it
  • left → L L is the standard abbreviation for left

The clue reads back to front: the single letter the sentence puts first is the one the answer puts last, which is exactly what 'after' is telling you.

Down

2 down INTESTINE

See two tins blended in food processor (9)

Definition: food processor · anagram

  • See → SEE three letters taken exactly as they stand
  • two tins → TINTIN a tin twice over, six more letters for the mix

'Food processor' is doing double duty: on the surface it is the gadget the tins are going into, and in the definition it is a piece of you.

3 down BURNHAM

First in by-election ballot box, someone over-performing? (7)

Definition: First in by-election ballot box, someone over-performing? · charade + &lit

  • by-election → B the letter it starts with
  • ballot box → URN an urn is the vessel votes are dropped into, a standing crossword equation
  • someone over-performing? → HAM an actor who over-plays a part is a ham

There is no separate definition to find: the sentence is the description and the wordplay at the same time, which is why every word of it also pays its way in letters.

4 down TRY

Conservative forking out nothing in tax (3)

Definition: tax · deletion

  • Conservative → TORY the Conservative party has been the Tory party for two centuries
  • nothing → O nothing is a zero, and a zero is written as this letter

'Nothing' for O is worth banking: nothing, love, duck and ring all hand you the same round letter, and a clue that wants one usually says one of the four.

5 down SERIOUS

One consigned to watery grave (7)

Definition: grave · container

  • watery → SEROUS serous fluid is the thin watery kind, the doctor's adjective for it
  • One → I the Roman numeral for one

The surface buries somebody at sea. Both of the words that matter have to change part of speech: 'watery' becomes a medical adjective and 'grave' stops being a hole.

6 down SCAFFOLDING

Cheap restaurant sold sandwiches in grand structure outside (11)

Definition: structure outside · container + charade

  • sold → SOLD taken as it stands, and it is the word that opens up
  • Cheap restaurant → CAFF a caff is the British greasy-spoon cafe
  • in grand → ING IN as it stands, then G, the abbreviation for grand

'Sold sandwiches' carries the whole surface - a cheap place selling food - while the wordplay wants 'sold' to open up and swallow the restaurant instead.

7 down REJOICE

Nearly all go back to church to celebrate (7)

Definition: celebrate · deletion + charade

  • go back to → REJOIN to go back to a group is to rejoin it, and this arrives a letter short
  • church → CE CE is the Church of England

The surface is a congregation heading home. Reading 'go back to' as one verb rather than three directions is the step that opens the clue.

8 down MOSES

Leader during flight sees half-hearted order overturned (5)

Definition: Leader during flight · deletion + charade + reversal

  • sees → SES SEES has a double E at its heart, and half a heart leaves one of them
  • order → OM the Order of Merit is OM after a name

'Flight' is the Exodus, not an aeroplane. OM for the Order of Merit is worth banking: a clue that says order and wants two letters usually means this one.

12 down CONTEMPLATE

Study cape worn by model (11)

Definition: Study · charade

  • cape → C C. is the map-maker's abbreviation for Cape
  • worn → ON clothes you are wearing are on you
  • model → TEMPLATE a template is the pattern everything else is modelled on

The surface dresses a model in a cape. Two conventions are worth keeping from it: C for Cape on any map, and 'worn' for ON.

15 down CLIENTELE

Following date, let Neil chat nurses up? (9)

Definition: Following · hidden word + reversal

  • date, let Neil chat → CLIENTELE start at the C of 'chat' and read backwards to the E of 'date'

The surface is a night out with Neil. A buried word running backwards is much harder to see than a forward one, so 'up' in a down clue is the flag to trust.

17 down AGAINST

Con advances towards guards (7)

Definition: Con · container

  • towards → AT both words point a movement in a direction
  • advances → GAINS an advance in a market or a battle is a gain

The surface is a prisoner walking towards the warders. The wordplay wants 'advances' as a plural noun and 'guards' as an instruction rather than a job.

18 down TANGLED

Annoying fly around light's in a muddle (7)

Definition: in a muddle · reversal + charade

  • Annoying fly → TANG the annoying fly is a gnat, and it arrives here facing the other way
  • light → LED the LED is the light inside a modern bulb or torch

Both halves hide behind one picture, an insect circling a lamp: the fly has to become a word and the lamp has to become the three letters stamped on it.

20 down VIOLATE

Victor and I love last minute break (7)

Definition: break · charade

  • Victor → V V is Victor in the radio alphabet
  • I → I the letter standing for itself
  • love → O love is a score of nothing at tennis, written as a zero
  • last minute → LATE doing a thing at the last minute is doing it late

Three of the four pieces are single letters, which is what makes a clue like this feel harder than it is: 'Victor' and 'love' are both standing conventions.

21 down SPOOL

Reel turning goes round (5)

Definition: Reel · reversal

  • goes round → SPOOL something that goes round loops, and those five letters turned about are the entry

A five-letter reversal has nowhere to hide, so the setter leans on 'Reel' and 'goes round' looking like the same idea - which they very nearly are.

23 down COS

Clubs then set up for speaking informally (3)

Definition: for speaking informally · charade + reversal

  • Clubs → C C is the card suit clubs
  • then → OS 'then' gives SO, and this is SO stood on its end

Three letters, three jobs: a card suit, a turn-around and a conjunction almost nobody writes down. 'Speaking informally' is the setter flagging the casual spelling.
