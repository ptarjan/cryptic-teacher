Across

1 across LUSTFUL

Passionate American twisted foot during pause, beginning to limp off (7)

Definition: Passionate · anagram + container + deletion + first letter

  • pause → LULL a lull is a pause in the noise
  • beginning to limp → L the first letter of Limp — the L that LULL gives up
  • American twisted foot → STFU US for American and FT for foot, shaken together

Two abbreviations do the quiet work here: US for American and ft for foot. The letter that leaves is named by 'beginning to limp', so it is the pause word that gets trimmed, not the anagram.

5 across JET PIPE

Stone interrupting ballet move? One may be letting off steam (3,4)

Definition: One may be letting off steam · container

  • ballet move → JETE a jeté is the leap where the dancer throws a leg out
  • Stone → PIP the stone of a fruit is its pip

The question mark concedes the liberty in calling a pip a stone. Read 'letting off steam' literally too — no one here is losing their temper.

9 across MECHANISM

Phi taking opening to include reverse of popular device (9)

Definition: device · charade + container + reversal

  • Phi → ME Phi is this setter's pen name, so he is ME
  • opening → CHASM a chasm is a gaping opening in the ground
  • popular → NI IN means popular, turned round

When a clue names the setter — Phi here — it almost always means ME or I. Bank that one: it turns an apparently impossible proper noun into two letters.

10 across KNIFE

Emptied kitchen provided with latest in fashionable kitchen tool? (5)

Definition: kitchen tool · charade + outer letters + last letter

  • Emptied kitchen → KN KitcheN with its insides scooped out
  • provided → IF 'provided' and 'if' introduce the same condition
  • latest in fashionable → E the last letter of fashionablE

Kitchen appears twice and does a different job each time — first as letters to hollow out, then as half the definition. Spotting the repeat is the whole clue.

11 across EDEN

Cumbrian river valley that’s perceived back to front (4)

Definition: Cumbrian river · cycling

  • valley → DENE a dene is a narrow wooded valley
  • back to front → read literally: the letter at the back moves to the front

Cycling is not reversal — only the last letter travels, and the rest keep their order. A word that came back reversed would read ENED, which is nothing at all.

12 across PERILOUSLY

Dispensation of your pills including MDMA, with a degree of risk (10)

Definition: with a degree of risk · anagram

  • your pills → YOUR PILLS anagram fodder
  • MDMA → E MDMA is ecstasy, and E is its street name
  • Dispensation of → anagram indicator — a dispensation hands things out

E for ecstasy is standard crossword shorthand, and 'dispensation' is both a pharmacy word and a shuffling instruction — the surface a chemist, the wordplay a drug deal.

14 across AMIENS

Concluding words about one French city (6)

Definition: French city · container

  • Concluding words → AMENS an amen is the word that concludes a prayer
  • one → I the Roman numeral one

'About' here means around, not approximately — and the praying surface sits neatly on a city whose cathedral is the largest in France.

15 across GODETIA

Flowering plant: leave it to revert, surrounded by a lot of wood (7)

Definition: Flowering plant · charade + container + reversal + deletion

  • leave → GO to leave is to go
  • a lot of wood → DEA deal is softwood board, and most of it is DEA
  • it → TI IT reverted

Deal is the timber trade's name for pine or fir board, and that piece of vocabulary is what the clue turns on; the colon does the work of 'is'.

18 across HAMMERS

Husband keeps talking, dismissing foremost London team? (7)

Definition: London team · charade + deletion

  • Husband → H H is the standard abbreviation for husband
  • keeps talking → YAMMERS to yammer is to talk on and on
  • dismissing foremost → the leading Y is dismissed, leaving AMMERS

H for husband is a workhorse abbreviation, and the deletion is signalled by position rather than by letter: 'foremost' says which one goes, never what it is.

20 across STRAWS

Net trimmed aboard ship - backbreaking stuff? (6)

Definition: backbreaking stuff · container + deletion

  • ship → SS SS, for steamship, is the crossword's standard vessel
  • Net trimmed → TRAW a trawl is a net, trimmed here of its last letter

The definition is the proverb: it is the last straw that breaks the camel's back. SS for ship is worth memorising — two letters that turn up constantly.

22 across MARATHONER

Athlete flailing another arm (10)

Definition: Athlete · anagram

  • another arm → ANOTHER ARM anagram fodder
  • flailing → anagram indicator — flailing limbs are out of control

The fodder is exactly ten letters and so is the answer, which is the tell: count a suspected anagram against the enumeration before you commit to it.

24 across PLUG

Give ear to piano in advertisement (4)

Definition: advertisement · charade

  • piano → P p is the musical direction for soft, short for piano
  • Give ear → LUG a lug is an ear in northern English

The clue asks you to listen, which is the last thing it means: 'give ear' is handing over a word for ear, and the piano is a marking on a score.

26 across TOPIC

Discussion subject in honour of film (5)

Definition: Discussion subject · charade

  • in honour of → TO a toast in honour of someone is 'to' them
  • film → PIC pic is the trade's short word for a picture

Pic for film comes out of the trade press, where a movie is a picture; the surface sounds like a film society and hides a plain two-part build.

27 across SOPRANINO

Bribe arranged in Ohio, offering notes in elevated positions? (9)

Definition: offering notes in elevated positions · charade

  • Bribe → SOP a sop is something handed over to placate someone
  • arranged → RAN whoever ran an event arranged it
  • in → IN the clue's own 'in', taken as it stands
  • Ohio → O O is the old abbreviation for the state of Ohio

'Arranged' looks like an instruction to shuffle and is not — it is a synonym for ran. Reaching for the anagram is exactly what the clue expects of you.

28 across RUSSELL

Philosopher’s stratagem involving singular lines (7)

Definition: Philosopher · container + charade

  • stratagem → RUSE a ruse is a stratagem
  • singular → S s. is the grammarian's abbreviation for singular
  • lines → LL l is a line, and doubling the letter makes it plural

Doubling an abbreviation to pluralise it — l and ll for lines, p and pp for pages — is a convention worth banking, because it turns up everywhere.

29 across ECLOGUE

Poem’s energy reflected in English crossword feature (7)

Definition: Poem · charade + container + reversal

  • English → E E is the standard abbreviation for English
  • crossword feature → CLUE the thing you are reading is a clue
  • energy → OG GO is energy — 'plenty of go' — turned round

'Crossword feature' pointing at the word clue is the setter winking at you, and GO for energy is the piece to remember: it turns up reversed as often as not.

Down

1 down LUMBER

Tradesman avoids parking useless stuff (6)

Definition: useless stuff · deletion

  • Tradesman → PLUMBER the tradesman you call about pipes
  • parking → P P is the road-sign abbreviation for parking — the letter that goes

P for parking comes off a road sign and setters use it constantly; the surface, with its tradesman dodging a meter, is pure misdirection.

2 down SOCCER MOM

Busy parent very much taken by cricket club - more perturbed over master (6,3)

Definition: Busy parent · charade + anagram

  • very much → SO 'so' is very much, as in 'so much the better'
  • cricket club → CC CC is the standard abbreviation for a cricket club
  • more → ERMO MORE with its letters perturbed
  • master → M M is the abbreviation for master, as on a school timetable

In a down entry 'over' means above rather than around, so the shuffled letters sit on top of the M instead of wrapping it.

3 down FLAP

Flustered state following drink (4)

Definition: Flustered state · charade

  • following → F f. is the reference-book abbreviation for 'following'
  • drink → LAP to lap is how a cat drinks

That f for following comes from indexes and footnotes, where 40f means page 40 and the one after — a small abbreviation setters lean on hard.

4 down LOITERS

Lingers over tucking into quantities of American wine (7)

Definition: Lingers · container

  • quantities of American wine → LITERS litres in the American spelling — the measures wine comes in
  • over → O O is the cricket scorer's abbreviation for an over

'American' is not part of the definition but a spelling instruction: it turns litres into LITERS, which is the only version with room for the O.

5 down JUMPING

Avoiding politician, elected, though held in prison (7)

Definition: Avoiding · container

  • prison → JUG jug is old slang for prison
  • politician → MP a Member of Parliament
  • elected → IN a candidate who wins is in
  • though → surface only — it lets the sentence pretend the man is jailed despite winning his seat

Jug for prison is Victorian slang that survives mostly in crosswords, and 'held in' works twice over: the surface's custody and the instruction to insert.

6 down TAKE ORDERS

Decide on a vocation to work the fast food till? (4,6)

Definition: Decide on a vocation · double definition

  • Decide on a vocation → to take holy orders is to be ordained into the priesthood
  • work the fast food till → the counter staff take what the customer asks for and ring it up

Holy orders and a burger counter are the two worlds the phrase lives in, and the clue slides from one to the other without a word of warning.

7 down IVIES

Struggles to get under one collection of invasive plants (5)

Definition: collection of invasive plants · charade

  • one → I the Roman numeral one
  • Struggles → VIES to vie is to struggle for advantage

In a down entry 'under' means further down the column, so the struggling ends up below the one — and that 'one' is the numeral, not a person.

8 down EVERYMAN

Hero of morality play, extremely beset by inversion of reputation (8)

Definition: Hero of morality play · container + reversal

  • reputation → EMAN your NAME is your reputation, written backwards
  • extremely → VERY extremely and very are the same intensifier

'Extremely' looks like an instruction to take outside letters, which is the trap — here it is only a synonym. The answer also names the Observer's own crossword.

13 down RECEPTACLE

Create place possibly from which article is taken - or where it may be stored? (10)

Definition: where it may be stored · anagram + deletion

  • Create place → CREATE PLACE the letters the anagram works on
  • article → A the indefinite article, and the letter taken out before the shuffle
  • possibly → anagram indicator

The article is not something kept in the box but a letter to remove, and the dash is where the wordplay stops and the definition starts.

16 down TOWELLING

NZ city almost recycled fabric (9)

Definition: fabric · deletion + cycling

  • NZ city → WELLINGTON Wellington is New Zealand's capital
  • almost → the closing N goes, leaving WELLINGTO

'Recycled' does two jobs at once: it sounds like reused cloth, and it asks for a cycle — letters travelling from one end to the other in their existing order.

17 down OHMMETER

Old King encountered old Queen - resistance showing here? (8)

Definition: resistance showing here · charade

  • Old → O O is the usual abbreviation for old
  • King → HM HM stands for His Majesty
  • encountered → MET encountered is met
  • old Queen → ER ER is Elizabeth Regina, the previous monarch

ER for Elizabeth Regina is the crossword's default queen, and since Charles's accession she is the old one — which is also why HM is a king again.

19 down SPOUSAL

America engaged in special survey, cut short, regarding the United States? (7)

Definition: regarding the United States · charade + container + deletion

  • special → S S is the abbreviation for special
  • survey, cut short → POL a poll is a survey of opinion, docked of its last letter
  • America → USA the country's own initials

The definition is the pun the clue turns on: marriage is a state of being united, and 'engaged' is quietly doing the same double work in the surface.

20 down STEEPLE

Lovers’ encounter, initially under thoroughly wet part of church? (7)

Definition: part of church · charade + first letters

  • thoroughly wet → STEEP to steep something is to soak it right through
  • Lovers’ encounter, initially → LE the opening letters of Lovers and Encounter

'Thoroughly wet' wants a verb, not an adjective — steep as in steeping tea — and in a down entry 'under' puts the initials at the foot of the word.

21 down IGNORE

Shun Italian guy doffing cap (6)

Definition: Shun · deletion

  • Italian guy → SIGNORE signore is Italian for gentleman or sir
  • doffing cap → the S at the head of the word comes off

Doffing a cap removes what sits on top, so it takes the first letter — and in a down entry that letter really is the topmost one.

23 down ROPES

Drunken person leaving new parties on a mountain (5)

Definition: parties on a mountain · anagram + deletion

  • person → PERSON the letters to be shuffled
  • new → N N is the abbreviation for new — the letter that leaves
  • Drunken → anagram indicator

The definition is a mountaineering term and reads as a social one, which is where the clue hides; 'drunken' belongs to the wordplay, not to the party.

25 down TAIL

Poor bats: upside down amid defoliation (4)

Definition: Poor bats · hidden word + reversal

  • defoliation → LIAT defoLIATion carries the letters, running the other way
  • upside down → a down entry reads bottom to top, so the buried letters climb

The colon lets 'Poor bats' stand alone as the definition, and in a down entry 'upside down' is the honest way to say reversed: there is no backwards on a vertical axis.
