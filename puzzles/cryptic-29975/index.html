1 across TOOTHLESS

Also, the ship outside left without power (9)

Definition: without power · charade + container

A toothless committee or law is one with no power to bite — it can say things but cannot enforce them.

  • Also → TOO 'also' and 'too' are the same small word of addition
  • the ship → THESS THE as it stands, then SS, the standing abbreviation for a steamship
  • left → L L is the everyday abbreviation for left
  • outside — Whatever stands outside has something else within it, so the letters named before this word close around the one named after.

A note adding that the ship lying outside has been left with no power of its own.

'Outside left' is a footballer's position on the wing, and reading it as one thing is what stalls this clue: the first word is the instruction and the second is a single letter.

6 across FURY

Boxer, turn into cook (4)

Definition: Boxer · container

Tyson Fury is a British world heavyweight champion, so 'boxer' names the answer as one example of the trade rather than defining the word.

  • cook → FRY to fry is to cook
  • turn → U the turn every driver knows is a U-turn, named for the shape of the letter
  • into — Going into something means finishing up within it, so the letter before this word is planted inside the word after.

A boxer being sent from the ring to the kitchen.

'Turn into' reads as a single phrase meaning become, and it is two separate instructions: which letter, and where it goes.

8 across FASTENER

Frenchman retires following quick tie (8)

Definition: tie · charade + reversal

A fastener is anything that holds two things together — a clip, a zip, a lace — which is what a tie does as a noun.

  • quick → FAST fast means quick
  • Frenchman → ENER René is an ordinary French forename, set down backwards
  • retires — To retire is to withdraw the way you came, and in an across answer that runs the name's letters from right to left.
  • following — What follows comes after, so this piece takes up its place behind the one already laid down.

A Frenchman heading for bed as soon as a hurried drawn match finishes.

'Quick tie' reads as a match settled in a hurry, so the definition looks like the wrong part of speech until you hear 'tie' as the thing that ties.

9 across DRAGON

Like TV’s fabulous creature? (6)

Definition: fabulous creature · charade

A dragon is the fire-breathing beast of fable and heraldry, so 'fabulous' here means out of fables rather than wonderful.

  • Like TV’s → DRAG TV is short for transvestite, and drag is the clothing that makes someone look the part
  • Like TV’s → ON looking like one means having that clothing on, and the wearing supplies the last two letters

Wondering whether something resembles that fabulous creature everybody has seen on television.

TV is the crossword's transvestite rather than the television set. This clue names the theme the whole grid is built on — How to Train Your Dragon.

10 across INCOME

Pay company to be involved in Minecraft? (6)

Definition: Pay · anagram + container

Income is the money that comes in over a year — wages, salary, rent — which is 'pay' as a noun rather than as a verb.

  • Minecraft → INME the game's name splits in two, and it is the four letters of its first half that come out reordered
  • company → CO CO is the abbreviation on any firm's letterhead
  • Minecraft — One word doing both jobs: to craft a thing is to work it into a new shape, and what gets reshaped is the four letters in front of that instruction.
  • to be involved in — Being involved in something means being mixed up inside it, so one piece ends up within the other.

Paying a firm to get itself involved in the video game Minecraft.

Minecraft is one word on the page and two instructions underneath it, the second half telling you what to do to the first.

11 across COSTUMED

In December on retreat, large corporation is in fancy dress (8)

Definition: in fancy dress · reversal + container + charade

Someone costumed is dressed up as somebody or something else, which is what being in fancy dress amounts to.

  • December → CED the month's three-letter abbreviation, written backwards
  • large → OS OS is outsize, the label sewn into large clothing
  • corporation → TUM a corporation is an old word for a paunch, and a tum is the same belly
  • In — The clue opens by saying where things go: everything named afterwards is planted inside the month.
  • on retreat — An army on retreat goes back the way it came, and in an across answer that runs the month's letters from right to left.

A December works retreat at which the big firm turns up dressed for a party.

Two words of the surface are pulling double duty: 'retreat' is a weekend away and an instruction to turn round, and a corporation was a comic word for a stomach long before it was a company.

12 across YOURTS

Dairy products not good in tents (6)

Definition: tents · deletion

A yourt is a variant spelling of yurt, the round felt tent Central Asian herders live in, so the plural names those tents.

  • Dairy products → YOGURTS yogurts are the dairy products, spelled the short way
  • good → G G is the standard abbreviation for good, and it is the letter that goes
  • not — Saying a thing is not there is saying it has to come out, so the letter named next leaves the word before it.

Dairy that has turned, which is never good, sitting in somebody's tents.

The answer is a variant spelling of yurt, which is the only reason six letters are left after one has gone. Setters lean on such spellings when the grid demands them, and Chambers backs this one.

15 across ESOTERIC

Private Harry cries over broken toe (8)

Definition: Private · anagram + container

Esoteric knowledge is kept inside a small circle of initiates, which is 'private' in the sense of not shared with outsiders.

  • cries → ESRIC the five letters reordered, then opened up to make room
  • toe → OTE the three letters reordered
  • Harry — To harry is to harass and hound, and letters hounded like that do not stay in the order they came in.
  • over — One thing over another lies across it, so the letters just shuffled are laid around the piece that follows.
  • broken — Something broken has its parts loose, so these three letters are free to go back in another arrangement.

Private Harry in tears after somebody trod on his broken toe.

'Private Harry' reads as a soldier's rank and name, and the two words are on opposite sides of the clue: one is the definition, the other an instruction to shuffle.

16 across CHARIOTS

Diagrams describe Number 10’s vehicles (8)

Definition: vehicles · container

A chariot is the two-wheeled horse-drawn car of the ancient world, so 'vehicles' defines the answer by the general class it belongs to.

  • Diagrams → CHARTS charts are diagrams of figures, of pop sales or of a coastline
  • Number 10 → IO the digits 1 and 0 taken as the two letters they are shaped like
  • describe — To describe a circle is to draw all the way round it, so the first word here goes round the pair that follows.

Diagrams setting out the fleet of cars kept at Number 10.

Number 10 is the Prime Minister's front door on the surface and a pair of letter-shaped digits underneath. Setters use that trick sparingly, so it is worth filing away.

19 across SPHINX

PR wins hearts with “Vote for Monster” (6)

Definition: Monster · container + charade

The sphinx of Greek myth is the winged lion with a woman's head who ate travellers failing her riddle, so 'monster' names the creature.

  • PR → SPIN spin is what a PR operation puts on a story
  • hearts → H H is the card player's abbreviation for the suit of hearts
  • Vote → X a British ballot paper is marked with an X beside the chosen name
  • wins — To win something is to take it in and keep it, so the letter named next is drawn inside the word before.

A publicity campaign winning people over with the slogan Vote for Monster.

The quoted slogan reads as an election poster and is two separate instructions underneath. Hearts as H and the ballot paper's X are both worth keeping in your head.

21 across BRUTALLY

British heavyweight losing no time with friend in a cruel manner (8)

Definition: in a cruel manner · deletion + charade

Brutally is the adverb from brute, so it describes an act done with a brute's savagery — that is, in a cruel manner.

  • British heavyweight → BRUNO Frank Bruno was Britain's world heavyweight champion, a household name in the nineties
  • no → NO the two letters that leave, taken from the word as it stands
  • time → T T is the standard abbreviation for time
  • friend → ALLY an ally is a friend
  • losing — Losing something means it is no longer there, so the two letters named next drop out of the name in front of them.

A British heavyweight wasting no time in getting to a friend of his.

'Losing no time' is an idiom for hurrying and an instruction with the letters to remove written into it. The heavyweight is a boxer's surname, which the puzzle has already used once.

22 across PECANS

Nuts in games headphones (6)

Definition: Nuts · charade

Pecans are the sweet nuts of an American hickory, the ones in a pecan pie, so 'nuts' names them by their family.

  • games → PE PE is physical education, the school lesson better known as games
  • headphones → CANS cans is studio slang for headphones

Nuts rattling about inside the headphones somebody wears for gaming.

'Nuts in games' reads as one idea, somebody mad about sport, and the definition is only the first word of it.

24 across VIKING

Scandinavian against topless rambling (6)

Definition: Scandinavian · charade + deletion

The Vikings were the Norse seafarers who raided and settled out of Scandinavia, so a Viking is a Scandinavian of that age.

  • against → V v is short for versus, the way a fixture list writes it
  • rambling → HIKING rambling across country is hiking, and its opening H is the letter that goes
  • topless — A topless word has lost the letter at its head, so the walking word here starts one letter later than it should.

A Scandinavian objecting to people hiking with nothing on above the waist.

'Rambling' looks like an anagram instruction and is not one: it is the walking sense, supplying a word that then gets trimmed.

25 across LANGUAGE

Communication network to estimate when metal gets twisted (8)

Definition: Communication · charade + anagram

A language is the system of words a group communicates in, so the answer is the vehicle that 'communication' travels in.

  • network → LAN a LAN is a local area network, the sort an office runs on
  • to estimate → GAUGE to gauge a thing is to estimate it
  • metal → no letters of its own: AU is the chemical symbol for gold, and it marks the pair in the piece above that swap over
  • gets twisted — Something twisted has been turned about on itself, so the two letters it points at trade places where they stand.

A phone network trying to estimate what happens when metal is bent out of shape.

The metal is gold, whose symbol AU sits inside the previous word, and swapping those two letters is the misspelling everyone makes anyway.

26 across BERK

Idiot stealing contents of bicycle rack (4)

Definition: Idiot · outer letters

Berk is mild British slang for a fool, common enough in speech that few users remember its rhyming-slang origin.

  • bicycle → BE the two ends of the word, with everything between them taken
  • rack → RK the same treatment again: two ends kept, middle stolen
  • stealing contents of — Steal the contents and the packaging is what is left, so each word here keeps only its first and last letters.

A fool caught robbing a bike rack.

The theme surfaces here too: Berk is the Viking island in How to Train Your Dragon as well as the everyday British insult.

27 across THE JET SET

Wealthy travellers’ bad teeth, black and small within (3,3,3)

Definition: Wealthy travellers · anagram + container + charade

The jet set are the rich who fly from one fashionable place to the next, so wealthy travellers is precisely who the phrase covers.

  • teeth → THEET the five letters reordered, then opened up to take what follows
  • black → JET jet is a black stone, which is where jet-black comes from
  • small → S S is the size label in the collar of a small garment
  • bad — Bad teeth are teeth out of line, and letters out of line are letters waiting to be put in another order.
  • within — What is within a thing sits in the middle of it, so the two pieces named before drop into the shuffled letters.

Rich holidaymakers with bad teeth, some of them black and some of them small.

'Bad teeth' is the picture and the instruction at the same time, and jet does two jobs as well: the black stone in the wordplay, the aircraft in the answer.

1 down TRAIN

Essentially stretches to exercise (5)

Definition: to exercise · middle letters

To train is to put a body through exercise, whether it is a boxer, a runner or a squad doing it.

  • stretches → TRAIN stretches are STRAINS, and this takes the core of that word once its two outer letters go
  • Essentially — The essence of a word is what sits at its core, so the outsides are dropped and only the middle survives.

Somebody warming up with stretches before getting down to real exercise.

'Stretches' reads as the warm-up in a gym and works as a noun to be trimmed. A word emptied of its outer letters is worth learning as a device — 'essentially' and 'basically' both signal it.

2 down OUTDOOR

Old Tudor building inspires love in the open air (7)

Definition: in the open air · charade + anagram + container

Outdoor describes anything that happens under the sky rather than under a roof — an outdoor pool, an outdoor concert.

  • Old → O O is the standard abbreviation for old
  • Tudor → UTDOR the five letters of the dynasty's name reordered
  • love → O love is a score of nothing in tennis, written as the zero it looks like
  • building — Building something is assembling its parts into a new arrangement, which is what happens to the letters that come before it.
  • inspires — To inspire is literally to breathe in, so what is breathed in ends up inside the piece doing the breathing.

An old Tudor house that stirs romance in anyone who stands looking at it in the fresh air.

'Tudor building' reads as one thing, a beamed black-and-white house, and the second word of it is an instruction. Love here is the tennis nothing rather than romance.

3 down HINGE

Joint fishing expedition or something else inside? (5)

Definition: Joint · hidden word

A hinge is the joint a door or a lid swings on, joint in the plain sense of a moving connection between two parts.

  • fishing expedition → HINGE five consecutive letters run across the gap between these two words
  • or something else → no letters of its own: the same five sit here too, so the setter has buried them twice over
  • inside — Something already inside the words needs no working out, only spotting, so this asks you to read across the letters rather than solve them.

Someone asking whether the fishing trip should be a joint one, or whether they should do something different.

'Joint' wants to be the adjective of 'joint expedition' and is the noun instead. Qaos has hidden the same letters in two separate spans, so either half of the clue gets you there.

4 down EARACHE

What? Bottle upended spilling a bit of fluid? That’s painful (7)

Definition: That’s painful · charade + reversal + deletion

An earache is a pain inside the ear, so the clue's closing verdict names the answer by the one thing it always is.

  • What? → EH 'eh?' is what you say when you did not catch what was said
  • Bottle → CARAFE a carafe is the bottle wine arrives in at a table
  • a bit of fluid → F the bit taken is the opening letter of 'fluid', and it is the letter that leaves
  • upended — Upended means stood on its head, and in an answer running down the grid that sends the letters back the way they came.
  • spilling — What is spilled is lost from whatever held it, so the letter named alongside drops out of the assembly.

Someone asking what happened and being told a bottle went over and lost a drop of what was in it.

The two question marks do different jobs: the first supplies the 'eh?' the wordplay needs, while the second admits that a bit of fluid is being read as one letter of the word.

5 down SIDESHOWS

Teams who struggle with scoring initially become secondary attractions (9)

Definition: secondary attractions · charade + anagram + first letter

A sideshow is the small booth alongside the main event at a fair, so sideshows are the secondary attractions by trade name.

  • Teams → SIDES in British sport a side is a team
  • who → HOW the three letters put back in a different order
  • scoring → S the first letter of the word, and only that
  • struggle — A struggle leaves things in disarray, so the three letters beside it come out in some other order.
  • initially — This asks for a word as it is at its start, which is its opening letter and nothing further.

Teams that keep failing to score ending up as a lesser draw than the main event.

'Struggle with scoring' reads as one phrase from a match report, and the instruction stops at 'struggle' — 'scoring' hands over a single letter.

6 down FEATURE

Fellow heading to Eastbourne – a funny, true story (7)

Definition: story · charade + anagram + first letter

A feature is the long article a paper runs away from the news pages, so 'story' is the journalist's sense of the word.

  • Fellow → F F is the abbreviation for fellow, as in Fellow of the Royal Society
  • Eastbourne → E the letter the town starts with
  • a → A the word goes in exactly as written
  • true → TURE the four letters reordered
  • heading to — A heading stands at the top of a thing, so what is wanted is the letter the next word begins with.
  • funny — Something funny is odd rather than right, and letters that are not right are letters out of their proper order.

A man on his way to the seaside at Eastbourne with an amusing tale that really happened.

'A funny, true story' reads as one description of an anecdote, and all four words of it are doing separate jobs in the build.

7 down RHODESIAN

Henry travelled around with Welsh woman and old African (9)

Definition: old African · container + charade

Rhodesia was the southern African state that became Zimbabwe and Zambia, so a Rhodesian belongs to a country that no longer exists.

  • travelled → RODE one who travelled on a horse or a bike rode
  • Henry → H the henry is the unit of electrical inductance, abbreviated H
  • Welsh woman → SIAN Siân is a common Welsh woman's name, the Welsh form of Jane
  • around — To go around a thing is to enclose it, so the travelling word closes over the letter named just before it.

A man called Henry going about the country with a woman from Wales.

Henry is a unit of inductance as well as a first name, which is what makes the capital letter safe to walk past. The definition needs both its words: the country has been gone since 1980.

13 down OTHERWISE

Novel she wrote about one being different (9)

Definition: different · anagram + container

Otherwise means in some other way, so as an adjective it is 'different': 'the facts are otherwise' says the facts are different.

  • she wrote → OTHERWSE the eight letters of these two words reordered
  • one → I I is the Roman numeral for one
  • Novel — Something novel is new and unfamiliar, and letters made unfamiliar are letters shifted out of the order they arrived in.
  • about — To be about a thing is to lie round it, so the shuffled letters close over the one named after this word.

A book by a woman, its plot turning on one character who is not like the others.

'Novel she wrote' reads as one phrase, a woman's book, and only its first word is an instruction — the other two are the letters that instruction works on.

14 down SPOTLIGHT

Best to be standing when overwhelmed by little attention (9)

Definition: attention · reversal + container

The spotlight is the beam that picks a performer out, and by extension the public attention that falls on whoever stands in it.

  • Best → POT the best is the top, written upwards
  • little → SLIGHT a slight amount is a little one
  • to be standing — Standing means set upright, and a word stood on its feet in a down answer reads from the bottom of the grid upwards.
  • overwhelmed by — To be overwhelmed is to be covered over entirely, so the piece named first ends up buried inside the one named after.

Advice that it is best to stay on your feet when a little attention is heading your way.

'Little attention' looks like one idea and splits down the middle: one word is wordplay and the next is the definition. Sitting on the fold like that is what makes the seam hard to find.

17 down RETHINK

Reconsider skating here round the resort (7)

Definition: Reconsider · container + anagram

To rethink a decision is to go back over it and think it through again, which is exactly what reconsidering it means.

  • skating here → RINK a rink is the place skating happens
  • the → ETH the three letters of the little word reordered
  • round — Going round a thing encloses it, so the place named before this word closes over the letters named after.
  • resort — Heard as re-sort, the word asks for the letters in front of it to be sorted into another order.

Having second thoughts about skating your way around the holiday resort.

'The resort' reads as a holiday town and hides re-sort, an instruction. Setters split words at a hyphen like that often enough that it pays to try it on any word ending in a verb.

18 down SKY-BLUE

Broadcaster unhappy with colour of Manchester club (3-4)

Definition: colour of Manchester club · charade

Manchester City play in a pale blue known as sky blue, so the phrase names the shade by the club that wears it.

  • Broadcaster → SKY Sky is the British satellite television company
  • unhappy → BLUE to feel blue is to feel unhappy

A television company put out about the colour a Manchester football team plays in.

Manchester has two famous clubs and this one is City, not United — United's red would give a quite different answer, which is the choice the definition quietly makes for you.

20 down HICCUPS

Minor setbacks; involuntary complaint (7)

Definition: Minor setbacks · double definition

One word twice: small interruptions that briefly set a plan back, and the involuntary spasm of the diaphragm that keeps repeating itself.

  • Minor setbacks → the sense a project manager uses — small hitches that hold things up without derailing them
  • involuntary complaint → the medical sense: a spasm of the diaphragm nobody can choose to stop

The semicolon is standing in for an equals sign, offering the same word twice over. A theme runs through this grid, and the hero of it is one letter short of this answer.

22 down PENCE

Criminal reducing volume of capital money? (5)

Definition: money · substitution

Pence are the small coins of British currency, so 'money' defines them by example — one country's smallest units rather than money at large.

  • Criminal → FENCE a fence is the criminal who buys and resells stolen goods
  • reducing volume → P f is forte and p is piano on a score, so quietening the music turns one letter into the other
  • capital → no letters at all: it dresses the sentence as big-city crime and does no work in the wordplay
  • reducing volume — Turning the sound down is what exchanges the loud musical letter for the soft one, and that exchange happens inside the word.

A crook working quietly to skim a little money in the capital.

The letters f and p are the musical marks for loud and soft, so turning the volume down really does change one into the other. Solvers on the blogs could not place 'capital' either.

23 down NIGHT

Drink served up by host on vacation after dark (5)

Definition: after dark · reversal + outer letters + charade

Night is the stretch of hours that begins when the light goes, so the phrase and the answer cover the same part of the day.

  • Drink → NIG the spirit is gin, set down backwards
  • host → HT the first and last letters of the word, its middle emptied out
  • served up — A drink served up comes back the way it went down, and in a down answer that means reading its letters from the bottom.
  • on vacation — A house whose owners are on vacation stands empty in the middle, so the word keeps only the letters at its two ends.

A drink poured by a host who is away on holiday, once the light has gone.

'On vacation' is the pun the clue turns on: the host is away, and the word he came from is left vacant in the middle.
