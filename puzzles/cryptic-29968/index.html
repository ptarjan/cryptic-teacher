Across

1 across ELECTROMAGNETIC

Currently attractive? (15)

Definition: Currently attractive? · &lit + charade

An electromagnetic field is made by a current and does the attracting, so both words of the clue describe it literally.

  • Currently → ELECTRO the current meant is the electric one, and electro- is the prefix that says so
  • attractive → MAGNETIC whatever hauls iron filings towards itself is attractive in the physicist's sense

Somebody who is good-looking at the moment, whatever they may look like later on.

'Currently' is the physicist's current rather than the adverb for right now, and the pull in 'attractive' is on iron, not on people. Read straight, the sentence describes the field itself.

9 across CHILE PINE

Tree sounding cool for long (5,4)

Definition: Tree · homophone + charade

The Chile pine is the monkey puzzle, the spiky South American conifer planted in Victorian front gardens.

  • sounding cool → CHILE cool weather is chilly, and said aloud that is the South American country
  • long → PINE to long for something is to pine for it
  • sounding — Sounding points at the ear rather than the page: what matters is the noise the word makes, not the way it is spelt.

A tree that has been fashionable for a very long time.

The two halves come from different worlds — one from the thermometer, one from yearning — and 'for' belongs to the second of them, since you pine for what you long for.

10 across TONGA

Weight gain shed in horse-drawn carriage (5)

Definition: horse-drawn carriage · charade + deletion

A tonga is the light two-wheeled horse-drawn carriage of India and Pakistan, still seen in country towns.

  • Weight → TON a ton is a large unit of weight
  • gain shed in → GA GAIN loses the letters I and N, the 'in' the clue names
  • shed — What is shed drops off and is gone, so the letters named next leave the word that was carrying them.

Weight somebody put on and has now managed to lose.

'Shed in' is two instructions in two words, one saying what goes and one saying which letters go, and the surface hides both inside an ordinary story about dieting.

11 across ANNATTO

Girl sent back too much food colouring (7)

Definition: food colouring · charade + reversal

Annatto is the orange-red dye made from achiote seeds, used to colour cheeses such as Red Leicester.

  • Girl → ANNA Anna is the girl's name, four letters and the same either way round
  • too much → TTO too much is over the top, OTT, and the reversal turns those three letters round
  • sent back — Something sent back retraces the way it came, so this piece is written in the reverse of its usual order.

A girl sent home for putting far too much colour in the food.

OTT is worth owning — over the top, the crossword's standard three letters for excess — and it is one of the few abbreviations that reads as a whole phrase.

12 across DECIBEL

Sound measure could be credible – not right! (7)

Definition: Sound measure · anagram + deletion

A decibel is the unit sound intensity is measured in, so 'sound measure' names it by exactly what it counts.

  • credible → CREDIBLE eight letters offered up before anything is taken away
  • not right → right is R, and 'not' is the word that throws that one letter out
  • could be — Could be admits the letters are only a possibility in the order given, which is licence to try them in another.

An account that would be believable if one detail in it were not wrong.

The exclamation mark makes 'not right!' read as indignation about the story, when it is a flat instruction to drop a letter before shuffling.

13 across CUE

Rod’s letter is spoken of (3)

Definition: Rod · homophone

A cue is the long tapering rod a snooker or billiards player strikes the ball with.

  • letter → CUE the seventeenth letter of the alphabet, whose name takes three letters to write down
  • spoken of — Spoken of asks for the sound of the thing rather than its written form, so say it aloud and write down what you hear.

Somebody discussing a letter that belongs to a man called Rod.

'Rod' is capitalised only because it opens the clue, and it is not a man's name at all but a plain noun doing the whole of the defining.

14 across ART DECO

C20th style affected trade company (3,4)

Definition: C20th style · anagram + charade

Art deco is the geometric decorative style of the 1920s and 1930s, so 'C20th style' dates it by its century.

  • trade → ARTDE the five letters of the word for business, rearranged
  • company → CO co. is the standard abbreviation after a firm's name
  • affected — An affected manner is not the natural one, so these letters are put on in an order other than their own.

A trading company that puts on airs.

'Affected' is also the setter's wink at a style its critics called mannered; as an instruction it reaches only as far as the word beside it, and the firm is added afterwards.

17 across DISTANT

Reserved half the area with worker (7)

Definition: Reserved · charade + deletion

A distant manner is a cool, unforthcoming one, which is what 'reserved' describes in a person.

  • the area → DIST an area is a district, and only the front half of that eight-letter word is wanted
  • worker → ANT the crossword's standard worker is the ant
  • half — Half a word is one of its two equal parts, and it is the opening four letters that are kept here.

Half a plot of land set aside, with a labourer thrown in.

'Reserved' means aloof rather than booked in advance, and that switch is what the surface's talk of land and labour is built to hide.

19 across IGNORES

Gives the cold shoulder to Italian fellow cycling (7)

Definition: Gives the cold shoulder to · cycling

To give someone the cold shoulder is to ignore them pointedly, so the phrase is the idiomatic equivalent of the answer.

  • Italian fellow → SIGNORE a signore is an Italian gentleman, and one of these letters is about to move house
  • cycling — Cycling sends the letter at one end round to the other, everyone else keeping their place in the queue.

An Italian gentleman out on his bicycle.

Every letter is there from the start and only the first one travels, which is why the word looks so nearly right before you touch it.

22 across REVERIE

Vicar on lake, musing (7)

Definition: musing · charade

A reverie is a spell of pleasant daydreaming, which is what musing names as a state of mind.

  • Vicar → REV Rev is the title written in front of a clergyman's name
  • lake → ERIE Erie is one of the five Great Lakes

A vicar out on a lake, lost in thought.

Erie is the crossword's lake of choice, four letters and three of them vowels, and the vicar in front of it is a title rather than a person.

24 across AGO

Previously Argo had leading role cut (3)

Definition: Previously · deletion + first letter

Ago and previously both place something at an earlier time, though ago follows its period — two years ago — where previously stands alone.

  • Argo → ARGO the ship Jason sailed in search of the golden fleece
  • role → the letter at its head is R, and that R is the piece taken out
  • leading — The leading letter of a word is the one at its head, and that single letter is what this instruction picks out.
  • cut — Cut takes away what has just been named, so the letter singled out is removed from the word before it.

A production of Argo that has lost its starring part.

'Leading role' is one phrase in the surface and two instructions in the wordplay: find the letter at the head of the second word, and lose it.

25 across NIELSEN

Composer is otherwise in inn, drunk (7)

Definition: Composer · anagram + container

Carl Nielsen was Denmark's leading symphonist, so 'composer' defines him by example rather than by any property of the word.

  • inn, drunk → NIN the three letters of the pub, staggering
  • otherwise → ELSE 'or otherwise' is 'or else'
  • in — In puts one piece inside another, so what follows is not laid alongside the rest but buried within it.
  • drunk — A drunk cannot walk a straight line, and letters in that state do not keep the order they were given.

A composer who would rather be anywhere else than in this pub.

Danish music is thin on the ground in crosswords, and this symphonist is the name that turns up: worth stocking on the shelf beside Grieg and Sibelius.

26 across SLOGGER

T20 batsman as small Canadian woodsman (7)

Definition: T20 batsman · charade

A slogger is a batter who hits hard and without finesse, exactly the type the short cricket format rewards.

  • small → S S is the clothing size small
  • Canadian woodsman → LOGGER a logger fells trees for timber, the lumberjack of the Canadian forests

A cricketer likened to a small Canadian lumberjack.

T20 is Twenty20, the twenty-over game in which batters swing at nearly everything, so the definition wants a hitter rather than a stylist.

28 across ARIEL

Heartless Elgar and I confused Shakespearean spirit (5)

Definition: Shakespearean spirit · anagram + deletion

Ariel is the airy spirit bound to Prospero in The Tempest, so the definition names the character by play and by kind.

  • Heartless Elgar → ELAR the composer's five letters lose the G sitting at their centre
  • I → I the clue's own I joins the letters to be mixed
  • Heartless — A heartless word has lost the letter at its very centre, and that letter is gone before the shuffle begins.
  • confused — Confused things are out of their proper arrangement, so these letters take up a new order.

The speaker and a hard-hearted Elgar, thoroughly muddled between them.

'Heartless' is a character judgement in the surface and a surgical instruction in the wordplay — one letter, taken from the exact middle of the name.

29 across RAISE CAIN

Cause trouble, as Adam and Eve did? (5,4)

Definition: Cause trouble · double definition

To raise Cain is to make a violent fuss; word for word it is also what Adam and Eve did in bringing up their firstborn son.

  • Cause trouble → the idiom for kicking up a violent fuss
  • as Adam and Eve did? → the first couple brought up a first son, which is these words read one at a time

Adam and Eve blamed for starting all the trouble there has ever been.

The question mark flags the second half as a joke rather than a dictionary sense: the phrase for an uproar is also, word for word, a description of the world's first parenting.

30 across KEEP AT A DISTANCE

Stay away from far off fortress (4,2,1,8)

Definition: Stay away from · charade

To keep someone at a distance is to hold them off and stay away from them, the idiom for refusing any closeness.

  • fortress → KEEP the keep is the strong central tower of a castle
  • far off → ATADISTANCE something far off is a long way from where you stand

Advice to give a remote castle a wide berth.

'Far off fortress' looks like one noun phrase with an adjective in front, and the two words are separate pieces that swap places on the way into the grid.

Down

1 down EXCLAMATION MARK

The last thing you’ll see here! (11,4)

Definition: The last thing you’ll see here! · &lit

An exclamation mark closes a sentence, so the final character of this clue is at once the definition and the thing defined.

  • The last thing you’ll see → read straight, a description of whatever stands at the very end of a written line
  • here! → 'here' is the clue in front of you, so look at the character it stops on

A warning that whatever you are looking at now is the last sight you will get.

'Here' means this clue, and the clue ends on the very piece of punctuation it is describing, so the setter has shown you the answer without spelling a letter of it.

2 down EVIAN

Turned up, unsophisticated in French spa town (5)

Definition: French spa town · reversal

Évian-les-Bains is a spa town on the French side of Lake Geneva, famous for the mineral water bottled there.

  • unsophisticated → EVIAN someone unsophisticated is naive, and those five letters are written back to front
  • Turned up — Turned up is an about-face on a down entry: the letters climb the column, arriving in the opposite order to the one the clue prints.

A rustic simplicity that turns up in a smart French watering place.

The town's name sits on restaurant tables all over the world as a brand of bottled water, which is why it reads as scenery rather than as a definition.

3 down TRESTLE

Support from unsettled settler (7)

Definition: Support · anagram

A trestle is the braced A-frame a plank rests on, so it supports a table top or a bridge span.

  • settler → SETTLER seven letters of fodder, the word the shuffle works on
  • unsettled — Something unsettled has been shaken out of its place, so these letters will not stay where the clue put them.

A pioneer who never managed to settle down.

The joke is the contradiction: an unsettled settler is a person who will not stay put, which is exactly what the letters of the word do here.

4 down ORINOCO

Gold coin tossed over Womble (7)

Definition: Womble · charade + anagram

Orinoco is one of the Wombles of Wimbledon Common, the burrowing television creatures each named after a place on the map.

  • Gold → OR or is gold in heraldry, the tincture used on a coat of arms
  • coin → INOC the four letters of the word for money, landed in a new order
  • over → O o is the cricket scorer's mark for an over of six balls
  • tossed — A tossed coin lands whichever way up it pleases, so the letters it names come down in a fresh order.

A gold coin flipped over the head of a small furry creature.

'Over' looks like part of the tossing and is a cricket abbreviation standing on its own, which is what makes the last letter so hard to place.

5 down AMENDED

Changed what happened at noon? (7)

Definition: Changed · charade

To amend something is to alter it, usually a text or a law, so 'changed' is the plain past-tense synonym.

  • at noon → AM the hours before midday are the a.m., and midday is where they stop
  • what happened → ENDED what the morning did when twelve o'clock arrived

A question about the event that took place at midday.

Nothing is being timed here: the clue asks what the morning itself did at twelve, and the reply to that question goes straight into the grid.

6 down NOTICES

Lack of commitments, around a hundred spies (7)

Definition: spies · container

To spy something is to catch sight of it, the plain verb sense rather than anything to do with espionage.

  • Lack of commitments → NOTIES a person with no ties has nothing holding them to anyone or anywhere
  • a hundred → C C is the Roman numeral for a hundred
  • around — To go around something is to close on both sides of it, so one piece wraps the other instead of following it.

A footloose life spent circling a hundred secret agents.

'Spies' is the everyday verb — catches sight of — and not the noun that the surface's hundred agents has you expecting.

7 down TONY BLAIR

Only a Brit worried former PM (4,5)

Definition: former PM · anagram

Tony Blair was Prime Minister from 1997 to 2007, so 'former PM' names him by the office he no longer holds.

  • Only a Brit → ONLYABRIT nine letters of fodder, exactly as the clue prints them
  • worried — A worried person cannot keep still, and letters described that way will not stay in the order printed.

One anxious Briton, alone in a room full of foreigners.

The fodder is a joke at the man's expense: a prime minister who took the country to war beside an American president is cut down here to being only a Brit.

8 down CHARLOTTE BRONTE

Acton let brother bother sister (9,6)

Definition: sister · anagram

Acton Bell was the pen name of Anne Brontë, whose elder sister Charlotte is the novelist the definition points at.

  • Acton let brother → ACTONLETBROTHER fifteen letters of fodder, and Acton Bell was the pen name of the youngest of three novelist sisters
  • bother — To bother someone is to disturb them, and letters disturbed like that come out in a different arrangement.

One sibling standing back while another one pesters a third.

The three sisters published as Currer, Ellis and Acton Bell, all of them men's names, and it is the eldest of the three that these fifteen letters rearrange into.

15 down TANGERINE

Fruit perplexed generations – not so! (9)

Definition: Fruit · anagram + deletion

A tangerine is the small loose-skinned citrus named after Tangier, so 'fruit' defines it by naming its kind.

  • generations → GENERATIONS eleven letters, the raw material the clue hands over
  • not so → the S and O of 'so' are the two letters that do not survive
  • perplexed — A perplexed person has lost track of how things go, and so do these letters once the instruction bites.

Generations of people left baffled, and somebody insisting it was not so.

'Not so!' reads as a flat contradiction and is a pair of letters to remove; the count gives it away, because eleven letters will never fill nine cells.

16 down COE

Canoe from which falls an athlete (3)

Definition: athlete · deletion

Sebastian Coe is the British middle-distance runner who won Olympic 1500m gold in 1980 and again in 1984.

  • Canoe → CANOE the small paddled boat, five letters
  • an → the article names its own two letters, A and N, and they are the ones lost
  • from which falls — Whatever falls from a thing leaves it behind, so the letters named drop out of the word standing before them.

An athlete tipping out of a canoe.

The picture is a capsize, and the instruction runs the other way round: it is the canoe that loses something, not the paddler.

18 down IDE

Fish that is around delta (3)

Definition: Fish · container

An ide is a silver freshwater fish of the carp family, common in northern Europe and also called the orfe.

  • that is → IE i.e., short for the Latin id est, is 'that is'
  • delta → D delta is D in the radio alphabet
  • around — Going around something means closing on both sides of it, so the short piece ends up in the middle.

A fish swimming about somewhere near a river delta.

Both halves are crossword staples worth keeping: the Latin abbreviation for 'that is', and the radio alphabet where Delta stands in for its own initial.

20 down ROSELLA

Two girls touching Australian parakeet (7)

Definition: Australian parakeet · charade

A rosella is one of the bright broad-tailed parakeets of Australia, common in gardens along the east coast.

  • Two girls → ROS the first of them is Ros, the short form of Rosalind
  • Two girls → ELLA the second is Ella, a name in its own right and the tail of several longer ones
  • touching — Two things touching are set side by side with nothing between, so the pieces run straight into each other.

Two girls standing shoulder to shoulder.

Neither name is signalled beyond the word 'girls', so the way in is to try short women's names against the crossing letters until a pair of them meet.

21 down SANGRIA

Squealed about tune for drink (7)

Definition: drink · charade + reversal

Sangria is the Spanish punch of red wine, fruit and soda, so 'drink' names it by its plainest category.

  • Squealed → SANG an informer who talks to the police has sung
  • tune → RIA a tune is an air, and here those three letters are written backwards
  • about — About here means turned about, so the piece it governs is written in the opposite direction to its ordinary spelling.

Somebody squealing about a tune in exchange for a drink.

'Squealed' is the gangster's sense, informing rather than shrieking, and 'about' does turning work instead of meaning 'concerning'.

22 down ROSSINI

Putin is so reactionary – some back out for composer (7)

Definition: composer · hidden word + reversal

Rossini wrote The Barber of Seville and William Tell, so 'composer' names him by his trade.

  • Putin is so reactionary → ROSSINI seven letters lie in a row across those words, and they are read from the right
  • some — Some of the words means part of them, so what is wanted is a run of letters already sitting in the clue.
  • back out — Back out asks for that run to be lifted in the opposite direction to the way it is printed.

A hard-line view of Putin, with some people quietly withdrawing.

'Reactionary' does two jobs at once: it lends its opening letter to the run of hidden letters, and its meaning is the setter's nudge to read them the other way.

23 down VIOLENT

Furious as flower’s name concealed (7)

Definition: Furious · container

Violent means marked by great force or fury, so 'furious' is a straight synonym in the sense of uncontrolled rage.

  • flower → VIOLET a violet is the small purple spring flower
  • name → N n is the standard abbreviation for name
  • concealed — Something concealed is put out of sight inside something bigger, so the letter goes within the word rather than beside it.

Somebody in a rage because a flower's name is being kept secret.

'Flower' is a genuine bloom here, not the crossword's usual trick of a thing that flows, and the name hidden in it is a single letter.

27 down GRAIN

Good supply of water for seed (5)

Definition: seed · charade

A grain is a single seed of a cereal such as wheat or rice, so 'seed' names it by what it is.

  • Good → G g is the standard abbreviation for good
  • supply of water → RAIN the water the sky supplies

Plenty of water, which is exactly what a seed wants.

'Good' is not describing the water at all but handing over a letter, and it is the abbreviation that turns up on a school report.
