1 across SEQUEL

Picture covering Queen’s long continuing story (6)

Definition: continuing story · container + charade

continuing story → SEQUEL: the book or film that carries an earlier story onward, a story continued rather than a fresh one.

  • Picture → SEE to picture something is to see it in your mind
  • Queen → QU Qu. is the standard short form for a queen, as in Qu. Victoria
  • long → L l is the abbreviation for long, the letter on a clothing label
  • covering — A cover lies over a thing on both sides, so the short piece ends up wrapped inside the word in front of it.

A film telling the long-running story of the Queen's reign.

'Picture' is a noun everywhere in the surface — a film about royalty — and a verb in the wordplay, the everyday sense of seeing a thing in your head.

5 across FLABBY

Fine scientific establishment busy cutting American slack (6)

Definition: slack · charade + deletion

slack → FLABBY: loose and lacking firmness, the sense of slack used of a rope or a waistline rather than an idle worker.

  • Fine → F f is the grade stamped on a fine pencil
  • scientific establishment → LAB a lab is where the science actually gets done
  • busy cutting American → BY American is US, and BUSY with those two cut out of it leaves B…Y
  • cutting — To cut something is to take a piece out of it, so the letters named next come away from the word in front.

A first-rate laboratory hard at work trimming the slack out of an American budget.

'Fine' opens as praise for the laboratory and is a single letter, the grade printed on the side of a pencil.

8 across TACITLY

Silently cheers Leicester perhaps securing lead in League (7)

Definition: Silently · charade + container + first letter

Silently → TACITLY: done without saying it aloud, the way a tacit agreement is one nobody ever spoke.

  • cheers → TA ta is the British thank-you, and cheers does the same job when a drink is handed over
  • Leicester perhaps → CITY Leicester City is the football club, so 'perhaps' offers the town as one example of the word that follows it
  • League → L the first letter of League
  • securing — To secure something is to hold it fast inside, so the letter goes within the word rather than after it.
  • lead in — The lead is whatever goes in front, so the first letter of the word following is the one taken.

Fans quietly cheering Leicester as the club takes top place in the league.

The whole sentence is football, and 'cheers' is not the noise from the terraces but the word you say when somebody buys you a drink.

9 across ORIGAMI

Paper craft has old sails etc, one master recalled (7)

Definition: Paper craft · charade + reversal

Paper craft → ORIGAMI: the Japanese art of folding paper into shapes, craft in the sense of skilled handiwork.

  • old → O o is the standard abbreviation for old
  • sails etc → RIG a ship's rig is its whole arrangement of masts and sails
  • one master → AMI one is the Roman numeral I and a master of arts is MA, the pair then turned round
  • recalled — Something recalled is called back the way it came, so those letters are read from the far end backwards.

An old sailing ship's rigging remembered by a single skipper.

'Paper craft' is the trap: in a clue full of sails it reads as a boat folded out of paper, and it is the definition.

11 across BALLROOM DANCING

Strictly’s content to condemn canoodling and mostly blame Romeo (8,7)

Definition: Strictly’s content · anagram + deletion

Strictly’s content → BALLROOM DANCING: what the television show consists of, the waltzes and quicksteps its couples perform week after week.

  • canoodling → CANOODLING anagram fodder, ten letters
  • blame → BLAM BLAME without its last letter
  • Romeo → R Romeo is R in the radio alphabet
  • condemn — A building condemned is declared unfit and pulled apart, so the word beside it comes down and is rebuilt.
  • mostly — Mostly means nearly all of it, so the word gives up its final letter and the rest goes into the mix.

Television judges tearing into a couple for canoodling and putting most of the blame on the romantic one.

Strictly Come Dancing is the BBC's Saturday-night contest, so its content is a fair definition, and 'condemn' passes as the judges' verdict rather than an instruction.

12 across EWER

Pitcher Webb, initially retained by phone company, gets sponsor in the end (4)

Definition: Pitcher · container + first letter + last letter

Pitcher → EWER: the tall wide-lipped jug that stands on a washstand, pitcher in its water-carrying sense.

  • phone company → EE EE is one of Britain's big mobile networks
  • Webb → W the first letter of the surname
  • sponsor → R the last letter of sponsoR
  • initially — Initially means at the very start, so only the opening letter of the name is used.
  • retained by — To retain something is to keep hold of it inside, so the letter sits within the word that follows.
  • in the end — The end is where a word's last letter lives, so that closing letter is the one taken.

A baseball pitcher called Webb kept on by a phone firm until a sponsor steps in.

'Pitcher' is a baseball player in the surface and a jug in the definition, and the phone company is EE, a British network a solver elsewhere would never have met.

13 across RECEPTACLE

Engineers dubiously accept abridged permit for vessel (10)

Definition: vessel · charade + anagram + deletion

vessel → RECEPTACLE: anything made to hold something, vessel in the bowl-and-jar sense rather than the ship the surface offers.

  • Engineers → RE RE is the Royal Engineers, the army's builders
  • accept → CEPTAC the six letters of ACCEPT in a new order
  • permit → LE to permit is to let, and that word is cut short here
  • dubiously — Something done dubiously is not done straight, so the letters beside it are not taken in the order they are given.
  • abridged — An abridged book has been cut short, so this word gives up its final letter.

Army engineers doubtfully taking a shortened licence for a ship.

'Engineers' opens the clue looking like the people who will do something and is two letters — the Royal Engineers are the crossword's standard sappers.

17 across PATRIOTISM

Some fellowship at riot is misrepresented as love for one’s country (10)

Definition: love for one’s country · hidden word

love for one’s country → PATRIOTISM: the noun for exactly that devotion, the word a dictionary offers for loving one's nation.

  • fellowship at riot is misrepresented → PATRIOTISM the letters run consecutively from inside the first word across to the start of the last, in the order given
  • Some — Only some of what follows is wanted — a run of letters inside those words rather than what they mean.

A group's friendship at a riot being spun into something it never was.

The clue accuses somebody of misrepresenting a fellowship, which reads as political commentary, and its first word is the only one telling you to look at letters.

18 across ASTI

It’s a revolution in sparkling wine (4)

Definition: sparkling wine · reversal

sparkling wine → ASTI: the sweet fizz from the Italian town of Asti, so the definition names the class it is one example of.

  • It’s a → ITSA the opening two words run together as four letters before anything is done to them
  • revolution — A revolution turns a thing full circle, so what comes before it is read the other way round.

A breakthrough being announced in the world of fizzy wine.

'It's a revolution' is the sort of line a wine advert uses, so the two words that carry the letters look like a verdict on something rather than material.

20 across ONCE IN A BLUE MOON

On rare occasions previously writer worked, unable to be idle (4,2,1,4,4)

Definition: On rare occasions · charade + anagram

On rare occasions → ONCE IN A BLUE MOON: the idiom for something that hardly ever happens, a blue moon being a rare second full moon in a month.

  • previously → ONCE previously means at some earlier time
  • writer → I the setter writing the clue refers to himself as I
  • unable → UNABLE the other six letters of the fodder, taken as they stand
  • be idle → MOON somebody idling about is said to be mooning around
  • worked — Dough that is worked is pushed about until it takes a new shape, and the letters beside it are handled the same way.

A writer who used to put in some work and now cannot sit still.

'Writer' as I is a standing crossword trick, the setter slipping himself into his own clue, and here he arrives as the person who worked.

23 across SHAKIER

Increasingly tentative, I ask her out (7)

Definition: Increasingly tentative · anagram

Increasingly tentative → SHAKIER: less steady than before, the comparative covering unsteady hands and an argument losing its footing alike.

  • I ask her → IASKHER anagram fodder, seven letters
  • out — Something out of place is not where it was put, so those letters are set down somewhere other than where they started.

Somebody losing their nerve while working up to asking a woman out.

'Ask her out' is one phrase in the surface, a man plucking up courage, and its last word is the instruction the letters need.

24 across LOGJAMS

Preserve records admitting gridlocks (7)

Definition: gridlocks · container

gridlocks → LOGJAMS: blockages nothing can move through, from timber jammed in a river, used of traffic and stalled talks alike.

  • records → LOGS a ship's log is the record kept of a voyage
  • Preserve → JAM jam is fruit preserved in sugar
  • admitting — To admit somebody is to let them in, so the word in front opens up and takes the other one inside.

Keeping the files that own up to how badly the traffic seized up.

'Preserve' opens as the verb the sentence seems to need and is the thing in the jar, the noun a crossword reaches for whenever preserves are mentioned.

25 across REIN IN

Check information held by inner assembly (4,2)

Definition: Check · anagram + container

Check → REIN IN: to hold something back the way a rider pulls on the reins, check in the sense of curbing rather than examining.

  • inner → REINN the five letters of INNER built up in a new order
  • information → I i is the letter on every tourist information sign
  • held by — Held by something means kept inside it, so this letter sits within the group rather than beside it.
  • assembly — To assemble is to put parts together in a new arrangement, so the letters of the word before it are built up afresh.

Checking a fact that one of the inner circle brought to the meeting.

'Inner assembly' reads as a select committee, the small group inside a bigger one, and neither word means anything of the sort here.

26 across DENOTE

Typify reactionary public school education (6)

Definition: Typify · charade + reversal

Typify → DENOTE: to stand for something as its sign, the sense in which a symbol denotes the thing it represents.

  • public school → ETON Eton is the best known of the fee-paying boarding schools the British confusingly call public schools
  • education → ED ed is the standard abbreviation, as in the Department for Education
  • reactionary — A reactionary wants everything to go back the way it came, so these letters are entered in the opposite direction.

A backward-looking view of how the private schools educate.

'Public school' means the opposite in Britain to what it means everywhere else — a fee-paying private school — and Eton is the one setters reach for.

2 down EXCELLENT

Select spreadsheet program and occasionally alter books (9)

Definition: Select · charade + alternate letters

Select → EXCELLENT: 'select' in the sense of choice, picked out as the best, as in a select few rather than the verb.

  • spreadsheet program → EXCEL Microsoft's spreadsheet is the one everybody names
  • alter → LE the second and fourth letters of aLtEr
  • books → NT NT is the New Testament, the crossword's usual books

Choosing a spreadsheet package and now and then quietly fiddling with the accounts.

3 down UPTURN

At university, go for advancement (6)

Definition: advancement · charade

advancement → UPTURN: a turn upwards in fortunes, the noun for things improving after a flat stretch.

  • At university → UP a British student in residence at Oxford or Cambridge is said to be up
  • go → TURN your go in a game is your turn

Advice to a student: aim high while you are still at university.

4 down LAY TO REST

Bury put ultimate market on street behind Rock (3,2,4)

Definition: Bury · charade + last letter

Bury → LAY TO REST: the gentle phrase used of a funeral, where saying 'bury' outright would sound blunt.

  • put → LAY to put something down is to lay it
  • market → T the last letter of markeT
  • Rock → ORE ore is rock with metal in it, hacked out of a mine
  • street → ST st is the standard abbreviation on any address
  • ultimate — The ultimate thing in a series is the final one, so only the closing letter of the word beside it is wanted.
  • on — In a down clue whatever is placed on something else is written above it, so this piece goes in first.
  • behind — Whatever stands behind a thing comes after it, so those two letters follow the piece just named.

Bury's last market being moved to the street behind the rock venue.

Bury is a town in Greater Manchester, which is what the capital letter and the market are for, and the definition is the plain verb.

major leak → FLOOD: water escaping in overwhelming quantity, a leak scaled up until it fills the room.

  • well → L the last letter of welL
  • finally — What comes finally is at the end, so the closing letter of the word before it is the one taken.

7 down BHAJI

side dish → BHAJI: the deep-fried onion fritter served beside an Indian main course, a dish that only ever turns up on the side.

  • Starters in — A starter comes first, so the opening letter of each word that follows is what the clue hands over.

An Indian meal where puffed-rice snacks arrive with something extra alongside.

Jhalmuri is a Bengali street snack of spiced puffed rice, so the sentence reads as a genuine menu — which is what hides the run of initials.

  • apostles on → APOSTLESON anagram fodder, ten letters

Church silverware showing the carved apostles, with a bishop worked into the design.

'Carved apostles' sounds like church woodwork and 'on' passes for a preposition, so the word that looks least like fodder is part of it.

10 down INGREDIENTS

  • a silly → ASILYL those six letters rearranged and dropped inside

22 down APRON
