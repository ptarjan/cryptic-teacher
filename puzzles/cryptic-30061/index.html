Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,061

Across

1 across SHOP TILL YOU DROP

Threat to informer? (4,4,3,4)

Definition: Threat to informer? · cryptic definition

  • informer → The surface is selling you a day at the mall, but in British slang to grass somebody up is to 'shop' them, and that is the sense the first word carries.
  • Threat → Said to a snitch rather than a customer, the advertising line stops being an invitation and turns into a warning about how long the grassing will go on.

Vlad turns a shop-window slogan into menace by changing what one verb means. There is no wordplay to unpick here — the whole clue is the joke, and spotting it is the solve.

9 across LOBSTER

Throw back nameless catch at sea (7)

Definition: catch at sea · charade + deletion

  • Throw → LOB to lob a ball is to throw it
  • back → STERN the stern is the back end of a boat
  • nameless → N is the standard abbreviation for name, and this is the word that strikes it off

'Throw back' reads as one action and is two separate jobs, which is the whole difficulty. N for name is worth learning: setters delete it constantly.

10 across RESCIND

Withdraw having committed a further offence as reported (7)

Definition: Withdraw · homophone

  • having committed a further offence → RESCIND to sin again is to re-sin, so somebody who has offended a second time has RE-SINNED

The joke is that a second offence and a cancellation sound identical. RE- for 'again' is worth filing away, since setters lean on it constantly.

11 across DOT

Newton going, ‘You shouldn’t point!’ (3)

Definition: point · deletion

  • You shouldn’t → DONT don't is what you say for you shouldn't
  • Newton → N N is the symbol for the newton, the unit of force

Vlad hides a physicist inside a telling-off, and the exclamation mark helps by making the last word look like rudeness rather than punctuation.

12 across ON CLOUD NINE

Previously keeping flashy eroticist very happy (2,5,4)

Definition: very happy · container + charade

  • Previously → ONCE once means previously, as in once upon a time
  • flashy → LOUD a loud tie is a flashy one
  • eroticist → NIN Anaïs Nin, the diarist who wrote erotica

The surface reads as gossip about somebody's taste in books, and nothing in it hints at delight. Anaïs Nin is standing crossword currency: file the three letters away.

13 across ABLE SEAMAN

Mainly competent rating (4,6)

Definition: rating · charade

  • competent → ABLE able means competent
  • Mainly → SEAMAN the main is the open sea, so the man described as 'mainly' is a man of the sea

The main is an old word for the open sea, and 'mainly' is pointing at that rather than at 'for the most part'. The surface passes as faint praise.

15 across AGES

Knocked back the drink (over a gallon) for some considerable time (4)

Definition: some considerable time · container + reversal

  • the drink → SEA the drink is the sea, as in falling into it
  • a gallon → G G is the abbreviation for gallon

Every word of the surface belongs in a pub and not one of them stays there: the drink is salt water, and knocking it back is an instruction rather than a swallow.

18 across SNIP

Coming back discontented, negotiates very low price, a bargain (4)

Definition: a bargain · deletion + reversal + charade

  • negotiates → NS NEGOTIATES emptied of everything between its ends leaves N and S
  • very low price → IP 1p is about as low as a price gets: the Roman numeral I and p for penny

The surface is a haggler at work, and the price in it is literal — one penny, written as a figure and a letter. Nothing is negotiated but the spelling.

20 across DEGENERATE

US comedian put away for drugs in decline (10)

Definition: decline · substitution

  • US comedian → DEGENERES Ellen DeGeneres, the American comic and chat-show host
  • drugs → Es are ecstasy tablets, and those are the two letters standing at the end of the name
  • put away → ATE to put away a big meal is to eat it, and that is what moves in

The name is spelt out in full and then loses its tail. E for ecstasy is standard crossword drug slang, and here the setter wants two of them at once.

23 across DESSERT WINE

Loudly complain after sailors originally aboard leave port? (7,4)

Definition: port · charade + container + homophone + first letter

  • leave → DESERT to desert a post is to leave it
  • sailors originally → S the opening letter of sailors
  • complain → WINE to complain is to whine, and whine said aloud is spelt this way instead

'Leave port' reads as one nautical phrase and the clue splits it in two, taking the first word for wordplay and the second for the definition.

25 across RIA

Take main road around valley (3)

Definition: valley · charade + reversal

  • Take → R R is the prescription sign for take, from the Latin recipe
  • main road → AI the A1 is the main road north, and its 1 is written as the letter I

Numbers standing in for letters is a Vlad habit: the 1 of the A1 is an I, exactly as the 1 in 14 down is. R for 'take' comes off a doctor's pad.

26 across RONDEAU

Neither partner recalled mentioning bread lines (7)

Definition: lines · charade + reversal + homophone

  • Neither partner → NOR neither always travels with nor, so that is its partner
  • bread → DEAU bread is dough, and the sound of dough is spelt out here in the French manner

Bread and dough are slang for money either way round, but here it is only the sound that matters. 'Lines' is the crossword's usual way of saying verse.

27 across ANISEED

Flavouring from America is in demand (7)

Definition: Flavouring · container + charade

  • America → A A is the standard abbreviation for America
  • demand → NEED a demand is a need
  • is → IS the word itself, taken as two letters

'In demand' is a phrase the surface needs and the wordplay pulls apart. The most ordinary word in the clue is the one carrying the instruction.

28 across CARNAL KNOWLEDGE

What Telford had a lot of to attract Romeo – sex (6,9)

Definition: sex · container

  • What Telford had a lot of → CANALKNOWLEDGE Thomas Telford built the canals, so canal knowledge is what he had in quantity
  • Romeo → R R is Romeo in the radio alphabet

Thomas Telford was the great canal engineer, and the joke is that fourteen letters of specialist expertise need only one more to turn into something quite different.

Down

1 down SALAD DAYS

Youth is speaking about another: ‘Dickhead!’ (5,4)

Definition: Youth · container + charade + first letter

  • is speaking → SAYS somebody who is speaking says
  • another → ALAD another of what the clue has just called a youth is a lad
  • Dickhead → D the first letter of Dick — its head

The rudeness is a red herring: only the head of the name Dick is wanted. And 'another' leans on the definition, because another youth is simply a lad.

2 down ORBITAL

B-list occasionally spoken about is going round (7)

Definition: going round · container + alternate letters

  • spoken → ORAL an oral exam is a spoken one
  • B-list occasionally → BIT every other letter of B-LIST: B, I and T

The surface is showbiz chatter and both instructions pass as ordinary description. Watch 'spoken': here it is a straight synonym, not an invitation to listen.

3 down TITMOUSE

Singer Tom with suite trashed (8)

Definition: Singer · anagram

  • Tom → TOM three of the eight letters, taken as they stand
  • suite → SUITE the other five letters of the fodder
  • trashed → anagram indicator

A rock star and a wrecked hotel suite make a perfectly good sentence, which is the point — neither word is doing the job it appears to be doing.

4 down LYRIC

I get emotional about returning to London’s original theatre (5)

Definition: theatre · container + reversal + charade + first letter

  • London’s original → L the opening letter of London
  • get emotional → CRY to get emotional is to cry
  • I → I the pronoun taken at face value as a letter

The Lyric on Shaftesbury Avenue is a real playhouse, so the last word is the definition even though it looks like part of the scene it describes.

5 down YARD OF ALE

Long for a drink? (4,2,3)

Definition: Long for a drink? · &lit + charade

  • Long → YARD a yard is a long measure, three feet of it
  • for → OF the two prepositions swap freely when a vessel is named by what it holds
  • a drink → ALE ale is the drink being measured out

Every word works twice: read straight it is a thirsty sigh, read as instructions it builds the thing itself. A clue that is all definition and all wordplay is called an &lit.

6 down UPSIDE

Turn left, perhaps, after park entrance – positive? (6)

Definition: positive · charade + first letter

  • Turn → U the turn that doubles back on itself is a U-turn
  • after park entrance → P the entrance to PARK is its opening letter
  • left, perhaps → SIDE left is one example of a side, which 'perhaps' flags

The comma after the first word is doing real work: that word stands alone rather than joining the driving instruction the rest of the surface promises.

7 down RAILING

Complaining in bar (7)

Definition: Complaining · double definition

  • Complaining → RAILING to rail at something is to complain loudly about it
  • bar → RAILING a piece of ironmongery: the bar-work guarding a staircase or a balcony

Three words, no wordplay and two meanings of one word: a verb of protest and a piece of ironmongery. The little 'in' is all that joins them.

8 down PADRE

Forces serviceman, might you say? (5)

Definition: Forces serviceman, might you say? · cryptic definition

  • Forces serviceman → Read straight this is any enlisted member of the armed services, which is the picture the surface wants: a soldier, a sailor, an airman.
  • might you say? → The question mark admits the pun. The man meant is the one who takes the service — the chaplain attached to a regiment, and no fighting man at all.

Everything turns on the word 'service': the Forces have one meaning for it and the chapel quite another, and the answer is the man who belongs to both.

14 down AWESTRUCK

Hit wicket – AUS 1 down (spinner finally bowled over) (9)

Definition: bowled over · anagram + deletion + last letter

  • wicket → WCKET WICKET hands over its letters once the 1 has gone
  • 1 down → 1 is the Roman numeral I, and one down is one out, so that I leaves WICKET
  • AUS → AUS the scoreboard's Australia, taken as three letters
  • spinner finally → R the last letter of spinneR
  • Hit → anagram indicator

A whole cricket scoreboard, and every word of it is working. The definition hides best of all, because being knocked over sounds like still more cricket.

16 down SKEDADDLE

Synonyms to begin with: fly, go off, make a run for it (9)

Definition: make a run for it · charade + first letter

  • Synonyms to begin with → S the opening letter of Synonyms
  • fly → KED a ked is the wingless fly that lives on sheep
  • go off → ADDLE eggs that go off addle

'Synonyms' is lovely misdirection: it promises a list of words meaning the same thing, and only its first letter is wanted. The ked is worth meeting.

17 down REMEDIAL

Call service engineers initially for correction (8)

Definition: for correction · charade

  • service engineers → REME the Royal Electrical and Mechanical Engineers, known to the army as REME
  • Call → DIAL to call somebody is to dial them

REME turns up whenever a clue mentions service engineers, and is worth knowing alongside RE for the Royal Engineers. The surface is a helpdesk; the answer is a classroom.

19 down INSANER

Less sensible son gets involved making little difference (7)

Definition: Less sensible · container

  • making little difference → INANER the more inane a thing is, the less difference it makes
  • son → S S is the abbreviation for son

Comparatives are what make this work: definition and wordplay are both '-er' forms, which is easy to miss while the surface is busy being about a family.

21 down AIRHEAD

Current Republican leading on the surface is a moron (7)

Definition: a moron · container + charade

  • leading → AHEAD the runner who is leading is ahead
  • Current → I I is the physicist's symbol for electric current
  • Republican → R R is the standard abbreviation for Republican

Politics is the misdirection: the opening two words are not a person at all but two single letters, one out of physics and one off a ballot paper.

22 down GENERA

A green revolution of sorts (6)

Definition: sorts · anagram

  • A green → AGREEN the six letters of the fodder
  • revolution → anagram indicator

'A green revolution' is a real phrase about farming, which is exactly why it does not look like six letters waiting to be rearranged.

23 down DORIC

Make editor ultimately responsible for such columns (5)

Definition: such columns · charade + last letter

  • Make → DO do and make share the sense of performing a task
  • editor ultimately → R the last letter of editoR
  • responsible for → IC i/c is short for in charge, which is what being responsible for something is

The surface is an office row about who carries the blame. Learn i/c for 'in charge': two letters that hide easily inside an innocent phrase.

24 down IMAGO

Vlad’s past being ideal (5)

Definition: ideal · charade

  • Vlad’s → IM the setter of this puzzle is Vlad, so Vlad's is I'm
  • past → AGO something past happened ago

Setters sign their own clues: whenever a Guardian puzzle names its setter, read it as 'I'. It is the same convention as 'the setter' meaning ME.
