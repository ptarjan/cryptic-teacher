Across

1 across SCATHING

Critical about weak golf on the back of lessons (8)

Definition: Critical · charade + last letter

Critical → SCATHING: a scathing review is criticism at its most withering, so the plain adjective covers it with room to spare.

  • lessons → S the closing letter of the word, the one thing everything else rides behind
  • about → CA CA is circa, the Latin short form a crossword uses for approximately
  • weak → THIN a thin excuse is a weak one, the adjectives swapping straight over
  • golf → G golf is G in the NATO spelling alphabet, nothing to do with the sport
  • on the back of — Riding on the back of something puts you behind it, and the back of a word is the letter it closes with.

The surface is a school report and the clue is four unrelated pieces. 'Golf' is the radio-alphabet letter, and 'about' is circa rather than an instruction to wrap anything round anything.

6 across CUT OFF

Right leaving rogue aristocrat disinherited (3,3)

Definition: disinherited · charade + deletion

disinherited → CUT OFF: an heir cut off without a penny has been struck out of the will, which is exactly what disinheriting does.

  • Right → R R is right on a map or a dance step, and here it is the letter that goes missing
  • rogue → CU a rogue is a CUR, less the R that has just left
  • aristocrat → TOFF a toff is British slang for a posh, upper-class man
  • leaving — Something leaving has gone from where it stood, so the letter named just before it walks out of the word that follows.

'Right leaving' is an instruction rather than part of the picture — nothing here is right-wing. The rogue loses a letter and the two words are then laid end to end.

9 across SNAG

Term for singultus? Have a go at hiccup (4)

Definition: hiccup · charade + first letter

hiccup → SNAG: a hiccup in a plan is a small setback that interrupts it, which is the everyday figurative sense the answer carries.

  • singultus → S singultus is medical Latin for a hiccup, and only its opening letter is wanted
  • Have a go at → NAG to have a go at somebody is to nag them, keeping on at them about something
  • Term for — A term standing for a long word is its shorthand, and the shorthand asked for here is loose — just the single letter it opens with.

The Latin is a joke on the last word — singultus is exactly what a hiccup is — but it sits in the clue for its first letter alone.

10 across ANTE MORTEM

Tube connected back on Franco's heart before death (4,6)

Definition: before death · charade + reversal + middle letters

before death → ANTE MORTEM: the Latin tag means precisely that, and English keeps it for statements and injuries dated to while the patient still lived.

  • Franco's → AN the two letters at the dead centre of the Spanish dictator's name
  • connected → TEM two people who connected have MET, and that word arrives the wrong way round
  • Tube → ORTEM the Paris underground is the METRO, turned round like the piece before it
  • back — An across entry read back runs right to left, so the two words named before it are written from their far ends.
  • on — In an across entry a piece landing on another follows it, so the reversed pair comes after the letters it lands on.
  • heart — The heart of a word is what lies at its centre, equally far from either end.

The Tube is a London one on the surface and a Paris one in the wordplay, and 'connected' is the past tense of meet rather than an instruction to join things up.

11 across EVENT HORIZON

Volts inside transformed the inner zoo's inescapable boundary (5,7)

Definition: inescapable boundary · anagram

inescapable boundary → EVENT HORIZON: it is the surface round a black hole past which nothing, not even light, can climb back out.

  • Volts → V V is the symbol printed on any battery for volts
  • the inner zoo → THEINNERZOO eleven letters of raw material, one short of the light until the V joins them
  • inside — The volts go inside the material rather than parking at one end, so that letter has to be counted into what gets rearranged.
  • transformed — Something transformed keeps its substance and loses its shape, which is what happens to a fistful of letters.

'Inside' looks like an insertion instruction, and the V does go inside — but there is nothing to insert it into until the other eleven letters have been shuffled.

14 across FIASCO

Breakdown company tails Italian cars, ignoring Tesla (6)

Definition: Breakdown · deletion + charade

Breakdown → FIASCO: when arrangements break down completely and in public, the result is a fiasco — a total and faintly comic failure.

  • Italian cars → FIAS the Italian cars are FIATS, the marque from Turin, one letter lighter
  • Tesla → T the tesla is the SI unit of magnetic flux density and its symbol is T
  • company → CO CO is how a company abbreviates itself on a letterhead
  • tails — A car that tails another drives along behind it, so the letters named do the same and settle on the end.
  • ignoring — What is ignored is left out altogether, so the letter named after it never joins the word at all.

Two makes of car and both are decoys: the American one is here as a unit of measurement, and 'breakdown' is a collapse of arrangements rather than of an engine.

16 across EXTOLLED

Highly regarded old partner rang (8)

Definition: Highly regarded · charade

Highly regarded → EXTOLLED: to extol is to praise something to the skies, and whatever gets that treatment is thereby held in high esteem.

  • old partner → EX your ex is the partner you used to have, the prefix doing duty as a noun
  • rang → TOLLED a bell that tolled rang slowly, one stroke at a time

Nothing marks the join, which is what makes a short charade hard: two everyday words end to end, with the definition in front and no link word between them.

17 across ANGELENO

A certain (Liberal perhaps) North American to the west (8)

Definition: A certain (Liberal perhaps) North American to the west · &lit + charade + reversal

The whole clue reads straight: this is a Los Angeles native, a North American out west, who might perfectly well be a Liberal.

  • North American → AN NA is the standard short form of North American, and it lands back to front
  • perhaps → GE perhaps is eg, for example, and that is turned round too
  • Liberal → L L is the ballot-paper abbreviation for a Liberal candidate
  • A certain → ENO a certain somebody is one somebody, and it arrives the wrong way round like the rest
  • to the west — West is leftwards on the grid, so an across answer built westward is written from its last letter back towards its first.

Every word does both jobs at once. Read straight the clue describes a particular westerly American; read as wordplay the same words are four abbreviations written east to west.

19 across DEPOSE

15 give evidence (6)

Definition: 15 · double definition

15 → DEPOSE is to overthrow a monarch; give evidence → DEPOSE is to testify on oath, one verb doing two entirely unrelated jobs.

  • 15 → a cross-reference: the number sends you to another light in this grid for the first sense
  • give evidence → the courtroom sense, to make a sworn statement out of court for use in one

A number in place of a word is a cross-reference, not an abbreviation, and the two senses share nothing but their spelling: one topples a ruler, the other swears an oath.

21 across CANTANKEROUS

Is oiler able to go finally, with America picking quarrels? (12)

Definition: picking quarrels · charade + last letter

picking quarrels → CANTANKEROUS: a cantankerous neighbour is bad-tempered and contrary, forever finding something to argue about.

  • able to → CAN if you are able to do something you can do it
  • oiler → TANKER an oiler is a ship carrying oil, which is a tanker
  • go → O the closing letter of that two-letter word
  • America → US US is the country's initials standing in for the whole name
  • finally — What comes finally is what stands at the very end, so only the last letter of the little word beside it is taken.

Read as English it is a question about a ship leaving port, and that is the whole of the misdirection: no piece moves anywhere, they simply follow one another as given.

24 across DECLARE WAR

Last month city turned greener, to announce start of campaign (7,3)

Definition: announce start of campaign · charade + reversal

announce start of campaign → DECLARE WAR: declaring war is the formal announcement that a military campaign has begun, so the phrase reads almost literally.

  • Last month → DEC December is the last of the twelve, and DEC is how a diary prints it
  • city → LA LA is Los Angeles in two letters, the way a ticket writes it
  • greener → REWAR a greener hand is a rawer one, less experienced, and it arrives back to front
  • turned — A word turned is facing the other way, so the piece beside it is read from its end back to its start.

'Greener' is a comparative about inexperience rather than about the environment, and the city is the same one 17 across comes from.

26 across LEEK

National emblem that's terrifying on lieutenant's face (4)

Definition: National emblem · charade + first letter

National emblem → LEEK: the leek is the national emblem of Wales, worn on St David's Day alongside the daffodil.

  • lieutenant's → L the front letter of the rank, which is also how the rank is abbreviated
  • that's terrifying → EEK the noise a frightened character makes in a comic strip
  • on — In an across entry a piece sitting on another comes straight after it, so the cry follows the letter it lands on.
  • face — A word's face is the front it shows the world, which is the letter it begins with.

'National emblem' could be almost anything, so this one is solved from the wordplay end: a rank's opening letter and the squeal of a startled cartoon.

27 across MARROW

Old lady spat on Republican, giving tissue (6)

Definition: tissue · charade

tissue → MARROW: marrow is the soft tissue filling the middle of a bone, so the definition names the general class it belongs to.

  • Old lady → MA MA is the affectionate short form for a mother
  • Republican → R R is the letter printed after an American Republican's name
  • spat → ROW a spat is a short quarrel, and a row is the noisier word for one
  • on — In an across entry a thing landing on another sits directly after it, so the quarrel follows the party's letter.

'Spat' looks like a past tense with someone doing the spitting, and it is a noun; the American party is in the clue for its initial and nothing else.

28 across YOSEMITE

Yeti wanders around some blasted mountain wilderness (8)

Definition: mountain wilderness · anagram

mountain wilderness → YOSEMITE: the California national park of granite cliffs and high Sierra country, defined here by the kind of place it is.

  • Yeti → YETI the beast's name is raw material, not a character in the story
  • some → SOME the vague-looking word is fodder too, four more letters for the pile
  • wanders around — A creature that wanders around keeps to no fixed route, and neither do these letters once they have been picked up.
  • blasted — Something blasted has been blown apart, so the little word beside it is shattered into loose letters as well.

Two indicators and two lots of fodder sit side by side, which is why it reads so smoothly as a sentence about a monster on a mountain.

2 down CONVENIENT

Easy to reach sister's house where pigeon regularly nests (10)

Definition: Easy to reach · container + alternate letters

Easy to reach → CONVENIENT: a convenient shop is close at hand and no trouble to get to, which is the handy sense rather than the merely suitable one.

  • sister's house → CONVENT the sister is a nun, and the house she lives in with the others is a convent
  • pigeon → IEN every second letter of the bird, taken from the second one onwards
  • regularly — Letters picked regularly come at a steady interval — here every second one of the bird's name, counting from the second.
  • nests — A bird that nests settles down inside something, so what it brings goes in the middle rather than on either end.

'Sister' is the religious sense rather than a sibling, and the bird does not hand over all of itself — only the letters standing in the even places.

3 down TIGRESS

Cub leader's ramble starting late with tons to carry (7)

Definition: Cub leader · charade + deletion

Cub leader → TIGRESS: a tiger cub's leader is the mother that raises and defends it, so the definition names her by the young she heads.

  • tons → T T is the standard abbreviation for tons on any weight or cargo label
  • ramble → IGRESS to ramble in speech is to DIGRESS, and that word here starts one letter late
  • starting late — A word starting late does not begin where it should: its first letter never arrives and the rest moves up to the front.
  • to carry — In a down entry the load rides above whatever bears it, so the thing carried is written on top.

The Scout hut is the trap. A Cub leader in Britain is the adult running a pack of Cub Scouts, but the cub here is a big cat's and its leader is its mother.

4 down INAPT

Turning up halfway through test panicky is not ideal (5)

Definition: not ideal · hidden word + reversal

not ideal → INAPT: inapt is the plain negative of apt, so something inapt is unsuited to the occasion rather than merely disappointing.

  • test panicky → INAPT five consecutive letters straddle the space between the two words, and they run the wrong way
  • Turning up — In a down entry up is the direction of travel, so the run of letters is read from the bottom end back towards the top.
  • halfway through — Halfway through the phrase is where to look: the letters sit dead centre, with three to spare on either side of them.

'Panicky' reads as an anagram signal and there is nothing to shuffle: the letters are already sitting in the clue, in order, spelled from the far end.

5 down GET ONE'S OWN WAY

Now newest yoga moves prevail (3,4,3,3)

Definition: prevail · anagram

prevail → GET ONE'S OWN WAY: to prevail is to have your preference win out over everyone else's, which is what the phrase describes in idiom.

  • Now newest yoga → NOWNEWESTYOGA thirteen letters of raw material — count them against the enumeration before starting
  • moves → anagram indicator
  • moves — Yoga moves are changes of position, and letters that change position finish somewhere other than where the clue printed them.

Thirteen letters spread over four short words are hard to see as one lump; the giveaway is that 'now newest yoga' is nonsense as English and exactly the right length.

6 down COMMITTED

Staunch Titoist cleared out communist dictator's original closets (9)

Definition: Staunch · container + outer letters

Staunch → COMMITTED: a staunch supporter is one who cannot be shifted, and that immovable loyalty is what the answer names.

  • communist → COMMIE COMMIE is the familiar clipping of the longer word
  • dictator's original → D the original letter of a word is the one it opens with
  • Titoist → TT empty out the middle and the two T's at either end are all that survive
  • cleared out — A room cleared out keeps only its walls, so a word cleared out keeps only the letters standing at its two ends.
  • closets — To closet somebody is to shut them away in a small room, so whatever is closeted goes inside rather than alongside.

The politics is scenery. Tito's followers are wanted for their two outside letters only, and the dictator hands over nothing but the letter he starts with.

7 down TURMOIL

Stir cooking fat after hearing how long it lasts (7)

Definition: Stir · homophone + charade

Stir → TURMOIL: a stir is a commotion that unsettles everyone present, and turmoil is that same confusion at full strength.

  • how long it lasts → TURM how long a sentence or a school year lasts is a term, and said aloud it comes out spelled this way
  • cooking fat → OIL the fat you fry in pours out of a bottle
  • after hearing — Hearing asks for the sound of a word rather than its spelling, and after sets the next piece down behind what you have just heard.

'Stir' is prison slang as well as a commotion, which is what makes the surface hold: the sound wanted is the length of a sentence, not the cooking.

8 down FEEL

Charge pound for love, perhaps (4)

Definition: love, perhaps · charade

love, perhaps → FEEL: loving is one instance of what a person can feel, so the general verb is defined by naming a particular emotion.

  • Charge → FEE the charge a solicitor or a gallery makes is its fee
  • pound → L L is the pound sign £, from the Latin libra — sterling, not weight

Two money words build one that has nothing to do with money, and 'perhaps' is the giveaway that the definition names an example rather than a synonym.

13 down PERSISTENT

Article taken from old Iranian's shelter hard to get rid of (10)

Definition: hard to get rid of · deletion + charade

hard to get rid of → PERSISTENT: a persistent cough or stain keeps coming back however often it is dealt with, which is what makes it hard to shift.

  • Article → AN AN is one of English's three articles, and it is the one that has to go
  • old Iranian's → PERSIS an old Iranian is a Persian, and the possessive S counts as part of the raw material
  • shelter → TENT the simplest shelter there is, canvas over a couple of poles
  • taken from — What is taken from a word is subtracted out of it, so the two letters named first come out of the longer word that follows.

'Article' is the grammatical kind rather than something in a newspaper, and the Persian has to be read with his apostrophe-S attached before the two letters can be lifted out.

15 down OVERTHROW

Obvious method to bag runs - fielding error (9)

Definition: fielding error · charade + container

fielding error → OVERTHROW: in cricket an overthrow is a wild return from the field that lets the batters run again, so it names a fielder's mistake.

  • Obvious → OVERT an overt act is one done in the open, which is the same as obvious
  • method → HOW the how of a thing is the method used — the question word doing duty as a noun
  • runs → R R is runs on a cricket scorecard
  • to bag — To bag something is to put it in a bag, so the letter that follows is shut inside the word before it rather than added after it.

The dash makes 'fielding error' look like an afterthought when it is the definition, and the only cricket in the wordplay is a single letter.

18 down EMAILER

Dealing with story about case of ageism upset correspondent (7)

Definition: correspondent · charade + container + outer letters + reversal

correspondent → EMAILER: a correspondent is simply somebody who writes to you, and these days that writing arrives electronically rather than by post.

  • Dealing with → RE RE heads a memo and means on the subject of, which is dealing with something
  • story → LIE a story told to deceive is a lie, the small three-letter kind
  • ageism → AM the two letters on the outside of the word, the ones its case is made of
  • about — One thing written about another goes round it, so what follows sits inside the word named before it.
  • case of — A case is what encloses something, so the case of a word is the pair of letters on its outside.
  • upset — Upset means overturned, and in a down entry an overturned assembly is read from the bottom of the light upwards.

'Dealing with' wants to be the definition, since correspondents deal with letters; it is really the two letters at the head of a memo, and the whole assembly is then read upwards.

20 down PROBLEM

Left aboard spaceship, Mike's worry reported to Houston? (7)

Definition: worry · container + charade

worry → PROBLEM: the thing on your mind that needs solving is your problem, the everyday sense in which a worry and a difficulty are one and the same.

  • spaceship → PROBE an unmanned craft sent off to another planet is a probe
  • Left → L L is left, as on a pair of gloves or a stage direction
  • Mike → M Mike is M in the NATO alphabet that flight crews actually speak
  • reported to Houston? → surface only: it nods at the Apollo 13 radio call, and 'reported' is not asking for a sound here
  • aboard — Whatever is taken aboard is carried within the vessel, so the letter goes inside the craft's name rather than beside it.

'Reported' is the trap, because it almost always calls for a homophone. Here it belongs to the picture — the famous line sent up from a crippled spacecraft.

22 down EUROS

French bread you proved, one hears (5)

Definition: French bread · homophone

French bread → EUROS: bread is slang for money and the money in France is the euro, so the two words together name a currency, not a loaf.

  • you proved → EUROS the pronoun is a single sound, and dough that has proved has risen — it rose
  • one hears — What is heard rather than read is a sound, so the two words before it are to be said aloud and written down some other way.

'French bread' is money rather than a baguette: bread is old slang for cash. The bakery is there only to make 'proved' sound like something out of a recipe.

23 down MEGA

Giant gnome gardener keeps inside (4)

Definition: Giant · hidden word

Giant → MEGA: mega is the informal adjective for enormous, the unit prefix meaning a million doing service as slang.

  • gnome gardener → MEGA four consecutive letters run across the gap between the two words
  • keeps inside — What somebody keeps inside is not on show, so the letters are held whole within the words rather than spelled out by them.

A garden gnome is exactly what the surface wants you to picture, and the definition is the one word in the clue with nothing to do with gardens.
