Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,062

Across

1 across GALANTINE

Old-fashioned lass, social worker at home beginning to eat cold dish (9)

Definition: cold dish · charade + first letter

  • Old-fashioned lass → GAL gal is a dated word for a lass
  • social worker → ANT an ant lives in a colony and labours for the nest — the crossword's standard worker
  • at home → IN someone at home is in
  • beginning to eat → E the beginning of Eat

Four short pieces laid end to end, and two of them are conventions worth banking on sight: ANT for 'social worker' and IN for 'at home'.

6 across GOAT

Attack nanny? (4)

Definition: nanny · charade

  • Attack → GO GO is the first word of the informal phrase for attacking someone, with the space between the two words closing up once they combine
  • Attack → AT AT is the second word, with the space between the two closing up

Two words closing into one is the smallest trick there is, and the hardest to spot in a four-letter answer, because the clue leaves no room for anything else to give it away.

8 across FOLDABLE

Story about ancient newspaper should be this (8)

Definition: newspaper should be this · container

  • Story → FABLE a fable is a story with a moral
  • ancient → OLD ancient = old

'Story' and 'ancient' are both busy making a picture of yesterday's paper, which is exactly why neither of them reads as a piece of the answer.

9 across PUNKAH

A husband trailing after sort of rock fan (6)

Definition: fan · charade

  • sort of rock → PUNK punk is a sort of rock music
  • A → A the clue's own A, taken at face value
  • husband → H h = husband, the standard abbreviation

The clue is written back to front — the two small pieces are named first and end up last. H for 'husband' is an abbreviation to learn early.

10 across MENIAL

Maiden friendly, no good as servant? (6)

Definition: servant · charade + deletion

  • Maiden → M m = maiden, the cricket over from which no runs are scored
  • friendly → GENIAL genial = friendly
  • good → G g = good — this is the letter that goes

The surface reads as a character reference and every word of it is machinery — even 'no good', which sounds like a verdict and is an instruction.

11 across TADPOLES

Little creatures told apes to scatter (8)

Definition: Little creatures · anagram

  • told apes → TOLDAPES anagram fodder, eight letters
  • to scatter → anagram indicator

The surface is a keeper losing control of the ape house, and the two words that build that picture are the two words that build the answer.

12 across SACHEM

Account penned by survivor of flood, political leader (6)

Definition: political leader · container

  • survivor of flood → SHEM Shem, the son of Noah who came through the Flood in the ark
  • Account → AC a/c = account, as on a bank statement

'Penned' does two jobs at once — writing an account and shutting something in — and only the second of them is the instruction.

15 across AVERMENT

Chaps entering prevent allegation (8)

Definition: allegation · container

  • prevent → AVERT to avert a disaster is to prevent it
  • Chaps → MEN chaps = men

Three words and no padding, which is what makes it hard: 'entering' is the only signpost, and 'prevent' has to be read as a verb before it gives anything up.

16 across PROPOSED

Intended to give reminder about model (8)

Definition: Intended · container

  • reminder → PROD a prod is a reminder, a nudge to get on with it
  • model → POSE to model for a painter is to pose

'Model' is the trap: it looks like a noun — a mannequin, or a miniature aeroplane — and the clue wants the verb.

19 across TURING

Mathematician on a round trip with nothing to lose (6)

Definition: Mathematician · deletion

  • on a round trip → TOURING somebody on a round trip is touring
  • nothing → O nothing = O, the letter shaped like a zero — this is the letter that goes

'With nothing to lose' reads as a gambler's shrug and is really an instruction to drop a letter. Not a word of the surface mentions a computer.

21 across SPILLAGE

Wise person swallowing tablet, water going all over the place? (8)

Definition: water going all over the place · container

  • Wise person → SAGE a sage is a wise person
  • tablet → PILL a tablet of medicine is a pill

'Swallowing tablet' is one picture doing two jobs: the sage taking medicine, and one word going inside another.

22 across DALASI

One green dish about to be served with bit of bread in Africa (6)

Definition: bit of bread in Africa · charade + reversal

  • One → I one = I, the Roman numeral
  • green dish → SALAD a salad is the green dish

Both halves of the clue are disguised as lunch. 'Bread' is the slang for money, and spotting that is what turns a meal into a currency.

24 across ZINNIA

Plant I found at back of small hotel in South Africa (6)

Definition: Plant · charade + container

  • small hotel → INN an inn is a small hotel
  • I → I the clue's own I, taken at face value
  • South Africa → ZA ZA is South Africa's international code, seen on cars and web addresses

ZA for South Africa is worth banking — the country's vehicle and internet code — and it is one of the few pairs of letters that can hold a word between them.

25 across SEAMOUNT

One must get excited about a large underwater feature (8)

Definition: large underwater feature · anagram

  • One must → ONEMUST anagram fodder, seven letters
  • a → A the article joins the fodder, making eight
  • get excited about → anagram indicator

The clue reads as advice about keeping calm, and 'One must' — which sounds like the opening of a rule — is material rather than instruction.

26 across TSAR

Ruler using skills, cycling round (4)

Definition: Ruler · cycling

  • skills → ARTS the arts are skills — think of the martial arts

Cycling is not an anagram: the letters keep their order and only the starting point moves. Begin ARTS two letters further along and you are there.

27 across EXPLOITER

Old lover quietly to hang around for abusive boss? (9)

Definition: abusive boss · charade

  • Old lover → EX an old lover is an ex
  • quietly → P p = piano, the musical direction to play quietly
  • to hang around → LOITER to loiter is to hang around with no purpose

P for 'quietly' comes from music, where piano is marked p — one of the abbreviations that repays learning, since setters reach for it constantly.

Down

1 down GROVE

Good dog taken short in wood? (5)

Definition: wood · charade + deletion

  • Good → G g = good, as on a school report
  • dog taken short → ROVE ROVER is the setter's default dog, docked of its final R

Rover is the dog crosswords reach for, and 'taken short' is one of the half-dozen phrasings that all mean the same thing — learn both on sight.

2 down LADDISH

The French g-girl deemed attractive – rude boy may be this (7)

Definition: rude boy may be this · charade

  • The French → LA 'the' in French, feminine singular
  • girl deemed attractive → DDISH a girl deemed attractive is a DISH, and the stammer gives that word's first letter twice

The surface is a boy too smitten to get the word out, and his stammer is the instruction rather than the scenery.

3 down NOBEL

European inventor not in the same league as American one, reportedly (5)

Definition: European inventor · homophone

  • not in the same league as American one → NOBEL the American inventor is Bell, of the telephone, and to call somebody 'no Bell' is to say they are not in that league

Two inventors in five letters: the Swede you want and the American you say. 'No Einstein' is the idiom the setter has borrowed and refitted.

4 down INERTIA

Trendy, endlessly tired, somehow having a disinclination to move (7)

Definition: disinclination to move · charade + anagram + deletion

  • Trendy → IN in = trendy, fashionable
  • endlessly tired → TIRE TIRED without its final letter
  • a → A the article joins the letters to be shuffled
  • somehow → anagram indicator

Only the tail of the answer is shuffled — the first two letters stand still while the rest are jumbled behind them, which is why the whole thing does not look like an anagram.

5 down EXPEDIENT

No longer wanting inept cluing, engaging editor – that’s advantageous (9)

Definition: advantageous · charade + anagram + container

  • No longer wanting → EX an ex is the partner no longer wanted
  • inept → PIENT the five letters of INEPT, in a new order
  • editor → ED ed = editor, as on a title page

The setter is winking at the trade: 'inept cluing' describes both a badly written clue and the state five of these letters are left in.

6 down GUNROOM

Mess in which a Parisian male tucks into curry (7)

Definition: Mess · container

  • curry → GROOM to curry a horse is to groom it
  • a Parisian male → UN 'a' in French is UN in the masculine and UNE in the feminine — 'male' chooses the form

'Curry' is the trap: the dish is a red herring and the verb, currying a horse, is what the clue means. 'Male' supplies no letter at all — it is there to pick between two French articles.

7 down AGAMEMNON

Greek king, one man troubled about leg (9)

Definition: Greek king · anagram + container

  • one man → ONEMAN anagram fodder, six letters
  • leg → GAM a gam is a leg — old slang, and a crossword regular
  • troubled → anagram indicator

'Leg' looks like a piece of the surface's grumbling and is really three letters of slang — the one lump that stays intact while everything else is rearranged around it.

13 down ATROPHIES

This opera, sadly, goes downhill (9)

Definition: goes downhill · anagram

  • This opera → THISOPERA anagram fodder, nine letters
  • sadly → anagram indicator

The fodder hides in plain sight because 'This' looks like a pointer to the clue itself rather than four letters to be used.

14 down MOSS AGATE

Second entrance blocked by donkey flipping over stone (4,5)

Definition: stone · charade + container + reversal

  • Second → MO a mo is a moment, and so is a second
  • donkey → SSA a donkey is an ASS, turned end for end
  • entrance → GATE a gate is an entrance

The picture is literal — the donkey is standing in the gateway — which is why the wordplay drops it between the two halves of the entrance rather than after them.

17 down PILSNER

Drink passing through children’s lips the wrong way (7)

Definition: Drink · hidden word + reversal

  • children’s lips → PILSNER the letters run backwards through childRENS LIPs, straddling both words

Hidden words are usually read forwards, so the direction is the surprise. Lips and a drink belong to the same picture, which is what keeps the join invisible.

18 down DRESS UP

Prepare to look smart in something with hem raised (5,2)

Definition: Prepare to look smart · charade

  • something with hem → DRESS a dress is the garment finished with a hem
  • raised → UP raised = up

'Raised' is not a reversal here, tempting as that looks in a down clue — it simply means UP, and the two pieces sit end to end.

20 down RULE OUT

Reject what could be seen as lure (4,3)

Definition: Reject · anagram

  • lure → not fodder but product: this is the word you get by obeying the answer, whose first half is four letters and whose second half tells you what to do with them

An anagram turned inside out. Nothing in the clue tells you to shuffle anything — the answer itself is the instruction, and the four-letter word in the clue is what obeying it produces.

22 down DRACO

Stars in old club perhaps being upset (5)

Definition: Stars · charade + reversal

  • old → O o = old, the standard abbreviation
  • club perhaps → CARD a club is one of the four suits, so a club is a card — 'perhaps' marks it as an example

The clue offers a club and lets you think of a nightclub or a weapon before the playing card, while 'stars' plays the same game with celebrities.

23 down SONAR

What enables one to hear what comes from some scratches on a record (5)

Definition: What enables one to hear · hidden word

  • scratches on a record → SONAR the letters are sitting in scratcheS ON A Record, straddling four words

A description rather than a synonym is usually the sign that the wordplay will be the easier half, and here the letters are lying in plain sight.
