Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,159

Across

1 across HEADBAND

Manage group in tennis gear (8)

Definition: tennis gear · charade

  • Manage → HEAD to head a department is to manage it
  • group → BAND a band is a group, of musicians or of anyone else

Both halves are office words on the surface — one runs a department, the other plays music — and only when they are read as one word does anything sporting appear.

5 across AT WAR

Fighting a dermal disorder after cycling (2,3)

Definition: Fighting · charade + cycling

  • a → A the article taken at face value, one letter
  • dermal disorder → TWAR a wart is a blemish of the skin, and its last letter rides round to the front

The surface is a cyclist with a skin complaint; the answer is two words about armies. 'Dermal' is there to say which kind of blemish is wanted.

9 across ROGET

Booked man of many words (5)

Definition: Booked · double definition

  • Booked → the surface offers cautioned or reserved; the sense wanted is that the name now sits on a spine on every reference shelf
  • man of many words → the Victorian physician who spent his retirement listing synonyms by the thousand

The thesaurus is so completely his that the surname alone has come to mean the book, and that is exactly what 'Booked' is playing on.

10 across CUCUMBERS

Before seconds, alternately crunch on brown salad components (9)

Definition: salad components · charade + alternate letters

  • crunch → CUC every other letter of the word, starting at the first
  • brown → UMBER umber is a brown earth pigment, raw or burnt
  • seconds → S s is the abbreviation on any stopwatch

Everything on the surface is edible, and only 'brown' is doing colour work: umber is a painter's pigment rather than a food, which is the switch the clue turns on.

11 across OVER-EMOTIONAL

Into amore ('love') - badly so? (4-9)

Definition: Into amore ('love') - badly so? · anagram + &lit

  • Into amore ('love') → INTOAMORELOVE thirteen letters of fodder, the bracketed translation counted in

The bracket is not an aside: the glossed word is fodder too. And the sentence doubles as the definition, which is what an all-in-one clue does.

13 across IRONY

Tough guy finally getting subtle humour (5)

Definition: subtle humour · charade + last letter

  • Tough → IRON iron stands for anything hard and unyielding — an iron will, an iron constitution
  • guy → Y the last letter of the word

'Tough guy' is one idea on the surface and two separate instructions underneath, which is the whole trick of the clue.

14 across WALKATHON

What is unusual and concerning about a 50k race no-one runs? (9)

Definition: race no-one runs · anagram + container + charade

  • What → WATH the four letters of the question word, re-ordered
  • a 50k → ALK a, then L for the Roman fifty, then k
  • concerning → ON on means concerning, as in a book on birds

The question mark concedes that the definition is a joke, and the distance is not measuring anything: '50k' is there to hand over three letters.

15 across EASY CHAIR

Laid-back committee head, one comfortably supporting you (4,5)

Definition: one comfortably supporting you · charade

  • Laid-back → EASY a laid-back person is unhurried and untroubled
  • committee head → CHAIR whoever chairs a meeting is simply the chair

The definition describes furniture but reads as a person, which is what keeps the committee surface standing up right to the last word.

17 across RAISE

Pull up fish escaping from trap (5)

Definition: Pull up · homophone

  • fish → RAISE rays are the flat cartilaginous sea fish, and 'fish' serves as its own plural

The surface sells a fish wriggling out of a fish trap, which sets you hunting for a deletion. Nothing is taken away here: the clue only asks you to say the word aloud.

18 across LIGHT-FINGERED

Disorder persisted - Spooner's inclined to steal (5-8)

Definition: inclined to steal · spoonerism

  • Disorder → FIGHT a fight is disorder breaking out
  • persisted → LINGERED what persisted has lingered on
  • Spooner's → LIGHTFINGERED say those two words with their opening sounds traded, F for L

A Spooner clue is easiest attacked from the definition end: think of the phrase for a thief first, then swap the sounds back and check the clue's own words appear.

21 across TEMPERING

Softening but hardening (9)

Definition: Softening · double definition

  • Softening → moderating something — taking the edge off a remark, a mood or a judgement
  • hardening → the smith's heat-and-quench treatment that toughens steel

Three words, and the middle one pretends to object. The contradiction is the clue: one word is used in opposite directions by moralists and metalworkers.

22 across MAMBA

Primarily mouthy African menace biting avians? (5)

Definition: Primarily mouthy African menace biting avians? · first letters + &lit

  • mouthy African menace biting avians → MAMBA the opening letter of each of the five words, read in order

Every word does two jobs: it hands over a letter and it describes the creature. The question mark covers the birds, which are a snack rather than a speciality.

23 across ASSET

Hound having chased away bishop: that's useful (5)

Definition: useful · deletion

  • Hound → BASSET a basset is a short-legged breed of hound
  • bishop → B b is the bishop in chess notation

The surface is a dog chasing a clergyman. The wordplay wants the breed rather than the animal, and the chessboard supplies the letter that leaves.

24 across STANDS BY

Supports - without doing anything? (6,2)

Definition: Supports · double definition

  • Supports → staying loyal to someone through trouble, refusing to move away from them
  • without doing anything → waiting in readiness, or simply looking on while something happens

The dash is doing the work of 'or', and the question mark flags the unflattering reading: the same phrase praises a friend and condemns a bystander.

Down

1 down HARMONISED

Admonisher, after massage, is reconciled (10)

Definition: reconciled · anagram

  • Admonisher → ADMONISHER ten letters of fodder — a scolder, the person who tells you off

Ten letters is a lot to shuffle blind, so the tell is the surface: a scolder having a massage is a sentence built to be taken apart, not believed.

2 down ANGLEPOISE LAMPS

We're known to be bent in the workplace and when we're not charged, you're in the dark (10,5)

Definition: We're known to be bent in the workplace and when we're not charged, you're in the dark · cryptic definition

  • We're known to be bent in the workplace → the surface accuses a pair of crooked office workers; read straight, being bent is the whole point of a jointed arm on a desk
  • when we're not charged, you're in the dark → 'charged' sits next to 'bent' so it reads as a police charge, and the darkness gives the real sense away: no power, no light

There is no wordplay to take apart. The first person plural is the objects talking, which turns 'we're bent' into a boast about design rather than a confession.

3 down BATTERY

I'll give you energy: like a pancake? (7)

Definition: I'll give you energy · charade

  • a pancake → BATTER a pancake is nothing but batter until it hits the pan
  • like → Y the -y ending English adds to mean 'resembling it': sugary, watery, chalky

The colon does the work of an equals sign, and the question mark is the setter admitting that the second half is a made-up adjective rather than a word you would find in a dictionary.

4 down NACHO

Chip's the ultimate in he-men, virile, topless (5)

Definition: Chip · charade + last letter + deletion

  • he-men → N the letter at the very end of the hyphenated phrase
  • virile → ACHO virile is macho, and that word beheaded leaves four letters

'Chip's' looks possessive and is really 'chip is', which is the join between definition and wordplay. The crisp on the plate has nothing to do with the muscle-bound surface.

5 down ARMENIA

Country song about chess pieces (7)

Definition: Country · container

  • song → ARIA an aria is the song a character sings in an opera
  • chess pieces → MEN the pieces on a board are collectively its men

The surface promises country and western; the definition means country in the atlas sense. Chess supplying MEN is a convention worth pocketing — it turns up constantly.

6 down WHEELCHAIR RAMPS

They're inclined to get you to leave (10,5)

Definition: They're inclined to get you to leave · cryptic definition

  • They're inclined → the surface reads this as 'disposed to'; the sense meant is the physical one, sloping rather than willing
  • to get you to leave → not a hint to clear off — it is about being able to get out of a building at all

Nothing here is spelled out of anything. The clue turns on one word carrying two senses at once, which is the whole of what a cryptic definition asks you to spot.

7 down RASP

Looking up, I'll add: a glimpse of rank and file (4)

Definition: file · charade + first letter + reversal

  • I'll add → PS a postscript is what you add after signing off
  • a → A the article standing as one letter
  • rank → R the opening letter of the word

'Rank and file' is one phrase on the surface and two jobs underneath: only 'rank' feeds the wordplay, and 'file' is the definition sitting at the end.

8 down SCHILLER

Romantic German in school, increasingly unwell (8)

Definition: Romantic German · charade

  • school → SCH the standard abbreviation, as printed on a map or a timetable
  • increasingly unwell → ILLER 'increasingly' asks for the comparative — more ill than before

The capital R is the clue's one piece of honesty: this is the literary movement, not a lovestruck tourist, and the man wanted is its poet and playwright.

12 down INCENDIARY

Nicer day in resort: very hot (10)

Definition: very hot · anagram

  • Nicer day in → NICERDAYIN ten letters of fodder, holiday brochure and all

'Very hot' is not about the weather the surface is selling: the answer is the word for a remark inflammatory enough to set a room alight.

14 down WEAR THIN

Some raw earth I neglected to grow ragged (4,4)

Definition: grow ragged · hidden word

  • raw earth I neglected → WEARTHIN the letters run unbroken through these words, starting partway into the first

A long hidden answer hides best behind a plausible sentence, and this one runs straight through a gardener's excuse. 'Some' is the only signpost you are given.

16 down CAGIEST

In America, GI esteemed as 'most sly' (7)

Definition: most sly · hidden word

  • America, GI esteemed → CAGIEST the letters run on across the end of one word and into the next two

The comma and the capital letters are all working for the surface, which is precisely where the eye stops looking for a join.

17 down REGIMEN

Most of greens, sadly, Everyman's eaten for diet (7)

Definition: diet · deletion + anagram + container

  • greens → REGEN drop the last letter of the vegetables and re-order the five that are left
  • Everyman's → IM the setter calls himself Everyman, so 'Everyman is' shortens to I'm

Everyman signs his own puzzles: whenever his name appears it means I or me, and it is worth learning because he uses it most weeks.

19 down INGOT

Leading characters in Irish netball won gold? (5)

Definition: gold · charade + first letters

  • Irish netball → IN the opening letter of each of the two words
  • won → GOT you got the prize you won

The question mark is the setter admitting that the bar need not be gold — silver and steel are cast the same way — while the surface keeps you on medals.

20 down ETNA

Estonian regularly ignored volcano (4)

Definition: volcano · alternate letters

  • Estonian → ETNA drop every second letter of the nationality and read what is left

An eight-letter nationality and a four-letter answer: the arithmetic tells you letters are being discarded before you have worked out which ones.
