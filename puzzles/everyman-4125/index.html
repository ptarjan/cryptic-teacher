Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,125

Across

1 across SWATCH

Small timepiece's piece of cloth (6)

Definition: piece of cloth · charade

  • Small → S S is the size printed on a clothing label for small
  • timepiece → WATCH a watch is the timepiece you wear on your wrist

The trap is reading 'Small timepiece' as one idea. 'Small' stands alone as its abbreviation, and the apostrophe-s that looks possessive is only the joint between wordplay and definition.

5 across AGEIST

Discriminating announcement of just one summary (6)

Definition: Discriminating · charade + homophone

  • just one → A just one of a thing is a single one of it, and the indefinite article is the one-letter way of saying so
  • summary → AGEIST the gist of a report is its summary, and spoken straight on from the article those two words make a single six-letter sound

'Discriminating' is praise when you say it of a palate and an accusation here — that flip is the whole misdirection, and only the ear gets you from two words to one.

8 across LOWER THE BAR

Elbow rather jittery: make things easier (5,3,3)

Definition: make things easier · anagram

  • Elbow rather → LOWERTHEBAR eleven letters from these two words, redealt

'Elbow' is a body part in the surface and raw material in the parse. The idiom comes from the high jump, where the bar drops to make the clearance easy.

11 across UNSCREWED

Some of bosun's crew edgy, ready to take off (9)

Definition: ready to take off · hidden word

  • bosun's crew edgy → UNSCREWED nine consecutive letters begin at the fourth letter of the first word and stop two short of the end of the last

Hidden words like to run straight across an apostrophe, because the eye reads possession instead of letters. 'Ready to take off' sounds like an aeroplane and describes a lid.

12 across REPEL

Disgust: social outcast returns (5)

Definition: Disgust · reversal

  • social outcast → REPEL a leper is the byword for somebody society will not go near, and those five letters are then read the other way round

'Disgust' is the verb here, not the noun — you repel somebody the way a smell disgusts them. The outcast wanted is the figurative leper, not the medical one.

13 across PERIDOT

Precious-ish, emeraldesque rock in demand; olivine trinket, primarily! (7)

Definition: Precious-ish, emeraldesque rock in demand; olivine trinket, primarily! · &lit + first letters

  • Precious-ish, emeraldesque rock in demand; olivine trinket → PERIDOT the opening letter of each of these seven words, read in the order they stand

An all-in-one: the sentence describes the gem and spells it at the same time, and the exclamation mark is the setter enjoying that. 'Emeraldesque' was invented for the job.

14 across PROJECT

Launch campaign? (7)

Definition: Launch · double definition

  • Launch → to throw something forward — the same root that gives a missile its name
  • campaign → a piece of organised work with an aim and an end date

Two definitions and no wordplay. The question mark owns up to the second one being loose: a campaign is a kind of the thing rather than another word for it.

15 across SLEEPER

Overnight train: what it will cross (7)

Definition: Overnight train · double definition

  • Overnight train → the service with berths, taken so the journey happens while you are asleep
  • what it will cross → the timber or concrete beams laid crosswise under the rails, holding them to gauge

The whole clue turns on 'it' — the train from the first half. Americans call those beams ties, which is why the second definition has to describe rather than name.

17 across REBUFFS

Snubs enthusiasts for Bible study (7)

Definition: Snubs · charade

  • Bible study → RE RE is religious education, the scripture lesson on a British school timetable
  • enthusiasts → BUFFS a buff is an enthusiast — a film buff, an opera buff

Everything after the first word reads as one phrase, enthusiasts for Bible study, and the parse cuts it in two with the school subject coming first.

20 across EXCEL

We hear 40 in Rome go far (5)

Definition: go far · homophone

  • 40 in Rome → XL Roman numerals put X for ten in front of L for fifty to mean fifty less ten
  • 40 in Rome → EXCEL those two numerals read out as the names of their letters make a five-letter word

Nothing here is Italian. The numeral is not translated but spoken, letter name by letter name, which is the whole trick, and 'go far' means getting on in life.

21 across KEYBOARDS

Frequently depressed characters? (9)

Definition: Frequently depressed characters? · cryptic definition

  • depressed → the surface offers low spirits; the sense in play is the physical one, pushed down under a finger
  • characters → not people of a gloomy sort but letters and symbols, one printed on each of the things pressed

There is nothing to take apart, only one phrase read twice. The office gloom of the surface turns into fingers on plastic, and the question mark is the setter admitting it.

22 across ADRIATIC SEA

Composed acid satire initially attacking Balkans' neighbour (8,3)

Definition: Balkans' neighbour · anagram + first letter

  • acid satire → ACIDSATIRE ten letters from these two words, waiting to be redealt
  • attacking → A the opening letter of this word, and the eleventh letter the enumeration asks for

'Composed' means calm in the surface, where a cool reviewer writes a sharp notice, and means assembled in the parse. That double life is the standard anagram misdirection.

23 across CHROME

Classic car's finish: Switzerland, then major Italian city (6)

Definition: Classic car's finish · charade

  • Switzerland → CH CH is Switzerland's international vehicle code, short for the Latin Confoederatio Helvetica
  • major Italian city → ROME the Italian capital, and the four-letter city this description is pointing at

'Finish' looks like an instruction to take a last letter, which is the trap. It is a noun here — the shiny plating itself — and the colon marks the only break.

24 across EARTHY

Mostly clear: thyme's not refined (6)

Definition: not refined · hidden word

  • clear: thyme's → EARTHY six consecutive letters begin at the third letter of the first word and stop three short of the end of the second

'Not refined' invites you to rough up the letters of thyme's. It is the definition instead, and nothing is jumbled: the surface only pretends to be a weather forecast.

Down

2 down WALL STREET CRASH

Encircles tree with garbage including carbon, depression begins now (4,6,5)

Definition: depression begins now · charade + container

  • Encircles → WALLS a hedge that encircles a field walls it in
  • tree → TREE the word goes in at face value, four letters off the page
  • garbage → TRASH trash is the American word for household garbage
  • carbon → C C is carbon's symbol in the periodic table, and it lands after the first letter of the rubbish

'Depression' offers a dip in the ground or a low mood long before it offers the economic one. Every join lands in the wrong place on purpose: the T that ends the middle word is the front of the rubbish.

3 down TOWERED

Time we rode off, Rose (7)

Definition: Rose · charade + anagram

  • Time → T T is the standard abbreviation for time, as on a stopwatch
  • we rode → OWERED the six letters of these two words, resettled in a new order

'Rose' looks like the woman being addressed as the riders leave, and the capital letter is the setter helping that along. It is the past tense of rise.

4 down HARD WATER

Mineral-rich fluid tumbling earthward (4,5)

Definition: Mineral-rich fluid · anagram

  • earthward → HARDWATER the nine letters of this single word, dealt out again

Fluid tumbling earthward is rain, which is what the surface wants you to picture. The one word that looks like scenery is the whole of the wordplay.

5 down APHID

A doctorate describing Citizen's second cause of Rosebud's loss (5)

Definition: cause of Rosebud's loss · charade + container + second letter

  • A → A the article is taken at face value
  • doctorate → PHD a PhD is the doctorate, and here it is split open to make room
  • Citizen's second → I the italics point at Citizen Kane, and it is the second letter of that first word that is wanted

Rosebud is the sled in the film, so the surface is all cinema. The garden reading is the one that counts: a rosebud lost off its bush.

6 down EMBARGO

Trade barrier: flickering amber, then green light (7)

Definition: Trade barrier · anagram + charade

  • amber → EMBAR the five letters of the traffic light's middle colour, rearranged
  • green light → GO green is the signal to go, and giving something the green light is telling it to proceed

The whole surface is a set of traffic lights, which is the joke: what gets built out of them is the opposite of permission to proceed.

7 down STRAPPED FOR CASH

Drops chaps after reshuffle: poor! (8,3,4)

Definition: poor · anagram

  • Drops chaps after → STRAPPEDFORCASH fifteen letters across three words, dealt out again

'After' looks like an instruction to put the chaps behind the drops. It is fodder, and every letter of all three words goes into the pot.

9 down PUMP

Shoe quiz (4)

Definition: Shoe · double definition

  • Shoe → in Britain this is the flat, soft-soled shoe worn for gym or for dancing
  • quiz → to question somebody persistently is to work information out of them, the way water is drawn from a well

A two-word clue is all definition and no wordplay, so the only thing to find is where one meaning stops and the next one starts.

10 down ALIT

Australian drunk disembarked (4)

Definition: disembarked · charade

  • Australian → A A is the short form of Australian that crosswords keep permanently on hand
  • drunk → LIT lit is old slang for drunk — lit up, like a Christmas tree

'Drunk' is the anagram indicator that isn't. Nothing here is jumbled: it is a plain synonym, and the tipsy Australian of the surface does all the misleading.

14 down PARTY LINE

Say conga is agreed policy (5,4)

Definition: agreed policy · double definition

  • conga → a conga is danced in single file, each dancer holding the hips of the one in front
  • agreed policy → the position a political group has settled on and expects its members to repeat

The same two words carry a dance floor and a party conference. 'Say' is the tell: it warns you that a conga is an instance, not a synonym.

15 down STEM

Upset, Everyman cut 50% of fats, being staunch (4)

Definition: staunch · charade + deletion + reversal

  • Everyman → ME the setter signs the puzzle Everyman, and a setter pointing at themselves in the wordplay is two letters
  • fats → TS the back half of a four-letter word, its first two letters gone

'Staunch' looks like the adjective for a loyal supporter and is the verb for stopping a flow. Everyman appearing in person is a habit of this setter worth banking.

16 down PALERMO

Lighter, twinkling, somewhere in Sicily (7)

Definition: somewhere in Sicily · charade

  • Lighter → PALER a lighter shade of a colour is a paler one
  • twinkling → MO in a twinkling means in a moment, and moment is shortened to two letters

'Lighter' is doing its colour job, not its flame job, and 'twinkling' is time rather than starlight — the surface offers the wrong sense of both at once.

18 down BROWSER

Dumb row's erupting, cancelling some characters - 23? (7)

Definition: 23? · hidden word

  • Dumb row's erupting → BROWSER seven consecutive letters begin at the last letter of the first word and run through the second into the third

'Erupting' pulls hard towards a jumble, or towards an upward move in a down clue, and it is neither: those letters sit still. The 23 points at another entry.

19 down SO-SO

Pest - and missing - neither good nor bad (2-2)

Definition: neither good nor bad · deletion

  • Pest → SOANDSO an irritating person is a so-and-so, the polite British way of not naming them
  • and → AND the linking word in the middle of that phrase is quoted here so that it can be lifted out

The dashes read as a parenthesis in a grumble about pests, and what they really fence off is an instruction. Britain's mildest insult stands in for a name you would rather not say.

21 down KNAVE

Two knights on the street; one's a rascal (5)

Definition: a rascal · charade

  • Two knights → KN chess writes a knight as N, while a knighthood is a K — one of each, and no repetition
  • the street → AVE AVE is how an avenue is shortened on a street sign

Two knights and two different abbreviations — the setter is not asking for the same letter twice. 'One's' does nothing but hold the sentence upright.
