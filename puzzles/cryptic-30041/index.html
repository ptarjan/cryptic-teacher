Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,041

Across

9 across ORBIT

Exorbitant housing revolution (5)

Definition: revolution · hidden word

  • Exorbitant → ORBIT hidden inside exORBITant

One long word is all it takes to hide a five-letter answer, and 'Exorbitant' is long enough that nobody thinks to look inside it.

10 across CURIOSITY

Our city is removing cat killer? (9)

Definition: cat killer? · anagram

  • Our city is → OURCITYIS anagram fodder, 9 letters
  • removing → anagram indicator — think of a house removal

'Removing' is a house removal, not a subtraction — that is the trick. And the definition is a whole proverb folded into two words: curiosity killed the cat.

11 across ETERNALLY

Always on the outside, having no vote (9)

Definition: Always · deletion

  • on the outside → EXTERNALLY externally = on the outside
  • vote → X you vote by marking an X on the ballot

X for a vote is worth remembering — it is the cross on the ballot paper. 'On the outside' looks like a definition and is really the word being trimmed.

12 across TRIAD

Some notes one introduced in jazz revival (5)

Definition: Some notes · container

  • one → I Roman numeral one
  • jazz revival → TRAD trad jazz, the revival of early jazz styles

Trad jazz genuinely was a revival, so 'jazz revival' is a straight substitution rather than an instruction. And 'Some notes' is fair for a chord of three.

13 across DOVECOT

A place for fliers to act almost timid when held in check (7)

Definition: A place for fliers · charade + deletion + container

  • to act → DO to act = to do
  • almost timid → CO COy, 'timid', loses its last letter
  • check → VET to vet something is to check it

Three separate moves in seven letters, which is why this one takes a while: 'almost' clips a letter, 'held in' inserts, and only then do the parts stack up.

15 across DREDGER

Working vessel’s doctor – creep, right? (7)

Definition: Working vessel · charade

  • doctor → DR Dr = doctor
  • creep → EDGE to creep along is to edge
  • right → R r = right

The dash and the question mark make the surface sound like an insult thrown across a room; nothing in it admits to three pieces stacked in order.

17 across NUDGE

Jog without clothes, without much money (5)

Definition: Jog · container

  • without clothes → NUDE nude = without clothes
  • much money → G a G is a grand — a thousand

The two 'withouts' mean different things — the first is 'lacking', the second is 'outside'. Same word, two jobs, a few syllables apart.

18 across RED

Wine that’s often put in a pocket (3)

Definition: Wine · double definition

  • Wine → RED red wine
  • that’s often put in a pocket → RED a snooker red, potted — put in a pocket

The surface wants you thinking of a hip flask. The second definition is snooker, where a red is put in a pocket fifteen times a frame.

20 across GILET

Maybe get laid after ace date’s removed top (5)

Definition: top · anagram + deletion

  • get laid → GETLAID the fodder before the deletions
  • ace → A A = ace, removed from the fodder
  • date’s removed → D = date also leaves
  • Maybe → anagram indicator

'removed top' reads as one phrase and does two jobs: 'removed' finishes the deletions and 'top' is the definition. The bawdy surface is there to stop you making that cut.

22 across SOFT TOP

Frequently to be found in station vehicle (4,3)

Definition: vehicle · container

  • Frequently → OFT oft = frequently, poetically
  • station → STOP a bus or tram station is a stop

'Frequently to be found in station' reads as a complete sentence about buses, so nothing marks the point where the definition takes over.

25 across MARINER

Prospector collecting top grade river tar (7)

Definition: tar · container + charade

  • Prospector → MINER a prospector mines for gold
  • top grade → A an A grade
  • river → R r = river

'tar' for a sailor is old slang worth knowing — it comes from tarpaulin. The prospector surface keeps you thinking of gold rather than of the sea.

26 across PENCE

Pressure, therefore, to dispose of hard cash (5)

Definition: cash · charade + deletion

  • Pressure → P p = pressure, as in physics
  • therefore → HENCE therefore = hence
  • hard → H H = hard, the pencil grading — this is the letter to dispose of

H for hard is stamped on the side of a pencil. Note that the deletion is aimed at the middle piece, not at the whole — 'hard' names the letter to lose.

27 across ALL THE WAY

What alley twists to the very end? (3,3,3)

Definition: to the very end · anagram

  • What alley → WHATALLEY anagram fodder, 9 letters
  • twists → anagram indicator

A twisting alley might well run all the way, so the surface and the answer describe the same thing — which is what makes this one read so beautifully.

30 across THE CAVERN

Legendary music venue later hosting explorer (3,6)

Definition: Legendary music venue · container

  • later → THEN then = later
  • explorer → CAVER an underground explorer

'hosting' is a venue word doing a container's job, which is why it hides so well in a clue about a music venue. The Cavern is the Liverpool cellar the Beatles played.

31 across ROVER

Collar at last on dog? (5)

Definition: dog? · charade + last letter

  • Collar at last → R the last letter of collaR
  • on → OVER on = over, as in 'a blanket over the bed'

The question mark admits not every dog answers to Rover. 'on' meaning OVER is the sort of tiny synonym that is hardest to see coming.

Down

1 down HOPE

Old comedian has pee in garden (4)

Definition: Old comedian · container

  • pee → P the letter P, spelled out
  • garden → HOE to garden is to hoe

The surface — someone weeing in the garden — is pure misdirection, and it works because a letter spelled out as a word is the last thing anyone looks for.

2 down OBSERVED

Bedsore very inflamed and spotted (8)

Definition: spotted · anagram

  • Bedsore → BEDSORE anagram fodder, 7 letters
  • very → V v = very
  • inflamed → anagram indicator

A grisly surface hiding a gentle answer. The step people miss is 'very' donating a single letter to the fodder — anagrams often take a stray abbreviation along.

3 down STUN

Tipped fanatic’s bowl over (4)

Definition: bowl over · reversal

  • fanatic’s → NUTS a fanatic is a nut; with the ’s it supplies NUTS

The apostrophe-s earns its keep: 'fanatic’s' hands you four letters, not three. In a down clue 'Tipped' means read them upwards.

4 down SCULPTOR

‘Curst LP’ smashed over Old Moore? (8)

Definition: Old Moore? · anagram

  • Curst LP → CURSTLP anagram fodder, 7 letters
  • over → O o = over, as on cricket scorecards
  • smashed → anagram indicator

'Old Moore?' is definition by example — Henry Moore is one sculptor among many, which is what the question mark concedes. O for 'over' comes off a cricket scorecard.

5 down FRAYED

After battle, English duke’s worn out (6)

Definition: worn out · charade

  • battle → FRAY a fray is a battle or scuffle
  • English → E E = English
  • duke → D d = duke

E for English and D for duke are the kind of one-letter abbreviations that vanish into a battlefield surface, which is exactly why the clue reads so smoothly.

6 down BOOTLEGGER

Sack Latin person encouraging a criminal (10)

Definition: a criminal · charade

  • Sack → BOOT to sack someone is to give them the boot
  • Latin → L L = Latin
  • person encouraging → EGGER someone who eggs you on

EGGER is the invented-looking piece that makes this hard — one who eggs you on. 'Sack Latin person' meanwhile reads like an order barked by an emperor.

7 down VIKING

Raider: short woman married to ruler (6)

Definition: Raider · charade

  • short woman → VI Vi, short for Violet
  • ruler → KING a ruler of the realm

'Married to' is only a joiner: it says the two pieces sit side by side and nothing more. Vi for Violet is the sort of shortened name setters use constantly.

8 down BYRD

English composer’s ‘Lark’ broadcast (4)

Definition: English composer · homophone

  • Lark → BYRD a lark is a bird

'The Lark Ascending' is by Vaughan Williams, and that is the trap. The lark here is just a bird, said aloud.

13 down DUNES

Deserts surrounding northern hills (5)

Definition: hills · container

  • Deserts → DUES your just deserts are your dues
  • northern → N n = northern

Neat trap: the surface points at Sahara sand, while 'Deserts' is the just-deserts sense, meaning dues. The sand only comes back in the answer.

14 down CHEW THE FAT

Gas: large escapes from hatch we left undone (4,3,3)

Definition: Gas · anagram + deletion

  • hatch we left → HATCHWEEFT the fodder once L has escaped
  • large escapes from → L = large leaves LEFT
  • undone → anagram indicator

'Gas' is the definition and it means to chat idly, not the thing escaping from the hatch — the surface is built entirely to keep you reading it the other way.

16 down ROTOR

Run after Troy punches Joey Turner (5)

Definition: Turner · container + charade

  • Run → R r = run, as on cricket scorecards
  • Troy → T t = troy, the weight system
  • Joey → ROO a joey is a baby kangaroo — a roo

Capital letters are free in a cryptic: 'Joey Turner' looks like a man and is really a baby kangaroo and a definition. T for troy weight is the obscure piece.

19 down DUMPLING

Throw away fish pudding (8)

Definition: pudding · charade

  • Throw away → DUMP to throw away is to dump
  • fish → LING a ling is a long cod-like fish

LING is one of a handful of fish setters keep for their letters — ide, ling, dace, eel. They are worth learning as a set.

21 down LONG WAVE

Extended gesture used by some broadcasters? (4,4)

Definition: used by some broadcasters? · charade

  • Extended → LONG long = extended
  • gesture → WAVE a wave is a gesture

Read as one phrase, an extended gesture is a long wave — the wordplay and the surface are the same picture, which is the whole joke.

23 down FINDER

Small telescope of superior quality includes detachable cap (6)

Definition: Small telescope · container + first letter

  • of superior quality → FINER finer = of superior quality
  • detachable cap → D the 'cap' — top letter, in a down clue — of Detachable

'cap' means top letter, and top only means first because this is a down clue — in an across clue the same word would have to do something else.

24 down PLANET

Mercury makes tree gain temperature? (6)

Definition: Mercury · charade

  • tree → PLANE the plane tree, a common street tree
  • temperature → T t = temperature

Mercury is one planet among eight, which is what the question mark concedes. The plane tree is the piece solvers walk past, since the surface is all thermometers.

26 down PATH

Way to get bed less cold (4)

Definition: Way · deletion

  • bed → PATCH a vegetable patch or flower bed
  • cold → C c = cold, as on a tap

C for cold comes off a tap and 'bed' is the garden sense: two small domestic swaps that let the surface read as ordinary grumbling.

28 down HERA

One pursuing female character in mythology (4)

Definition: character in mythology · charade

  • female → HER her = female pronoun
  • One → A the indefinite article — one

'One' for the single letter A is a small, common trick: it is the indefinite article. 'pursuing' only tells you which piece comes second.

29 down YARN

Drug-free period, end of addiction – do not swallow one! (4)

Definition: do not swallow one! · deletion + last letter

  • Drug-free period → YAR YEAR, a period, without E — the drug ecstasy
  • end of addiction → N the last letter of addictioN

E for ecstasy is standard in modern cryptics. The definition is the joke — 'do not swallow one' means do not believe the tall tale.
