Across

1 across FEMALE

Iron Man is a woman (6)

Definition: woman · charade

  • Iron → FE Fe is the chemical symbol for iron, from the Latin ferrum
  • Man → MALE a man is a male of the species

The capital M does all the misdirection: 'Iron Man' reads as the Marvel hero, and the two words have to be prised apart and taken one at a time.

4 across RAP MUSIC

Criticise character from Athens, thus creating sort of poetry (3,5)

Definition: sort of poetry · charade

  • Criticise → RAP to rap someone is to criticise them sharply — a rap on the knuckles
  • character from Athens → MU mu is the twelfth letter of the Greek alphabet
  • thus → SIC sic is Latin for 'thus', printed in brackets to say a quoted mistake really was there

The surface is about an Athenian being insulted; the answer is a modern genre, and the hinge between them is the word editors print after somebody else's misspelling.

9 across NACHOS

Snack: portion of spinach ossobuco (6)

Definition: Snack · hidden word

  • spinach ossobuco → NACHOS six consecutive letters run across the gap between the two dishes

The colon makes those two words read as a dish on a menu, which is exactly why the join between them is easy to skate straight past.

10 across PICAROON

Photo, a character from long ago: outspoken rogue (8)

Definition: rogue · charade + homophone

  • Photo → PIC pic is the everyday short form of picture
  • a → A the clue's own article, taken at face value
  • character from long ago → ROON a rune is a letter of the old Germanic alphabet — a character from long ago — and saying it aloud respells it

'Outspoken' looks like a description of the rogue and is really doing sound duty, which is why the old letter has to be said before it is written.

11 across JULIAN CALENDAR

Engaged in drink, CIA annulled cryptic system of old Rome (6,8)

Definition: system of old Rome · container + anagram

  • drink → JAR a jar is a pint of beer — 'fancy a jar?'
  • CIA annulled → ULIANCALEND eleven letters, the agency's initials among them, all resorted

'Annulled' wants to be an instruction to delete something; it is fodder. The drink is split into its first letter and its last two, and everything else goes in the gap.

13 across RIFLE STOCK

Plunderers’ music fest in part of Winchester? (5,5)

Definition: part of Winchester? · charade

  • Plunderers’ → RIFLE to rifle a drawer is to go through it and take what you want, which is what a plunderer does
  • music fest → STOCK Woodstock gave festivals their ending, and -stock has been stuck on the back of them ever since

Winchester wants to be the cathedral city and is the American gun, and a plunderers' festival is a joke name built on the back of Woodstock.

14 across ANTI

Prey for an aardvark: one Contra (4)

Definition: Contra · charade

  • Prey for an aardvark → ANT the aardvark's whole diet, and the crossword's standard worker
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one

The capital C is surface only — it points at the Nicaraguan Contras of the 1980s — and the word underneath it is just the Latin for 'against'.

16 across SUEZ

God backflips into canal (4)

Definition: canal · reversal

  • God → ZEUS the chief god of the Greeks, the thrower of thunderbolts

'God' opening the clue reads as the Christian one, which is exactly why the Olympian who fits — turned end for end — takes a moment to find.

18 across FIELD EVENT

Lifted off around two, say: it may require a shot (5,5)

Definition: it may require a shot · container + anagram

  • Lifted → FIELDT six letters rearranged, and they end up split in two
  • two → EVEN two is an even number, one instance among many

'Lifted off' reads as a take-off and is really fodder plus its shuffle; the shot is the athlete's iron ball, not a drink, an injection or anything on a golf course.

21 across ST GEORGE'S CROSS

Get ogress irritated and annoyed with symbol (2,7,5)

Definition: symbol · anagram + charade

  • Get ogress → STGEORGES nine letters shaken up, and they come out as a saint's name with a possessive on the end
  • annoyed → CROSS cross is the everyday British word for annoyed

The surface has one angry monster and one angry person; the answer is a flag. 'Annoyed' is the word that has to be read as a noun rather than an adjective.

23 across OPERATOR

Smooth talker on X, say (8)

Definition: Smooth talker · double definition

  • Smooth talker → the stock phrase pairs 'smooth' with this word for a slick, plausible mover who plays people rather than machines
  • X, say → the multiplication sign is one example of the symbols that act on the numbers in an equation

The same noun twice over: the person who works an angle, and the sign that works on numbers. 'On X' looks like the social network and is only holding the sentence together.

24 across COLLAR

Arrest in general location reversed (6)

Definition: Arrest · hidden word + reversal

  • general location → COLLAR six consecutive letters cross the gap between the two words, though not in the direction you are reading

'General' and 'location' both offer themselves as definitions of a place, and neither is one: the two words are there to be read through, backwards.

25 across SAD TALES

Sob stories in Slate? (3,5)

Definition: Sob stories · charade + anagram

  • Sob → SAD a sob story is a sad one, so this word does double duty — definition and wordplay at once
  • Slate → TALES the American magazine's five letters, resorted

The clue defines itself twice: the whole phrase means the answer, and its first word is also a chunk of the build. The italics mark Slate as a title, not a roof.

26 across AGENDA

Turkish bigwig covers arse, it may be hidden (6)

Definition: it may be hidden · container

  • Turkish bigwig → AGA an aga was a commander or grandee of the Ottoman empire
  • arse → END your end is your rear, the part you sit on

The definition is a phrase rather than a word: what may be hidden is a plan, and the adjective that always goes in front of it is the whole point of the clue.

Down

1 down FINE

Satisfactory punishment (4)

Definition: Satisfactory · double definition

  • Satisfactory → the everyday 'that's OK by me' sense: acceptable, good enough
  • punishment → the sum a court makes you pay for an offence

Two senses sit side by side with nothing between them, which is the whole trick of the type: the clue reads as one phrase about a sentence being reasonable.

2 down MACDUFF

Ingredient of comfort food covering rear end of Shakespearean (7)

Definition: Shakespearean · charade

  • Ingredient of comfort food → MAC mac is the macaroni of macaroni cheese, the comfort dish it is named for
  • rear end → DUFF duff is American slang for the backside — 'get off your duff'

Nothing in the surface warns you that a name is coming: 'Shakespearean' looks like an adjective describing the food, and two slang words underneath it hide a thane.

3 down LIONISED

Celebrated rugby player offside (8)

Definition: Celebrated · charade + anagram

  • rugby player → LION a Lion is a player picked for the British and Irish Lions, the touring team drawn from four countries
  • side → ISED four letters resorted to close the word

'Offside' is one word in the clue and two separate jobs in the parse; the rugby surface is there precisely to stop you splitting it.

5 down ANIMALCULES

Amoebae and such travelled nautical miles, giving up sex in retrospect (11)

Definition: Amoebae and such · anagram + deletion + reversal

  • nautical miles → NAUTICALMILES thirteen letters to start from, two of which will not survive
  • sex → IT 'it' is the old euphemism for sex — 'they were at it' — and turned round it is the pair that goes

The surface is a sea voyage and none of it is a measurement: the only instruction in the clue is that a small word leaves before the letters are shaken. 'It' for sex is worth filing away.

6 down MEANER

Lower, but greater (6)

Definition: Lower · double definition

  • Lower → the comparative of 'mean' in its base sense: more ignoble, more shabby, further down
  • greater → American colloquial 'mean' is praise — a mean fastball, a mean chilli — and its comparative points the opposite way

One word pulled in two directions, base and admirable, and 'but' is the setter promising you the contradiction is deliberate rather than a slip.

7 down STOOD IN

Originally, source (excessively loud noise) was a sub (5,2)

Definition: was a sub · charade + first letter

  • source → S the first letter of 'source'
  • excessively → TOO too much is excessively much
  • loud noise → DIN a din is a racket loud enough to drown out talk

The bracket is the giveaway once you spot it: the aside is not describing the source, it is two more chunks of the build. And 'sub' here means substitute, not sandwich or submarine.

8 down CON ARTIST

Hustler’s damage includes train wreck (3,6)

Definition: Hustler · container + anagram

  • damage → COST 'what's the damage?' asks what something cost
  • train → NARTI five letters smashed out of shape and dropped in after the first two

'Train wreck' is one phrase in the surface and two separate jobs in the parse; hustler is wanted in its swindling sense, not the sense of somebody who works fast.

12 down NAT KING COLE

Singer wanting cabbage to whip up piece in advance (3,4,4)

Definition: Singer · charade + reversal

  • whip → NAT to tan a hide is to whip it, and those three letters then climb
  • piece → KING the piece a whole game of chess is played to protect
  • cabbage → COLE cole is the cabbage family — the cole of coleslaw

Cabbage, whipping and a piece all sound like one kitchen scene; only the singer at the front tells you they are three separate chunks of somebody's name.

13 down REST STOPS

Don’t play at stalls where Yanks relieve themselves? (4,5)

Definition: where Yanks relieve themselves? · charade

  • Don’t play → REST a rest is the mark in a score telling the player to stay silent for a beat
  • stalls → STOPS an engine that stalls comes to a halt

The stalls are a theatre's seats in the surface and an engine's dying in the parse, and the question mark is there because the definition is a joke about American highway signs.

15 down BEER PONG

Drunken peer falls into brewski-chugging contraption in drinkers’ diversion (4,4)

Definition: drinkers’ diversion · container + anagram

  • brewski-chugging contraption → BONG a bong is the tube-and-funnel gadget a drink is poured down in one go, and brewski is US slang for a beer
  • peer → EERP the lord's four letters, tipped over and dropped in after the first

'Drunken peer' reads as one phrase and is really an instruction plus the material it works on, and the contraption is split open to take the result.

17 down EGGHEAD

E, for Einstein? (7)

Definition: E, for Einstein? · cryptic definition

  • E → one letter, and the one his surname begins with: in crossword terms, the head of Einstein
  • for Einstein? → the question mark asks you to take him as an example, and what the world takes him as an example of is brains

Both halves are hiding in plain sight: E is Einstein's head, and the sort of head Einstein is famous for having is an egg one — EGGHEAD.

19 down EPSILON

€ is based on this Greek character harboured by lone lunatic (7)

Definition: € is based on this · container + anagram

  • lone → ELON four letters resorted, ending up on either side of the middle
  • Greek character → PSI psi is the Greek letter shaped like a trident, and it goes inside

'Greek character' wants to be the definition and is the wordplay — the letter it names is a different one, tucked inside. The euro sign really was drawn from a Greek letter with two bars added.

20 down SONATA

75% of plátanos turned into something romantic (6)

Definition: something romantic · deletion + reversal

  • plátanos → ATANOS the Spanish for bananas, minus its opening pair

The italics tell you the word is foreign and the arithmetic tells you how much of it to keep; 'into' is only a link, not an instruction to insert anything.

22 down ORCA

Killer imprisoned in Windsor Castle (4)

Definition: Killer · hidden word

  • Windsor Castle → ORCA four consecutive letters straddle the space between the royal house and its home

Windsor Castle is one place-name in the surface and two words to be read across in the parse; the killer is the whale, not a murderer.
