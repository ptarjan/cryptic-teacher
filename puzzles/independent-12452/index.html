Independent Cryptic Crossword No 12,452

Across

8 across LAIR

Attitude after line in retreat (4)

Definition: retreat · charade

  • line → L L is the standard short form for line
  • Attitude → AIR an air is the manner someone carries about them, as in an air of authority

'in retreat' reads as one phrase about a beaten army, and that is the trap: 'in' only joins the halves, and 'retreat' on its own is the definition.

9 across INNUENDOES

Bar menu not initially ruined - works at possibly dodgy suggestions (10)

Definition: possibly dodgy suggestions · charade + deletion + anagram

  • Bar → INN a bar in the village-pub sense, which is where the surface starts
  • menu → ENU the word with its first letter gone, and those three get shuffled
  • works → DOES works as a verb — he works, he does — the plain third-person substitute

The dash is doing the job of a full stop, and 'ruined' reaches back over only three letters, not over the bar as well.

10 across FURNACES

Challenges putting pot in ovens (8)

Definition: ovens · container

  • Challenges → FACES to face a difficulty is to challenge it head on, and this is the outer word
  • pot → URN an urn is a big pot, for ashes or for tea, and it goes within

'Challenges' at the head of the clue looks like the definition; it is the container instead, and the plural 'ovens' at the end is what the answer must match.

11 across SHYEST

Mum certainly tense - most likely to withdraw (6)

Definition: most likely to withdraw · charade

  • Mum → SH mum means keeping quiet, and SH is the noise that asks for it
  • certainly → YES answer certainly to a question and you have said yes
  • tense → T T is the grammarian's short form for tense, as in past T

The dash marks the seam: everything before it builds the word and everything after defines it. 'Mum' is the silence sense, nothing to do with mothers.

12 across PECULIAR

Particular probe gutted by copper taking on unreliable source (8)

Definition: Particular · charade + outer letters

  • probe gutted → PE the outside letters of probe, with its middle emptied out
  • copper → CU the chemical symbol for copper, from the Latin cuprum
  • unreliable source → LIAR a source who cannot be relied on is a liar, and that is the whole of this piece

The surface offers a detective leaning on a dubious informant, and every word of it is doing some other job — 'copper' here is the metal, not the policeman.

14 across NASEBY

Head back round South, ending in legendary battleground (6)

Definition: battleground · charade + reversal + container + last letter

  • Head → BEAN bean is old slang for the head, as in 'off his bean'
  • South → S S is the compass point in one letter, and it goes inside
  • legendary → Y the last letter of the word, tacked on at the end

The 1645 battle broke the King's cause in the Civil War, which is why 'legendary' sits so comfortably in the surface while quietly handing over a letter.

16 across BEAUFORT SCALE

Meteorological measure is absolute farce in a storm (8,5)

Definition: Meteorological measure · anagram

  • absolute farce → ABSOLUTEFARCE anagram fodder, thirteen letters
  • in a storm → anagram indicator

Rear Admiral Francis Beaufort drew the scale up for the Royal Navy in 1805, and the anagram indicator naming the very thing being measured is the clue's joke.

18 across USAGES

Our group adds wrinkles to ways of working (6)

Definition: ways of working · charade

  • Our group → US the pronoun for the group that includes whoever is speaking
  • adds wrinkles → AGES what ages a face puts wrinkles on it, so the verb does the same work

'Adds wrinkles' is a verb phrase dressed up as skincare copy; the answer is a plural noun, which is the test of whether the definition really fits.

20 across SANCERRE

More reasonable about bringing in college’s white wine (8)

Definition: white wine · charade + container

  • More reasonable → SANER saner is the comparative of sane, reasonable in the plain sense
  • college → C C is the abbreviation for college as it appears in institution names
  • about → RE RE means about or concerning, as at the head of a memo

'About' looks like an instruction to wrap one piece round another, and it is a piece of the answer instead — RE, the word at the top of a memo.

22 across ECLAIR

Cake - first answer provided by every cook at the outset (6)

Definition: Cake · charade + first letters

  • every cook at the outset → EC the opening letters of those two words, taken in the order they are given
  • first answer → LAIR a cross-reference: this puzzle's first across solution, LAIR at 8, is borrowed whole

The dash hides a cross-reference. 'first answer' does not describe the cake at all — it points at another light in this grid and hands you four of six letters.

24 across MOURNING

Regretting our name being appropriated by China (8)

Definition: Regretting · charade + container

  • China → MING the Chinese dynasty whose porcelain the word china is named for, so the capital letter is a disguise
  • our → OUR the pronoun at face value, three letters as written
  • name → N N is the standard abbreviation for name

China is the crockery, not the country, and the dynasty that made it is the piece to find. The capital letter is the setter's licence and the whole of the disguise.

25 across CEPHALONIA

I will enter a chapel on rocky Greek island (10)

Definition: Greek island · anagram

  • a chapel on → ACHAPELON anagram fodder, nine letters
  • I → I the tenth letter, joining the fodder before the shuffle
  • rocky → anagram indicator

'on' belongs to the letters, not to the picture of a chapel standing on a hill — which is why the fodder counts out to ten and not nine.

26 across OBOE

Old tidal wave eliminating river reed (4)

Definition: reed · charade + deletion

  • Old → O O is the standard abbreviation for old
  • tidal wave → BORE a bore is the wave that runs up a river on a rising tide, as on the Severn
  • river → R R is the map's short form for river, and it is the letter struck out

The Severn bore is a real tidal wave that surfers ride miles upstream, which is how 'tidal wave' and 'river' can sit side by side so innocently.

Down

1 down NATURE

Living things turning brown, certainly missing sun (6)

Definition: Living things · charade + reversal + deletion

  • brown → TAN brown as a colour is TAN, and it arrives the other way round
  • certainly → SURE certainly and surely agree, so SURE is the word to work on
  • missing sun → S the diary shortens Sunday to S, and that is the letter that walks out

Sun for S is one of the shortenings a solver simply has to own; the comma after 'brown' is the setter's punctuation and carries no instruction at all.

2 down ORANG-UTANG

Organisation receiving articles about stomach in the ape (5-5)

Definition: ape · container

  • Organisation → ORG the everyday three-letter shortening of the word, split to make room
  • articles → AN AN the indefinite article twice over, which is why the clue says articles rather than article
  • stomach → GUT gut is the plain word for the stomach, and it goes between that pair

Phi spells the ape with a final G, the older variant you will find in Chambers; 'articles' being plural is the quiet signal that the same little word appears twice.

3 down MISCHIEF

According to Fleming it’s M for misbehaviour? (8)

Definition: misbehaviour · charade

  • M → M the letter at face value, the name given to Bond's boss
  • it’s → IS the contraction unpacked to 'it is', and only the verb is wanted
  • According to Fleming → CHIEF in Ian Fleming's novels M runs the Secret Service, so his job title is the word the wordplay wants

Read the wordplay as a flat statement about who M is in the Bond books and it writes itself out; the question mark covers the joke of 'M for misbehaviour'.

4 down INNS

Legal establishments showing limits to imagination nonetheless (4)

Definition: Legal establishments · charade + outer letters

  • imagination → IN the outside letters of the word, its first and its last
  • nonetheless → NS the same treatment applied to the word that follows

'Legal establishments' reads at first like licensed premises, which is exactly the pull the setter wants: the Inns of Court are chambers, not bars.

5 down NELSON

Important naval figure seen in hold? (6)

Definition: Important naval figure · double definition

  • Important naval figure → NELSON the one-armed admiral who beat the French at Trafalgar in 1805 and stands on the column in the London square named after him
  • hold → NELSON a wrestling grip applied from behind, an arm threaded under the opponent's and the hand pressed on their neck — the full version uses both arms

The two senses meet only in the spelling, and the question mark is the setter admitting how far apart they are. Capital letter or not is the solver's clue.

6 down EDGY

Nervous European Democrat generally cleared out (4)

Definition: Nervous · charade + outer letters

  • European → E E is the standard single-letter shortening for European
  • Democrat → D the American party is reduced to its initial, as on a ballot
  • generally → GY the first and last letters of the word, with everything between them gone

Two of crosswording's commonest one-letter abbreviations sit side by side here; the political surface is decoration, since neither letter depends on which party.

7 down SENSIBLE

Prudent to visit, taking in relative in Holland (8)

Definition: Prudent · container

  • to visit → SEE to visit someone is to see them, and these three letters wrap round the rest
  • relative → SIB a sib is a brother or sister, the short form crosswords use for kin
  • in Holland → NL NL is the Netherlands' international code, and the relative sits between its two letters

Setters use Holland loosely for the whole of the Netherlands — strictly it is only two of its provinces — because the country's two-letter code is what the wordplay needs.

13 down RO-ROS

Bounders impounding river ferries (2-3)

Definition: ferries · container

  • Bounders → ROOS roos are kangaroos, the animals that get about by bounding
  • river → R R is the map-maker's abbreviation for river, as in R. Thames

'Bounders' first reads as cads out of P. G. Wodehouse, and that is the misdirection: these bounders are animals that bound.

15 down SHAME ON YOU

Yahoo menu’s edited? I thought that was very bad (5,2,3)

Definition: I thought that was very bad · anagram

  • Yahoo menu’s → YAHOOMENUS anagram fodder, ten letters
  • edited → anagram indicator

A yahoo is a lout, Swift's word from Gulliver's Travels, so the surface reads as a complaint about a website while the capital letter does nothing at all.

16 down BASS CLEF

B_____ fool almost cut through item on score (4,4)

Definition: item on score · charade + deletion

  • B_____ → B an insult bowdlerised down to its first letter, and that letter is all the wordplay hands over
  • fool → ASS an ass is a fool, the donkey standing in for the idiot
  • cut through → CLEFT something cut through has been cleft, and the past participle is the word to trim

The row of dashes is the joke and the wordplay at once: a paper printing a word it will not print leaves you the initial, and the initial is what the clue needs.

17 down SINGULAR

Odd crime newspaper picked up, involving university lecturer (8)

Definition: Odd · charade + reversal + container

  • crime → SIN a sin is a crime against a moral code, and it leads off
  • newspaper → RAG rag is the mildly sneering word for a newspaper, and it is written the other way up
  • university → U U is the standard abbreviation for university
  • lecturer → L L is the short form for lecturer in academic listings

'Odd' at the head of a clue is usually an instruction to take alternate letters. Here it is simply the definition, which is what makes the clue hard to open.

19 down SHRILL

High-pitched soprano damaging after brief time (6)

Definition: High-pitched · charade

  • soprano → S S is the score's abbreviation for soprano, the highest of the singing voices
  • brief time → HR an hour is a stretch of time, and HR is how a diary writes it
  • damaging → ILL ill means harmful, as in ill effects, so it stands in for damaging

Soprano's S and the diary's hour are both shorthand a solver collects once and keeps; the noisy singer of the surface is only there to hold them together.

21 down RANDOM

Haphazard components of read-only memory (6)

Definition: Haphazard · charade

  • read-only memory → R read-only memory shortens to ROM, and this is the first of the three letters it is made of
  • components → AND list a set of components aloud and an 'and' goes in before the last of them, which is where these three letters come from
  • read-only memory → OM the other two letters of that same abbreviation, in the order they come

ROM is the memory chip that keeps its contents when the power goes off. The trick is that the clue wants its letters read out as a list rather than as an abbreviation.

23 down ACHE

Hearts absorbed by cool desire (4)

Definition: desire · container

  • cool → ACE ace means excellent or cool in everyday slang, and it is the outer word
  • Hearts → H H is the card player's abbreviation for hearts

Both halves come from games — a card suit's initial and the top card standing in for 'cool' — while the surface sells you a romance.

24 down MYNA

Lots of people, some upsetting bird (4)

Definition: bird · charade + reversal

  • Lots of people → M lots of people are MANY, and its opening letter is the one left standing
  • some → YNA some is ANY, which is the rest of MANY, and it comes back up the grid

The clue's own word for 'lots of people' has a word for 'some' sitting inside it, and that overlap is the whole device; the comma is there to hide the join.
