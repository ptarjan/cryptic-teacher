Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,086

Across

1 across SPOTLIT

Specially illuminated set of old books torn on the outside (7)

Definition: Specially illuminated · container

  • torn → SPLIT something torn has been split
  • set of old books → OT OT is the Old Testament, the older half of the Bible and so a set of old books

OT is worth banking: any 'old books', 'old writings' or 'part of scripture' in a clue is nearly always those two letters.

5 across HAYCOCK

Wine that’s drunk always cold makes a pile for the country dweller (7)

Definition: a pile for the country dweller · container + charade

  • Wine → HOCK hock is the Rhine white, the crossword's usual wine
  • always → AY ay is the poets' word for ever and always, as in 'for ay'
  • cold → C C is cold, the letter on a tap

Two old conventions in one short clue, and the surface hides both by talking about a chilled glass of white.

10 across SNOW

Coke spells out end of dryness for the moment (4)

Definition: Coke · charade + last letter

  • dryness → S the letter this word finishes on
  • for the moment → NOW for the moment is for now

'For the moment' is worth banking: at present, just now and for now all buy the same three letters in this game.

11 across CLAMBERING

Moving awkwardly using clutch when straddling warning sign (10)

Definition: Moving awkwardly · container

  • clutch → CLING to clutch at something is to cling to it
  • warning sign → AMBER amber is the middle traffic light, the warning that a stop is coming

'Clutch' is a verb here, not the pedal the driving surface is steering you towards, and that is the whole misdirection.

12 across CARLOS

Old rustics admitting love for pretender Don (6)

Definition: pretender Don · container

  • Old rustics → CARLS a carl is an old word for a countryman or churl
  • love → O love is nothing in tennis, and nothing is O

Love has been zero since tennis borrowed it, and a carl is Chaucer's peasant — old vocabulary standing in for a Spanish court.

13 across LACHESIS

Fate is pursuing learner with pains (8)

Definition: Fate · charade

  • learner → L a learner driver carries L-plates
  • pains → ACHES aches are dull pains
  • is → IS the clue's own verb, taken as two letters

'Fate is pursuing' reads as one ominous phrase, which hides the fact that the 'is' has already stopped being grammar and become letters.

14 across FLOWERING

Blooming fellow needs taking down (9)

Definition: Blooming · charade

  • fellow → F F is fellow, the letter in FRS after an academic's name
  • taking down → LOWERING to take something down is to lower it

Three words, two pieces and no indicator at all: the only trick is that 'blooming' is the definition and not the mild swear word.

17 across FALSE START

A useless beginning – what’s after lasts, possibly (5,5)

Definition: A useless beginning · anagram

  • after lasts → FALSE START the ten letters of these two words, rearranged

The dash is where the clue splits, and the (5,5) is the tell: the two words on the right have exactly ten letters between them.

19 across ESOTERICA

A coterie’s unusual mysteries? (9)

Definition: mysteries · anagram

  • A coterie’s → ESOTERICA the nine letters of these words in a new order

A coterie is a small circle that keeps itself to itself, so the surface and the answer describe the same clique — only the letters move.

23 across PRISONER

Punished rogue primarily, one is in stir, right? (8)

Definition: Punished rogue primarily, one is in stir, right? · &lit + charade + first letters + anagram

  • Punished rogue → PR the first letters of the two words
  • one is → ISONE these five letters stirred into another order
  • right → R R is right, as on a map or a stage direction

Every word works twice: the sentence describes the man and builds him at the same time, with 'stir' meaning jail and mixture at once.

24 across LOG OUT

See problem with drinking too much port? Switch off (3,3)

Definition: Switch off · charade

  • See → LO lo is the old imperative meaning see or behold
  • problem with drinking too much port → GOUT gout is the joint disease long blamed on port and rich living

Gout was the rich man's complaint, pinned for two centuries on fortified wine, which is why the port is doing all the work in the surface.

26 across ABRASIVELY

Leader of America having courageous style? Is twisted inside, showing bluntness (10)

Definition: showing bluntness · charade + container + reversal + first letter

  • America → A the letter this word begins with
  • courageous style → BRAVELY to act in a courageous style is to act bravely
  • Is → SI the clue's own word, turned back to front

'Courageous style' means 'in a courageous manner', which is how a setter clues an adverb without ever using the word manner.

27 across MAUD

Muck covering a woman lured into the garden? (4)

Definition: woman lured into the garden · container

  • Muck → MUD muck is mud
  • a → A the article, taken as a single letter

Four letters, three of them muck, and the whole clue turns on remembering one Victorian song lyric.

28 across ACADEMY

Little woman grabbing baddy, head of English in school (7)

Definition: school · container + charade + first letter

  • Little woman → AMY Amy March is one of Alcott's Little Women
  • baddy → CAD a cad is the mild-mannered villain of older fiction
  • English → E E is the standard shortening of English

Both people are stock characters of the crossword rather than of the school story, which is exactly what makes them findable.

29 across COVERED

What Adam became after realisation of nudity? Fellow looking embarrassed (7)

Definition: What Adam became after realisation of nudity · charade

  • Fellow → COVE a cove is an old slang fellow, Dickens's word rather than a bay
  • looking embarrassed → RED embarrassment shows as going red in the face

The question mark warns you the first half is a story rather than a synonym, and the second half is two pieces of plain slang.

2 down PINTAIL

Bird, one on lake after daily drink? (7)

Definition: Bird · charade

  • daily drink → PINTA a pinta is a pint of milk, from the doorstep advertising slogan
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one
  • lake → L L is the map abbreviation for lake

'Drinka pinta milka day' put PINTA into English and it lives nowhere else, which is exactly why the clue has to say daily.

3 down TOWEL

The royal I found in upsetting fate – it’s absorbing? (5)

Definition: it’s absorbing · reversal + container

  • fate → LOT your lot is your fate, what falls to you
  • The royal I → WE a monarch says 'we' where the rest of us say 'I' — the royal we

The dash is the join: everything before it builds the word, and the comment after it is the definition dressed up as a remark about the story.

4 down INCISOR

One bit of nut is stuck in my tooth (7)

Definition: tooth · charade + container + first letter

  • One → I the Roman numeral for one
  • nut → N the front letter of nut
  • my → COR 'cor!' and 'my!' are the same exclamation of surprise
  • is → IS the clue's own word, taken as it stands

The surface is a dental emergency and the step that unlocks it is hearing 'my' as an exclamation rather than a possessive.

6 down ABBACY

A bishop must get by, having welcomed a Catholic in ecclesiastical office (6)

Definition: ecclesiastical office · charade + container

  • A → A the clue's own opening letter, taken as it stands
  • bishop → B B is the bishop on a chess scoresheet
  • by → BY the preposition taken whole, as two letters
  • a → A the article, taken as a letter again
  • Catholic → C C is the standard shortening of Catholic, as in RC

Everything here is an abbreviation, which is why the pious surface runs so smoothly — nothing in it has to mean anything at all.

7 down CORRECTOR

Conservative minister entertaining those from the ranks – someone putting the record straight? (9)

Definition: someone putting the record straight · charade + container

  • Conservative → C C is Conservative on a ballot paper
  • those from the ranks → OR OR is other ranks, the army's name for everyone who is not an officer
  • minister → RECTOR a rector is a parish minister

OR for other ranks earns its keep here: any clue mentioning soldiers, men or the ranks is usually asking for those two letters.

8 down CONKIER

Like tree worse affected by fungus – one penetrating horse chestnut (7)

Definition: Like tree worse affected by fungus · container

  • horse chestnut → CONKER a conker is the seed of the horse chestnut
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one

The clue turns on two lives of one word: the conker children play with, and 'conk', the shelf fungus a rotting trunk puts out.

9 down WATLING STREET

The old way engineer catches fish with something wooden (7,6)

Definition: The old way · container + charade

  • engineer → WATT James Watt is the crossword's default engineer
  • fish → LINGS the ling is a long cod-like sea fish
  • something wooden → TREE a tree is the wooden thing par excellence

The joke is that the road's second word is never clued as a road at all — it arrives as a tree, inside the engineer's own name.

15 down WASHSTAND

Rod collects wood and stone to make item of furniture (9)

Definition: item of furniture · container + charade

  • Rod → WAND a wand is a slender rod
  • wood → ASH ash is a hardwood, a wood before it is a residue
  • stone → ST ST is the abbreviation for the stone, fourteen pounds

Both materials are being used in their crossword senses rather than their obvious ones, which is what makes the builder's-yard surface hold together.

18 down AEROBIC

Beginning of intensive care organised to save old boy reliant on oxygen (7)

Definition: reliant on oxygen · anagram + container + first letter

  • intensive → I the letter this word starts with
  • care → CARE four more letters of fodder, taken as they stand
  • old boy → OB OB is an old boy, a former pupil of a school

'Intensive care' and 'old boy' both read as hospital furniture; only one letter of the first survives, and the second is an abbreviation.

20 down TALLY-HO

Cry in the country – gentry ultimately trapped in multi-storey house? (5-2)

Definition: Cry in the country · container + charade + last letter

  • multi-storey → TALL a building of many storeys is tall
  • gentry → Y the last letter of this word
  • house → HO HO is the abbreviation for house on an address

'Multi-storey' promises a car park and delivers an adjective, and the question mark is there because the house is an abbreviation, not a home.

21 down COUTURE

Stylish dressmaking not in fashion – external remedy needed (7)

Definition: Stylish dressmaking · container

  • remedy → CURE a remedy is a cure
  • not in fashion → OUT what is no longer in fashion is out

The medical half of the clue is doing the wrapping, and 'not in fashion' sits in a clue about fashion without being about it at all.

22 down INFIRM

Trendy business not enjoying healthy state (6)

Definition: not enjoying healthy state · charade

  • Trendy → IN what is trendy is in
  • business → FIRM a firm is a business

IN for trendy is the crossword's other 'in' — fashionable rather than inside — and here it doubles as the prefix that reverses the word after it.

25 down GIMME

Let me have a piece of string immediately (5)

Definition: Let me have · hidden word

  • string immediately → GIMME the last letter of the first word joins the first four of the second

Hidden answers nearly always straddle a join between words, and this one takes a single letter from the first — the least you can take and still call it a piece.
