1 across WISE MAN

Ladies’ organisation names retiring magician (4,3)

Definition: magician · charade + reversal

A wise man is a magus, and the magi of the nativity were astrologers and conjurors; folk tales still use the phrase for the village sorcerer.

  • Ladies’ organisation → WI the WI is the Women's Institute, the British countrywomen's organisation of jam and Jerusalem
  • names retiring → SEMAN NAMES written out from the back
  • retiring — A retiring person backs out of the room, and the word in front of this one backs out of the clue the same way, letter by letter.

A women's group announcing the name of a bashful stage conjuror.

Reid Wiseman commands Artemis II, and three more of that crew are hiding elsewhere in this grid at 19, 20 and 21. 'Retiring' is shyness in the surface and an instruction in the wordplay.

5 across ARTEMIS

Bonaparte mistress’s a goddess (7)

Definition: a goddess · hidden word

The Greek goddess of the hunt and the moon is one particular Olympian, so 'a goddess' defines by example rather than by class.

  • Bonaparte mistress → ARTEMIS seven consecutive letters, starting five letters into the emperor's name and finishing inside the second word
  • ’s — Read it as 'has': the two words in front of it have something sitting inside them, running across the join.

Napoleon's lover, spoken of as though she were divine.

Napoleon had famous mistresses — Joséphine, Marie Walewska — so the surface sends you hunting for a name. The letters you want are lying still, in order, across the gap between the two words.

9 across ORION

‘Old flower’, term for bargain hunter (5)

Definition: hunter · charade + last letter

The hunter of Greek myth was set among the stars with his belt and his club, so the definition names a constellation by the figure it draws.

  • Old → O o is the standard abbreviation for old
  • flower → RIO a flower is a thing that flows, meaning a river, and rio is Spanish for one
  • bargain → N the last letter of 'bargain'
  • term — A term is an end point, the terminus of a line, so it asks for the final letter of the word it points at.

A nickname — 'old flower' — for somebody forever chasing a discount.

The quotation marks are the setter's tip-off that the flower is not botanical: in crosswords a flower is a thing that flows. Orion is also the capsule that carries this puzzle's Artemis crew.

10 across SUPERNOVA

A sun proven briefly to be unstable (9)

Definition: A sun proven briefly to be unstable · &lit + anagram + deletion

A star that turns catastrophically unstable and blows itself apart is what the whole sentence describes, which is why every word can be read straight.

  • A sun proven briefly → ASUNPROVE nine letters of fodder once 'proven' has been cut short
  • unstable → the shuffling instruction
  • briefly — Said briefly, a word gives up its last letter, and the shortened form is what the wordplay uses.
  • unstable — Something unstable will not hold the shape it was handed, so these letters do not stay in the order the clue lists them.

A star shown, for a moment, to be anything but steady.

'Briefly' has to be spotted or the count comes out wrong — nine letters of fodder, not ten. Read straight, the same word says the instability did not last long.

11 across STRAGGLERS

Those last wrestlers maybe united being acknowledged at first (10)

Definition: Those last · substitution + first letter

Stragglers are the ones trailing at the back of a march or a race, so the definition names them by where they end up in the line.

  • wrestlers maybe → STRUGGLERS wrestlers grapple, and anyone grappling with something is a struggler
  • united → U u is the standard abbreviation for united, as in the football clubs
  • acknowledged at first → A the opening letter of 'acknowledged', which moves into the place vacated
  • maybe — Flags a loose fit: wrestlers are one kind of the people the wordplay word covers, not the whole of them.
  • being — Marks an exchange — one letter stops being itself and the letter named next takes over its place.
  • at first — Only the front letter of the word beside it is taken, the rest of that word doing nothing.

The last few wrestlers, brought together and finally getting some recognition.

'Those last' looks like the start of a longer definition about wrestlers, and it is the whole of it. Wordplay word and answer differ by a single letter, U giving way to A.

12 across ISLE

We’re told Kite will land in water (4)

Definition: land in water · homophone

An isle is a piece of land with water all round it, which is what the definition says the long way round.

  • Kite will → ISLE Kite is the setter of this puzzle, so 'Kite will' is him saying 'I will'
  • We’re told — What we are told reaches us by ear, so the wordplay describes a noise rather than a spelling.

The setter's kite is going to come down in the water.

Kite is the name at the top of this puzzle, so 'Kite will' is the setter talking about himself. Setters do this often enough that the byline is worth reading before you start.

14 across A PIG IN A POKE

Pa OK, with a pie and gin cocktail or a bacon sandwich? (1,3,2,1,4)

Definition: a bacon sandwich · anagram

A poke is an old word for a bag; a bacon sandwich puts the pork inside one, which turns a phrase about buying unseen into a literal description.

  • Pa OK → PAOK four letters of fodder
  • a pie → APIE four more
  • gin → GIN the last three, eleven letters in all
  • cocktail → the mixing instruction
  • cocktail — A cocktail is several things shaken up together, so the letters offered before it are served in a new order.

Dad is content with a pie, a gin cocktail and a bacon sandwich.

'Cocktail' is the only instruction in a line that otherwise reads as a lunch order, and the eleven letters of fodder are spread over three separate scraps of that menu.

18 across AT ALL EVENTS

Anyway, slave with talent for cooking (2,3,6)

Definition: Anyway · anagram

'At all events' means whatever happens, in any case — the job 'anyway' does when you wave an objection aside and carry on regardless.

  • slave → SLAVE five letters of fodder
  • talent → TALENT six more, eleven in all
  • cooking → the mixing instruction
  • cooking — Cooking mixes its ingredients past recognition, so the letters in front of it come out in a different arrangement.

A kitchen slave who happens to have a real gift for cooking.

'For cooking' reads as the thing the slave is good at, and 'cooking' is the instruction. Eleven letters is a lot to hold in the head, so count them against the enumeration first.

21 across KOCH

German scientist has floor with central heating (4)

Definition: German scientist · charade

The bacteriologist who identified the bacilli behind tuberculosis and cholera was German, so the definition points at one particular scientist rather than the class.

  • floor → KO to floor somebody is to knock them out, and KO is boxing shorthand for it
  • central heating → CH ch is the estate agent's abbreviation for central heating

A German scientist whose flat has floors and central heating.

'Floor' is a verb, not a storey — to floor a man is to knock him down. Christina Koch flies on Artemis II, joining three more of that crew in this grid.

22 across ENDOTHELIA

Not ideal, he reused tissues (10)

Definition: tissues · anagram

Endothelia are the thin cell layers lining blood vessels and the heart, so the definition names them by their biological class, in the plural.

  • Not ideal, he → NOTIDEALHE ten letters of fodder, the whole opening phrase
  • reused → the rearranging instruction
  • reused — Something reused is put back to work in a new arrangement, which is what happens to the letters offered in front of it.

Somebody less than ideal, using the same paper tissues twice over.

'Tissues' reads as the paper kind and means the biological kind, which is the whole distance between the surface and the answer. Ten letters, so count them before moving any.

25 across TOOTHCOMB

It’s fine, so they say now, very thin, evenly-divided dress (9)

Definition: It’s fine, so they say now · charade + deletion

It is the close-toothed comb you search hair with, and 'so they say now' owns up to the modern habit of writing the tool's name as one word.

  • very → TOO too hot means very hot
  • thin → TH the four-letter word split down the middle, front half kept
  • dress → COMB to dress hair is to comb it, the sense a hairdresser uses
  • evenly-divided — Cut into two equal halves, and it is the front half of the four-letter word that is kept.

A dress so thin and so neatly halved that someone insists it is perfectly fine.

'Dress' is hairdressing, not clothing: to dress hair is to comb it. The definition runs all the way to 'now', which is the setter's nod to how people say the phrase these days.

26 across OAVES

Idiots’ heads not used at the beginning (5)

Definition: Idiots · deletion

The answer is an old plural of 'oaf', the big clumsy stupid one, so the definition matches it in both sense and number.

  • heads → LOAVES a loaf is a head in British slang — 'use your loaf' means use your head — so heads in the plural are loaves
  • not used at the beginning → the opening L is the letter dropped
  • not used at the beginning — The letter standing at the front is the one left out, so the word starts a letter later than it should.

Fools who fail to use their heads before they begin.

Nothing in the line looks like an instruction until you notice that the last five words are one, and the surface goes on reading as a complaint about people who never think first.

27 across RE-ENTRY

Note record for return of 8 (2-5)

Definition: return of 8 · charade

Coming back down through the atmosphere is what a spacecraft does at the end of a flight, and 8 down is the crew making that return.

  • Note → RE re is the second note of the sol-fa scale, do re mi
  • record → ENTRY an entry in a ledger is the record of a single transaction

Making a note of the record set by something coming back.

The 8 is a cross-reference to 8 down, so the definition only opens up once that one is solved — a habit worth expecting whenever a grid has a theme running through it.

28 across TEST RUN

Check score of 1 in game (4,3)

Definition: Check · charade

A test run is a trial to see whether something works, and 'check' is that same noun: the check you give a system before you trust it.

  • game → TEST a Test is the five-day international match at the top of cricket
  • score of 1 → RUN one run is the smallest score a cricket batter can add

Looking up the one-point score from a game.

Both halves are cricket: a single run, scored in a Test match. The clue names them in the reverse of the order the answer needs them.

1 down WHOOSH

High-speed train sound covering mostly quiet first of whistles at the outset (6)

Definition: High-speed train sound · charade + deletion + first letter

A whoosh is the rushing noise made by something tearing past, and an express through a station is where most people hear it.

  • first of whistles at the outset → W the opening letter of 'whistles', posted to the very start
  • covering mostly → HOO a hood is a covering, and most of it is everything except the final letter
  • quiet → SH sh is the noise you make when you are asking for quiet
  • mostly — Most of a word is all of it bar the final letter, so that last letter is left behind.
  • first of — Only the first item out of a set is wanted, which for a word means its opening letter and nothing else.
  • at the outset — This says where the letter belongs rather than which letter it is: right at the front of the assembly.

A train's noise blanketing a quiet platform where the whistles are only just starting.

'Covering' looks like a container instruction telling you to wrap one piece around another. It is a noun here — the hood on a coat — and it hands over letters of its own.

2 down SKIERS

PM with tummy upset on board getting slippers (6)

Definition: slippers · container + reversal

Skiers slip downhill on purpose, so the definition is the word read literally — people who slip — rather than the things you put on indoors.

  • on board → SS SS is the prefix on a steamship's name, and being on board puts everything else between the two letters
  • PM with tummy upset → KIER Keir Starmer is the British prime minister, and the tummy of his name is the middle pair of letters, tipped over
  • upset — Something upset has been tipped over, and the pair of letters it applies to come out in the opposite order.
  • on board — Anything carried on board sits inside the ship, so what the clue names next goes between the two letters the ship supplies.

A seasick prime minister on a ship, being handed a pair of slippers.

'Slippers' wants to be footwear and means people who slip instead. 'Tummy' is a playful name for the middle of a word, and only that middle pair gets turned round.

3 down MANAGEABLE

Person largely mature and fit is compliant (10)

Definition: compliant · charade + deletion

Something manageable does what you want without a fight, which is the sense of 'compliant' applied to a thing or a task rather than to a person.

  • Person → MAN man in its old general sense of any human being
  • largely mature → AGE aged is mature, cut short by one letter
  • fit → ABLE someone fit for a job is able to do it
  • largely — The large part of a word is everything except its final letter, and that letter is dropped here.

A mostly grown-up, healthy person who does as they are asked.

The definition is the last word, sitting behind 'is', so the first five words are all wordplay — and 'largely' is a trimming instruction, not a comment on how grown-up anybody is.

4 down NASAL

Nosy neighbour’s opening a half of salmon (5)

Definition: Nosy · charade + first letter

Nasal means of the nose, and 'nosy' is being used in that flat anatomical sense rather than its everyday one about prying.

  • neighbour’s opening → N the first letter of 'neighbour'
  • a → A the word is lifted straight out of the clue
  • half of salmon → SAL the first three of the fish's six letters
  • opening — The opening of a word is its first letter, and that single letter is all this contributes.
  • half of — Exactly half the letters are wanted, and it is the front half of the six-letter fish that gets used.

A prying next-door neighbour turning up for a share of the fish.

'Nosy' is doing anatomy rather than gossip, and the neighbour peering over the fence is there to keep you reading it the other way.

5 down APPORTION

Share program or no one backs saving time (9)

Definition: Share · charade + reversal + container

To apportion is to divide something into shares and hand them out, so the definition matches as a verb: you apportion blame, you share it out.

  • program → APP an app is the program on your phone
  • or → OR the word is used as it stands
  • time → T t is the standard abbreviation for time
  • no one backs → ION NO plus I for one, then written out backwards
  • backs — To back out is to go the other way, so the letters it applies to are written from the end to the start.
  • saving — Saving something means keeping it safe inside, which is where the letter named here ends up.

Nobody is backing the time-saving software, so it may as well be split up.

'Backs' reads as support in the surface and as a turnaround instruction in the wordplay, and 'one' is the Roman numeral I — a convention the clue never signals.

6 down TURN

Change act (4)

Definition: Change · double definition

Milk turns when it changes, and a turn is a single act in a variety bill — the comic who does five minutes and goes off.

  • Change → the verb: weather and milk both do this when they go the other way
  • act → the noun: one performer's spot on a music-hall bill

The two halves are not even the same part of speech — a verb then a noun — which is most of the disguise available in a clue this short.

7 down MOONSHOT

Ambitious project – show behind picture? (8)

Definition: Ambitious project · charade

A moonshot is a wildly ambitious undertaking, the word borrowed from the Apollo programme for any goal that needs a decade and a nation behind it.

  • show behind → MOON to moon is to bare your backside at somebody
  • picture → SHOT a shot is a photograph, or one frame of film

A grand scheme that turns out to be baring your backside for the camera.

'Behind' is the noun, not the preposition, so 'show behind' is one rude verb rather than an instruction about where a piece goes. The question mark is the setter flagging the joke.

8 down SPACEMEN

These fellows wear tight-fitting headgear or small bowlers (8)

Definition: These fellows wear tight-fitting headgear · charade

Snug helmets and the soft comm caps worn under them are the working headgear of men sent into orbit, so the clue defines them by what is on their heads.

  • small → S s is the clothing-label abbreviation for small
  • bowlers → PACEMEN in cricket the pacemen are the fast bowlers, the ones who bowl at speed rather than spin

Fellows in headwear, whether a snug cap or a little bowler hat.

Bowlers are hats in the surface and cricketers in the wordplay, and 'or' joins the definition to the wordplay instead of offering you a choice between two things.

13 down FALSEHOODS

She with loads of bananas and pork pies (10)

Definition: pork pies · anagram

Pork pies is Cockney rhyming slang for lies, usually shortened to 'porkies', and it matches the answer in the plural.

  • She → SHE the first three letters of the fodder
  • loads of → LOADSOF seven more, the phrase taken whole
  • bananas → the shuffling instruction
  • bananas — Going bananas is going wild, and letters told to go wild will not stay in the order they arrived in.

A woman turning up with heaps of fruit and a few pork pies.

'Bananas' looks like more of the shopping, sitting between the loads and the pies, and it is the one word in the clue giving an order rather than letters.

15 down INVENTORY

Trendy Conservative including detailed sale in register (9)

Definition: register · container + deletion

An inventory is the itemised list of what a house or a shop holds, and a register is that same sort of formal list kept as a record.

  • Trendy → IN something in is in fashion
  • detailed sale → VEN vend is a sale in Chambers, docked of its last letter
  • Conservative → TORY a Tory is a British Conservative, the party's nickname since the 17th century
  • including — What a thing includes sits inside it, so the letters that follow are pushed in between the two pieces already named.
  • detailed — Read as de-tailed rather than thorough: the word loses its tail, which is its final letter.

A fashionable Conservative putting an itemised sale into the record.

'Detailed' looks like an adjective promising a thorough sale and is really a verb. The two outer pieces already stand in the clue in the order the answer wants them.

16 down DARK STAR

Duke’s vessel starting to sink given sailor’s not a light body (4,4)

Definition: not a light body · charade + first letter

A dark star is a stellar body that gives out no visible light, so the definition describes it plainly and leaves the astronomy for you to supply.

  • Duke → D d is the standard abbreviation for duke
  • vessel → ARK Noah's ark, the crossword's favourite vessel
  • starting to sink → S the first letter of 'sink'
  • sailor’s → TAR a tar is an old name for a sailor, from the tarpaulin clothing they wore
  • starting to — Something only starting has just its first letter showing, and that letter is the whole of its contribution.

A duke's boat begins to go down, and the sailor proves to be no lightweight.

'Not a light body' looks like a remark about the sailor's build and is the definition, which leaves everything in front of it as four pieces laid end to end.

17 down PARCLOSE

Standard finish for screen (8)

Definition: screen · charade

A parclose is the carved screen that fences off a chapel or a tomb inside a church, so the definition is the word's plain architectural sense.

  • Standard → PAR par is the standard score for a golf hole, and 'up to par' means up to standard
  • finish → CLOSE to close a meeting is to finish it

The ordinary paint finish applied to a screen.

The answer is a church word: parcloses are the screens that shut a side chapel off from the nave. Both wordplay words are everyday ones, which is what hides the join between them.

19 down GLOVER

Craftsman makes good partner (6)

Definition: Craftsman · charade

A glover makes and sells gloves — Shakespeare's father was one — so the definition names a trade rather than any particular skill.

  • good → G g is the standard abbreviation for good
  • partner → LOVER the partner you are romantically attached to

A skilled craftsman turning out to be somebody's ideal partner.

Victor Glover pilots Artemis II, and three of his crewmates are at 1, 20 and 21. 'Makes' is only joinery, though the trade in the definition does literally make things.

20 down HANSEN

German/English name for leprosy pioneer (6)

Definition: leprosy pioneer · charade

The Norwegian doctor who identified the leprosy bacterium in 1873 gave the disease the name it carries in medicine today, and 'pioneer' points at him.

  • German → HANS Hans is the stock German first name, their equivalent of John
  • English → E e is the standard abbreviation for English
  • name → N n is the standard abbreviation for name

A name that serves in German and in English, worn by a pioneer of leprosy research.

The slash does no work beyond the surface: German and English are two separate pieces of wordplay rather than one hyphenated nationality. Jeremy Hansen is Artemis II's Canadian mission specialist.

23 down ORBIT

Piece of gold circle (5)

Definition: circle · charade

An orbit is the closed loop one body travels round another, so the definition is the shape it traces rather than any drawn ring.

  • gold → OR or is gold in heraldry, straight from the French
  • Piece → BIT a bit is a piece of something

A fragment broken off a gold ring.

The two wordplay words arrive in the opposite order to the letters they give, so 'piece of gold' has to be read as the piece going after the gold rather than before it.

24 down CHAT

Bird or rabbit (4)

Definition: Bird · double definition

A chat is a small songbird — stonechat, whinchat — and to rabbit is British slang for talking on and on, which is a chat by another name.

  • Bird → the ornithological reading: a family of small perching birds named for their scolding call
  • rabbit → the slang reading: to rabbit on is to talk at length

A bird and a rabbit, named in the same breath.

'Rabbit' is a verb here. It comes from the rhyming slang rabbit and pork, meaning talk, and nothing in the clue admits the two words are animals in the surface only.
