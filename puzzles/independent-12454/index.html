Independent Cryptic Crossword No 12,454

Across

1 across MADNESS

Lear's affliction created mostly by head (7)

Definition: Lear's affliction · charade + deletion

  • created mostly → MAD 'created' is MADE, and most of MADE is its first three letters
  • by head → NESS a ness is a headland, the ending of British place names like Sheerness, and 'by' sets it alongside

Shakespeare's king is diagnosed as though his trouble were self-made, something cooked up in his own head.

'Head' looks like an instruction to take a first letter. It is a noun instead, the geographical ness that ends so many coastal place names.

5 across PROSPER

Flourish from magician, making ring disappear (7)

Definition: Flourish · deletion

  • magician → PROSPERO Prospero is the magician who raises the storm in The Tempest
  • making ring → a ring is the letter O, and this is the letter conjured away

A conjuror's showy gesture on stage, with a ring vanishing between his fingers.

'Flourish' wants to be the magician's sweep of the hand. It is the verb meaning to thrive, and it is the definition.

9 across GLOBE

The earth? Throw into empty grave (5)

Definition: The earth? · container + outer letters

  • Throw → LOB a lob is a throw, the high gentle kind
  • empty grave → GE GRAVE hollowed out keeps only the letters at its two ends

A mourner lobbing something into a grave that has already been cleared out.

A burial and a planet share one short sentence, and 'The earth?' sits at the front where a solver expects the story to begin rather than the definition.

10 across IDENTICAL TWIN

Dromio or Antipholus potentially land in it twice by accident (9,4)

Definition: Dromio or Antipholus potentially · anagram

  • land in it twice → LANDINITTWICE anagram fodder, thirteen letters
  • by accident → anagram indicator

Two characters from a farce who keep landing in the same trouble twice over, by pure mishap.

Dromio and Antipholus are the two pairs of brothers in The Comedy of Errors. 'Twice' looks like an instruction to double something and is thirteen letters of plain fodder instead.

11 across TRUNCATION

Curtailment of holiday (3rd-8th) after travelling chest's end falls off (10)

Definition: Curtailment · charade + deletion

  • travelling chest's end falls off → TRUN a travelling chest is a trunk, and it loses the letter at its end
  • holiday (3rd-8th) → CATION a holiday is a vacation, and the third to the eighth of its letters are the ones wanted

Someone's holiday is cut short when the end falls off their travelling chest.

The bracketed '(3rd-8th)' reads as holiday dates in the surface. It is an instruction: count into the word for a holiday and take that run of letters.

12 across LOVE

Left because of almost nothing (4)

Definition: nothing · charade + deletion

  • Left → L L is the standard short form of left, as marked on a pair of gloves
  • because of almost → OVE 'because of' is 'over', as in a quarrel over money, stopped one letter short

Somebody walked out on account of the merest trifle.

'Left' looks like the definition of a departure and is one letter of wordplay; the definition is the last word, and it is a tennis score.

14 across SHAKESPEARE

Starter of soup, fish second, fruit compote finally for Anne's husband perhaps (11)

Definition: Anne's husband perhaps · charade + first letter + last letter

  • Starter of soup → S the opening letter of soup, the course a meal starts with
  • fish → HAKE hake is a food fish of the cod family, common on British menus
  • second → S s is the standard abbreviation for a second of time
  • fruit → PEAR the fruit is a pear, and it goes in whole
  • compote finally → E the last letter of compote

A menu recited in order: soup to start, then fish, then a fruit compote to finish.

Five pieces hide as a set meal, and the join between courses is where the letters change hands. 'Perhaps' is honest: plenty of Annes have husbands.

18 across REMONSTRATE

Argue against Sartre and Monet in review (11)

Definition: Argue against · anagram

  • Sartre and Monet → SARTREMONET anagram fodder, eleven letters
  • in review → anagram indicator

A philosopher and a painter reconsidered side by side in a piece of French criticism.

'Review' is exactly the kind of word that could mean turn round; here it means go over again and reorder. The two Frenchmen have nothing to do with the meaning.

22 across SORDIDNESS

Delivery of blade and nastily snide son lead to immoral actions (10)

Definition: immoral actions · homophone + anagram + charade

  • Delivery of blade → SORD the blade is a sword, and English has never sounded the w in that word
  • snide → IDNES the five letters of the clue's own word, resettled
  • son → S s is the usual abbreviation for son

A blade handed over, plus a spiteful man's boy, between them producing something thoroughly seedy.

Sword is one of the few English words with a silent w, and that silence is what lets 'Delivery' turn a blade into four letters worth building on.

25 across IMITATIVE

Fake current Lloyd Webber musical with brief interval making a comeback (9)

Definition: Fake · charade + deletion + reversal

  • current → I I is the physicist's symbol for electric current
  • Lloyd Webber musical → EVITA Evita is Lloyd Webber's musical about Eva Peron
  • brief interval → TIM an interval is a stretch of time, docked of its last letter

An electrified revival of a Lloyd Webber show, its interval cut short, is written off as counterfeit.

'Making a comeback' does two jobs at once: it is what a revived musical does, and it is the instruction to read the back half of the wordplay the other way.

26 across GREED

Consented to take top off, resulting in desire (5)

Definition: desire · deletion

  • Consented → AGREED to have consented is to have agreed
  • take top off → the letter at the front is the one taken away

Someone agreed to take their top off, and what it produced was appetite rather than admiration.

The surface's top is a garment, which is the whole of the misdirection: a word wears one too, and it is the letter the word begins with.

27 across SINE DIE

Trespass originally every day, in other words indefinitely (4,3)

Definition: indefinitely · charade + first letters

  • Trespass → SIN to trespass, in the biblical sense of the Lord's Prayer, is to sin
  • originally every day → ED the opening letters of the two words that follow the instruction
  • in other words → IE i.e. is what you write for 'that is', the phrase that puts something in other words

Somebody owns up to trespassing every single day, and then rephrases the confession.

The answer is a Latin tag from court reports, where a case adjourned with no date fixed may never be heard again. The comma is punctuation for the surface, not a break in the wordplay.

28 across MASTERS

Gains power over turbulent streams (7)

Definition: Gains power over · anagram

  • streams → STREAMS anagram fodder, seven letters
  • turbulent → anagram indicator

Somebody getting the better of a stretch of fast, rough water.

'Power over turbulent streams' reads as one idea, the taming of a river, so the seven letters sitting in plain sight at the end are easy to walk straight past.

Down

1 down MIGHTY

I hear setter's hot beverage is strong (6)

Definition: strong · homophone

  • setter's hot beverage → MIGHTY the setter refers to himself, so 'setter's' is 'my', and the hot beverage is tea

Someone reports that the person who writes the puzzle takes their hot drink strong.

'Setter' is the crossword's word for whoever wrote the clue, so 'setter's' is a first-person possessive; the drink beside it is the ordinary British cuppa.

2 down DOOFUS

Party of America supplying dimwit (6)

Definition: dimwit · charade

  • Party → DO a do is a party — a works do, a birthday do
  • of → OF the clue's own two letters, taken at face value
  • America → US US is the standard short form of the United States

An American political party is credited with turning out an idiot.

The little word 'of' is not joinery here but two letters kept whole, which is why the charade reads as an ordinary phrase instead of a list of parts.

3 down EXERCISING

Driving out evil energy, replacing nothing troubling (10)

Definition: troubling · substitution

  • Driving out evil → EXORCISING driving an evil spirit out of someone is exorcising them
  • energy → E E is the physicist's symbol for energy, and it is the letter moving in
  • nothing → the round letter that stands for nothing is the one giving up its place

An exorcism in which energy is swapped in for nothing at all, and the ritual goes badly.

The definition is the very last word, and it is the formal sense of 'exercise' meaning to worry someone — the question exercising the committee.

4 down SHIFT

Change key (5)

Definition: Change · double definition

  • Change → a change of position, opinion or working hours — the noun, and also the verb
  • key → read literally: one of the keys on a keyboard, the one held down for capitals

A musician is being told to modulate partway through the piece.

Two senses sit side by side with no joinery at all, which is why the clue is only two words long — the shortest clues in a puzzle are nearly always double definitions.

5 down PHENOMENA

Remarkable things: a mixture of hope 'n' amen! (9)

Definition: Remarkable things · anagram

  • hope 'n' amen → HOPENAMEN anagram fodder, nine letters
  • a mixture of → anagram indicator

A recipe of sorts: stir a little hope together with an amen and see what wonders come out.

The apostrophes round 'n' make it look like decoration from a phrase such as rock 'n' roll, and it is a plain letter of the fodder.

6 down OATH

Pledge made by prisoner of hero at Hyperborea (4)

Definition: Pledge · hidden word

  • hero at Hyperborea → OATH four consecutive letters run across the gap between two of these words

A promise extracted from a captive held by a hero in a far northern land.

Hyperborea is the Greeks' mythical country beyond the north wind, which is what lets the clue read as a scrap of legend rather than as an instruction.

7 down PECTORAL

Friend cradling decapitated Trojan close to chest (8)

Definition: close to chest · container + deletion

  • Friend → PAL a pal is a friend, and these three letters split to make the outside
  • decapitated Trojan → ECTOR the Trojan is Hector, the prince who leads Troy's defence in the Iliad, minus his first letter

A friend holds a beheaded Trojan warrior tight against his own ribs.

'Close to chest' reads as the end of the surface picture, and it is the definition — an anatomical adjective rather than the description of a hug.

8 down RELIEVED

Freed soldier survived swallowing drug (8)

Definition: Freed · charade + container

  • soldier → RE RE is the Royal Engineers, the British Army corps whose men are the sappers
  • survived → LIVED to have survived is to have lived, and this word opens up to take a letter
  • drug → E E is ecstasy, the crossword's default drug

A soldier who came through and is still on something is let go.

'Freed' points at the duty sense of the verb rather than the emotional one, which is the sense the surface's survivor pushes you towards.

13 down APPENDAGES

Maybe branches hang on over generations (10)

Definition: Maybe branches · charade

  • hang on → APPEND to append is to hang something on the end of what is already there
  • generations → AGES ages are the long stretches of time the surface calls generations

Family branches that have somehow hung on down the generations.

'Maybe' is the setter flagging a definition by example: branches are one sort of thing that hangs off something bigger, not the only sort.

15 down AUTHORISE

Writer reporting parliamentary assents gets permit (9)

Definition: permit · charade + homophone

  • Writer → AUTHOR a writer is an author
  • parliamentary assents → ISE in the Commons a vote in favour is an aye, and the ayes said out loud are the ending of a verb

A journalist writing up the ayes of a Commons division is handed a pass for it.

The -ise ending of an English verb sounds exactly like the ayes counted in a parliamentary division, which is the whole of the joke.

16 down PRETTIES

Ugly stripe concealing alien's knick-knacks! (8)

Definition: knick-knacks · anagram + container

  • stripe → PRTIES these six letters resettled, then split to make the outside
  • alien's → ET ET is the crossword's standard alien, after the film

The stripes on something ugly turn out to have an extraterrestrial hiding among them.

'Ugly' opens the clue as a description and works as the shuffle instruction, and what it shuffles ends up meaning very nearly the opposite.

17 down AMBITION

Boring piece I hone endlessly following morning drive (8)

Definition: drive · charade + deletion

  • morning → AM AM marks the hours before noon
  • Boring piece → BIT the bit is the piece of a drill that does the boring
  • I → I the clue's own letter, taken straight
  • hone endlessly → ON HONE surrenders the letter at each end

A dull object somebody keeps sharpening for ever, after a morning at the wheel.

'Boring' is not the surface's tedium but a hole being drilled, and 'drive' at the end is not the motoring one — both words change meaning between the story and the parse.

19 down SERENE

Tranquil moon goddess changes sides (6)

Definition: Tranquil · substitution

  • moon goddess → SELENE Selene is the Greek goddess who drives the moon across the sky
  • sides → R the sides are left and right, L and R, and the name's L is traded for the other

A moon goddess so unruffled that she switches allegiance without anyone minding.

Left and right are L and R in almost every cryptic, which is what makes 'changes sides' an instruction about one letter rather than about loyalty.

20 down ASIDES

Words spoken privately since ill-omened day (6)

Definition: Words spoken privately · charade

  • since → AS 'as' stands in for 'since' in the causal sense — as it was raining, we stayed in
  • ill-omened day → IDES the Ides of March is the date the soothsayer warns Caesar about

A remark muttered under the breath ever since one unlucky date on the calendar.

The ides were only the middle of any Roman month; it is Caesar's assassination on the ides of March that makes one of them ill-omened.

23 down DREAM

Fancy doctor exfoliates and moisturises initially (5)

Definition: Fancy · charade + first letters

  • doctor → DR Dr is the everyday written title for a doctor
  • exfoliates and moisturises initially → EAM the opening letters of the three words, kept in the order given

A skincare routine — exfoliate, then moisturise — prescribed by an elegant doctor.

'Fancy' reads as an adjective describing the doctor, which is what the beauty-counter surface wants; it is the definition, and a noun.

24 down BARD

14's pointless facial hair (4)

Definition: 14 · deletion

  • facial hair → BEARD the facial hair is a beard
  • pointless → E is a compass point, and it is the letter that goes

The playwright's whiskers dismissed as serving no purpose whatever.

Compass points are the crossword's favourite letters to steal, so 'pointless' nearly always means one of N, S, E or W has been taken out.
