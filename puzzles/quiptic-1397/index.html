Across

8 across STONE AGE

Wild goat seen in prehistoric period (5,3)

Definition: prehistoric period · anagram

  • goat → GOAT four letters taken for their spelling; the animal itself never appears
  • seen → SEEN the other four, joined to the first set before anything is rearranged

'Wild goat' reads as one creature and sets you hunting for an ibex or a markhor. The adjective is not describing the goat at all; it is describing what to do with the letters of both words.

9 across NOUGAT

Sweet nothing when alpha ousts husband (6)

Definition: Sweet · substitution

  • nothing → NOUGHT nought is nothing spelled out as a word, the zero itself
  • husband → H H is the standard abbreviation for husband, and it is the letter driven out
  • alpha → A alpha is the Greek A, and the same letter in the pilots' alphabet

'Sweet nothing' is a whispered endearment, and that is exactly the reading to drop: 'sweet' is the definition on its own and 'nothing' is a word to be written out and then meddled with.

10 across GO TO SEED

Poor stooge and journalist decline through neglect (2,2,4)

Definition: decline through neglect · anagram

  • stooge → STOOGE six letters for the shuffle; a stooge is a comedian's fall guy
  • journalist → ED ED is the crossword's editor, the journalist who runs the desk

A garden nobody tends runs to seed and stops flowering, which is where the phrase for decay comes from. 'Poor stooge' asks for sympathy; it is an instruction.

11 across HOOKAH

Mentioned rugby forward’s pipe (6)

Definition: pipe · homophone

  • rugby forward → HOOKAH the man in the middle of the front row, who hooks the ball back with his foot; in most British speech that final R is not sounded

In non-rhotic British English the two words are a single sound, which is the whole trick. 'Pipe' is the water pipe of a Middle Eastern café rather than anything plumbers fit.

12 across BARBER OF SEVILLE

Locksmith that’s scored? (6,2,7)

Definition: Locksmith that’s scored? · cryptic definition

  • Locksmith → the surface offers the tradesman who cuts keys; the locks meant are the ones on a head, and this is the man who deals with those
  • that’s scored → not a goal but a musical score — the man in question is the hero of a Rossini opera, written out for voices and orchestra

Read 'Locksmith' as a trade practised on hair and 'scored' as set to music, and you land on Rossini's comic opera about Figaro, the scheming barber of a Spanish city. The question mark flags the pun.

15 across FIEND

Ally ousts Republican devil (5)

Definition: devil · deletion

  • Ally → FRIEND an ally is a friend, the word taken at full length before anything is removed
  • Republican → R R is the standard abbreviation for Republican, as printed on a US ballot paper

American politics supplies both the letter and the picture: read straight, the clue is one party's man pushing another out of office. Only the single R actually goes.

16 across VERGE

Clergyman cut grass border (5)

Definition: grass border · deletion

  • Clergyman → VERGER the church officer who looks after the building and carries the mace ahead of the clergy in procession

A verger is strictly a lay official rather than an ordained man, so 'clergyman' is a shade loose and the setter is trading on the church setting. On British roads the strip of grass beside the tarmac is the verge.

20 across THE GIFT OF THE GAB

Capacity to talk big: ‘get off that,’ he exploded (3,4,2,3,3)

Definition: Capacity to talk · anagram

  • big → BIG three letters for the pile — the word is not describing the talking
  • ‘get off that,’ → GETOFFTHAT the quoted shout supplies ten more letters, punctuation ignored
  • he → HE the pronoun is fodder too, easy to miss because it reads as the person doing the shouting

'Talk big' is the phrase the surface hands you, and 'big' belongs to the letters instead — which is why the count reaches fifteen only once you include it and the stray 'he'.

21 across WORSEN

Sick owners deteriorate (6)

Definition: deteriorate · anagram

  • owners → WORSEN the six letters of the people in the surface, resettled into another word

The surface is a landlord with a bad cold. 'Sick' is not describing him at all; it is describing what has to happen to the six letters that follow it.

23 across FLAMINGO

Passionate love for bird (8)

Definition: bird · charade

  • Passionate → FLAMING a flaming row is a passionate one — the adjective for anything blazing with feeling
  • love → O in tennis love is a score of nothing, and nothing is written as a circle

'Passionate love' looks like a single idea and the setter wants it as two, a fiery adjective followed by a tennis nought. 'For' is nothing but a joint.

25 across DELETE

Cancel permit to enter river (6)

Definition: Cancel · container

  • river → DEE the Dee, which runs through Chester and Aberdeen — a river crosswords keep on hand for its three letters
  • permit → LET to let somebody do a thing is to permit it

Rivers are the crossword's small change and this is one of the commonest, so 'river' promises three letters before you know which three. The surface, a licence for a boat, is pure decoration.

26 across BRAINBOX

Clever clogs needing support with primary email folder? (8)

Definition: Clever clogs · charade

  • support → BRA the undergarment is the crossword's usual support, and the only one that comes in three letters
  • primary email folder? → INBOX the folder new mail lands in first, which is what 'primary' is pointing at

'Clever clogs' is British for a show-off know-all, said half in affection. The support is underwear rather than a bracket, and that swap is the whole joke.

Down

1 down OTTOMAN

Old male cat occupying bronze padded seat (7)

Definition: padded seat · charade + container

  • Old → O O is the standard crossword shorthand for old
  • bronze → TAN bronze is a brownish colour as well as a metal, and a tan is that shade of skin
  • male cat → TOM a tom is a male cat, and it slots in after the opening letter of that colour

The surface is a stray asleep on the furniture. Bronze wants to be a metal and it is a colour, which is the one swap the clue turns on; the seat itself is the Turkish footstool that took the empire's name.

2 down UNDOUBTING

Definite nasty bug in donut (10)

Definition: Definite · anagram

  • bug → BUG three letters for the pile, taken for their spelling and not the insect
  • in donut → INDONUT 'in' is fodder as well, not the joinery it looks like, so seven more letters go in

The little word 'in' reads as a place — a bug inside a doughnut — and it is really two more letters for the shuffle. Leave it out and the count comes up short of ten.

3 down TAKE

Nick cheers on vacuous karaoke (4)

Definition: Nick · charade + outer letters

  • cheers → TA 'ta' is the everyday British thank-you, and 'cheers' is used for exactly the same purpose
  • karaoke → KE only the outer pair survives, the opening letter and the closing one

'Nick' is the British slang verb for stealing rather than a name, and until you see that the clue looks like a man being cheered at a karaoke bar.

4 down SEND OFF

Dismiss son, nothing to add over fine (4,3)

Definition: Dismiss · charade

  • son → S S is the standard abbreviation for son on a family tree
  • nothing to add → ENDOF 'end of' is the British way of saying there is no more to be said on the subject
  • fine → F F is the pencil grade meaning fine, and the usual shorthand for the word

British speech closes an argument with 'end of', meaning nothing more can be added, and the setter buries that phrase in a sentence about a family. The comma after 'son' is punctuation for the surface only.

5 down IN THE EVENT

As things transpired, where athletes would compete (2,3,5)

Definition: As things transpired · double definition

  • As things transpired → the storyteller's phrase for how something actually turned out, as against what was expected
  • where athletes would compete → read literally: an event is one item on an athletics programme, the 100 metres or the high jump

The same three words are handed over twice with only a comma between them, once as an idiom about outcomes and once as a plain statement of place. No letters move at all.

6 down DURO

Spanish American silver peso found in corduroys (4)

Definition: Spanish American silver peso · hidden word

  • corduroys → DURO four consecutive letters sit inside this single word, beginning after its third

One word hides the whole thing, which is why no join is visible anywhere. A duro was the old five-peseta piece, the Spanish dollar that circulated across the colonies.

7 down EATABLE

Shell of areca (betel) nuts fit for consumption (7)

Definition: fit for consumption · anagram + outer letters

  • areca → AA its outer pair, the opening letter and the closing one, with everything between discarded
  • betel → BETEL five more letters, taken whole for their spelling rather than the palm they name

Areca and betel are the same nut chewed across South Asia, so the surface names one thing twice — and both words are there to be taken apart, not understood.

13 down RING FINGER

On which being in a union is often indicated (4,6)

Definition: On which being in a union is often indicated · cryptic definition

  • being in a union → the surface suggests a trade union and a membership card; the union the setter means is a marriage
  • often indicated → the sign is worn rather than announced, and 'often' concedes that not everybody wears one

The union is a marriage rather than a trade union, and the answer is a place rather than a thing: in Britain the fourth finger of the left hand, where the band goes.

14 down INGREDIENT

Component of engine dirt ruined (10)

Definition: Component · anagram

  • engine → ENGINE six letters for the pile, taken from the machine's name and not its meaning
  • dirt → DIRT the remaining four, which supply the second I and the only R

'Component of engine' reads as a car part and sends you hunting for pistons and valves. The words are only letters, and what they build belongs in a kitchen.

17 down THEOREM

Article more dodgy – it requires proof (7)

Definition: it requires proof · charade + anagram

  • Article → THE the definite article itself, spelled out in full
  • more → OREM the same four letters, resettled into another order

The dash works as an equals sign: everything to its left builds the word and everything to its right describes it. 'Article' is the grammarian's term, not a piece in a newspaper.

18 down CONFABS

Votes against suppressing super chats (7)

Definition: chats · container

  • Votes against → CONS in a debate the cons are the votes against, the other half of the pros
  • super → FAB fab is sixties British slang for marvellous, and 'super' is the same praise a generation earlier

'Suppressing super chats' reads as a moderator deleting messages, which is the wrong picture entirely: 'chats' is the definition, and 'super' is only there as dated slang.

19 down BANGKOK

Overseas capital gains initially put into Barclays? All right (7)

Definition: Overseas capital · container + charade + first letter

  • Barclays? → BANK Barclays is one of the British high-street banks, and the question mark admits it is only an example of the general word
  • gains → G just the initial letter of the word, which is all that is asked for
  • All right → OK OK is the everyday spelling of 'all right' used as assent

'Overseas capital gains' reads as a tax phrase, and that is the trap: the definition stops at 'capital', and 'gains' is in the clue for its first letter alone.

22 down SUEZ

For some, Jupiter rises in Middle Eastern port (4)

Definition: Middle Eastern port · reversal

  • For some, Jupiter → SUEZ the Romans' Jupiter is the Greeks' Zeus, and 'for some' marks that change of pantheon; those four letters are then read from the bottom up

Two names, one god: only the Greek one is any use here, and 'for some' is the setter's quiet signal to make the swap. The port sits at the southern mouth of the canal.

24 down ALAS

Sadly, dish of cold vegetables mostly thrown up (4)

Definition: Sadly · deletion + reversal

  • dish of cold vegetables → SALAD a cold dish of vegetables is a salad, the word taken whole before anything happens to it
  • mostly thrown up → ALAS the last letter comes off, and the four that remain are read from the foot of the column upwards

The surface is somebody being ill after a bad meal, and both of those words are instructions rather than description: one shortens, one turns. The definition is the single word out in front.
