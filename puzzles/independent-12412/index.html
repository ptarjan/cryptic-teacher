Across

1 across ATTRACT

Draw an area of land in hearing (7)

Definition: Draw · homophone

  • an area of land → ATTRACT a tract is a stretch of open country, and the article runs straight onto it when you say the phrase

'Draw' wants to be a sketch or a tied match; it is the magnet's sense here, and the rest of the clue is one spoken phrase rather than any spelling.

5 across FLAPPER

Fellow gets drink for each girl in 20s (7)

Definition: girl in 20s · charade

  • Fellow → F F is the standard abbreviation for fellow, as in Fellow of the Royal Society
  • drink → LAP a cat drinks by lapping, and to lap up liquid is to drink it
  • for each → PER per means for each, as in miles per hour

The 20s are the 1920s, not an age bracket: bobbed hair, dropped waists and the Charleston are what the definition is pointing at.

9 across IN PASSING

Carol touring Spain first, incidentally (2,7)

Definition: incidentally · anagram + charade

  • Spain → INPAS the country's five letters, shaken into a new order
  • Carol → SING to carol is to sing, especially outside somebody's front door in December

Carol reads as a woman's name in the surface and is a verb in the parse; 'first' is not part of the definition but the instruction that puts the shuffled country in front.

10 across IONIA

Roman couple stopping close to a classical region (5)

Definition: classical region · container + charade

  • Roman couple → II two of the Roman numeral for one, and they part company to let something in
  • close to → ON a village on the river is close to it, so the little preposition carries the sense of nearness
  • a → A the article is taken at face value, one letter lifted from the clue

The surface offers a pair of Roman tourists. The couple is really two numerals, and the innocuous 'close to' is doing a preposition's work.

11 across SWEET NOTHINGS

Teen songs with incoherent, lovey-dovey words (5,8)

Definition: lovey-dovey words · anagram

  • Teen songs with → SWEETNOTHINGS thirteen letters of fodder, counting the little word that looks like joinery

'Teen songs' and 'lovey-dovey' pull in the same direction, which is the disguise: one of those phrases is raw material and the other is the definition.

13 across NOTABLES

Schedules denial to be put before VIPs (8)

Definition: VIPs · charade

  • denial → NO the flattest two-letter refusal there is
  • Schedules → TABLES a timetable is a schedule, and the plural matches the plural in the clue

'Schedules' opens the clue and looks like the definition; the definition is the three letters at the far end, and the schedules are a synonym waiting to be found.

15 across DICTUM

Formal pronouncement by police, turning on corporation (6)

Definition: Formal pronouncement · reversal + charade

  • police → CID the CID is the detective branch of a British police force, and it faces the other way here
  • corporation → TUM corporation is an old joke word for a large stomach; this is the nursery word for the same thing

Nothing here is a business: 'corporation' is Victorian slang for a paunch, and the police force is only three letters facing backwards.

17 across EYELID

Shutter of Leica only opening to one diameter? Yes, as reported initially (6)

Definition: Shutter · charade + homophone + first letter

  • Yes → EYE aye is the vote in favour, and said aloud it is a part of the face
  • Leica → L the first letter of the camera's name
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one
  • diameter → D d is the standard symbol for diameter in geometry

Every scrap of this is camera talk hiding a single letter, and 'initially' is not an instruction to take initials: it says where the spoken piece belongs.

19 across RING-PULL

Close-knit criminals, perhaps, Force can part (4-4)

Definition: can part · charade

  • Close-knit criminals → RING an organised gang of crooks is named for the closed circle it forms
  • Force → PULL a tug towards you is one kind of force, the opposite of a push

'Force can part' reads as a police raid breaking the gang up. In the parse 'can' is a drinks can and 'part' is a component of one.

22 across LONG TIME NO SEE

On meeting old Les: 'Fantastic - we haven't met in yonks' (4,4,2,3)

Definition: we haven't met in yonks · anagram

  • On meeting → ONMEETING nine letters of fodder, straight from the clue
  • old → O O is the standard abbreviation for old
  • Les → LES the name joins the mix as its own three letters

The greeting two old friends exchange in the surface is spelled out by the letters of the meeting itself, with 'yonks' the British word for ages.

25 across CABER

Pole dancing bare at clubs (5)

Definition: Pole · anagram

  • bare → BARE four letters of fodder, straight from the clue
  • clubs → C C is the card table's abbreviation for clubs

'Pole dancing' is the trap the whole clue is built on: the two words belong to opposite halves of the parse, and the pole is a Highland Games one.

26 across SPEED TRAP

What lies in wait for those over 30, sometimes? (5,4)

Definition: What lies in wait for those over 30, sometimes? · cryptic definition

  • those over 30 → the surface offers an age bracket, everyone past their thirtieth birthday
  • lies in wait → meant literally: something concealed at the roadside, patient, watching for whoever comes past too quickly
  • sometimes → the qualifier concedes that not every stretch of road has one

British limits are posted in miles per hour and 30 is the standard one through a town, so 'over 30' describes a driver rather than a birthday.

27 across SLEDDED

Travelled down in snow in silver Dodge periodically used by Penny (7)

Definition: Travelled down in snow · charade + alternate letters

  • silver Dodge → SLEDDE every other letter of the two words, counting from the first
  • Penny → D d was the British penny before decimal currency, the abbreviation coming from the Roman denarius

'In silver Dodge' paints an American car and Penny sounds like a passenger; the car is a source of letters and Penny is a pre-1971 coin.

28 across TEASHOP

Dance with short guy in genteel establishment (7)

Definition: genteel establishment · deletion + charade

  • guy → TEAS to guy someone is to tease them, and that word turns up a letter short
  • Dance → HOP a hop is an informal dance in a village hall

'Guy' is a verb here rather than a bloke: to guy someone is to make fun of them, and that is the one step the surface works hardest to hide.

Down

1 down ANIL

Dye source in Manila envelope discarded (4)

Definition: Dye source · hidden word

  • Manila → ANIL four consecutive letters of the city's name, once the ends have gone

'Manila envelope' is a real thing in a stationery cupboard, which is why the setter puts those two words together; only the first of them is fodder.

2 down TYPESET

Extremists in Tory pressure group get ready to print (7)

Definition: get ready to print · charade + outer letters

  • Tory → TY the two ends of the party's name
  • pressure → PE the same treatment again, the letters at the front and back of the word
  • group → SET a set is a group of things taken together

'Tory pressure group' is one phrase in the surface and three separate jobs in the parse: two words plundered for their ends and a third swapped for a synonym.

3 down AISLE

The author will read out loud a passage (5)

Definition: a passage · homophone

  • The author will → AISLE the person writing the clue is 'I', and 'I will' shrinks to a single syllable

Setters call themselves 'the author', 'the setter' or 'this compiler' and mean the one letter I. Everything after that is just the contraction said aloud.

4 down THISTLES

Potentially injurious plants she tilts alarmingly (8)

Definition: Potentially injurious plants · anagram

  • she tilts → THISTLES eight letters of fodder, straight from the clue

'Potentially injurious' is a long way of saying prickly, and it reads like a warning label on the surface while the two little words beside it quietly hand over every letter.

5 down FOGBOW

Spooner's marshland enemy, that shines faintly in mist (6)

Definition: that shines faintly in mist · spoonerism

  • marshland → BOG waterlogged marshy ground, the first word of the pair
  • enemy → FOE an enemy is a foe, the second word of the pair
  • Spooner's marshland enemy → FOGBOW trade the opening consonants of those two words and write down what you hear

The thing itself is the pale, almost colourless cousin of a rainbow, seen when the sun catches fog: droplets too small to split the light properly.

6 down ALIGHTING

Getting down after trimming gas street lamps, possibly (9)

Definition: Getting down · charade + middle letter

  • gas → A the outer letters of the three-letter word come off and the middle one survives
  • street lamps → LIGHTING street lamps are one example of the general word for lamps at work

'Trimming gas street lamps' sounds like a lamplighter's job description, which is the disguise: the phrase splits in two and 'gas' is only three letters waiting to be pruned.

7 down PINE NUT

Re-order punnet containing single salad item (4,3)

Definition: salad item · anagram

  • punnet → PUNNET six letters of fodder; a punnet is the little basket soft fruit is sold in
  • single → I the Roman numeral for one, the crossword's usual single

The surface is a greengrocer rearranging his baskets. 'Single' is not part of what gets shuffled in the surface reading, but it is one more letter joining the mix.

8 down REASSEMBLE

About to suppress rising chaos in island and put things back together (10)

Definition: put things back together · charade + container + reversal

  • About → RE re is the 'about' at the head of a memo or an email subject line
  • island → ELBA Napoleon's island of exile, and it climbs the grid rather than descending it
  • chaos → MESS a mess is chaos, and this one climbs the grid too

Two separate things are read upwards here, one inside the other, and only then does the memo heading go on top of the pair.

12 down INTERLACES

Mixes up seasonal posts - openings go begging (10)

Definition: Mixes up · charade + deletion

  • seasonal → INTER winter is the season meant, and it turns up here without its W
  • posts → LACES posts in the sense of places, that word starting a letter late

'Mixes up' sits at the front looking exactly like an anagram instruction and is really the definition: nothing in this clue gets shuffled.

14 down BLISTERED

Berated retired surgical pioneer? (9)

Definition: Berated · container

  • retired → BED gone to bed, the word opening up after its first letter to make room
  • surgical pioneer → LISTER Joseph Lister brought antiseptics into the operating theatre in the 1860s

The question mark admits the surface joke about a surgeon who has hung up his scalpel; the wordplay wants the man himself, tucked up for the night.

16 down TINNIEST

Canned drinks in Perth? Tango's most cheap (8)

Definition: most cheap · charade

  • Canned drinks in Perth → TINNIES in Australian slang a tinnie is a can of beer, and Perth is the city on Australia's west coast
  • Tango → T Tango is T in the radio alphabet

There is a Perth in Scotland too, which is what the question mark is apologising for; the drinks are Australian and so is the slang.

18 down ENNOBLE

French maid turns up - the French turn to peer? (7)

Definition: turn to peer · reversal + charade

  • French maid → BONNE bonne is the French word for a housemaid, and it climbs the grid
  • the French → LE 'the' in French is le, in its masculine form

Both halves of the wordplay are in French, the maid and the article alike. The peer at the end is British and belongs to the definition.

20 down UNEARTH

Discover caribou near the hides (7)

Definition: Discover · hidden word

  • caribou near the → UNEARTH seven letters running consecutively across the joins between these three words

The surface is a wildlife hide with deer grazing in front of it, and a picture that good is exactly what stops you counting letters across the gaps.

21 down AMUSED

Victoria wasn't thought to follow Albert's lead (6)

Definition: Victoria wasn't · charade + first letter

  • Albert's → A the initial of the prince consort's name
  • thought → MUSED to muse is to think a matter over

The definition is a quotation: 'We are not amused' is the remark every schoolbook hangs on Queen Victoria, so what she was not is what the grid wants.

23 down SEDNA

Boosted range of remote orbiter (5)

Definition: remote orbiter · reversal

  • range → ANDES the South American mountain range, which then climbs the grid

'Range' is the mountain kind rather than the distance kind, which is a neat trap in a clue that has just said 'remote'.

24 down K-POP

Football stand filled with soft music (1-3)

Definition: music · container

  • Football stand → KOP the Kop is the steep terrace behind a goal at a British football ground, Liverpool's the most famous of them
  • soft → P p is piano in music, the direction to play softly

The whole surface is a Saturday afternoon in England and the definition arrives from Seoul, which is the jolt the clue is built for.
