Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,138

Across

1 across CLUB

Nightspot strike (4)

Definition: Nightspot · double definition

  • Nightspot → CLUB the place with a late licence, a dance floor and a queue outside — the noun sense
  • strike → CLUB to hit something hard with a heavy stick, which is the verb sense of the same four letters

Two words, nothing to shuffle and no indicator anywhere: a noun and a verb are simply set side by side, and the clue never says which of them is the definition.

3 across PRECOCIOUS

Affected to welcome company that's smart, irritatingly so (10)

Definition: smart, irritatingly so · container

  • Affected → PRECIOUS a precious manner is an affected one — mannered, too refined by half
  • company → CO CO is the tag that follows a firm's name, as in Smith & Co

The surface offers a smug host greeting a business partner. 'Affected' is not the feelings sense but the mannered one, and it is the word that has to open up.

9 across NORM

How to describe 'nudism' in the extreme? It's accepted behaviour (4)

Definition: accepted behaviour · charade + outer letters

  • 'nudism' → N the opening letter of the quoted word
  • How to describe → OR with two candidates and no way to choose, the honest description names one alternative or the other, and the joining word gets written down too
  • 'nudism' → M the letter the same quoted word finishes on

The word in quotation marks is not there to be defined, only measured. Name its two ends and the little word that joins any pair of alternatives sits between them.

10 across AFTER A SORT

Like some laundry ready to put away? Ish (5,1,4)

Definition: Like some laundry ready to put away? · double definition

  • Like some laundry ready to put away? → AFTER A SORT washing only reaches the drawer once somebody has divided it into piles, and this is the state it is in then
  • Ish → AFTER A SORT the suffix people tack onto a word to mean roughly, or up to a point

The question mark marks the joke: one reading needs the everyday noun for a sorting, the other is the idiom for approximately, and the three words are identical.

12 across LIQUID LUNCH

£51: midday meal that includes booze (6,5)

Definition: midday meal that includes booze · charade

  • 51 → LI Roman numerals: fifty is L and one is I
  • £ → QUID a quid is a pound in British slang, so the sign is read as the word you would say
  • midday meal → LUNCH the meal eaten in the middle of the day

£51 is written sign first and said number first — fifty-one quid — so the wordplay follows the ear. 'Midday meal' then earns its keep twice, once as letters and once inside the definition.

15 across STIPEND

A little bookie's tip endangered a little bit of money (7)

Definition: a little bit of money · hidden word

  • bookie's tip endangered → STIPEND the run begins on the last letter of the first word and stops four letters into the last, crossing both gaps

'Endangered' is a long word doing very little on the surface, and a setter reaching for one that ungainly is usually building a hiding place.

16 across ENNOBLE

Dignify the Spanish worthy across the Pyrénées heading west (7)

Definition: Dignify · charade + reversal

  • the Spanish → EL el is the masculine definite article in Spanish
  • worthy → BONNE the feminine of bon — a worthy cause is 'une bonne cause' in French

Two languages in one clue: the article is Spanish, the adjective French, and the mountain range in the middle is what tells you the second one has changed country.

17 across VIETNAM

Drive around volcano somewhere in Indochina (7)

Definition: somewhere in Indochina · container

  • Drive → VIM vim is energy or push — 'vim and vigour' — the noun, not the verb for steering
  • volcano → ETNA Etna is the Sicilian volcano, four convenient letters every setter keeps handy

The surface has somebody motoring past a mountain. 'Drive' is the trap: not the journey but the get-up-and-go.

19 across AMBIENT

Reformed inmate welcoming drop of bonhomie - it's all around (7)

Definition: all around · anagram + container + first letter

  • inmate → AMIENT six letters shaken into a new order and then opened up
  • drop of bonhomie → B bonhomie is French for hearty good humour, and only its opening letter is taken

'Reformed inmate' tells a whole story in two words, and both of them are machinery — the story is the disguise, and the dash hides the join to the definition.

20 across LEVI-STRAUSS

England cricket captain no more after charge announced, he wrote (4-7)

Definition: he wrote · charade + homophone

  • charge → LEVI a levy is a charge imposed by an authority, and said aloud it gives no hint of which spelling is meant
  • England cricket captain no more → STRAUSS Andrew Strauss captained England at cricket and retired years ago, which is what 'no more' marks

Two other men crowd in: the cricketer whose name is borrowed whole, and the maker of jeans the surname brings to mind. The one wanted is neither.

23 across TURNAROUND

Unexpected event: performer getting drinks for everyone (10)

Definition: Unexpected event · charade

  • performer → TURN in music hall each act on the bill was a turn, and the word covers the performer as well as the performance
  • drinks for everyone → A ROUND in a British pub one person buys a round, a drink for every member of the group at once

The surface is a night out, and the colon is the only thing dividing the definition from the wordplay — the first two words are all the definition there is.

24 across NOVA

On reflection, Bard's river is one full of light (4)

Definition: one full of light · reversal

  • Bard's river → AVON the river at Stratford, which is why Shakespeare is the Bard of Avon

'On reflection' opens the clue as though the setter were having second thoughts, and it is the instruction. The water in the surface is what makes the reflection plausible.

25 across HUMANITIES

Geography / history unit: shame I failed? (10)

Definition: Geography / history · anagram

  • unit: shame I → HUMANITIES ten letters in all, counting the single letter I as one of them

The slash is not punctuation to skip past: it separates two example subjects from the wordplay, and 'unit' joins the mixture rather than describing it.

26 across I SEE

Everyman - scenes - regularly falling out - that's understood (1,3)

Definition: that's understood · charade + alternate letters

  • Everyman → I the setter signs the puzzle with that name, and a setter writing about himself uses the first-person pronoun
  • scenes → SEE the first, third and fifth letters, once the other three have dropped away

The setter's own name in a clue is nearly always the pronoun rather than a person, and the dashes make three separate fragments look like one aside.

Down

1 down CONCLUSIVE

Vice-consul unwisely decisive (10)

Definition: decisive · anagram

  • Vice-consul → CONCLUSIVE ten letters go in and ten come out, with the hyphen counting for nothing

A vice-consul is a junior diplomat, and a job title dropped into a clue with no work to do on the surface is nearly always ten letters waiting to be shaken.

2 down UNREQUITED

Merrily reunited, embracing queen that's snubbed (10)

Definition: snubbed · anagram + container

  • reunited → UNREITED the eight letters re-ordered first, then opened up in the middle
  • queen → QU Qu. is the dictionary's short form for queen, the pair of letters rather than the single Q of chess

The surface is a cheerful family reunion with royalty present, and every word of it is machinery. Qu. for queen is the abbreviation a dictionary keeps and a solver forgets.

4 down RUFFLED

Disturbed, with hair on end, ran away (7)

Definition: Disturbed · charade + reversal

  • hair → FUR fur is the hair an animal wears, and it climbs the grid before it joins anything
  • ran away → FLED the past tense of flee is what somebody who ran away did

'Disturbed' sits at the head of the clue looking exactly like an anagram signal, and nothing beside it is shuffled: it is the definition.

5 down CLEANSE

Having entered church, tends to purify (7)

Definition: purify · container

  • church → CE CE is the Church of England, the standard shortening on any British form
  • tends → LEANS a mast that tends to starboard leans that way — the inclining sense, not the caring one

The surface has a devout gardener at work, and the trap is 'tends': not looking after anything, but sloping.

6 down CRASH AND BURN

King, seeing that, to pass waterway in fold (5,3,4)

Definition: fold · charade

  • King → CR CR is Charles Rex, the cipher of the current British king, the way ER stood for the late Queen
  • seeing that → AS as can mean since — 'as it's raining, we'll stay in'
  • to pass → HAND pass me the salt and hand me the salt are the same request
  • waterway → BURN a burn is a stream in Scotland and the north of England, as in Bannockburn

The surface is a monarch fording a river on his way somewhere. The definition is the last word, in the sense a poker player or a failing business uses it.

7 down OBOE

Cockney tramp that's found among the reeds (4)

Definition: found among the reeds · deletion + homophone

  • tramp → HOBO a hobo is a wanderer who rides the freight trains, the American word for a tramp
  • Cockney tramp → OBOE with the aitch gone, the two syllables that remain are said exactly the way the four-letter spelling is

'Reeds' pulls you to a riverbank, and the reeds meant are the strips of cane a woodwind player blows between. Everything else in the clue is pronunciation.

8 down SATS

Posed first of stiff challenges for schoolchildren (4)

Definition: challenges for schoolchildren · charade + first letter

  • Posed → SAT somebody who sat for a portrait posed for it
  • first of stiff → S the letter that word opens on

'Posed' reads as the verb for asking a question, which is how challenges get into the surface. The sense wanted is what a portrait sitter does.

11 down LIVE AND LEARN

Vandal all but leering unpleasantly: let's move on (4,3,5)

Definition: let's move on · anagram + deletion

  • Vandal → VANDAL six letters straight off the page, thrown into the mix untouched
  • all but leering → LEERIN six more, the whole of that word except the G at the end

'Vandal' and 'leering' build a nasty little scene and neither means anything: they are twelve letters. The colon is where the sentence turns into what people say after a mistake.

13 down OBSEQUIOUS

Bisque's prepared with coconut, regularly aiming to please (10)

Definition: aiming to please · anagram + alternate letters

  • Bisque's → BISQUES a bisque is a smooth shellfish soup, and the apostrophe-s hands over a seventh letter
  • coconut, regularly → OOU the second, fourth and sixth letters of the word, read in order

The surface is a recipe. The apostrophe-s is the giveaway: it is not a verb here at all, just one more letter for the mixture.

14 down DEATH STARE

A setter had to dance: it's not a good look (5,5)

Definition: not a good look · anagram

  • A setter had → DEATH STARE ten letters go in and every one of them is used once

'A setter' normally means the compiler himself, or a breed of dog. Here it is neither, just three short words holding ten letters.

18 down MOVE OUT

Emotionally affect topless hooligan in retreat (4,3)

Definition: retreat · charade + deletion

  • Emotionally affect → MOVE a film that moves you has worked on your feelings
  • hooligan → LOUT a lout is a loud, aggressive young man, and he arrives here without his first letter

A definition-shaped phrase opens the clue, which is where a definition usually sits — and here those two words are the wordplay, with the definition left alone at the end.

19 down ABSENCE

Lack awareness of stomach muscles, did you say? (7)

Definition: Lack · charade + homophone

  • stomach muscles → ABS abs is the gym shorthand for the abdominal muscles
  • awareness → ENCE a sense of something is an awareness of it, and run straight on from the piece before it the S is heard only once

Say the two pieces one after another and the join vanishes: a single S does the work of two, which is why the spelling that comes out looks nothing like the parts.

21 down ETCH

Leaving France, get to make lasting impression (4)

Definition: make lasting impression · deletion

  • get → FETCH to fetch something is to go and get it
  • France → F F is the international code for France, the letter on the back of French cars

'Leaving France' opens the clue like the start of a travel story and is pure instruction; the impression meant is bitten into metal, not made on a person.

22 down PRIM

Primarily punctilious, reserved in manner? (4)

Definition: punctilious, reserved in manner? · &lit + first letters

  • Primarily punctilious, reserved in manner → PRIM primarily means in the first place, so each of the four words that follow hands over the letter it begins with, in the order printed

An &lit: read once, the whole sentence — first word included — describes a fussily correct sort of person; read again, four of its words give up their opening letters and nothing is left over.
