Across

9 across ESPERANTO

2 sat wagging tongue (9)

Definition: tongue · anagram

A tongue is a language, and Esperanto is the invented one, so the definition uses the broad sense rather than the thing in your mouth.

  • 2 → OPENER the numeral sends you to the answer at 2 down, which supplies six of the letters
  • sat → SAT three more letters, taken as written
  • wagging — A wagging tongue swings from side to side, and letters that will not stay still are letters being reordered.

Two people sitting there with their tongues wagging.

A bare number in a Guardian clue points at another entry, so this one cannot be started until 2 down is in — and it reads as 'two sat', which is the joke.

10 across NAIRA

West African rhino has Unitarian retreating (5)

Definition: West African rhino · hidden word + reversal

Rhino is old British slang for cash, and the naira is Nigeria's currency, so the definition asks for West African money.

  • Unitarian → ARIAN five consecutive letters at the tail of the word, waiting to be turned round
  • has — 'Has' is the loosest word here: it says the money holds these letters, with the word order stretched to keep the surface upright.
  • retreating — A retreat is a going back the way you came, and in an across entry that means right to left.

A rhino in West Africa backing away from a passing Unitarian.

'Rhino' for money is Victorian slang the crossword keeps alive, and the surface protects it by making the animal West African too.

11 across DENIM

Material taken from ground from the east (5)

Definition: Material · reversal

Denim is the heavy cotton twill jeans are cut from, so 'material' is a plain fabric synonym with no colour in it.

  • taken from ground → MINED what has been dug out of the ground has been mined
  • from the east — Something coming from the east travels westwards, and in an across entry that is right to left.

Ore dug out of the ground somewhere east of here.

The word 'from' turns up twice doing different jobs — once as part of the digging, once as the instruction — which is what makes the turn hard to spot.

12 across EDELWEISS

White flower displayed by Wise when playing with Leeds (9)

Definition: White flower · anagram

Edelweiss is the woolly white daisy of the high Alps, so the definition names it by its colour and its kind.

  • Wise → WISE anagram fodder, four letters
  • Leeds → LEEDS anagram fodder, five more
  • when playing — Letters caught at play are letters off duty, and off-duty letters do not stay in the positions they were given.

A footballer called Wise showing off a bit of skill in a Leeds shirt.

Only the first two words have anything to do with botany; the rest reads as a team sheet, which is where nine letters sit in plain view.

13 across BAROQUE

Extravagantly ornate vessel loaded with cargo at the back (7)

Definition: Extravagantly ornate · container + last letter

Baroque names the heavily decorated seventeenth-century style, and by extension anything piled with more ornament than it needs.

  • vessel → BARQUE a barque is a sailing ship of three or more masts
  • cargo at the back → O the last letter of the word 'cargo'
  • loaded with — A vessel loaded with cargo has it stowed inside, so the letter goes within the other word.
  • at the back — The back of a word is its final letter, which is the one taken here.

A ship being loaded up, with the cargo stowed towards the stern.

'Vessel' is a ship here rather than the jar or the blood vessel setters also use, and the dockside surface keeps you well away from any gilded chapel.

14 across INSISTS

Popular sibling without hesitation beginning to suggest demands (7)

Definition: demands · charade + deletion + first letter

To insist on something is to demand it, and both verbs sit in the same third-person form here, so the swap is exact.

  • Popular → IN in = popular, fashionable
  • sibling → SISTER a sibling can be a sister
  • without hesitation → SIST ER is the written form of a hesitant noise, and it drops out of the sister
  • beginning to suggest → S the opening letter of 'suggest'
  • without — 'Without' means lacking rather than outside, so what it names is taken out of the word before it.
  • beginning to — The beginning of a word is its opening letter, and that is all this takes.

A well-liked brother or sister starting, without any hesitation, to make demands.

ER is the crossword's spelling of a hesitation, and nothing here announces a deletion — 'without hesitation' is exactly how you would describe someone speaking up.

17 across IMBUE

Hat is miserable, lacking will ultimately to inspire (5)

Definition: inspire · charade + deletion + last letter

To imbue a person with an idea is to fill them with it, which is what inspiring someone does, and both are transitive verbs.

  • Hat is → IM Hat is the setter of this puzzle, so 'Hat is' can be read as I'm
  • miserable → BLUE blue = miserable, down in the mouth
  • will ultimately → L the last letter of 'will', and this is the letter that goes missing
  • lacking — What is lacking is missing, so the letter named next never makes it into the assembly.
  • ultimately — 'Ultimately' points at the very end of the word before it, meaning its final letter.

The setter feeling low and short of willpower.

Setters sign their grids and then walk into their own clues: Hat is the byline over this puzzle, so 'Hat is' is the setter saying I'm.

19 across FEE

Regularly offered payment (3)

Definition: payment · alternate letters

A fee is what a professional charges for a service — a solicitor's or a doctor's payment, agreed in advance rather than earned by the hour.

  • offered → FEE the second, fourth and sixth letters of the word, read in order
  • Regularly — Doing something regularly is doing it at a fixed interval, so letters are taken at every second position.

A payment that gets offered on a regular basis.

Three-letter answers give you almost nothing to aim at, so the instruction has to be found first — and only one word here is long enough to hide a run of alternate letters.

20 across EGYPT

Torture admitted by alien nation (5)

Definition: nation · container

Egypt is a country, so 'nation' is about as plain a synonym as a definition gets — the work is in seeing where the definition starts.

  • alien → ET ET, the film's extra-terrestrial, is the crossword's standard alien
  • Torture → GYP gyp is British for sharp pain — a bad knee gives you gyp
  • admitted by — To admit someone is to let them in, so the alien takes the other letters inside itself.

An alien owning up to torture.

Nothing in the surface is remotely geographical, which is why the last word reads as part of the story rather than as the definition standing on its own.

21 across PIRANHA

Quiet country hotel, first-class fish (7)

Definition: fish · charade

The piranha is the South American river fish with the notorious teeth, so 'fish' defines it by the family it belongs to.

  • Quiet → P p is piano, the musical direction to play quietly
  • country → IRAN a country, taken whole
  • hotel → H H is hotel in the NATO alphabet
  • first-class → A first-class work is graded A

A quiet, first-class country hotel.

Three single letters come from standing conventions and the fourth piece is a country sitting in plain sight, while the surface reads as a line from a hotel review.

22 across CRUMBLE

Last slice of Arctic roll for pud (7)

Definition: pud · charade + last letter

A crumble is baked fruit under a rubbly flour-and-butter topping, and pud is the British shorthand for the sweet course.

  • Last slice of Arctic → C the closing letter of the word 'Arctic'
  • roll → RUMBLE thunder rolls, that is, it rumbles
  • Last slice of — A slice off the end of a word is one letter, and the end meant here is the far one.

The last slice of an Arctic roll, served up for pudding.

An Arctic roll is a British ice-cream pudding in a sponge sleeve, so the surface offers one dessert while the definition quietly asks for another.

24 across GREEN BEAN

Inexperienced head that’s likely to get chopped (5,4)

Definition: that’s likely to get chopped · charade

A green bean is topped, tailed and chopped before it goes anywhere near a pan, so the definition names the vegetable by what the cook does to it.

  • Inexperienced → GREEN green = raw, new to the job
  • head → BEAN bean is slang for the head, as in 'use your bean'

A new boss who looks likely to be sacked before long.

The definition is the whole second half and it works by function rather than synonym: getting the chop is a sacking on the surface and a chopping board underneath.

26 across ATLAS

Broad-shouldered bloke getting time off finally (5)

Definition: Broad-shouldered bloke · deletion

Atlas is the Titan condemned to hold up the sky, so any man built to carry a weight on his shoulders can be called one.

  • finally → ATLAST finally can be said as 'at last', which is where the letters come from
  • time → T T is the abbreviation for time, and this is the letter taken away
  • off — 'Off' takes away what has just been named, so the letter it follows never appears.

A big bloke finally getting some time off work.

'Finally' is neither definition nor instruction but a phrase to be spelled out, and the Titan carrying the heavens is the broad-shouldered bloke of the first two words.

28 across SOLAR

Former garret, very large area, right? (5)

Definition: Former garret · charade

A solar was the private upper room of a medieval house, so 'former garret' asks for a room at the top in a word that is now only historical.

  • very → SO so = very, as in 'so kind of you'
  • large → L L is the clothing size
  • area → A standard abbreviation
  • right → R standard abbreviation

Somebody running through the details of an old attic room: very large, on the right.

'Former' is a label rather than a piece of wordplay: a solar is the upper chamber of a medieval house, a word you meet in castles and never in an estate agent's window.

29 across CHOCOLATE

Teach cool new Topic? (9)

Definition: Topic · anagram

A Topic is a British hazelnut bar, so the definition hands over one brand and asks for the stuff it is made of — which the question mark flags.

  • Teach cool → TEACHCOOL anagram fodder, nine letters
  • new — Something new is made afresh out of what was already there, and here that means the same letters in another order.

A teacher trying to make a subject sound cool and new.

Topic is the British chocolate bar advertised as having a hazelnut in every bite, and the question mark is the setter owning up: the definition is one example of the answer, not a synonym for it.

Down

1 down TEND

Watch over third of Penelope Cruz covers (4)

Definition: Watch over · container + third letter

To tend someone is to watch over them, the nursing sense of the verb rather than the tending that means to lean a certain way.

  • Cruz → TED the Cruz wanted is Ted, the Texas senator, not the actor the surface offers
  • third of Penelope → N the third letter of the name, counting from the front
  • third of — 'Third of' counts along the letters of the name beside it and stops on the third one.
  • covers — A cover lies over a thing on both sides, so the name named next is wrapped round the letter.

A film star's magazine covers, with somebody keeping an eye on them.

Two Cruzes are in play and the clue leans on the wrong one: Penelope is there for the surface, Ted is there for his letters.

2 down OPENER

Batter, work Frenchman over (6)

Definition: Batter · charade + reversal

An opener in cricket is one of the two batters who go in first, so 'batter' names the answer by the job it does at the crease.

  • work → OP op is a work, as in a composer's opus number
  • Frenchman over → ENER the French name RENE, written back to front
  • over — Turning a thing over shows you its other side, and a name turned over is written from its far end back.

An order to a heavy: batter the Frenchman, then work him over.

The whole sentence sounds like a beating, and the comma after the first word is the only sign that the beating is not the definition. Cricket is nowhere in the surface.

3 down DRAMA QUEEN

Run out of tent seized by darn crazy hysteric (5,5)

Definition: hysteric · anagram + container + deletion

A drama queen makes a crisis out of nothing, which is what 'hysteric' means as a noun for a person rather than as a medical term.

  • darn crazy → DRAN the four letters of 'darn' reordered, and they open up round the middle piece
  • Run out of tent → MAQUEE the tent is a MARQUEE, and the R of run comes out of it
  • Run out of — R is the scorer's letter for a run, and 'out of' takes it away from the word that follows.
  • seized by — What seizes something grabs it and holds it, so the tent ends up gripped inside the other letters.
  • crazy — Something crazy will not keep to the order it was given in, and neither will these four letters.

A camper left with no tent, because some maddened hysteric has grabbed it.

R for run comes off a cricket scorecard, and 'run out of' is doing an instruction's work while sounding like ordinary English about a shortage.

4 down ANNEXE

Military standard unveiled by East Wing (6)

Definition: Wing · charade + middle letters

A wing of a house is a section built out from the side of it, which is exactly what an annexe is.

  • Military standard unveiled → ANNE a military standard is a BANNER, here with the outer letters taken off it
  • by → X by is the multiplication sign, as in a plank two by four
  • East → E the compass abbreviation
  • unveiled — A veil is a covering, so the flag unveiled loses the letters covering it at either end.

A flag being unveiled at a ceremony run by the East Wing.

'By' looks like the joinery that holds a charade together and is a letter in its own right: two by four is 2 x 4. Miss that and the wordplay will not add up.

5 down YOSEMITE

The Times: Oysters Turned Up Outside Sam’s Home (8)

Definition: Sam’s Home · hidden word + reversal

Yosemite Sam of the Looney Tunes cartoons takes his name from the California national park, so the definition asks for the place behind the name.

  • The Times: Oysters → ETIMESOY eight consecutive letters starting on the last letter of the first word and stopping two into the last, running the wrong way
  • Turned Up — Turned up, and a down entry read from the bottom of the grid upwards runs against the letters as printed.
  • Outside — 'Outside' points at letters lying across the outer edges of the words rather than tucked away inside any one of them.

A newspaper item about oysters, dug up from somewhere near Sam's house.

Capital letters on every word is Hat's camouflage: they make the clue look like a headline so that the join between 'The' and 'Times' never gets read as a join. Yosemite Sam is the cartoon gunslinger.

6 down ANEW

A congested city rises again (4)

Definition: again · charade + reversal

Anew means over again, from the start, and it is the adverb that drops straight into 'begin again' without changing the sense.

  • A → A taken as it stands
  • congested city rises → NEW a WEN is an overgrown city, and it goes up the grid rather than down it
  • rises — What rises goes up, and in a down entry that means the letters are read from the bottom towards the top.

A city choked with traffic picking itself up and starting over.

WEN is worth owning: William Cobbett called London the Great Wen, a wen being a swelling on the skin, and setters have used it for an overgrown city ever since.

7 down NIJINSKY

Judi exhausted, carried by terribly skinny chap in tights (8)

Definition: chap in tights · anagram + container + outer letters

Vaslav Nijinsky was the Ballets Russes star, so 'chap in tights' is a male ballet dancer described by his costume rather than his art.

  • terribly skinny → NINSKY the six letters of 'skinny' rearranged, leaving a gap two letters in
  • Judi exhausted → JI the first and last letters of the name, with everything between them gone
  • exhausted — Something exhausted has had everything drained out of it, so the name keeps only the letters at its two ends.
  • carried by — What is carried travels inside its carrier, so this pair of letters sits within the shuffled ones.
  • terribly — Done terribly, and letters handled that badly do not end up in the order they were given.

An exhausted Judi being carried along by a very thin man in tights.

Nijinsky leapt for Diaghilev's Ballets Russes, and the clue dresses him down to a costume; the surface hands you Dame Judi so that 'Judi' reads as a person rather than four letters.

8 down DAIS

Welshman’s platform (4)

Definition: platform · charade

A dais is the low platform a speaker or a top table stands on, raised just enough to be seen from the back of the room.

  • Welshman → DAI Dai, short for Dafydd, is the crossword's stock Welshman
  • ’s → S the possessive ending is taken as a letter of the answer

A Welshman with a platform to speak from.

Two words, and one of them is an apostrophe: the possessive s is not grammar joining the halves of the clue but the last letter of the answer.

13 down BLIMP

Low character, Liberal, one probing British parliamentarian (5)

Definition: Low character · charade + container

Colonel Blimp is the pompous reactionary drawn by David Low, so 'Low character' means a character belonging to the cartoonist Low.

  • British → B standard abbreviation
  • Liberal → L standard abbreviation, as on a ballot paper
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one
  • parliamentarian → MP a member of parliament
  • probing — A probe goes into the thing it examines, so these letters push in among the others rather than sitting beside them.

Somebody of low character digging into the affairs of a British MP.

Low is a surname, not an adjective: David Low drew Colonel Blimp for the papers in the 1930s, and the walrus-moustached old reactionary has been a byword since.

15 down SPECULATOR

Vegas’s last crook gambler (10)

Definition: gambler · charade + last letter

A speculator gambles on shares, land or currency rather than cards, so 'gambler' catches the money-market sense of the word.

  • Vegas’s last → S the closing letter of the city's name
  • crook → PECULATOR to peculate is to embezzle money you were trusted with, so a peculator is a crook
  • last — 'Last' asks for the final letter of the word standing in front of it, and nothing else of it.

The last crook to get out of Las Vegas.

Peculator is the rare word to learn — an embezzler — and the Vegas surface is built so that every other word is already about money and cheating.

16 down SATIE

French barman posed, tied, naked (5)

Definition: French barman · charade + middle letters

Music is written in bars, so a composer can be called a barman, and the French one the letters build is Erik Satie.

  • posed → SAT to pose for a portrait is to sit for it
  • tied, naked → IE the word 'tied' with its outer letters stripped off
  • naked — Something naked has lost what covered it, so the word beside it gives up the letters at both ends.

A French barman who posed for a photograph tied up and with nothing on.

'Barman' is the joke and the definition at once: not a man behind a bar but a man who works in bars, which is what a composer does all day.

18 down BORDELLO

Nick rolled over, rolled over topless in brothel (8)

Definition: brothel · charade + reversal + deletion

A bordello is a brothel, the borrowed Italian word for the same house, which is why the definition can be the blunt English one.

  • Nick rolled over → BOR to nick is to ROB, and here it lands the other way up
  • rolled over topless → DELLO the word 'rolled' loses the letter at its top and then turns over
  • rolled over — Rolling a thing over lands it the other way up, and the clue does it twice — the second time to the word 'rolled' itself.
  • topless — The top of a down entry is where it starts, so a topless word has lost its first letter.

Nick turning over in bed, topless, in a brothel.

The same two words appear twice a comma apart and mean different things: the first pair is an instruction, the second is a word to be operated on with its own instruction attached.

19 down FLAMENCO

Corporal perhaps put on fine, shiny material for dance (8)

Definition: dance · charade

Flamenco is the Andalusian dance of stamping heels and clapped rhythm, so the bare word 'dance' is a broad but honest synonym.

  • fine → F F is the pencil grade meaning fine
  • shiny material → LAME lame is cloth woven through with metallic thread
  • Corporal perhaps → NCO a corporal is one rank of non-commissioned officer, and 'perhaps' flags the example
  • put on — 'Put on' is loose about position: it adds the officer to the other pieces without saying which end he goes.

A corporal turned out in something fine and shiny for a dance.

'Corporal perhaps' points the opposite way from most definitions by example: the rank is the instance and the abbreviation for the category is what you write down.

22 down CONDOR

Republican supporting Tory Party high flier (6)

Definition: high flier · charade

The condor is the Andean vulture that soars higher than almost any bird, so 'high flier' is meant literally rather than as the ambitious-person idiom.

  • Tory → CON Con is how the Conservative party appears on a British ballot paper
  • Party → DO a do is a party, a bash
  • Republican → R standard abbreviation
  • supporting — What supports a thing sits underneath it, and in a down entry underneath means later in the answer.

An American Republican backing the Tory party.

Both political words are letters rather than politics, and 'Party' with its capital is the one to distrust: it is the party you go to, not the party you vote for.

23 down BILBAO

£1 filled Chinese bun pintxos sold here (6)

Definition: pintxos sold here · container

Pintxos are Basque bar snacks pinned to a slice of bread, and Bilbao is the largest city of the Basque Country, where they are sold everywhere.

  • Chinese bun → BAO a bao is the steamed Chinese bun
  • £1 → ILB read the other way round as 1lb: I for one and lb for pound
  • filled — 'Filled' does double duty: it describes the bun on the surface and puts one set of letters inside another underneath.

A filled Chinese bun going for a pound, next to the bar snacks.

Both pounds in this clue are the same word underneath — £ and lb each come from the Latin libra — which is the licence for reading £1 as 1lb.

24 down GUST

Stomach punched by second blow (4)

Definition: blow · container

A gust is a blow of wind, using the noun sense of a sudden rush of air rather than the punch the surface has in mind.

  • Stomach → GUT the gut is the stomach
  • second → S standard abbreviation for a second of time
  • punched by — A punch lands in the middle of what it hits, so the letter goes inside rather than alongside.

Someone taking a second punch to the stomach.

'Blow' is the trap: the surface has just landed a punch, and the definition means a rush of wind.

25 down NORM

Average type, and not married (4)

Definition: Average type · charade

A norm is the standard that other things get measured against, so 'average type' asks for the ordinary run of things rather than a person.

  • and not → NOR nor is the one word that means exactly 'and not'
  • married → M standard abbreviation, as on a form

An average sort of person, and unmarried with it.

'And not' looks like joinery or a negative instruction and is neither: it is a conjunction being defined, letter for letter, by its own meaning.

27 down SMEW

For starters, some marinated eel with duck (4)

Definition: duck · first letters

The smew is a small white sawbill duck that winters on northern European lakes, so 'duck' names it by its family.

  • some marinated eel with → SMEW the opening letters of four consecutive words, taken in order
  • For starters — 'For starters' asks for the letters that start the words while pretending to order the first course.

A starter of marinated eel, with duck to follow.

The menu runs top to bottom and 'with' is the word to watch: it looks like the joinery between two courses and is one of the four words donating a letter.
