Guardian Quiptic Crossword No 1,394

Across

1 across CHAIR

Check appearance for president (5)

Definition: president · charade

  • Check → CH ch is how a chess scoresheet writes check
  • appearance → AIR an air is the appearance someone gives off

Two pieces of standing vocabulary: ch from the chess board, and air for the manner a person presents. The surface pretends to be about vetting a candidate.

4 across SIDESTEP

Avoid part of process on border (8)

Definition: Avoid · charade

  • border → SIDE a border is the side of something, its edge
  • part of process → STEP a process is made of steps

In an across clue 'on' means 'after', so the step goes on the side and the side is written first. Down clues use the same word the other way round.

8 across ALL TOGETHER NOW

Every one gathered with present get invitation to join in (3,8,3)

Definition: invitation to join in · charade

  • Every one → ALL every one of them = all
  • gathered → TOGETHER gathered = together, in one place
  • present → NOW the present is now

Three plain synonyms in a row; the difficulty is that the surface reads as a sentence about a party. 'Present' as a time rather than a gift is the pivot.

10 across HOW'S THAT

Is it ok to ask for a repeat? (4,4)

Definition: Is it ok · double definition

  • Is it ok → HOWSTHAT asking whether what you have just done will do
  • ask for a repeat → HOWSTHAT the same words also mean 'say that again'

One question with two jobs: it asks for approval, and it asks the speaker to go back. Cricketers use a third sense, appealing to the umpire.

11 across AT REST

Relaxed as Republican enters a challenge (2,4)

Definition: Relaxed · container

  • a challenge → ATEST a challenge is a test, with its 'a' in front — the wrapper
  • Republican → R R = Republican, as printed beside a US candidate's name

R for Republican, D for Democrat: standard shorthand whenever a clue turns American. Note the answer breaks 2,4 while the wordplay breaks elsewhere.

12 across ABDUCTING

Carrying off a second rate set of pipes (9)

Definition: Carrying off · charade

  • a → A lifted straight into the answer
  • second rate → B second-rate is grade B, the mark below A
  • set of pipes → DUCTING ducting is the run of pipes in a building

'Second rate' shrinking to a single letter is the piece that hides best here. The surface sells you plumbing; the answer is a kidnapping.

15 across BIG UP

Exaggerate large raise (3,2)

Definition: Exaggerate · charade

  • large → BIG large = big
  • raise → UP to up something is to raise it — up the ante

Two everyday words nose to tail. The answer is modern slang — to big someone up is to talk them up — which lets the surface pose as a pay rise.

17 across RUN-IN

Dispute arrest (3-2)

Definition: Dispute · double definition

  • Dispute → RUNIN a slang term for a quarrel or confrontation
  • arrest → RUNIN to run someone in is police slang for taking them into custody

A two-word clue is nearly always two definitions. Here one sense is a noun and the other a verb, and the (3-2) enumeration quietly settles the hyphen.

18 across GLIDE PATH

Piglet had disrupted aircraft’s landing approach (5,4)

Definition: aircraft’s landing approach · anagram

  • Piglet had → GLIDEPATH an anagram of PIGLET HAD, 'disrupted'

The capital P is a setter's licence: Piglet reads as Pooh's friend and is only material. Nine letters is a big anagram, so write them out before you start.

19 across EYELID

Almost cry out during festival – it obscures your vision (6)

Definition: it obscures your vision · container + deletion

  • festival → EID Eid, the Muslim festival — the wrapper
  • cry out → YEL to cry out is to yell; 'Almost' lops the last letter off

Eid is crossword furniture worth banking, and 'almost' always takes the last letter off, never the first. The dash keeps the definition clear of the wordplay.

21 across LEAVE OFF

Holiday cancelled? You must be joking (5,3)

Definition: You must be joking · charade

  • Holiday → LEAVE leave is time off work — annual leave, compassionate leave
  • cancelled → OFF an event that is off has been cancelled

The answer is an exclamation, which is why the definition is a whole sentence rather than a synonym. Both halves are ordinary words in ordinary senses.

24 across ENGAGEMENT RING

Call after date – it’s a sign of serious commitment (10,4)

Definition: a sign of serious commitment · charade

  • date → ENGAGEMENT an engagement is an appointment in the diary
  • Call → RING to call someone on the phone is to ring them

'After' sends the ring to the back even though 'Call' opens the clue — the first thing you read is the last thing you write.

25 across BEDSOCKS

Deck boss turned out in nightwear (8)

Definition: nightwear · anagram

  • Deck boss → BEDSOCKS an anagram of DECK BOSS, 'turned out'

'Turned out' does double duty on the surface — dressed, and rearranged — while the deck boss, a bosun, never reaches the answer at all.

26 across SWEET

Tart ironically described like this on menu (5)

Definition: Tart ironically described like this on menu · cryptic definition

  • Tart → not the sharpness the word first offers, nor the slang for a person: the pastry, which is the thing a menu is listing
  • ironically described like this on menu → a British menu heads the pudding course with a word naming the very quality a tart hasn't got

A cryptic definition: the menu's word for the pudding course names the one flavour a tart is not, and 'ironically' is the setter owning up to the joke.

Down

1 down CRASH BARRIER

Spooner lecturing arrogant airline – it can reduce accidents (5,7)

Definition: it can reduce accidents · spoonerism

  • arrogant → CRASH arrogant = brash, and Spooner hands its B over for a C
  • airline → BARRIER an airline is a carrier, and it takes the B that BRASH gave up

A spoonerism swaps the opening sounds of two words — brash carrier is what you say before the swap. The Reverend's name is the standing signal to do it.

2 down ALLOWED IN

Admitted a lone wild drunk (7,2)

Definition: Admitted · anagram

  • a lone wild → ALLOWEDIN an anagram of A LONE WILD, stirred by 'drunk'

'Drunk' is doing the shuffling rather than describing the loner — the surface would much rather you read it as a sad little scene at a bar.

3 down ROOST

Southern part of base provides resting area (5)

Definition: resting area · container

  • base → ROOT the root of something is its base — the wrapper
  • Southern → S S = Southern, as on a compass or a map

'Base' means root here, not the bottom of anything, while the surface offers an army camp. Compass letters turn up in a clue or two most days.

4 down SEE-SAWING

Notice cutting type of irresolute behaviour (3-6)

Definition: type of irresolute behaviour · charade

  • Notice → SEE to notice something is to see it
  • cutting → SAWING cutting with a saw

The (3-6) enumeration announces a hyphen, which is the hint that the two halves stay separate words. The surface pretends a notice is being trimmed.

5 down DOHA

Half of dons hate city (4)

Definition: city · charade + first letters

  • dons → DO the front half of DOns
  • hate → HA the front half of HAte

'Half of' governs both words, not just the one it stands in front of. One indicator doing two jobs is most of the difficulty in a four-letter clue.

6 down SCRUTABLE

Easy to read report of cheat on board (9)

Definition: Easy to read · charade + homophone

  • report of cheat → SCRU to screw someone is to cheat them
  • board → TABLE board = table, as in bed and board

In a down clue one piece sits 'on' another, so the sound goes above. You will have met the answer far more often wearing its IN- prefix.

7 down ERODE

Weirdo’s diet regularly includes crumble (5)

Definition: crumble · alternate letters

  • Weirdo’s diet → ERODE the even letters of wEiRdO’s DiEt, taken 'regularly'

'Regularly' asks for every other letter — the even ones here. Count through the letters and ignore the apostrophe: punctuation never joins in.

9 down STOP THE FIGHT

Finish quarrel by counting to ten? (4,3,5)

Definition: Finish quarrel · double definition

  • Finish quarrel → STOPTHEFIGHT the plain reading: bring the row to an end
  • counting to ten? → STOPTHEFIGHT the referee's count over a floored boxer — reach ten and the bout is over

The clue splits after 'quarrel'. The question mark admits the second half is playful: a referee's ten-count is not a synonym so much as the act itself.

13 down CANNIBALS

Writer blocking waterways? They’ll have him for breakfast (9)

Definition: They’ll have him for breakfast · container

  • waterways → CANALS canals are man-made waterways — the wrapper
  • Writer → NIB the nib is the part of a pen that does the writing

'Writer' meaning the pen's nib rather than the person is the step that unlocks it, and the question mark warns you the waterways are not really blocked.

14 down GUILELESS

I see gulls circling? That’s natural (9)

Definition: natural · anagram

  • I see gulls → GUILELESS an anagram of I SEE GULLS, set 'circling'

'Circling' does the shuffling while the surface has birds wheeling overhead. 'Natural' here means artless and unaffected — nothing to do with nature.

16 down GLAMORISE

Add some sparkle to gem sailor messed with (9)

Definition: Add some sparkle to · anagram

  • gem sailor → GLAMORISE an anagram of GEM SAILOR, 'messed with'

The British -ISE spelling is what makes the letters come out; an American -IZE would leave you a letter short. The surface has a sailor ruining a jewel.

20 down ERNIE

Wise man spots current in Irish river (5)

Definition: Wise man · container

  • Irish river → ERNE the Erne runs through Fermanagh and Donegal — the wrapper
  • current → I I is the physicist's symbol for electric current

'Wise man' is not a sage but a man called Wise — the shorter half of Morecambe and Wise. I for current and the Erne are both standing crossword furniture.

22 down VOTES

Partially apologise to Vatican over ballots (5)

Definition: ballots · hidden word + reversal

  • apologise to Vatican → VOTES apologiSE TO Vatican holds SETOV, which 'over' turns round

Two instructions stacked: take part of the text, then turn what you took round. The run starts in the middle of a word, which is what makes it hard to spot.

23 down MERC

Starts to manufacture exceptionally reliable cable car (4)

Definition: car · first letters

  • manufacture exceptionally reliable cable → MERC the first letter of each of the four words

'Starts to' flags initials, and the four words behind it are chosen so the surface can talk about a cable car — a vehicle the answer has nothing to do with.
