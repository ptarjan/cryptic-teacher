Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,879

Across

1 across SMALL FORTUNE

Sergeant major really in favour of cough sweet gets mint (5,7)

Definition: mint · charade

  • Sergeant major → SM SM is the army's shorthand for the rank, the two initials on a duty roster
  • really in favour → ALLFOR somebody really in favour of a plan is all for it, six letters that arrive as one phrase
  • cough sweet → TUNE Tunes were a British brand of menthol lozenge, sold in a roll for a blocked nose

The surface keeps you in the sweetshop right to the last word, and that last word is the definition: not the herb or the confectionery but the money sense.

9 across DINGO

Dog performing with duck coming last? (5)

Definition: Dog · deletion + charade

  • performing → DING to be performing is to be doing, and these four letters are what is left when the bird's is taken out
  • duck → O a batsman out for no runs has scored a duck, and no runs is written as a nought

'Performing with duck' reads as a circus act. The duck is cricket's nought, and the question mark is there because the letter will not stay where its own word puts it.

10 across SAFETY PIN

Fixing device you can't see the point of? (6,3)

Definition: Fixing device · cryptic definition

  • Fixing device → read straight: a small thing for holding two pieces of fabric together, the sort that lives in a sewing tin
  • you can't see the point of? → the surface says pointless; the setter means the sharp end really is out of sight, folded away under its little metal clasp

There are no letters to collect here. The clue is one joke told twice over: no point as futility, and a point genuinely hidden so that it cannot prick anybody.

11 across POSTERIOR

Is trooper on manoeuvres bringing up the rear? (9)

Definition: bringing up the rear · anagram

  • Is trooper → ISTROOPER nine letters of raw material: the soldier is not a soldier here, only a supply of letters

The definition is adjectival, not the polite noun for a backside. Whatever brings up the rear is the hindmost of its line, and that is the sense wanted.

12 across RERUN

Programme you might have seen about sport, endless one (5)

Definition: Programme you might have seen · charade + middle letter

  • about → RE RE is the shorthand at the head of a memo, meaning 'about'
  • sport → RU RU is rugby union, one of the games a British crossword shortens to two letters
  • endless one → N strip a letter off each end of the number and a single one survives

The tempting move is to dock the number at the tail and get ON. Endless means no ends at all, and only then do the pieces come to five letters.

13 across EBBING

Missouri town at the cinema, going out (6)

Definition: Missouri town at the cinema · double definition

  • Missouri town at the cinema → the invented town of the 2017 film 'Three Billboards Outside ---, Missouri', so the place exists only on screen
  • going out → said of the tide: the twice-daily retreat of the sea back down the beach

Nothing to take apart. The two senses have nothing to do with each other, and the comma is doing all the work of hiding the seam between them.

15 across LETTUCES

Greens sanctioned peace agreements, putting Republican out (8)

Definition: Greens · charade + deletion

  • sanctioned → LET to let a thing happen is to sanction it
  • peace agreements → TRUCES a truce is an agreed pause in the fighting, and the plural is six letters long before anything goes
  • Republican → R R is the standard abbreviation for a Republican in American politics, and this is the letter that goes

The surface is a party-political headline and every word of it is doing a second job: the sanction is permission, the peace deals are a plural noun, and the party is one letter.

18 across GOOD SHOW

Congratulations are expected at event, according to Spooner (4,4)

Definition: Congratulations · spoonerism

  • are expected → GOOD if you are expected somewhere then you should be there; this word gives its opening SH away and takes a G
  • at event → SHOW to be at an event is to go to it, and this word collects the SH that its neighbour dropped

Say the plain version first - somebody expected at an event should go - then trade the openings. What comes out is British praise, as dated as the reverend himself.

19 across ROLL-UP

Come and see this smoke (4-2)

Definition: Come and see · double definition

  • Come and see → the fairground barker's shout, two words hollered outside a tent to pull a crowd in
  • smoke → the noun: a cigarette somebody has made for themselves out of loose tobacco and a paper

Five words and no wordplay at all. The shout and the cigarette have nothing in common, and 'this' is the nudge saying the second half is a thing rather than an action.

21 across NOTED

Roger rather than Edward (5)

Definition: Roger · charade

  • rather than → NOT choosing one thing rather than another is choosing it and not the other
  • Edward → ED Ed is the everyday short form of the name

The trap is hunting for a man. 'Roger' is not a person here but radio-speak for 'understood', and it is the word doing the defining.

23 across OVERSTATE

Exaggerate love with poem full of rubbish (9)

Definition: Exaggerate · charade + container

  • love → O in tennis a score of nothing is called love, and nothing is written as a nought
  • poem → VERSE a poem is verse, and these five letters part company before the last of them
  • rubbish → TAT tat is cheap and shoddy stuff, the goods on a bad market stall

A love poem full of rubbish is a complete picture, and that is what makes the seams hard to find: three separate pieces, and the middle one has to split.

25 across EARTHFALL

Father lost everything in landslide (9)

Definition: landslide · anagram

  • Father → FATHER six letters handed over as material rather than read as a person
  • everything → ALL everything is all of it, three more letters for the pile

Read as English it is a man ruined at the tables. Read as instructions it is nine letters and a signal to rearrange them, and the signal is one small word.

26 across ALIAS

Fake ID unfortunately getting one imprisoned (5)

Definition: Fake ID · container

  • unfortunately → ALAS alas is the old-fashioned word for unfortunately, and it splits after its second letter
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one is a single upright stroke

'Fake ID unfortunately' reads as one rueful phrase. The definition stops after two words, and the sigh that follows is the wordplay.

27 across DOOM-MERCHANT

Date weird chat room men? I say it'll end badly (4-8)

Definition: I say it'll end badly · anagram

  • Date → D D is the standard abbreviation for date, and it joins the letters being shuffled
  • chat room men → CHATROOMMEN eleven letters of raw material, there for their spelling and not their meaning

The surface tells a small sad story about internet dating. Even the first word is fodder, which is why the shuffle needs twelve letters where the phrase supplies eleven.

Down

2 down MINUS

Without good weather one's upset (5)

Definition: Without · charade + reversal

  • good weather → SUN fine weather comes from the sun, three letters laid down first
  • one's → IM 'one' in the formal sense is the speaker, so 'one's' is written I'm before anything is turned

It is the whole stack that turns, not each piece on its own: build the two bits in clue order, then read the five letters back up the grid.

3 down LOOSE ENDS

Untidy business spotted in toilet, diapers emptied out? (5,4)

Definition: Untidy business · container + outer letters

  • toilet → LOO the everyday British word for one, and it opens up to take a guest
  • spotted → SEEN whatever was spotted was seen, and this is what drops into the gap
  • diapers emptied out → DS the outside letters survive and the five in the middle are thrown away

'Business' is not trade here but the vaguer sense of a matter or affair. 'Diapers' is the American word, which is why nothing British-sounding is being emptied.

4 down FOSSIL

Thin metal ship has cracked, being rock-bound (6)

Definition: being rock-bound · container

  • Thin metal → FOIL kitchen foil is metal rolled thin, and it splits after its second letter
  • ship → SS SS is the prefix on a steamship's name, and it drops into the split

The surface has a vessel in trouble; underneath, the vessel is two letters and the trouble is what lets them in. 'Being' is a creature, not the participle it looks like.

5 down REFORMER

Change advocate dealing with ex (8)

Definition: Change advocate · charade

  • dealing with → RE RE heads a memo to say what it deals with, the standard two letters for 'concerning'
  • ex → FORMER an ex-anything is a former one, plain synonym

The tempting split is 'Change' on its own, leaving 'advocate' to supply letters. Both words are the definition together, and the wordplay is only the last three.

6 down UTTER

Perfect golf club short of parking (5)

Definition: Perfect · deletion

  • golf club → PUTTER the club a golfer takes out on the green, six letters before anything goes
  • parking → P P is the letter on a British road sign for parking, and this is the one that leaves

'Perfect' is not the verb but the intensifier, the sense in 'a perfect fool' - which is why an adjective meaning complete is what answers it.

7 down EMPIRICAL

April ice melting around start of month, going by experience (9)

Definition: going by experience · anagram + first letter

  • April ice → APRILICE eight letters of raw material: the month and the frozen water are there to be dissolved, not read
  • start of month → M the first letter of the word that follows, joining the pool

'Around' tempts you to build a container, with something wrapped round an M. The melting has already dissolved everything, so the M is simply one letter of the nine.

8 down ON ONE'S UPPERS

Short of cash, after a single drink for each son (2,4,6)

Definition: Short of cash · charade

  • after → ON 'on arrival' means after arriving, so 'on' is one of the words English uses for after
  • a single → ONE a single of anything is one of it
  • drink → SUP to sup is to drink, the old verb that survives in 'supping ale'
  • for each → PER per is Latin for each, as in miles per hour
  • son → S S is the standard family-tree abbreviation for son

The joins never fall where the wordplay does: the S that closes the second word is the front of the drink, and the son's S is the very last letter of all.

9 down DOPPELGANGER

Two pegs Ronald fashioned in identical spirit (12)

Definition: identical spirit · anagram

  • Two pegs → PEGPEG the word is written out twice over, six letters of raw material rather than a description
  • Ronald → RONALD the name goes into the mix whole, all six of its letters

Count before you shuffle. The fodder only reaches twelve letters if 'Two pegs' means the word spelled out twice, which is the step the surface hides.

14 down BLOOTERED

Distribute beer to old Glaswegian drunk (9)

Definition: Glaswegian drunk · anagram

  • beer to old → BEERTOOLD nine letters of raw material, three ordinary words handed over for their spelling

The surface has somebody handing out drink and the wordplay hands out letters. 'Old' is fodder rather than the usual single O, which is what makes the count come right.

16 down TAOISEACH

PM thanks Tories regularly, every single one (9)

Definition: PM · charade + alternate letters

  • thanks → TA ta is the everyday British way of saying thanks
  • Tories regularly → OIS the second, fourth and sixth letters of the party, taken in order
  • every single one → EACH every single one of a group is each of them

Irish politics, not British: the Tories in the clue and the prime minister in the definition sit in different parliaments, which is the joke and the trap in one.

17 down COCO PALM

Tree by the beach still sheltering old policeman (4,4)

Definition: Tree by the beach · container + charade

  • still → CALM still water is calm, and these four letters part company after the first
  • old → O O is the usual single letter for old
  • policeman → COP a cop is a policeman, informally

'Still' is the tempting word. It wants to mean 'even now', and the wordplay needs the quiet sense, water without a ripple on it.

20 down PEDLAR

Travelling salesman set up rural department stores (6)

Definition: Travelling salesman · hidden word + reversal

  • rural department → PEDLAR six letters in a row straddle the space between the two words, sitting there back to front

Every word of the surface belongs to retail, which is the disguise: the shop talk hides a run of letters that has to be read the wrong way along the clue.

22 down DOHYO

Homeric quote you cut short in ring with heavyweights (5)

Definition: ring with heavyweights · charade + deletion

  • Homeric quote → DOH the Homer wanted is Homer Simpson, and his groan of self-reproach is his whole contribution
  • you cut short → YO the pronoun gives up its final letter

'Homeric' points at the Iliad and means the cartoon. Once you accept Springfield rather than Greece, the quote is a single syllable.

24 down ALIEN

Strange story that article covers (5)

Definition: Strange · container

  • article → AN one of English's three articles, and it opens up between its two letters
  • story → LIE a story in the sense a child is told off for telling, an untruth

'Strange story' reads as one phrase and the definition is only the first word of it. The story is the fib sense, which is the step most solvers miss.
