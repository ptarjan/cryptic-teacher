Across

1 across HEADMISTRESS

Teacher made knots in his hair (12)

Definition: Teacher · container + anagram

  • his hair → HISTRESS a tress is a length of hair, and with HIS in front the eight letters split open after the first
  • made → EADM the four letters of MADE, tangled into a new order

The surface sells a scruffy schoolmaster and the answer is a woman: 'his' belongs to the wordplay only, and never to the person being defined.

9 across MOOTERS

Practising lawyers troubling to Morse (7)

Definition: Practising lawyers · anagram

  • to Morse → TOMORSE seven letters of fodder, exactly the length the enumeration asks for
  • troubling → anagram indicator

Morse is the Oxford detective of the television series, and the surface wants you reading him as a code. He is seven letters and a preposition.

10 across AMANITA

Bad mushroom trip's beginning in a frenzy (7)

Definition: Bad mushroom · container + first letter

  • a frenzy → AMANIA a frenzy is a mania, with the article riding in front; the six letters split before the last one
  • trip's beginning → T trip hands over its opening letter and nothing else

A bad mushroom trip is the picture, and mushrooms really are the subject. This genus holds the death cap and the destroying angel.

11 across REINSPECT

Vet again checks animal that's caught cold (9)

Definition: Vet again · charade + container

  • checks → REINS reins are what check a horse, and to rein in is to check
  • animal → PET a pet is the animal kept at home, and its three letters open up after two
  • cold → C C is the letter on the cold tap

'Vet' is the verb — to examine something closely before approving it — and not the animal doctor the pet in the wordplay keeps hinting at.

12 across LAGER

Cheap fizz from Majestic brought round (5)

Definition: Cheap fizz · reversal

  • Majestic → LAGER majestic means regal, and those five letters are turned round

Majestic is a British wine chain, which is what makes the surface read as a shopping trip. The wordplay wants the adjective regal, spelled backwards.

13 across HATFUL

Hampshire's opener exploited fault as much as bowler could bear? (6)

Definition: as much as bowler could bear? · charade + anagram + first letter

  • Hampshire's opener → H the county's name gives up its first letter and nothing more
  • fault → ATFUL the five letters of FAULT, shaken into another arrangement

Every word points at cricket: Hampshire, an English county side, its opening batsman, and a bowler taking wickets. The answer is about millinery.

15 across WOOLLIES

Offer suit to sell back, keeping current winter clothing (8)

Definition: winter clothing · charade + reversal + container

  • Offer suit → WOO to pay suit to somebody is to woo them, the old sense of courting
  • sell → LLES the four letters go in the other way round, and they open up before the last of them
  • current → I I is the physicist's symbol for electric current

'Offer suit' is the courtship sense rather than a tailor's, and 'current' is the electrical one. Two everyday words, both doing crossword jobs.

18 across LEATHERS

Biking kit verges on lingerie where she lives (8)

Definition: Biking kit · charade + outer letters

  • verges on lingerie → LE the first and last letters of the garment, with everything between them dropped
  • where she lives → ATHERS if something is at hers, it is where she lives, and the two words run together

The surface is all underwear and roadside verges, and neither survives the parse: 'verges on' is an instruction rather than a description.

19 across ONAGER

Ass kept hidden in apprehension, age-related (6)

Definition: Ass · hidden word

  • apprehension, age-related → ONAGER six letters in a row, running across the comma and the hyphen, from the tail of one word to the middle of the next

Nothing in the surface is about animals, so there is no reason to suspect a species lying across three words of a sentence about worry.

21 across POTUS

Premier turned up to speak, missing point? (5)

Definition: Premier · anagram + deletion

  • up to → UPTO four letters of fodder, taken exactly as they stand
  • speak, missing point → S a point is a peak, and lifting PEAK out of SPEAK leaves a single letter behind

'Point' is a peak, and spotting that is the whole clue: four ordinary letters and one survivor, shuffled into the acronym Washington uses every day.

23 across MAUSOLEUM

Keep quiet pinching gold, alone in the Taj Mahal? (9)

Definition: the Taj Mahal · container

  • Keep quiet → MUM to keep mum is to say nothing, and the three letters open after the first
  • gold → AU AU is the chemical symbol for gold
  • alone → SOLE sole means alone, the only one there is

The definition is doing quiet work: the Taj Mahal is a tomb, built by Shah Jahan for his wife, which is why silence and solitude suit the surface so well.

25 across ABANDON

Freedom from restraint which Glastonbury stage might have? (7)

Definition: Freedom from restraint · charade

  • Glastonbury stage → A BAND what a festival stage has standing on it is a band, article and all
  • might have → ON a band that is playing is on, the word a festival uses for whoever is performing

Glastonbury is Britain's biggest summer music festival, held on a Somerset dairy farm, so the surface is entirely literal and the answer is a mood.

26 across MONSOON

Sun now implying this rain? (7)

Definition: rain · charade

  • Sun → MON the calendar abbreviation for the day that follows Sunday's
  • now → SOON if it is Sunday at this moment, the next day is not far off

'Sun' reads as the thing in the sky right up to the moment you notice 'now'. It is a day of the week on a calendar, and the answer is a season of rains.

27 across KINDERGARTEN

School nerd retaking sports (12)

Definition: School · anagram

  • nerd retaking → NERDRETAKING twelve letters of fodder, exactly the length the enumeration asks for
  • sports → anagram indicator

The surface is a resit and a games afternoon. The answer is German for children's garden, borrowed whole into English for the youngest class.

2 down EMOJI

Meaningful picture from reading out Mog's letters (5)

Definition: Meaningful picture · homophone

  • Mog's letters → EMOJI the name is spelled M, O, G, and each of those three letters has a spoken name of its own

Say the three letter-names one after another and they run together into a word borrowed from Japanese, where it means picture-character.

3 down DEEP SOUTH

Tear up unfashionable husband's old cotton belt? (4,5)

Definition: old cotton belt · reversal + charade

  • Tear → DEEPS to tear along a road is to speed, and those five letters go in backwards
  • unfashionable → OUT what is out is no longer in fashion
  • husband → H H is the standard abbreviation for husband, as on a form

'Tear' is the verb that rhymes with air, not the one that comes with crying. The Cotton Belt is the old plantation country of the southern United States.

4 down ISSUED

Faces court released to the public (6)

Definition: released to the public · charade

  • Faces → IS a defendant who faces something is up against it, and the passive phrase that says so needs this two-letter verb to stand it up
  • court → SUED to take someone to court is to sue them, so the person in the dock has been sued

The whole trick is where the space goes: IS SUED, two words about a courtroom, run together into one word with no law in it at all.

5 down TRACTION

Grip case with empty travelator ahead (8)

Definition: Grip · charade + outer letters

  • empty travelator → TR the moving walkway keeps only the letter it starts with and the letter it ends with
  • case → ACTION an action is a case brought before a court of law

'Case' sits next to a travelator and looks like luggage at an airport. It is the legal sort, and the airport never comes into it.

6 down EMAIL

Message from girl I am excited about (5)

Definition: Message · hidden word + reversal

  • girl I am excited → EMAIL five letters in a row, starting in the middle of the first word and stopping just inside the last, read backwards

The girl is never named and 'I am' is not fodder to be shuffled. Every letter is already sitting in the clue, running the wrong way.

7 down SWINGEING

Severe wife burning trousers (9)

Definition: Severe · container

  • burning → SINGEING singeing is burning at the edges, and the word opens up after its first letter
  • wife → W W is the usual abbreviation for wife

'Trousers' is a verb, British slang for quietly pocketing something, and that is the only word telling you one letter goes inside another.

8 down HARRY S TRUMAN

Play guitar supporting Prince, an old 21 (5,1,6)

Definition: old 21 · charade

  • Prince → HARRY the younger of the two British princes born to Charles and Diana
  • Play guitar → STRUM to strum is to play a guitar by sweeping a hand across the strings
  • an → AN the article goes in as the two letters it is spelled with

A bare number sends you to that clue for the definition, so solve 21 across first. The middle initial stands for nothing: his parents could not choose between two grandfathers.

9 down MARSHALL PLAN

8's scheme for regenerating height with everyone in chocolate factory shrunk? (8,4)

Definition: 8's scheme for regenerating · container + deletion

  • chocolate → MARS a Mars is a chocolate bar sold in every British newsagent
  • height → H H is the standard abbreviation for height
  • everyone → ALL all means everyone, every last one of them
  • factory shrunk → PLAN a factory is a plant, and it comes up one letter short of that

The surface is selling Roald Dahl. The wordplay wants a confectionery brand and an industrial plant instead, and only the brand survives whole.

14 down TOAST RACK

B&B owner might stand round here (5,4)

Definition: B&B owner might stand round here · cryptic definition

  • B&B owner might stand round → The surface has a landlord standing a round of drinks for his guests, which is the wrong sort of bar altogether.
  • stand round here → A round is one slice off a loaf, and 'stand' is literal: at a bed-and-breakfast table the browned slices are stood upright in a wire frame.

There is no separable wordplay to hunt for — the sentence simply means the object twice over. British breakfast tables stand the slices up so they cool and stay crisp.

16 down LANDOWNER

Title holder's fallen during road race at the start (9)

Definition: Title holder · container + first letter

  • road → LANE a lane is a narrow road, and its four letters split after the third
  • race at the start → R race supplies its opening letter alone
  • fallen → DOWN someone fallen is down, and the word goes in whole

'Title holder' sounds like a champion defending a belt. The title meant is the deed that proves who owns a piece of ground.

17 down BROMANCE

Platonic relationship troubled Macron, in bed mostly (8)

Definition: Platonic relationship · container + anagram + deletion

  • bed mostly → BE bed drops its last letter, and the two that remain part company to take something in
  • Macron → ROMANC the six letters of the French president's surname, rearranged

The surface reads as a headline about Macron's private life. The word itself is a portmanteau, brother spliced onto romance, coined for friendships between men.

20 down NUTMEG

Spice in fruitcake Margaret reduced (6)

Definition: Spice · charade

  • fruitcake → NUT a fruitcake is a nut in the slang sense, somebody crazy
  • Margaret reduced → MEG the short form a Margaret goes by

Both halves turn on the same move away from the kitchen: the fruitcake is a person rather than a cake, and Margaret is here only to be shortened.

22 down SEDAN

Date with Mr Bean from the telly outside saloon in US (5)

Definition: saloon in US · container

  • Mr Bean from the telly → SEAN the Bean who is on television is the actor Sean Bean, and his first name opens after two letters
  • Date → D D is the standard abbreviation for date

Mr Bean pulls hard towards Rowan Atkinson. The Bean meant is Sean Bean, the Sheffield actor known here for Sharpe and Game of Thrones.

24 down EVOKE

Call up the day before collecting permit (5)

Definition: Call up · container

  • the day before → EVE the eve of an event is the day before it, and the word opens after two letters
  • permit → OK to OK something is to permit it

'Call up' looks like a summons to the army, and the thing being summoned is a memory instead. Neither little word in the wordplay is doing anything unusual.
