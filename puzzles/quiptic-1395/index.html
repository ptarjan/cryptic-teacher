Across

1 across MORSEL

Left to follow Oxford detective a bit (6)

Definition: a bit · charade

  • Oxford detective → MORSE Colin Dexter's inspector works out of Oxford
  • Left → L L is the one-letter abbreviation for left, as on a pair of gloves

A capital L at the start of a clue always looks like the political party; here it is the direction. Television detectives are standing crossword vocabulary — Morse, Frost and Regan all fit short answers.

5 across PAVILION

Old man getting six: hero in part of cricket ground (8)

Definition: part of cricket ground · charade

  • Old man → PA pa is the familiar word for a father
  • six → VI six in Roman numerals
  • hero → LION a lion of the resistance is one of its heroes — the figurative sense

The colon lets the whole thing read as a batsman's scorecard, with six runs and a hero. Roman numerals hide best behind ordinary number words, and 'six' is one of the commonest.

9 across CLARINET

Instrument’s note in peculiar recital (8)

Definition: Instrument · anagram

  • note → N n is the musical shorthand for a note
  • recital → RECITAL seven letters of fodder, eight once the note joins them

'Note in recital' reads so smoothly as a concert programme that the anagram signal sitting between the two words is easy to walk straight past.

10 across ROTATE

Corruption at Eton’s beginning to produce revolution (6)

Definition: produce revolution · charade + first letter

  • Corruption → ROT moral rot is corruption — decay used figuratively
  • at → AT the preposition is taken at face value, letter for letter
  • Eton’s beginning → E the school's opening letter

Corruption, Eton and revolution build a political scandal on the surface while the answer turns out to be a piece of engineering. Watch for the little preposition doing no work beyond supplying two letters.

11 across WELSH RAREBIT

Snack dished up in white barrels? (5,7)

Definition: Snack · anagram

  • white barrels → WHITEBARRELS twelve letters of fodder, exactly what the (5,7) wants

'Dished up' does both jobs at once: it is what you do with a snack and it is the instruction. The question mark covers 'white barrels', which is a picture and nothing more.

13 across MEMO

Note, second presented by the writer (4)

Definition: Note · charade

  • the writer → ME the setter composing the clue calls himself the writer
  • second → MO a mo is a moment — 'hang on a mo'

'Second' looks like an ordinal and is really the informal unit of time. A setter referring to himself — as 'the writer', as I, as me — is a convention you will meet in every puzzle.

14 across MELODIST

Tunesmith revamping old times (8)

Definition: Tunesmith · anagram

  • old times → OLDTIMES eight letters of fodder, exactly the enumeration

'Old times' is a nostalgic phrase pressed into service as raw material. Tunesmith follows wordsmith and gunsmith: the pattern names a maker of whatever comes in front.

17 across ATHLETIC

Soccer team starts to have lower expectations in topmost level (8)

Definition: Soccer team · container + first letters

  • topmost level → ATTIC the attic is the topmost level of a house
  • have lower expectations → HLE the opening letters of the three words, in the order they come

'Starts to have lower expectations' reads as a manager's gloom, which is exactly why nobody counts its initials. The answer is a club name here, not the everyday adjective.

18 across ABUT

Join, except when led by American (4)

Definition: Join · charade

  • American → A A is the standard one-letter abbreviation for American
  • except → BUT 'everyone but me' and 'everyone except me' say the same thing

The comma after 'Join' is the only mark of where the clue splits, and the surface — someone signing up under an American — depends on your not seeing it.

20 across GRAND OPENING

Launch opportunity having put $1000 up front (5,7)

Definition: Launch · charade

  • $1000 → GRAND a grand is a thousand, in dollars or in pounds
  • opportunity → OPENING an opening is a chance to act

'Up front' does double duty: it is the payment idiom the surface wants, and it is the instruction saying which chunk goes first. Money paid up front is exactly how a launch gets funded.

23 across NUNCIO

Convent member leading company welcoming one papal ambassador (6)

Definition: papal ambassador · charade + container

  • Convent member → NUN the resident of a convent is a nun
  • company → CO Co. is the abbreviation tacked onto a firm's name
  • one → I one in Roman numerals

The surface reads as a church committee and every word of it is doing double duty. The definition runs to two words at the very end, which is where a long clue usually leaves it.

24 across SKIN-DEEP

Superficial type gripped by bishopric’s power (4-4)

Definition: Superficial · container + charade

  • bishopric → SEE a bishop's see is the district he presides over
  • type → KIND 'that type of thing' and 'that kind of thing' are interchangeable
  • power → P p is the physicist's abbreviation for power

The enumeration (4-4) says hyphenated rather than two loose words, which narrows the field a long way before any letters arrive. The surface — a diocese flexing its muscle — hides a compound adjective.

25 across CHARLTON

League initially brought in graph about footballing legend (8)

Definition: footballing legend · container + charade + first letter

  • graph → CHART a chart is a graph
  • League initially → L the first letter of League
  • about → ON 'a paper on cricket' and 'a paper about cricket' say the same thing

The whole clue reads as sports journalism, definition included, so there is no obvious seam to pull at. Reaching the surname means trusting 'initially' over the story the words are telling.

26 across GENOME

Set of chromosomes – information some at first ignored (6)

Definition: Set of chromosomes · charade + deletion

  • information → GEN gen is British informal for information — 'give me the gen'
  • some → OME SOME with its opening S taken away

The dash is the only signpost, and after it every word is doing letter work. 'Some' looks like a quantity word and is really four letters waiting to be trimmed.

Down

2 down OSLO

Part of no Slovenian city (4)

Definition: city · hidden word

  • no Slovenian → OSLO the letters run straight across the gap: n(O SLO)venian

Naming one country is a good way to hide the capital of another, and 'Slovenian' points your eye at Ljubljana while Norway sits under it. Hidden words that straddle a word break are the hardest to see.

3 down STRAW POLL

Head of league engaged in revision of sport law and quick survey (5,4)

Definition: quick survey · anagram + first letter

  • Head of league → L the opening letter of League and nothing else
  • sport law → SPORTLAW eight letters of fodder, nine once the L is engaged in them

The surface is a rule change in sport, which is exactly why 'revision of' reads as legislation rather than as an instruction. Counting nine letters against the (5,4) is the confirmation.

4 down LONELY

Liberal working with cathedral, having no support? (6)

Definition: having no support? · charade

  • Liberal → L L is how a Liberal candidate is marked on a ballot paper
  • working → ON a machine that is working is on
  • cathedral → ELY Ely, the Cambridgeshire cathedral city, is the short cathedral crosswords reach for

The question mark is the setter admitting a stretch: support here means company rather than a prop holding something up. 'Working' for ON is worth banking — it turns up about once a puzzle.

5 down PUT THE MOCKERS ON

Stop – let’s get a cynical view (3,3,7,2)

Definition: Stop · double definition

  • Stop → to put the mockers on a plan is to scupper it — the jinx that stops the thing happening
  • let’s get a cynical view → read the words at face value: mockers are people who scoff, and you put them on the way you put on a coat

The dash is the split. On one side an idiom meaning to scupper something, on the other the same four words taken at face value — no letters change hands anywhere in this clue.

6 down VARIABLE

Part of equation? Feasible to include answer, excluding content (8)

Definition: Part of equation? · container + outer letters

  • Feasible → VIABLE a feasible plan is a viable one
  • answer → AR ANSWER stripped of everything but its first and last letters

'Content' for a word's insides is a setter's staple, the same idea as 'heartless' or 'gutted'. The surface reads as a maths lesson while the clue is quietly hollowing a word out.

7 down LATTE

Some quiet talk over coffee (5)

Definition: coffee · hidden word + reversal

  • quiet talk → ETTAL quiET TALk holds the run, and this down entry reads it back up the column

The surface is café chatter, which is why nobody looks at the letters. 'Over coffee' is such a natural pairing that splitting it — one word working, one word defining — is the whole difficulty.

8 down OXTAIL SOUP

Pot Louisa stirred, filled with unknown broth (6,4)

Definition: broth · anagram

  • Pot Louisa → POTLOUISA nine letters of fodder, one short of the ten the enumeration asks for
  • unknown → X x is the algebraist's unknown quantity, and crosswordland's too

A cook at a stove is such a complete picture that 'Pot Louisa' never reads as a bag of letters. Counting them against the (6,4) is what gives the device away.

12 down SEE-THROUGH

Fail to be deceived by description of audacious dress? (3-7)

Definition: Fail to be deceived by · double definition

  • Fail to be deceived by → to see through a lie is to look straight past it and not be taken in
  • description of audacious dress? → fabric you can look right through is what makes such a dress daring

One phrase, two grammars: a verb you do to a trick, and an adjective you apply to a fabric. Nothing is assembled here — the whole clue is meaning, which is why the hyphen in (3-7) is the only structural clue.

15 down DRAGGED ON

Last members of daring little band, beset by monster, kept going (7,2)

Definition: kept going · container + last letters

  • monster → DRAGON the storybook monster, and a nine-letter answer needs a long container
  • daring little band → GED the closing letters of the three words: darinG littlE banD

Three words giving one letter each is the fiddliest kind of fodder, and 'daring little band' is written to read as a description rather than as a list. The monster is the easy half.

16 down STOOD OUT

Tubby, seen around ordinary party, was prominent (5,3)

Definition: was prominent · container

  • Tubby → STOUT a stout figure is a tubby one
  • ordinary → O o is the standard abbreviation for ordinary
  • party → DO a do is a party — 'a bit of a do'

The surface has someone circling a modest gathering, which is why two of crosswordland's shortest abbreviations read here as plain description. The comma after 'Tubby' is the seam.

19 down MINING

Industrial activity repeatedly popular in classic British car firm (6)

Definition: Industrial activity · container

  • classic British car firm → MG MG, short for Morris Garages, is the classic British sports-car marque
  • repeatedly popular → ININ someone popular is 'in', and here that little word is wanted twice

Every word of this reads as a comment on the British motor trade, definition included, so there is no obvious seam. Finding it means asking which two of those words could be an abbreviation.

21 down NICER

Pleasanter to turn up in Empire Cinema? (5)

Definition: Pleasanter · hidden word + reversal

  • Empire Cinema → RECIN empi(RE CIN)ema holds the run, and turning it up the column reverses it

A cinema called the Empire is such an ordinary thing to name that the letters spanning the two words go unread. The question mark admits the picture house is invented for the occasion.

22 down HELM

Direct line cutting border (4)

Definition: Direct · container

  • border → HEM a hem is the finished border of a garment
  • line → L l is the standard abbreviation for line, as in a reference to l. 12

'Direct line' reads as a single phrase, which is the trap: the definition stops after the first word. L for line is worth banking — it recurs constantly.
