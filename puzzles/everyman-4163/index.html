Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,163

Across

1 across NEAR MISS

Seminar's incoherent: that's close to a disaster (4,4)

Definition: close to a disaster · anagram

  • Seminar's → SEMINARS the eight letters of the word, apostrophe ignored, are the whole of the mixture

The colon does the misdirecting: it makes what follows sound like a verdict on the seminar rather than the definition of the answer.

5 across AGHAST

American checks out of dilapidated Gasthaus, horrified (6)

Definition: horrified · anagram + deletion

  • American → US US is the everyday shorthand for American, and it is what departs
  • Gasthaus → GASTHA the German guest house loses its U and its S, leaving six letters to be shaken

The italicised German word is chosen because it has an American already lodged in it, so the tourist of the surface and the mechanism underneath are the same picture.

9 across ONE SECOND PLEASE

Dueller's request: give me a mo (3,6,6)

Definition: Dueller's request · double definition

  • Dueller's request → a duellist needs a second, the friend who attends him at the field, so that is the person he asks for
  • give me a mo → a mo is a moment, and asking politely for one is the everyday sense of the phrase

The colon looks like an announcement and is really the seam between two readings of the same fifteen letters: one asks for a person, the other for a moment.

11 across LEI

Floral wreath that'll symbolise Hawaii, ultimately? (3)

Definition: Floral wreath · last letters

  • that'll symbolise Hawaii → LEI the closing letters of the three words in turn: that'l-L, symbolis-E, hawai-I

Three words, one letter each, and the clue is also describing itself — the garland really is the thing that ends up standing for Hawaii.

12 across TUTANKHAMUN

Quivering in autumn, thank young monarch (11)

Definition: young monarch · anagram

  • autumn → AUTUMN the season goes in whole, six letters
  • thank → THANK five more letters, taken exactly as they stand

Eleven letters is a lot to juggle, so write them out and count them against the enumeration. 'Quivering in autumn' paints leaves on a branch and is really an instruction.

13 across HACIENDA

Can I head off to find Spanish mansion? (8)

Definition: Spanish mansion · anagram

  • Can I head → CAN I HEAD eight letters, the stray pronoun counted in with them

The fodder is a complete spoken sentence — somebody asking to leave early — which is why it never looks like material waiting to be rearranged.

14 across DUNCE

Nitwit implicated in horrid uncertainty (5)

Definition: Nitwit · hidden word

  • horrid uncertainty → DUNCE the word straddles the gap between the two: horri-D UNCE-rtainty

Hidden answers love a long adjective and noun together, because a phrase like this reads as mood rather than as material with something buried in it.

17 across AIOLI

When choosing starters, alliums in oil, lemon intertwined ...? (5)

Definition: alliums in oil, lemon intertwined · first letters + &lit

  • alliums in oil, lemon intertwined → AIOLI the initials of the five words in order: Alliums In Oil Lemon Intertwined

Garlic is an allium, so the clue is a recipe for the very thing it spells. 'Starters' is the menu and the letter instruction at the same time.

19 across SUSPENSE

S American writes start of erotic film in Hitchcock style (8)

Definition: Hitchcock style · charade + first letter

  • S → S the abbreviation for South on the surface, and no more than the letter itself underneath
  • American → US US does duty for American throughout crosswords
  • writes → PENS to pen a letter is to write it
  • erotic film → E the opening letter of the two-word phrase

'S American' invites you to read South American in one gulp and is really two pieces. Hitchcock was billed as the Master of the thing defined, which is the other half.

22 across DISINTERRED

Dug up abuse given to Milanese footballers (and one from Liverpool) (11)

Definition: Dug up · charade

  • abuse → DIS to dis somebody is to abuse them, the shortening of disrespect
  • Milanese footballers → INTER Inter, the other club in Milan
  • one from Liverpool → RED Liverpool play in red and their players are the Reds

Three football sides stacked end to end. The joke is that 'inter' is itself the verb for burying, sitting in the middle of a clue about digging things up.

24 across BOA

Smell a snake (3)

Definition: snake · charade

  • Smell → BO BO is body odour, the polite initials for a smell
  • a → A the article joins on as a letter of its own

The clue leans on 'smell a rat', so it sounds like a warning to be suspicious rather than two pieces laid end to end.

25 across SHOOTS THE BREEZE

Makes film about movement of air, Gasses? (6,3,6)

Definition: Gasses · charade

  • Makes film → SHOOTS a director shoots a scene, so making film is shooting
  • movement of air → THE BREEZE a breeze is a light movement of air, and the phrase brings its article with it

Taken literally the wordplay is a documentary about wind, which is the joke. The italics flag 'Gasses' as slang: to gas is to talk on and on about nothing.

26 across SPARSE

Practises boxing with Earl that's slight (6)

Definition: slight · charade

  • Practises boxing → SPARS to spar is to box in practice, without the fight
  • Earl → E E is the peerage abbreviation for an earl

'Boxing' usually tells you to put one thing inside another, and here it means only what it says, which is the trap the clue is built on.

27 across UNDERDOG

Where you might find blanket, one you expect to lose? (8)

Definition: Where you might find blanket · double definition

  • Where you might find blanket → split into two words it is a place: the blanket a dog has settled on is under the dog
  • one you expect to lose → the competitor the odds are against, the one nobody is backing

The first half only works once you re-split the answer into two words, which is the whole trick; the second half is the plain meaning everybody knows.

Down

1 down NOODLE

Ribbon for simpleton (6)

Definition: Ribbon · double definition

  • Ribbon → a flat ribbon of pasta, the kind that comes dried in a nest
  • simpleton → calling somebody a noodle is a mild old word for a fool

Nothing to take apart: 'for' is only joinery, and the trick is that a word out of the kitchen doubles as a gentle Victorian insult.

2 down AMERICANO

Coffee brewing, nice aroma (9)

Definition: Coffee · anagram

  • nice aroma → NICE AROMA nine letters straight off the page and into the mixture

Every word of the surface is about coffee, which is why the instruction hides so well: 'brewing' is doing kitchen duty and letter duty at once.

3 down MAESTRI

Musicians playing Artemis (7)

Definition: Musicians · anagram

  • Artemis → ARTEMIS seven letters of a title, italicised because it is a work being performed

Italics in an Everyman clue usually flag a title or a foreign word, and here the title is nothing but raw material for the shuffle.

4 down SHORT AND SWEET

Perhaps a whisky with some ice cream, but not outstaying welcome (5,3,5)

Definition: not outstaying welcome · charade

  • Perhaps a whisky → SHORT a short is a measure of spirits, and 'perhaps' warns that whisky is only one example of one
  • with → AND one thing with another is one thing and another
  • some ice cream → SWEET a sweet is the pudding course, of which ice cream is one helping

Thirteen letters out of a bar order and a dessert menu. The definition sits at the far end and reads as part of the same evening, which is what buries it.

6 down GALAHAD

Festivity with bore that's noble type (7)

Definition: noble type · charade

  • Festivity → GALA a gala is a festivity, a day of races or a grand night out
  • bore → HAD she bore three children is she had three children — bore as the past of bear

'Bore' looks like the tedious guest at the party, which is the joke: a dull evening on the surface, the purest knight of the Round Table underneath.

7 down ALARM

Fear a long tentacle (5)

Definition: Fear · charade

  • a → A the article is taken at face value, one letter
  • long → L L is the standard shortening of long, the same L that sizes a long leg on a pair of trousers
  • tentacle → ARM an octopus's tentacles are its arms

The surface offers one creature and the wordplay is three separate pieces laid end to end, with the definition standing alone at the front.

8 down THE ANDES

Serving of absinthe and espresso somewhere in S America (3,5)

Definition: somewhere in S America · hidden word

  • absinthe and espresso → THE ANDES the run crosses three words in order: absin-THE, then AND, then ES-presso

Two drinks on a menu with a mountain range lying across them. Eight letters is a long hide, and the join at 'and' is the piece the eye skates over.

10 down DENTAL SURGEON

If I work hard, I get a little plaque (6,7)

Definition: If I work hard, I get a little plaque · cryptic definition

  • If I work hard → the surface offers a diligent employee, the sort whose long service is eventually recognised
  • I get a little plaque → as a reward that is an engraved plate on the wall; the setter means the soft film that forms on teeth, which is a day's work to remove

No wordplay to find here, only a pun on one word. The 'I' of the clue is the person speaking, so the whole sentence has to be read in their voice.

15 down NOSEBLEED

Boxer's injury: lobe needs to be treated (9)

Definition: Boxer's injury · anagram

  • lobe needs → LOBE NEEDS nine letters: the ear's lobe and the verb that follows it

The colon sets up a doctor's note, so the second half reads as medicine rather than as fodder. Nine letters is big — write them out before guessing.

16 down FADDISTS

Tot embraced by bunches of five enthusiasts (8)

Definition: enthusiasts · container

  • bunches of five → FISTS a bunch of fives is old slang for a fist, and the clue asks for more than one
  • Tot → ADD to tot up a column of figures is to add it

'Tot' offers a small child and 'bunches' a handful of flowers; both are pulling in the wrong direction. The definition is the single word at the end.

18 down IGNITES

Arrived with US soldier, offered up torches (7)

Definition: torches · charade + reversal

  • Arrived → SET IN when winter arrives it sets in — five letters, before anything is turned
  • US soldier → GI GI is the American private, from the army's 'government issue'

'Torches' looks like a noun, something carried in a hand, and is the verb 'sets alight' — spotting that is harder than the wordplay it defines.

20 down ENDORSE

Back to destroy heroin in E London (7)

Definition: Back · charade + deletion

  • destroy → END to end a thing is to put a stop to it
  • heroin → ORSE heroin is horse in dealers' slang, and the East End leaves the H off

The surface is a police report and every word of it is machinery. 'Back' at the head of a down clue looks like a turning instruction and is the definition.

21 down BAD EGG

Preference to suppress dated Everyman gags, primarily unsavoury sort (3,3)

Definition: unsavoury sort · container + first letters

  • Preference → BAG your bag is the thing you favour — 'not my bag' means not to my taste
  • dated Everyman gags → DEG the initials of the three words, in the order printed

The setter drops his own name into the middle of the clue as raw material, which is a habit worth knowing: 'Everyman' is a word to be used, not a signature.

23 down SKODA

While entertained by Jamaican music, do up Czech car (5)

Definition: Czech car · container + reversal

  • Jamaican music → SKA ska is the Jamaican dance music that came before reggae
  • do → OD the two letters of DO, taken from the bottom upwards
  • While → surface only: it turns the clue into a scene and contributes no letters

'Do up' reads as restoring an old banger and is two letters climbing the column instead. The definition is the pair of words at the end.
