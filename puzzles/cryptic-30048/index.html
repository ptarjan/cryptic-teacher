Across

9 across CONSTRAIN

Study air pressure (9)

Definition: pressure · charade

  • Study → CON to con a text is to study it closely, an old sense crosswords keep alive
  • air → STRAIN an air is a tune, and so is a strain — both mean a melody

Three words, three separate jobs, and the surface makes them one phrase from a weather forecast. Short clues like this hide the joins by having no joins at all.

10 across RAITA

Educated character fed a refreshing side dish (5)

Definition: refreshing side dish · container

  • Educated character → RITA Rita is the hairdresser taking an Open University course in Willy Russell's play Educating Rita
  • a → A taken as it stands

'Character' is the pointer: it says a name is wanted, and the title of the play supplies one that has been educated.

11 across TIE-IN

Link that is apparently like spam? (3-2)

Definition: Link · container

  • like spam → TIN Spam is the tinned meat, so what resembles it comes in a tin
  • that is → IE i.e. is the everyday short form of the Latin id est, that is

Spam pulls you straight to junk email, which is where the meat's name ended up. The clue wants the tin the meat came in.

12 across LILY-WHITE

Pure flower with foul fragrance, ultimately (4-5)

Definition: Pure · charade + anagram + last letter

  • flower → LILY the flower named is a lily, handed over whole
  • with → WITH four letters of raw material, not the joinery the word pretends to be
  • fragrance, ultimately → E the last letter of fragrancE

'With' reads as grammar and is fodder; 'foul' reads as part of the smell and is the instruction. That double disguise is what makes a short anagram hard.

13 across PISSOIR

Capital in Paris is at the end of the day in France – go there? (7)

Definition: go there? · charade + first letter

  • Paris → P Paris starts with a capital letter, and that single letter is all the wordplay wants
  • is → IS the word taken as it stands
  • at the end of the day in France → SOIR soir is the French for evening, the end of the day over there

'Capital in Paris' invites you to think of France's capital city and asks only for the letter P. The euphemism in the definition is doing the same trick in English.

14 across GNASHER

Tooth that’s new in cutter? (7)

Definition: Tooth · container

  • cutter → GASHER something that gashes is a cutter, the -er turning the verb into the thing that does it
  • new → N n = new, the standard abbreviation

'Cutter' looks like a boat or a trade and is an invented agent noun. Gnasher is also the Beano dog, which is why the clue can afford the question mark.

19 across JAY

Bird with 14 mentioning celebrated baker? (3)

Definition: Bird · homophone

  • 14 → the answer to 14 across is borrowed for its sound, and the G it opens with is silent
  • celebrated baker? → JAY Jane Asher is the British actress whose celebration-cake business made her a household name for baking; say this entry and then 14 across and you have said her name

Two of the puzzle's own answers spoken one after the other make a real person's name, so 14 is borrowed for its sound and never for its letters.

20 across LORRY

Author Lee articulated – something like that? (5)

Definition: something like that? · homophone

  • Author Lee → LORRY Laurie Lee wrote Cider with Rosie, and his first name said aloud is spelled quite differently

One word does both jobs: it tells you to hear the author's first name, and it is the adjective the vague definition is quietly pointing at.

21 across ROMANOV

House where fellow in groovy clothing undressed (7)

Definition: House · container + deletion

  • groovy → ROOV GROOVY with the letter at each end taken off
  • fellow → MAN a fellow is a man

'House where fellow' reads like a place with a lodger in it. The house wanted is a dynasty, the sense in which we speak of the House of Windsor.

22 across DEHISCE

Pop is attending church: he had relapsed earlier (7)

Definition: Pop · charade + reversal

  • he had relapsed → DEH HE'D is 'he had' shortened, and it has slipped back the other way round
  • is → IS the word taken as it stands
  • church → CE CE is the Church of England, the church a crossword usually means

The colon makes the second half sound like gossip about a man at the service. Dehiscence is the botanist's word for a seed pod splitting along its seam.

24 across FRANGLAIS

Amusing language in which the following might sound like laughter? (9)

Definition: Amusing language · cryptic definition

  • the following → read as English it is a pointer, meaning whatever comes next; the setter wants it read as a phrase waiting to be translated, 'the' and 'after'
  • might sound like laughter → translate only the first half and leave the second in English, the way the answer does: l'after, said aloud, is laughter

Nothing to take apart, and no letters to collect. Do to 'the following' what the answer is named for: turn half of it French, leave half English, and you get l'after — which spoken aloud is laughter. The clue is a specimen of the language it defines, hence the question mark.

26 across BINGO CALLER

House visitor for whom knock at the door comes after 4? (5,6)

Definition: for whom knock at the door comes after 4? · charade

  • House → BINGO 'House!' is the shout that claims a win in a British bingo hall
  • visitor → CALLER somebody who calls at your door is a visitor

Two words of wordplay and the rest of the sentence is the definition, describing the person at the microphone by one line of patter.

28 across MANGO LASSI

Imported into Vietnam, Angola’s simple fruit drink (5,5)

Definition: fruit drink · hidden word

  • Vietnam, Angola’s simple → MANGOLASSI ten consecutive letters, beginning inside the first country and ending inside the last word

Two countries side by side make a good hiding place, and the run crosses a comma, an apostrophe and two word breaks without a single letter moving.

29 across LOWESTOFT

Central European fliers circling on the occidental side of English port (9)

Definition: English port · container

  • Central European fliers → LOT LOT is the national airline of Poland, a country in central Europe
  • on the occidental side of → WESTOF the occident is the west, so this is the plain phrase 'west of' spelled out

'On the occidental side of English' looks like an instruction to take the western half of a word, and it is six letters written out longhand.

Down

1 down SCOT

Old payment of course skimmed off? (4)

Definition: Old payment · deletion

  • course → ASCOT Ascot is the Berkshire racecourse of the royal meeting, offered here as one course among many

Read 'of course' as the throwaway aside and there is nothing to work on; read it as 'of a course' and a famous racecourse is standing there waiting to be trimmed.

2 down ANGERS

Gauls reportedly in French city? (6)

Definition: French city · homophone

  • Gauls → ANGERS the ancient French people said aloud are galls, and to gall somebody is to make them cross

The surface asks you to picture Gauls wandering round a French town, which is exactly the wrong place to look: the Frenchness is in the definition, and the Gauls are only a sound.

3 down STENTORIAN

Booming ace in tennis, or tennis’s first shot (10)

Definition: Booming · anagram + first letter

  • ace → A the ace of a suit is written A at the card table, a standard single-letter sense
  • tennis, or → TENNISOR anagram fodder, eight letters, comma and all
  • tennis’s first → T the letter the word tennis opens with

'Ace in tennis' wants to be a service winner and 'first shot' wants to be the serve. Both are material and instruction instead, which is why the clue reads so smoothly.

5 down ENOLA GAY

Bomber, a girl I sent up ending in atrocity (5,3)

Definition: Bomber · charade + reversal + last letter

  • a → A taken as it stands
  • girl → GAL gal is the informal spelling of a girl
  • I → ONE the Roman numeral I is one, written out as a word here
  • ending in atrocity → Y the last letter of atrocitY

The surface is a man teasing a girl, and 'atrocity' is the one word that does not fit it — the giveaway that something much darker is being named.

6 down DRAW

No-win situation The Guardian’s responsibility to turn around? (4)

Definition: No-win situation · reversal

  • The Guardian’s responsibility → WARD a guardian is the person legally responsible for a ward

'No-win situation' is normally an idiom for a hopeless predicament; here it is meant literally, as a result in which nobody wins. The newspaper is a joke on the setter's own paper.

7 down FINISHER

One crossing the line in entering shipping forecast area (8)

Definition: One crossing the line · container

  • shipping forecast area → FISHER Fisher is one of the sea areas read out on the BBC's Shipping Forecast, off the Danish coast
  • in → IN the word itself, used as two letters

'In' looks like grammar holding the sentence up and is the piece being inserted, which is the whole disguise. Every other word points at athletics or the sea.

8 down MAKE GOOD

Rectify state of mind guzzling a barrel of beer (4,4)

Definition: Rectify · container

  • state of mind → MOOD your mood is the state of mind you happen to be in
  • a barrel of beer → A KEG beer comes in a keg, and the A in front is taken as it stands

The answer runs on into 25 down. Everything in the clue after the first word is a drinker's evening, and none of it is the definition.

13 down POLAR

Average outside cold, with coat off – freezing! (5)

Definition: freezing · container + deletion

  • Average → PAR par is the normal or average standard, as in up to par
  • cold, with coat off → OL COLD wearing nothing on the outside loses the letter at each end

The dash makes the last word look like an exclamation about the weather in the story, and it is the definition standing on its own.

15 down ALL THE BEST

Lethal whipped cream – take care! (3,3,4)

Definition: take care! · anagram + charade

  • Lethal → ALLTHE six letters of fodder, rearranged into the two short words at the front
  • cream → BEST the cream of anything is the best of it

'Whipped cream' is one thing on the surface and two jobs underneath: only the first word is shuffled, and the cream is the pick of the crop.

16 down RHYME

Mode of ode, say? (5)

Definition: Mode of ode, say? · cryptic definition

  • Mode of ode → the surface reading: a manner or fashion belonging to an ode, the way the poem is put together
  • say? → the for-example marker — the first word is being offered as an instance, and what it is an instance of is its relation to the second

Nothing here to take apart. The two words are doing it to each other: say 'mode' and 'ode' aloud and the answer is what one is to the other.

18 down SAMBAING

Big man, as dancing, doing just that? (8)

Definition: doing just that? · anagram

  • Big man, as → BIGMANAS anagram fodder, eight letters

The definition is a pointer rather than a word: 'just that' means whatever was said a moment earlier, and what was said a moment earlier is the shuffling instruction.

19 down JOVIALLY

Marine’s carried through in cheerful manner (8)

Definition: in cheerful manner · container

  • Marine’s → JOLLY a jolly is Royal Navy slang for a Royal Marine
  • through → VIA going through a place is going via it

Jove is Jupiter, and astrologers held that anyone born under his planet turned out merry — which is where the cheerfulness in the answer comes from.

22 down DISOWN

Reject is blocking sink (6)

Definition: Reject · container

  • sink → DOWN to sink is to go down
  • is → IS the word itself, dropped in whole

Three short words reading like a note left for the plumber, with a separate job hidden in each of them. Short clues rarely mean short work.

23 down SENIOR

One is doddery, right? (6)

Definition: One is doddery, right? · &lit + anagram

  • One is → ONEIS anagram fodder, five letters
  • right → R r = right, as in a stage direction or a map reference

An all-in-one: read straight, the sentence describes the person; read again, the same words are the recipe. 'Right?' is both the question tag and a single letter.

24 down FAME

Musical combination of two notes (4)

Definition: Musical · charade

  • two notes → FA fah is the fourth degree of the sol-fa scale, written as two letters
  • two notes → ME me is the third degree of that scale in the British spelling of it

'Musical' looks like an adjective and is a noun — the name of a show. The sol-fa scale is where setters go whenever they want short notes: doh, ray, me, fah, soh.

27 down OATH

Curse of Portillo, a Thatcherite (4)

Definition: Curse · hidden word

  • Portillo, a Thatcherite → OATH four letters running straight on from the end of the surname into the words that follow it

Michael Portillo was a Conservative cabinet minister under Margaret Thatcher, so the sentence reads as ordinary political comment — and the letters sit inside it in order.
