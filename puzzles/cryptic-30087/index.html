Across

9 across AMBLE

Not the first to risk travel at a leisurely pace (5)

Definition: travel at a leisurely pace · deletion

  • risk → AMBLE to risk is to GAMBLE, and that word arrives without its opening G

'Not the first to risk' sounds like caution about going early, which is the wrong reading entirely: 'first' is counting letters, not people.

10 across LANDSLIDE

Rocks falling and getting into dell is ruinous (9)

Definition: Rocks falling · anagram

  • dell → DELL a dell is a small wooded hollow; four letters of the mix
  • and → AND the clue's own and, three more letters
  • is → IS the last two letters of the mix come from is

Nine letters sit in plain view across three ordinary little words, and the collapsing valley the surface describes is the same picture the answer paints.

11 across ORIGINALS

Old nip of rum I drink too, without finishing first shots (9)

Definition: first shots · charade + first letter + deletion

  • Old → O O is the standard short form of old
  • rum → R one letter poured out of rum
  • I → I the clue's own I, taken as the letter
  • drink → GIN the drink is gin
  • too → ALS too is ALSO, stopped one letter short

Two spirits and neither of them wanted whole: the bar vocabulary is what makes this read as a night out instead of a spelling exercise.

12 across EMPTY

Space put by, but oddly nothing inside (5)

Definition: nothing inside · charade + alternate letters

  • Space → EM printers measure space in ems, an em being the width of a capital M
  • put by → PTY the odd letters of put by: P, T and Y

'Space' is a printer's term here, which is the hard step; 'put by', meaning saved for later, is in the clue only to be counted through.

13 across FLIPPED

Turned and ran away, clutching Zippo but dropping case (7)

Definition: Turned · container + middle letters

  • ran away → FLED ran away is fled, and it opens up to take a passenger
  • Zippo → IPP the lighter drops its outer Z and O

'Dropping case' is a neat double sense: a Zippo really has a case, and in crosswords dropping the case means losing the letters at both ends.

15 across ORBITAL

Part in test regarding the way planets travel (7)

Definition: regarding the way planets travel · container

  • test → ORAL an oral is a test you sit by speaking; it splits after two letters
  • Part → BIT a part of something is a bit of it

The whole clue rests on one piece of vocabulary: an oral is a spoken examination, so 'test' is a noun to be swapped, not a verb.

17 across OUNCE

Snow leopard jumps without limits (5)

Definition: Snow leopard · middle letters

  • jumps → OUNCE jumps is BOUNCES, and stripping the B and the S leaves these five

Nothing in the surface hints at letters: 'without limits' reads as unbounded enthusiasm, and 'jumps' has to be turned into a longer word before anything can be taken from it.

18 across RAW

Harsh fightback (3)

Definition: Harsh · reversal

  • fightback → WAR the fight buried in the word is a war; the -back on the end is the instruction, not fodder

One word of the clue is carrying two jobs: on the surface 'fightback' is a single noun, and underneath it is a piece of fodder with its instruction stuck to the end.

20 across INNER

Central pub evacuated earlier (5)

Definition: Central · charade + outer letters

  • pub → INN a pub is an inn
  • earlier → ER clear out earlier and the E and R at its ends survive

Five letters, two of them from a word contributing no meaning at all: 'earlier' is in the clue purely so that it can be hollowed.

22 across DEBATES

Be taken the wrong way during rendezvous, creating arguments (7)

Definition: arguments · container + reversal

  • rendezvous → DATES rendezvous are dates, and the word opens after its D
  • Be → EB the clue's own Be, turned round

'Be taken the wrong way' is what happens to a remark in a row, so the instruction is hiding inside a description of the very thing the answer names.

25 across ORGANIC

Naturally produced grain ruined through company backtracking (7)

Definition: Naturally produced · anagram + container + reversal

  • grain → RGANI the five letters of grain in a new order
  • company → OC company shortens to CO, which backtracks to OC and then opens around the rest

CO for company is a workhorse abbreviation. Here it is reversed and then split, so its two letters finish at opposite ends of the answer.

26 across MINER

Pitman’s system finally starts to introduce new, easy recording (5)

Definition: Pitman · charade + last letter + first letters

  • system → M the last letter of system
  • introduce new, easy recording → INER the initials of the four words, in the order they come

Pitman's shorthand is the joke — the clue pretends to be about the system, while the definition is only the man's job.

27 across RELIGIOUS

Devout priest, German, in South American city with us (9)

Definition: Devout · container + charade

  • South American city → RIO Rio de Janeiro, which splits open after its first letter
  • priest → ELI Eli is the priest who raised Samuel, crosswordland's standard clergyman
  • German → G G is the usual abbreviation for German
  • us → US the clue's own us, added at the end

Eli is worth remembering: three letters, a priest, and very nearly the only one setters ever use. The comma-heavy surface hides a straightforward stack.

30 across FOOD CHAIN

Commanding Officer had info about who’s hunting whom? (4,5)

Definition: who’s hunting whom? · anagram

  • Commanding Officer → CO a Commanding Officer is the CO
  • had info → HADINFO seven more letters of the mix, taken as they stand

The military surface is a decoy: the officer is only an abbreviation, and 'about' is the shuffle rather than the subject of the intelligence.

31 across BUILT

Constructed crowns of bronze using intricate latticework techniques (5)

Definition: Constructed · first letters

  • bronze using intricate latticework techniques → BUILT the initials of the five words, in the order they come

Five words of craft vocabulary and only their first letters matter; the latticework is there to keep you thinking about metalwork.

Down

1 down TACO

Mexican snack stack – hot, without any wrappers (4)

Definition: Mexican snack · charade + middle letters

  • stack → TAC peel the S and the K off stack and TAC is what is left
  • hot → O hot loses its H and its T, leaving one middle letter

The surface is pure street food, and that is the misdirection: 'stack' and 'hot' are being treated as spellings on a page, not as things to eat.

2 down OBLIVION

Forgetfulness leads to open bolts – then four being eaten by wild cat (8)

Definition: Forgetfulness · charade + first letters + container

  • open bolts → OB the front letter of each of the two words
  • wild cat → LION the wild cat is a lion, and it is the one doing the swallowing
  • then four → IV four in Roman numerals is IV, and it goes in after the lion's opening L

'Forgetfulness leads to open bolts' reads as a sentence about mislaying things, which is what hides the instruction: 'leads' is asking for initials, not for consequences.

3 down YETI

Still one imagined terrifying creature from the Himalayas (4)

Definition: imagined terrifying creature from the Himalayas · charade

  • Still → YET still and yet both mean 'even now'
  • one → I one as a Roman numeral is I

Two of the smallest words in the language do all the work, and everything after them is definition — the mountains are described, never named.

4 down OLEANDER

Sister Edna eloped, heading north, carrying flowering plant (8)

Definition: flowering plant · hidden word + reversal

  • Sister Edna eloped → OLEANDER sisteR EDNA ELOped holds the unbroken run REDNAELO, and read the other way it is the answer

The sister and the elopement are pure story. Nothing needs solving: the letters simply run on through three words, and a down entry read upwards is what 'heading north' asks for.

5 down UNESCO

In France a supermarket sacking head to become international organisation (6)

Definition: international organisation · charade + deletion

  • a → UN a in French is un
  • supermarket → ESCO the supermarket is Tesco, and its head T has just been sacked

Crossword French is worth banking early: UN, LE, EST and their friends turn up constantly, and 'in France' is the usual flag that one of them is wanted.

6 down ASSEMBLING

Absolute chaos, upset jewellery – piecing it all together? (10)

Definition: piecing it all together? · anagram + charade

  • Absolute chaos → ASSEM absolute chaos is A MESS, and those five letters come back in this order
  • jewellery → BLING bling is showy jewellery

The question mark is earning its place: the clue describes its own construction, since putting the pieces back together is exactly what it is asking you to do.

7 down LIMPET

Floppy elephant with no stuffing for clingy type (6)

Definition: clingy type · charade + outer letters

  • Floppy → LIMP limp is floppy
  • elephant → ET hollow the elephant out and its outer E and T remain

Nothing in the surface is about shellfish: 'clingy type' does the defining, and the elephant is in the clue only because its two ends spell what is wanted.

8 down LEVY

Tax bill came at last – virtually cleared out (4)

Definition: Tax · charade + last letters + outer letters

  • bill came → LE the final letters of bill and of came
  • virtually → VY clear everything out from between the V and the Y

Every word here is doing spelling work rather than meaning work — the bill, the arrival and the 'virtually' are all just letters to be trimmed.

13 down FLOOD

Scourge of old? (5)

Definition: Scourge of old? · anagram + &lit

  • of old → FLOOD the five letters of 'of old' are the five letters of the answer, in a new order

Three words, and every one of them is working twice — the clue defines and spells at the same time, which is what makes it an &lit.

14 down PUERTO RICO

Our copter’s flying around island territory (6,4)

Definition: territory · anagram

  • Our copter’s → OURCOPTER nine letters of the mix; the ’s is only 'is' holding the sentence up
  • island → I I is the map abbreviation for island

'Island' is doing two jobs at once — one letter of the mix, and the picture the answer turns out to fit. The (6,4) tells you how big the mix has to be.

16 down LYRIC

Inadvertently Rickrolled – but only part of the words! (5)

Definition: the words · hidden word

  • Inadvertently Rickrolled → LYRIC the run bridges the two words: inadvertent(LY RIC)krolled

Being Rickrolled is being sent that song when you were expecting something else, so the surface jokes about quoting it accidentally — and it really is quoted here.

19 down WOODLAND

An odd owl disturbed some trees (8)

Definition: some trees · anagram

  • An odd owl → WOODLAND the eight letters of AN ODD OWL, rearranged

'Odd' looks like the standard instruction to take alternate letters and is really part of the material — a reliable Guardian trap worth learning.

21 down NON-TOXIC

Safe ‘n’ sound? Two extinct every second! (3-5)

Definition: Safe · alternate letters

  • ‘n’ sound? Two extinct → NONTOXIC step through N-S-O-U-N-D-T-W-O-E-X-T-I-N-C-T taking the first, third, fifth and so on

'Safe 'n' sound' is a set phrase and that is the trap: the apostrophe-n is not standing in for 'and', it is the first letter you count from.

23 down BANGOR

Welsh city’s non-governmental organisation gathered by bar (6)

Definition: Welsh city · container

  • bar → BAR the bar is taken as it stands and splits after two letters
  • non-governmental organisation → NGO NGO is the standard abbreviation, used by every news bulletin

An abbreviation from the news does the work here; the aid-agency-and-pub scene on the surface is entirely the setter's invention.

24 down STREAM

Flow of vapour engulfing opening of railway (6)

Definition: Flow · container + first letter

  • vapour → STEAM the vapour off a kettle is steam
  • railway → R the first letter of railway, which is also its usual abbreviation

Steam and railways belong together on the surface, and that is what stops you noticing that one is a word to be opened up and the other only a single letter.

26 down MUFF

Greek character with very loud furry garment (4)

Definition: furry garment · charade

  • Greek character → MU mu is a letter of the Greek alphabet
  • very loud → FF fortissimo is written ff on a score

The music abbreviations are worth banking: p is soft, f is loud and ff is very loud, and setters clue them by volume every time.

28 down GOBI

Desert old boy, captured by US soldier (4)

Definition: Desert · container

  • US soldier → GI a GI is an American soldier
  • old boy → OB OB is the standard short form of old boy

'Desert old boy' reads as an instruction to abandon a friend, which is the whole trick: the first word is the definition and has nothing to do with the verb.

29 down SITE

Place, place and, finally, place (4)

Definition: Place · charade + last letter

  • place → SIT to place something is to sit it somewhere
  • place → E the final letter of the word place itself

One word repeated three times over, doing three different jobs — definition, synonym, single letter — and that repetition is the whole of the joke.
