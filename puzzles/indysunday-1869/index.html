Across

1 across PROCLAIM

Shout out for Hearts in matches, yelling "Goal" (8)

Definition: Shout out · charade + middle letters

  • for → PRO pro is the Latin for 'for', as in pro-Europe or the pros and cons
  • matches → C seven letters, so the middle one is the fourth
  • yelling → L another seven-letter word giving up its middle letter the same way
  • Goal → AIM a goal is what you aim at, the target you set yourself

The surface is football commentary, so 'Hearts' looks like the Edinburgh club Heart of Midlothian. It is really the anatomical sense: the very centre of a thing.

6 across MOPISH

Army doctor shifted hips down a bit (6)

Definition: down a bit · charade + anagram

  • Army doctor → MO MO is the Medical Officer, the doctor attached to a British army unit
  • hips → PISH the same four letters in a different order

A dance-floor surface, with 'shifted' pretending to describe the hips while quietly instructing the letters. MO is worth learning: it turns up wherever a service doctor does.

9 across URANUS

American backing Trump's third term in newspaper article - strange world (6)

Definition: strange world · charade + middle letter + last letter

  • Trump's → U the middle letter of the five
  • newspaper → R the last letter of the word
  • article → AN an is the indefinite article used before a vowel
  • American → US US does duty for the United States and for anything American

A clue full of political news, none of which matters: 'third' and 'term' are two separate instructions rather than the phrase they pretend to make together.

10 across STRIKING

Half undressed man checked out (8)

Definition: out · charade + deletion

  • undressed → STRI undressed is stripped, and only the front half of that eight-letter word is taken
  • man checked → KING the man who gets checked is the chess piece the whole game turns on

The definition is the single word at the very end, which is what lets the rest run on as an ordinary sentence. Workers who are out have downed tools.

11 across AFRO

Mop tea from cups (4)

Definition: Mop · hidden word

  • tea from → AFRO four consecutive letters here, running across the gap between the two words

A four-letter answer in a five-word clue leaves nowhere to hide, so the run has to straddle a word break — the one place the reading eye slides straight over.

12 across EDNA O'BRIEN

Irish writer on a bender beginning to imagine novel (4,6)

Definition: Irish writer · anagram + first letter

  • on a bender → ONABENDER nine letters across three words, to be reassembled
  • imagine → I its first letter, joining the other nine

'Novel' sits at the end doing two jobs at once: it is the signal that letters move, and it quietly tells you what the woman being defined wrote for a living.

13 across EVERY SO OFTEN

English really get mushy about love at times (5,2,5)

Definition: at times · charade + container

  • English → E E is the standard abbreviation for English
  • really → VERY really and very are interchangeable intensifiers: really good, very good
  • get mushy → SOFTEN to soften is to go mushy, and this word opens up after its first letter
  • love → O love is nil in tennis, written on the scoreboard as a nought

The surface is a sentimental Englishman, and every word of it is doing a second job. Love for O comes from tennis, where a nought is the score you dread.

17 across WATERING HOLE

Greek character returned call in full pub (8,4)

Definition: pub · container + reversal

  • full → WHOLE the whole of a thing is all of it, and this word splits after its first letter
  • Greek character → ETA eta is the seventh letter of the Greek alphabet, and it goes in backwards
  • call → RING to ring somebody is to call them on the telephone

Two pieces go into the gap rather than one, and the definition is the last word standing alone. 'In full' reads as a phrase and is really two separate instructions.

20 across CONSUMMATE

Prisoners' tea mum arranged, complete with relations (10)

Definition: complete with relations · charade + anagram

  • Prisoners' → CONS a con is a convict, so prisoners are cons
  • tea mum → UMMATE six letters across the two words, reassembled

The definition is the whole tail of the clue, and it is the marriage sense of the verb: a marriage is consummated when the couple first sleep together.

23 across CIAO

Duck after spies see you in Rome (4)

Definition: see you in Rome · charade

  • spies → CIA the CIA is the American foreign intelligence service
  • Duck → O a duck in cricket is a score of nothing, written on the card as a nought

Cricket is where duck means nought, from duck's egg — the round nothing beside a batsman's name. It is one of the commonest single-letter conventions in these puzzles.

24 across TABLE TOP

Legless board work on drug dose (5,3)

Definition: Legless board · charade

  • drug dose → TABLET a tablet is a measured dose of a drug, pressed into a pill
  • work → OP op is short for opus, a composer's work, as in Op. 27

'Legless' does double duty: in the surface it means drunk, and in the definition it means exactly what it says, the flat part with the legs taken off.

25 across TAILOR

Fitter batters you'd expect to make fewer runs otherwise (6)

Definition: Fitter · charade

  • batters you'd expect to make fewer runs → TAIL in cricket the tail is the last few batsmen in the order, picked for their bowling and not expected to score
  • otherwise → OR or is the everyday alternative: this, or otherwise that

'Fitter' looks like a comparative — more fit — and is really a trade, somebody who fits things, which is what lets the definition sit at the front.

26 across PARSEC

Parking behind college a considerable distance (6)

Definition: a considerable distance · charade

  • Parking → P P is the sign on a parking space
  • behind → ARSE the behind is the backside, and arse is the blunt British word for it
  • college → C C is the abbreviation for college in names like Trinity C

A clue that reads as a note about the campus car park is really two abbreviations with a rude word between them — the joke the smooth surface is hiding.

27 across SHANGHAI

Forcibly make a sailor dangle aloft, caught on spar's tip (8)

Definition: Forcibly make a sailor · charade + homophone + first letter

  • spar's tip → S the tip of a word is its first letter, and a spar is a ship's mast or boom
  • dangle → HANG to hang is to dangle from something, suspended by one end
  • aloft → HAI aloft means high up, in the rigging of a ship, and the sound of that word is spelt out here in three quite different letters

The nautical surface is the point: it is exactly the world the definition comes from, where a man could be drugged in a port bar and wake up crewing a ship.

Down

2 down RAREFY

Become thinner? Order a fryer (6)

Definition: Become thinner · anagram

  • a fryer → RAREFY six letters across the two words, to be reassembled

'Order a fryer' reads as a line from a catering catalogue, which is what hides the instruction: order here is a verb aimed at the letters, not at a supplier.

3 down CANTONESE

Chinese toilet you're in starts to test everything (9)

Definition: Chinese · charade + container + first letters

  • toilet → CAN the can is American slang for the lavatory
  • test → T its first letter alone
  • you're → ONES one's does duty for your in formal speech: one does one's best
  • everything → E the first letter again, closing the sandwich

'Chinese toilet' is there to make you picture plumbing. The pivot is 'you're': read it as the formal one's and it becomes the filling that two initials close around.

4 down AISLE

Route through grandstand is covered with beer (5)

Definition: Route through grandstand · container

  • beer → ALE ale is the standard three-letter beer, and it opens up after its first letter
  • is → IS the word is taken at face value and dropped straight in

Grandstands, beer and a route through them make one smooth picture, which is why 'is' reads as grammar rather than as two letters waiting to be used.

5 down MASONRY

Madonna covers numbers Rolling Stones worked on (7)

Definition: Stones worked on · container + reversal

  • Madonna → MARY the Madonna is the Virgin Mary, and she opens up after two letters
  • numbers → NOS nos. is the everyday abbreviation for numbers, and it goes in turned round

'Rolling Stones' is the trap: the eye takes the band name whole, and the clue splits it, keeping 'Rolling' for the wordplay and handing 'Stones' to the definition.

6 down MARCO POLO

Travel writer back from Cairo cut old stuff up (5,4)

Definition: Travel writer · charade + reversal + last letter

  • Cairo → O the last letter of the city's name
  • cut → LOP to lop is to cut something off, usually a branch
  • old → O O is the standard abbreviation for old
  • stuff → CRAM to cram a bag is to stuff it full

The surface is a traveller's itinerary, so 'back from Cairo' reads as a journey home rather than as an instruction to keep one letter. O for old is worth pocketing.

7 down POKER

E.g. stud to sleep with queen (5)

Definition: E.g. stud · charade

  • to sleep with → POKE to poke is coarse slang for having sex with somebody
  • queen → R R is Regina, the Latin queen stamped on British coins and postboxes

The surface is about horses, where a stud is a breeding stable. Keep 'e.g.' in mind wherever it appears: it warns that the words after it name only an instance.

8 down SUNDERED

Cooking nerd used clove (8)

Definition: clove · anagram

  • nerd used → SUNDERED eight letters spanning the two words, to be reassembled

'Clove' looks like the spice sitting in a recipe, which is exactly what the kitchen surface wants. It is the last word of the clue and it is a verb.

14 down ENIGMATIC

Doctor came in GTi without an obvious explanation (9)

Definition: without an obvious explanation · anagram

  • came in GTi → ENIGMATIC nine letters, counting the three of the car badge, to be reassembled

'Doctor' opens the clue looking like the subject of a sentence about a man arriving in a hot hatch, and it is the instruction. GTi is a sporty trim badge.

15 down FLETCHING

Putting feathers on lady's head, going to get clips (9)

Definition: Putting feathers on · container + first letter

  • going to get → FETCHING going to get something is fetching it, and the word opens up after its first letter
  • lady's head → L the head of a word is its first letter

'Clips' reads as a noun in the surface — film clips, hair clips — and it is really a verb, telling one word to take hold of a letter.

16 down PANORAMA

Landscape painter introduced to father, or rather mother (8)

Definition: Landscape · charade + container

  • father → PA pa is the familiar word for a father
  • or rather → NO the phrase is the clue correcting itself in mid-sentence, and the word of refusal you say when you do that is the piece wanted
  • painter → RA RA is a Royal Academician, the crossword's standard painter
  • mother → MA ma is the familiar word for a mother

The middle of the clue is a self-correction: father, then a refusal, then mother. The refusal is a word in its own right, and it supplies the letters between the two parents.

18 down GRAMPUS

Dolphin swims up Margate's harbours on the ebb (7)

Definition: Dolphin · hidden word + reversal

  • swims up Margate's → GRAMPUS seven consecutive letters here, read from the far end backwards and spanning two word breaks

The seaside surface does a lot of work: Margate is a Kent resort with a harbour, so 'harbours' reads as a noun until the moment you need it as a verb.

19 down PAGODA

Trouble that you have to step across climbing ornate tower (6)

Definition: ornate tower · charade + reversal

  • Trouble → ADO ado is fuss or trouble, as in much ado about nothing
  • that you have to step across → GAP a gap in the ground is exactly the thing you have to step over

'Trouble' and the long phrase after it are both clued straight; the only cryptic instruction in the clue is 'climbing', and it applies to the pair of them at once.

21 down SALES

Retailer's exchanges left in envelopes to be returned (5)

Definition: Retailer's exchanges · container

  • envelopes to be returned → SAES an SAE is a stamped addressed envelope, the one you enclose so a reply can be posted back to you; more than one is SAEs
  • left → L L is left, the standard abbreviation on maps and in stage directions

'To be returned' is not an instruction to turn anything round: it is the part of the phrase that makes an envelope a stamped addressed one.

22 down EXTRA

Half of fans in restaurant? More (5)

Definition: More · deletion

  • fans in restaurant → EXTRA the fans that clear the air over a restaurant cooker are its extractors, and only the front half of that ten-letter word is kept

'Fans' wants to be a crowd of supporters. The question mark is the setter flagging that the fans he means are the extractor fans in a restaurant kitchen.
