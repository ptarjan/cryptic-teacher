Across

1 across ABRAHAM LINCOLN

God involved in aggressive act by desperately ill conman as president (7,7)

Definition: president · container + anagram

  • aggressive act → ABH ABH is actual bodily harm, the charge sheet's name for an assault
  • God → RA Ra, the Egyptian sun god, is the crossword's favourite deity
  • ill conman → AMLINCOLN the nine letters of ILL CONMAN in a thoroughly desperate state

ABH is police shorthand for actual bodily harm, which is the aggressive act you are asked to picture. The crooked politician of the surface is pure misdirection: the man wanted here is the honest one.

10 across NICHE

Mediterranean resort with hotel in bay (5)

Definition: bay · container

  • Mediterranean resort → NICE Nice is the resort on the Cote d'Azur
  • hotel → H H is hotel in the NATO alphabet, the pilot's H

Every letter of the NATO alphabet is fair game, and 'hotel' is among the busiest. 'Bay' is architectural here — an alcove in a wall — though the surface would much rather you saw the coast.

11 across TRIUMVIRI

Edging around spot of unrest, runs into seven Roman rulers (9)

Definition: Roman rulers · container + first letter + charade

  • Edging → TRIM trim is the edging sewn round a garment
  • unrest → U the spot taken off the front of UNREST
  • seven → VII seven written as Roman numerals, which the surface quietly recommends
  • runs → R R is the cricket scoreboard's abbreviation for runs

There are two separate wrappings here and the second is disguised as a number. 'Roman' earns its keep twice over, pointing at the numerals as well as at the men in the definition.

12 across OVERARM

Offer excessive backing for military service action? (7)

Definition: action? · charade

  • excessive → OVER over- is the prefix English puts in front of anything done to excess
  • backing for military service → ARM to arm a service is to back it with weapons, and an arm is also a branch of the forces

Both cricket and tennis use the word — a bowler delivers this way and a server strikes this way — which is why the definition can be so vague and still be fair.

13 across SCREECH

Check behind rocks for possible sign of distress (7)

Definition: possible sign of distress · charade

  • rocks → SCREE scree is the loose rock that slides down a mountainside
  • Check → CH ch. is the chess notation for check

'Rocks' looks like an instruction to shake the letters up and is nothing of the kind — it is stone, doing straight duty. Chess supplies the CH, an abbreviation worth memorising.

14 across MOIST

Hardline communist, not a wet (5)

Definition: wet · deletion

  • Hardline communist → MAOIST a Maoist follows Mao's hardline brand of communism
  • a → A the single letter the clue asks you to take out

The letter that leaves is hiding as the humble 'a', which is why it is so easy to read past. 'Wet' is a political word too — a soft Tory — so the whole surface hangs together.

16 across TOWN CRIER

Keep holding note, intermittently acerbic, for old newsreader (4,5)

Definition: old newsreader · container + alternate letters + charade

  • Keep → TOWER the keep of a castle is its central tower
  • note → N n. is the dictionary's abbreviation for a note
  • acerbic → CRI every other letter of aCeRbIc

A keep is a castle's tower, and what it holds is two unrelated scraps rather than one word. 'Old newsreader' is the joke: this man read the news in the street centuries before television existed.

19 across COMMANDER

Officer's subordinate losing head after signal to pause (9)

Definition: Officer · deletion + charade

  • signal to pause → COMMA a comma is the punctuation mark that signals a pause
  • subordinate → UNDER an underling is under you, and the U at its head is the letter that goes

A comma really is the signal to pause, which is a nice piece of punctuation as vocabulary. Beheadings always take the first letter — no clue ever means the last one by 'head'.

20 across LAPEL

50-50 about a sports jacket feature (5)

Definition: jacket feature · container + charade

  • 50-50 → LL L is fifty in Roman numerals, and the clue asks for it twice
  • a → A the letter taken straight out of the clue
  • sports → PE PE is what a school timetable calls sport

'50-50' looks like odds and is really two Roman fifties. PE for sport comes off a timetable and is one of the most reliable two-letter fillers a setter owns.

22 across SEA LEGS

Ease when floating, for example, among marine mammals (3,4)

Definition: Ease when floating · container

  • marine mammals → SEALS seals are marine mammals, the ones barking on the rocks
  • for example → EG eg is the standard abbreviation for 'for example', from the Latin exempli gratia

EG is a workhorse abbreviation and here it hides in plain sight as part of the sentence. The definition is an idiom: you get yours after a day of not falling over.

25 across BETWIXT

Splitting chocolate bar to be eaten by hazard (7)

Definition: Splitting · container

  • hazard → BET to hazard money is to bet it
  • chocolate bar → TWIX Twix is the two-finger chocolate bar

'Hazard' is a verb here — to risk — not the danger it looks like. And a brand name is fair game: setters treat a chocolate bar as ordinary vocabulary.

27 across PRONATION

Way of putting one's foot down in support of country (9)

Definition: Way of putting one's foot down · charade

  • in support of → PRO pro means in favour of, as in pro-European
  • country → NATION a nation is a country thought of as its people

PRO for 'in support of' turns up constantly — pro-democracy, pro-am — and it is the only abbreviation in play. The difficulty is all in the definition, which describes rather than translates.

28 across THANE

Lord of most of Kent region (5)

Definition: Lord · deletion

  • Kent region → THANET the Isle of Thanet is the north-east corner of Kent, where Margate and Ramsgate sit

Thanet is the whole clue once you spot it, and it is the sort of place name worth collecting. Macbeth supplies the definition — he was Thane of Cawdor before he was anything worse.

29 across THICK AS THIEVES

Fat fool keeping almost emaciated First Lady very close (5,2,7)

Definition: very close · container + deletion + charade

  • Fat → THICK thick is fat when you are describing a slice of bread
  • fool → ASS an ass is a fool
  • emaciated → THI thin, with its last letter never written
  • First Lady → EVE Eve was the first lady of all, long before any president had one

'First Lady' points at Washington on the surface and at Eden underneath. The phrase means close in the sense of confiding in each other, never close in distance.

Down

2 down BACTERIUM

I trace criminal drilling behind cell (9)

Definition: cell · anagram + container

  • behind → BUM the behind you sit on
  • I trace → ACTERI the six letters of I TRACE rearranged

'Behind' is the body part, not a position — the one word that looks like a positional instruction and is really fodder. The definition, for once, is literal biology rather than a prison joke.

3 down ARENA

Scene of conflict a long while back (5)

Definition: Scene of conflict · reversal

  • a long while → AN ERA an era is a long while, and these five letters are the whole of the wordplay

The 'a' is not an article propping up the sentence but a letter you write down, which is what makes such a short wordplay so easy to walk past.

4 down ATTEMPTED

Had a go at casual worker after attending inspirational talk (9)

Definition: Had a go at · charade

  • attending → AT to attend something is to be at it
  • casual worker → TEMP a temp is the office's casual worker, hired by the week
  • inspirational talk → TED a TED talk is the modern byword for an inspirational talk

'Inspirational talk' is the giveaway: TED has become the generic name for one, so a setter can call on it without ever printing the capitals.

5 down LOINS

Private area's security measures no good (5)

Definition: Private area · deletion

  • security measures → LOGINS logins are the security measures guarding a computer
  • good → G G is the standard abbreviation for good, as on a school report

A deletion clue makes you name the letter that leaves, and here it is G for good. The surface reads as a gated garden, which is exactly what it is meant to.

6 down NUMERICAL

Damaged manicure on left of digits (9)

Definition: of digits · anagram + charade

  • manicure → NUMERICA the eight letters of MANICURE in a new order
  • left → L L is the everyday abbreviation for left

Digits are fingers on the surface — hence the manicure — and numbers in the definition. That double sense is the joke, and it is why the nail bar looks like part of the meaning.

7 down OPINE

Share view of deforestation's impact? (5)

Definition: Share view · charade

  • impact? → O count the trees left standing once the loggers have finished: zero, which is written O
  • deforestation's → PINE deforestation leaves nothing where the PINE trees stood; that 'nothing' is spelled with the letter O, placed directly before PINE to build the answer

Read the answer as two words and the question mark explains itself: it is a picture of what is missing, not a description of a tree.

8 down NEITHER

Not one in three, sadly (7)

Definition: Not one · anagram

  • in three → NEITHER the seven letters of IN THREE, sadly rearranged

'Not one in three' reads as a statistic, and that is the trap — the numbers are letters. The answer counts two things, not three, so the surface even has the arithmetic wrong.

9 down INFORM

Impart knowledge during class (6)

Definition: Impart knowledge · charade

  • during → IN during the war, in the war — the two prepositions do the same job
  • class → FORM a form is a school class, as in sixth form

British schools number their classes as forms, a convention setters lean on constantly. Nothing here misleads you: the surface picture and the wordplay are made of the same classroom.

15 down TRADEMARK

Jazz on electronic record label (9)

Definition: label · charade

  • Jazz → TRAD trad is the traditional New Orleans jazz of Acker Bilk
  • electronic → E e- is the prefix for anything electronic, as in email
  • record → MARK to mark something down is to record it

'Record' is a verb here, not the vinyl the jazz invites you to imagine, and that switch is the whole difficulty. Trad and e- are both conventions you will meet again next week.

17 down WORKBENCH

Effort to get section of Commons supporting Labour platform (9)

Definition: Labour platform · charade

  • Effort → WORK work is effort, the noun rather than the job
  • section of Commons → BENCH MPs sit on the front and back benches of the Commons

The surface is all Westminster and the answer is all carpentry. The capital L on Labour is the misdirection: the definition wants labour with a small one, the kind done by hand.

18 down IMPLICATE

Point to pet I claim is abandoned (9)

Definition: Point to · anagram

  • pet I claim → IMPLICATE the nine letters of PET I CLAIM, thoroughly rearranged

The setter needs 'is' to make the sentence work and it carries nothing; everything either side of it is either the fodder or the signal to shake it.

19 down CESSPIT

Gratuities dry up where turds gather? (7)

Definition: where turds gather · charade + reversal

  • Gratuities → TIPS tips are the gratuities left on a table
  • dry → SEC sec is what a French wine label calls dry

Sec for dry is worth learning — setters use it constantly, and it never announces that it is French. The question mark apologises for a definition that describes rather than translates.

21 down LITTER

Rubbish batch of small buns? (6)

Definition: Rubbish · double definition

  • Rubbish → the crisp packets and cans dropped in a park
  • batch of small buns? → bun is an old word for a rabbit, and a doe's young all arrive at once

Two unrelated senses share one word, and the second is well buried: 'bun' for a rabbit is old dialect, which is what the setter's question mark is apologising for.

23 down AIOLI

Portion of filo I added, served up as garnish (5)

Definition: garnish · hidden word + reversal

  • filo I added → ILOIA filO I Added conceals five consecutive letters, waiting to be turned round

Two instructions are stacked on one another here, which is why the hiding place is so hard to see: finding the run of letters is only half the job.

24 down SKIPS

Doesn't go to sleep on board ship (5)

Definition: Doesn't go · container

  • ship → SS SS is the steamship prefix painted on a hull
  • sleep → KIP a kip is British slang for a sleep

Kip and SS are both everyday crossword currency, and the surface they build — sleeping aboard — is exactly the picture they describe. Only the definition is pulling the other way.

26 down TITLE

Right winger the heart of Athletic (5)

Definition: Right · charade + middle letters

  • winger → TIT a tit is a small bird, and a bird is a thing with wings
  • Athletic → LE the two letters at the centre of athLEtic

'Right winger' reads as politics or football and is really two separate jobs bolted together — the definition stops after a single word. Athletic is a football club on the surface, which hides the split beautifully.
