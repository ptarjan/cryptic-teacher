Across

7 across SAFE

Those backing hopeless idea off Blue Peter (4)

Definition: Peter · last letters

Peter -> SAFE: peter is old thieves' slang for a strongbox, which is why a safe-breaker is a peterman, so the definition is underworld rather than everyday.

  • hopeless idea off Blue → SAFE the closing letter of each of these four words, taken in the order they are printed
  • Those backing — The ones at the back of each word are its closing characters, so only the final letter of every word named is kept.

Blue Peter is a BBC children's television programme, which is why the two words read as one thing. The cryptic reading splits them, and only the second is the definition.

8 across AMBULATORY

Criminal out by alarm capable of being altered (10)

Definition: capable of being altered · anagram

capable of being altered -> AMBULATORY: in law a will is called ambulatory because it stays revocable and can be changed right up until the testator dies.

  • out by alarm → OUTBYALARM ten letters supplied by these three words exactly as printed
  • Criminal — Something criminal is bent out of true, and letters bent out of true no longer stand in the order they were handed to you.

The word usually means able to walk, and a solver who only knows the hospital sense will not recognise the definition at all: this is the lawyer's use of it.

10 across CHAINS

Bondage gear in SEATs Republican dropped off around noon (6)

Definition: Bondage gear · deletion + container

Bondage gear -> CHAINS: chains are the standard restraint of the dungeon and the fetish shop, so the definition points at what they are used for, not at ironmongery.

  • SEATs → CHAIRS seats and chairs are the same furniture; the capitals belong to the Spanish car marque in the surface and carry no instruction
  • Republican → R r is the standard abbreviation for Republican, and this is the letter that goes
  • noon → N n is the abbreviation for noon, as in 12n on a timetable
  • dropped off — Something dropped off a list has gone from it, so the letter just named leaves before anything else is done.
  • around — What goes around a thing sits outside it, so the letters already assembled close over the one that follows.

The capital letters turn SEAT into the Spanish car maker, which is the only reason the sentence reads as motoring news. Nothing else in the clue needs a car at all.

11 across TOWERING

Very high trail by European circular path (8)

Definition: Very high · charade

Very high -> TOWERING: what towers rises far above everything near it, so the adjective carries height plus the sense of dominating whatever stands below.

  • trail → TOW to tow is to drag something along behind you, which is what trailing a thing amounts to
  • European → E e is the standard abbreviation for European
  • circular path → RING a ring is a circular path, the shape a ring road traces round a town

Nothing here is bent: the surface reads as a walking route on the continent because all three pieces are ordinary words, and the only trick is seeing where each one stops.

12 across RIGHTFUL

Just terrible having to lose face (8)

Definition: Just · deletion

Just -> RIGHTFUL: a rightful heir is the one justice would award the title to, so both words describe a claim that is proper and deserved.

  • terrible → FRIGHTFUL frightful means terrible, in the slightly old-fashioned way an aunt would use it
  • face → F the face is the letter at the front, and that single letter is what goes
  • to lose face — A word's face is the letter it shows first, so losing face here costs it that opening character and nothing else.

"Just terrible" reads as one phrase of complaint, and that is the trap. The first word is the definition and the second is the raw material the wordplay works on.

13 across VESPER

Service Italian scooter after picking it up (6)

Definition: Service · homophone

Service -> VESPER: vespers is the evening office of the church, and the singular names that evening prayer, so this is the religious sense of the word.

  • Italian scooter → VESPER the Vespa is the wasp-shaped motor scooter built in Italy since 1946, and in British speech a final -a and a final -er are the same sound
  • picking it up — Picking something up is catching it by ear, the way you pick up a remark across a room, so what matters is the sound and not the spelling.

British English is non-rhotic: the r at the end of a word is not pronounced, so the scooter and the answer are indistinguishable aloud and only differ on the page.

15 across DISPATCH RIDER

One to deliver notes and perform with Free and Queen (8,5)

Definition: One to deliver notes · charade

One to deliver notes -> DISPATCH RIDER: this is the motorcyclist who carries written messages between headquarters, so "notes" are the messages and "one" is the person.

  • perform → DISPATCH to dispatch a task is to carry it out promptly, the sense in "done with dispatch"
  • Free → RID to rid a place of something is to free it of that thing, and the capital F belongs to the band in the surface only
  • Queen → ER ER is Elizabeth Regina, the monogram a crossword reaches for whenever a queen is mentioned

Free and Queen are 1970s rock bands, which is all the capital letters are for. Underneath, the clue is a straight three-part build with no music in it anywhere.

18 across MY LOVE

Relocate around outskirts of Yeovil? That's dear! (2,4)

Definition: dear · container + outer letters

dear -> MY LOVE: used as a form of address, dear means beloved rather than costly, and it is the same endearment one person calls another.

  • Relocate → MOVE to relocate is to move, whether house or office
  • Yeovil → YL the first and last letters of the town in Somerset, its edges only
  • around — A thing placed around another closes over it from both sides, so these letters split and sit one part in front, one behind.
  • outskirts of — The outskirts of a town are its edges, so the word gives up its first and last letters and nothing in between.

"That's dear!" wants to be a complaint about the price, which is the reading to kill: here it is an endearment. Yeovil is chosen because its edges are the letters wanted.

20 across UNCLOTHE

Take kit off a French fool heroically ended at front (8)

Definition: Take kit off · charade + first letters

Take kit off -> UNCLOTHE: kit is British English for the clothes a person has on, so removing it is precisely what this verb describes.

  • a French → UN un is French for a, the masculine article
  • fool → CLOT a clot is British for an idiot, mild and half affectionate
  • heroically ended → HE the opening letter of each of these two words, in the order printed
  • at front — The front of a word is where it starts, so each word named hands over its opening letter and no more.

Kit is British for clothing or equipment, so the definition is undressing rather than dismantling. The surface is a war story where "heroically ended" reads as a death.

22 across VERANDAS

A red van travelling close to lightless porches (8)

Definition: porches · anagram + last letter

porches -> VERANDAS: a veranda is a roofed platform along the front of a house, which is a porch stretched out, and both words here are plural.

  • A red van → AREDVAN seven letters supplied as printed, ready to be rearranged
  • lightless → S the last letter of the word, and the eighth letter of the mixture
  • travelling — What is travelling is not where it began, and letters that have moved no longer spell what they used to.
  • close to — The close of a word is its final letter, so only the last character of the word named joins the mixture.

The van is nothing but letters, and the dark street exists to hide an instruction: "close to lightless" is the setter asking politely for one character.

24 across CONDOM

Tory Party first to mandate Anti-Labour measure (6)

Definition: Anti-Labour measure · charade + first letter

Anti-Labour measure -> CONDOM: labour here is childbirth rather than the party, and a contraceptive is exactly the measure taken to prevent one.

  • Tory → CON con is short for Conservative, which is what a Tory is on a British ballot paper
  • Party → DO a do is a party in British English, an office do or a birthday do
  • mandate → M the opening letter of the word
  • first — Whatever comes first in a word is the letter it opens with, so that single character is all this word contributes.

Labour and the Conservatives are the two big British parties, so the surface is a political row. The Labour of the definition is the other kind, the one that ends in a birth.

25 across COLLATERAL

Security alert local relayed incorrectly (10)

Definition: Security · anagram

Security -> COLLATERAL: this is the asset a borrower pledges against a loan, which is precisely what a lender means by security.

  • alert local → ALERTLOCAL ten letters from these two words exactly as printed
  • relayed incorrectly — To relay a thing is to lay it down again, and doing that incorrectly puts every letter back in the wrong place.

The surface is a neighbourhood safety bulletin and "alert" does double duty, scenery and raw material at once. The definition is the banking word, not the military one.

26 across NEED

Require massage someone has mentioned (4)

Definition: Require · homophone

Require -> NEED: an everyday synonym in the plain verb sense, so the swap holds in any sentence: you need a visa, you require a visa.

  • massage → NEED to knead is to work dough or a stiff muscle with the hands, and its opening k is silent
  • someone has mentioned — A word somebody has spoken reaches you as a sound, so what matters is how it is said rather than how it is written down.

The silent k is the whole trick: the two words are indistinguishable aloud and share nothing on the page, which is why the definition can sit at the very front.

Down

1 down BAD HAIR DAY

Time of year a beehive might be unmanageable? (3,4,3)

Definition: Time of year a beehive might be unmanageable? · cryptic definition

The clue describes the thing itself: hair heaped up into that shape and refusing to sit down turns the whole of somebody's day into a fight with it.

  • a beehive → the surface offers the insects' nest, which in high summer really is hard to handle; the setter means the piled-up, teased-out hairstyle of the 1960s, which took the same domed shape and the same name
  • Time of year → this reads as a season, which is what keeps the bees alive in the sentence; what the answer needs is one square on the calendar, and the grander phrase is there to send you looking for a longer stretch

There is no wordplay to separate out. Both senses of the hairstyle word are live at once, and the joke lands the moment you drop the insects and keep the hair.

2 down DELIGHTS

Dutch boats crossing line gives great pleasure (8)

Definition: gives great pleasure · charade + container

gives great pleasure -> DELIGHTS: the verb means to fill someone with joy, and the third-person form matches, as in "this news delights me".

  • Dutch → D d is the standard abbreviation for the Dutch language
  • boats → EIGHTS an eight is a racing shell rowed by a crew of eight, the boat of the Oxford and Cambridge race
  • line → L l is the standard abbreviation for line
  • crossing — A boat crossing a line passes through it, so the letter named goes into the middle of the piece beside it rather than alongside.

"Dutch boats" looks like one phrase and a canal full of barges. It is two separate pieces, an abbreviation and a rowing crew, and the line they cross is a single letter.

3 down MASSIF

One small missile rising by fighter in formation (6)

Definition: formation · charade + reversal

formation -> MASSIF: a massif is a compact block of mountain peaks standing together, and geologists call such a mass of rock a formation.

  • One small missile → MASSI one is the Roman I, small is S and a SAM is a surface-to-air missile; those five letters climb back up the column
  • fighter → F F is the letter the air forces put in front of a fighter aircraft, as in F-16
  • rising — In a down entry the letters run from top to bottom, so a piece said to be rising is written back up the column and reads the other way round.

The surface flies an air display, where aircraft travel in formation. The definition is geology, and the whole missile-and-fighter picture is there to keep you looking at the sky.

4 down BLOW OVER

Black and blue balls ... settle down! (4,4)

Definition: settle down · charade

settle down -> BLOW OVER: a row or a scandal that blows over dies away of its own accord, so it is trouble settling rather than a person doing so.

  • Black → B b is the standard abbreviation for black, as on a chess or snooker score
  • blue → LOW feeling blue is feeling low, the melancholy sense rather than the colour
  • balls → OVER in cricket an over is the set of six balls bowled from one end before play switches

"Black and blue" reads as one phrase, a bruise, which is exactly why it is two pieces. The trailing dots keep the quarrel running that the definition tells to subside.

5 down STORKS

Stumped by labours? Not with those delivering babies! (6)

Definition: those delivering babies · charade + deletion

those delivering babies -> STORKS: nursery folklore has these long-legged birds carrying newborns to the door in a sling, so the phrase names them by that job.

  • Stumped → ST st is how a cricket scorecard records a batter stumped by the wicketkeeper
  • labours → WORKS as plural nouns these are the same thing, a body of toil
  • with → W w is the standard abbreviation for with, and it is the letter taken out
  • Not — A thing named and then refused is a thing to leave out, so the letter that follows never reaches the finished word.

"Stumped by labours" reads as a woman worn out in childbirth, which is the setter's joke: the delivery in the definition belongs to the nursery, not the maternity ward.

6 down WREN

Singer about to break into Welsh number (4)

Definition: Singer · container

Singer -> WREN: the bird is a songbird, so the definition works by example, one particular singer standing in for the whole class of them.

  • Welsh → W w is the dictionary abbreviation for Welsh
  • about → RE re means concerning or about, the word at the head of a memo
  • number → N n is the abbreviation for number, as in no. 6
  • to break into — Breaking into a place puts you inside it, so this piece is planted in the middle of the letters standing either side.

The surface offers a phone number in Wales. The answer is a tiny bird whose song is startlingly loud for its size, which is what makes "Singer" fair.

9 down BATTLE CRUISER

Craft beer trails TUC organised (6,7)

Definition: Craft · anagram

Craft -> BATTLE CRUISER: craft is the collective word for a vessel of any size, so the definition names this warship by its class rather than its guns.

  • beer trails TUC → BEERTRAILSTUC thirteen letters supplied by these three words exactly as printed
  • organised — Something organised has been put into a fresh arrangement, and what is being arranged here is letters rather than delegates.

The TUC is the Trades Union Congress, the federation of British trade unions, so the surface is a story about a march. Nothing of it survives but the letters.

14 down EARTHMOVER

Caterpillar of a sort is one amazing lover! (10)

Definition: Caterpillar of a sort · double definition

Caterpillar of a sort -> EARTHMOVER: a digger from that company's range; one amazing lover -> EARTHMOVER: from "did the earth move", so the person who makes it move.

  • Caterpillar of a sort → Caterpillar Inc. is the American maker of yellow heavy plant, and "of a sort" asks for one machine from its range rather than the insect the capital letter hides
  • one amazing lover → the compliment behind "did the earth move for you?", the question asked after sex in a thousand films

The insect is the trap, since caterpillars crawl and tractors are not obviously creatures. The capital letter is doing quiet work, naming a company rather than a species.

16 down ANECDOTE

Ruined coat? Need a little yarn? (8)

Definition: a little yarn · anagram

a little yarn -> ANECDOTE: a yarn is a spun tale, and the diminutive fits because this is the short one told in passing rather than a full story.

  • coat? Need → COATNEED eight letters from these two words, the question mark between them counting for nothing
  • Ruined — Something ruined has been knocked out of shape, and letters knocked out of shape no longer stand in the order they came in.

The surface is a knitter looking at a wrecked garment, and "yarn" is the pivot: it is wool in that picture and a story in the definition.

17 down DROWNING

Swallowing without restraint primarily? (8)

Definition: Swallowing without restraint primarily? · &lit + container + first letter

Read straight, the clue describes what happens to someone taking in water with nothing at all holding it back, which is exactly the state the answer names.

  • Swallowing → DOWNING to down a pint is to swallow it in one, so this is the drinker's word for it
  • restraint → R the opening letter of the word, and the letter that ends up enclosed
  • without — Without carries its older sense of outside, as in the wall without the castle, so the piece named wraps around what follows instead of being missing from it.
  • primarily — What is primary comes first, so only the opening letter of the word beside it is taken.

The whole sentence is definition and wordplay at once, which is what the question mark nods at: it describes the thing and builds it in the same four words.

19 down ORACLE

Old contest involving Liberal know-it-all (6)

Definition: know-it-all · charade + container

know-it-all -> ORACLE: an oracle is the source with an answer for everything, so the word is used mockingly of a person who claims the same authority.

  • Old → O o is the standard abbreviation for old
  • contest → RACE a race is a contest, the plainest sort there is
  • Liberal → L l is the abbreviation for Liberal, the party's letter on a British ballot paper
  • involving — To involve someone is to draw them in, so the letter named lands inside the word standing next to it.

Calling somebody a know-it-all is an insult, while the priestess at Delphi was consulted in deadly earnest. The setter is enjoying the distance between the two.

21 down COCKLE

Male fowl left on top of eggs one shelled (6)

Definition: one shelled · charade + first letter

one shelled -> COCKLE: a cockle is a small edible bivalve, so the phrase defines it by what it wears, a creature that comes with its own shell.

  • Male fowl → COCK the male bird of any poultry yard
  • left → L l is the standard abbreviation for left
  • eggs → E the first letter of the word, taken from the top of it
  • on top of — In a down entry a piece on top of another sits above it, and the top of a word is its first letter: the phrase is doing both jobs at once.

The surface is a farmyard, with the bird settled over a clutch. The definition steps sideways to the shellfish, the one the nursery rhyme counts as cockleshells.

23 down EROS

Love God? Explain racists or suffering for starters! (4)

Definition: Love God · first letters

Love God -> EROS: the Greek god of desire, so the definition names his portfolio, the deity in charge of love rather than anybody in it.

  • Explain racists or suffering → EROS the opening letter of each of these four words, in the order they are printed
  • for starters — Starters come at the beginning of things, so the opening character of each word in the run is all that is wanted.

The surface is an angry question about hatred, which is why those particular four words are lined up. The answer is the Greek god whose statue stands in Piccadilly Circus.
