Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,871

Setter: Filbert · Published: Sunday 4 January 2026 · Difficulty: Tough (harder than 90% of the puzzles here) · Grid: 15×15

Across

1 across BOATHOOK

Crooked pole musketeer shortened in novel (8)

Definition: Crooked pole · container + deletion

  • novel → BOOK a novel is a book, and this one opens up after its first two letters
  • musketeer → ATHO Athos is one of Dumas' three musketeers, minus his last letter

Two words here look like anagram signals and neither is one: 'Crooked' belongs to the definition, and 'novel' means book, not new.

5 across REEBOK

Antelope's smell masked by even nastier one (6)

Definition: Antelope · container

  • smell → REEK a reek is a strong smell, and it opens up after three letters
  • even nastier one → BO BO is body odour, the nastier smell the clue is measuring against

The one you have heard of is the sportswear brand, which took its name from the animal. 'Even nastier one' is another smell, not another beast.

9 across REMINDER

Jog about with bodyguard (8)

Definition: Jog · charade

  • about → RE RE is the standard shorthand for about, as at the head of a memo
  • bodyguard → MINDER a minder is British slang for a hired heavy who keeps you safe

'Jog' is the noun, not the run: the sort you give someone's memory. Both halves of the wordplay are conventions worth keeping.

10 across CANNON

Gun shot that ricochets to hit second target (6)

Definition: Gun · double definition

  • Gun → the artillery sense, the heavy barrel on a carriage
  • shot that ricochets to hit second target → the billiards and snooker sense, where the cue ball strikes two others in turn

The clue reads as one continuous description of artillery, so the seam between the two definitions falls in an awkward place: after the very first word.

12 across OASES

Watering holes in prime spots along road, she sees (5)

Definition: Watering holes · prime letters

  • road, she sees → OASES run through these letters ignoring the comma and the spaces, and keep the second, third, fifth, seventh and eleventh

Nothing else in the grid counts like this, so the giveaway is 'prime' sitting where a solver expects a word about hiding or shuffling.

13 across PHONE CALL

Ring Spooner's dropping down from pine tree? (5,4)

Definition: Ring · spoonerism

  • dropping down from pine tree → CONE FALL what drops from a pine is a cone, and a drop downwards is a fall
  • Spooner's → PHONE CALL swap the opening sounds of the two words above and this is what the ear hears, spelt the ordinary way

The question mark admits the stretch, since a falling cone is not a set phrase. 'Ring' is the noun a solver reads as jewellery first.

14 across TORA TORA TORA

Three peaks followed by a war film (4,4,4)

Definition: war film · charade

  • peaks followed by a → TORA a tor is a rocky hilltop, the Dartmoor kind, with A written after it
  • peaks followed by a → TORA the same pairing again, because the clue counted three of them
  • peaks followed by a → TORA and a third time, which fills the grid

Tors are the bare granite outcrops of Dartmoor, so 'peaks' is honest. Tora was the Japanese code word radioed at Pearl Harbor, and the title repeats it.

18 across ALL AND SUNDRY

Everyone's bags in laundry switched at the start (3,3,6)

Definition: Everyone · container + reversal

  • laundry switched at the start → ALUNDRY LAUNDRY trades its opening L and A, and what results opens up after two letters
  • bags → LANDS to bag a prize is to land it, so bags and lands are the same verb

'Everyone's' hides its verb — the apostrophe-s is 'is', joining the definition to the wordplay — and 'bags' is that verb for catching something, not luggage.

21 across NO WORRIES

Today trucks not starting? That's OK (2,7)

Definition: That's OK · charade + deletion

  • Today → NOW now is today, the moment you are standing in
  • trucks → ORRIES a truck is a lorry in Britain, and the L is left behind

The question mark flags the odd reading: 'trucks' is the American word and lorries the British one, and making that swap is the step the clue leaves you.

23 across SOCHI

Winter Olympics venue incredibly cold with hail (5)

Definition: Winter Olympics venue · charade

  • incredibly → SO so is the plain intensifier: incredibly cold is so cold
  • cold → C C is the abbreviation for cold, the letter on a tap
  • hail → HI to hail someone is to greet them, and hi is the shortest greeting there is

'Incredibly' looks like an anagram signal and is only an intensifier, and 'hail' looks like weather to match 'cold' but is a greeting.

24 across ENOUGH

Canteen ought to accommodate as many as required (6)

Definition: as many as required · hidden word

  • Canteen ought → ENOUGH the six letters run straight on out of one word and into the next, across the gap

'Canteen ought' is a deliberately awkward pairing, and awkwardness is the tell: those two words are next to each other only to carry letters.

25 across DOMINATE

Party at home hosted by friend's boss (8)

Definition: boss · charade + container

  • Party → DO a do is a party, the British word for a bash
  • friend → MATE a mate is a friend, and it opens after its first letter
  • at home → IN someone at home is in, which is the standard crossword pairing

Three everyday British conventions in one clue: a do for a party, a mate for a friend, in for at home. All three come up again and again.

26 across SIERRA

Chain I blunder into when reversing (6)

Definition: Chain · container + reversal

  • when → SA when is AS, and that little pair is turned round before anything goes into it
  • I → I the letter stands for itself, at face value
  • blunder → ERR to blunder is to err, the verb crosswords use for a mistake

'Chain' pulls you towards jewellery or high-street shops; it is the geographer's chain of mountains. And 'when' is fodder, not joinery.

27 across SMUGNESS

Dragon losing a head, showing complacency? (8)

Definition: complacency · charade + deletion

  • Dragon losing a → SMUG Smaug is the dragon of The Hobbit, and the A he loses leaves four letters
  • head → NESS a ness is a headland: Dungeness and Sheerness are both named for one

'Losing a head' reads as a beheading, and it is not one. The dragon loses the letter A, and 'head' is a separate word doing separate work.

Down

1 down BARTON

Lowly lord keeps Tesla in farmyard (6)

Definition: farmyard · container

  • Lowly lord → BARON a baron sits on the bottom rung of the peerage, which is what makes him lowly
  • Tesla → T T is the symbol for the tesla, the unit of magnetic field strength

Tesla is the car in the surface and the physicist in the wordplay, which is the whole trick of the clue.

2 down ALMOST

Just about time to support charity that's collected nothing (6)

Definition: Just about · container + charade

  • charity → ALMS alms are charity in the old sense: money handed to the poor
  • nothing → O O is nothing, the zero written as a circle
  • time → T T is the physicist's abbreviation for time

The clue names the single letter first and the word it props up second, which is a habit of down clues worth getting used to.

3 down HANDS-DOWN

Crew eat as if it's a picnic (5-4)

Definition: as if it's a picnic · charade

  • Crew → HANDS hands are a ship's crew, as in all hands on deck
  • eat → DOWN to down food or a drink is to get it inside you fast

The surface has a ship's crew making light work of a meal. The answer is the phrase for winning without effort, which is a different sort of easy.

4 down ONE-UPMANSHIP

Human pope struggling with sin, trying to be better (3-9)

Definition: trying to be better · anagram

  • Human pope → HUMANPOPE nine letters of fodder, and the papal picture is only there to hold them
  • sin → SIN three more, which makes the twelve the enumeration asks for
  • struggling → anagram indicator

A pontiff wrestling with temptation is exactly the picture 'trying to be better' wants you to read as moral improvement rather than as scoring points off a rival.

6 down ELATE

Make happy story up with treasure at the end (5)

Definition: Make happy · reversal + last letter

  • story → ELAT a story is a TALE, and here it climbs the entry instead of running down it
  • treasure → E the closing letter of TREASURE, and nothing else of it is wanted

Filbert leaves 'treasure' whole in the surface and takes a single letter from it, which is the commonest way an odd letter gets smuggled into a short answer.

7 down BUNGALOW

Cork area, cheap house that's typical in Ireland (8)

Definition: house that's typical in Ireland · charade

  • Cork → BUNG a bung is the stopper you cork a barrel with
  • area → A A is the standard abbreviation for area
  • cheap → LOW a low price is a cheap one, and low does the job of cheap on its own

Cork is an Irish city and county, which is what the surface is selling; in the wordplay it is the stopper in a bottle.

8 down KING LEAR

Broken ankle curtailed girl's play (4,4)

Definition: play · anagram + deletion

  • ankle → ANKLE five letters of fodder, taken at face value rather than as an injury
  • girl → GIR GIRL gives up its last letter, leaving three more letters to shuffle
  • Broken → anagram indicator

'Broken ankle' is a whole picture in itself, and that is what stops you counting letters. The definition is the single word at the end, easy to walk straight past.

11 down GO DOWN A STORM

Bananas grown at Sodom are happily taken up ... (2,4,1,5)

Definition: happily taken up · anagram

  • grown at Sodom → GROWNATSODOM twelve letters of fodder, which is exactly what the enumeration asks for
  • Bananas → anagram indicator

Fruit farming in a biblical city is a picture built purely to hold letters, and nothing in the answer has anything to do with either.

15 down ARRESTING

Very noticeable Mark's inside with his feet up (9)

Definition: Very noticeable · middle letters + charade

  • Mark → AR take the M and the K off MARK and this pair is what is left
  • with his feet up → RESTING a man with his feet up is taking a rest

'Feet up' in a down clue looks certain to be a reversal instruction. It is not: nothing here turns round, and the man is simply having a sit down.

16 down SAUNDERS

Associate of French commandos hides below (8)

Definition: Associate of French · container

  • commandos → SAS the SAS is the Special Air Service, the British army's special forces regiment
  • below → UNDER below and under are the same idea in two words

The capital F makes 'French' look like a nationality, which is the whole point: it is a surname, and the pair were a fixture of British comedy for twenty years.

17 down BLOWHOLE

Punch weed, catching left nostril that suddenly spurts (8)

Definition: nostril that suddenly spurts · charade + container

  • Punch → BLOW a blow is a punch, one landed with a fist
  • weed → HOE to weed a bed is to hoe it, and the tool gives the letters
  • left → L L is the standard abbreviation for left

The definition runs from 'nostril' to the end of the clue, which is easy to miss when 'left nostril' reads as such a natural phrase.

19 down SCRAPE

Fix electronic stuff that's discarded, swapping parts (6)

Definition: Fix · charade + reversal

  • stuff that's discarded → SCRAP scrap is what gets thrown out, the stuff a scrapyard buys by weight
  • electronic → E E is the standard prefix for electronic, as in e-mail

E-scrap is a real term for discarded electronics, and that is the joke: the clue hands you the compound, then asks for its halves the other way round.

20 down PIXELS

Small screen parts taken up by EastEnders' Lexi Pearce (6)

Definition: Small screen parts · hidden word + reversal

  • EastEnders' Lexi Pearce → PIXELS six consecutive letters run from the end of the soap's name into the character's, and the entry reads them from the bottom up

EastEnders is the BBC's long-running London soap, so the italics are a title and not an instruction. 'Small screen' is doing two jobs: telly, and a display.

22 down ROGER

King and I upset, queen understood (5)

Definition: understood · charade + reversal

  • King → R R is Rex, the Latin king that crosswords lean on
  • I → OGE the I of psychology is the EGO, turned over here
  • queen → R R is Regina as well, so one letter serves both monarchs

Rex and Regina give the same letter, which is why a clue can put a king at one end and a queen at the other and still be symmetrical.
