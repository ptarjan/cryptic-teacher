Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,864

Across

1 across BENEFACTRESS

Name muscle-bound actor as Fairy Godmother (12)

Definition: Fairy Godmother · container + charade

  • muscle → BEEF beef is muscle, both the meat and the bulk a weightlifter puts on
  • Name → N N is the standard abbreviation for name, the one printed on a form
  • actor → ACTRESS a female actor is an actress, which is how the setter gets the feminine ending

'Muscle-bound' looks like one word describing the performer. It is really two jobs at once: a word meaning muscle, and an order to tie something up inside it.

8 across CLUSTER

Collect Charlie Sheen on Wall Street? (7)

Definition: Collect · charade

  • Charlie → C C is Charlie in the NATO alphabet used by pilots and radio operators
  • Sheen → LUSTER a sheen is a soft gloss — lustre in Britain, luster in America

Charlie Sheen is an actor and Wall Street is one of his films, so the surface reads as a name and a title. The clue wants the two words separated and the spelling changed.

9 across BUDGE UP

University replacing last of cheap parking to make room (5,2)

Definition: make room · substitution + charade

  • cheap → BUDGET a budget airline or a budget hotel is a cheap one
  • University → U U is the standard abbreviation for university, and it stands in for the T at the end of the previous word
  • parking → P P is the standard abbreviation for parking, the letter on the road sign

The surface is a university buying up cheap parking. The trap is reading 'last of cheap' as the letter P: it is the last letter of a word that MEANS cheap.

11 across UPPER CASE

Capital, winning an event (5-4)

Definition: Capital · charade

  • winning → UP a side that is winning is up, as in two goals up
  • an → PER per stands in for 'a' or 'an' in phrases like five pounds a head
  • event → CASE a case is an instance or occurrence — in any event, in any case

'Capital' is the trap: with 'winning an event' beside it, it wants to be a city or a fortune. Here it is the printer's sense, a capital letter.

12 across HORDE

Migrants coming through Sandwich or Deal (5)

Definition: Migrants · hidden word

  • Sandwich or Deal → HORDE Sandwich and Deal are neighbouring towns on the Kent coast, and five letters in a row inside these three words are what is wanted

Sandwich and Deal look like lunch and a bargain, which is the joke. Both are real Kent towns a few miles from the Channel, so the surface reads as a news bulletin.

13 across INTO

Hot on piebald horse, wiping head (4)

Definition: Hot on · deletion

  • piebald horse → PINTO a pinto is the American horse with irregular white patches — piebald, in other words

'Hot on' is the definition and looks like more of the riding picture, an eager rider on a spotted pony. Both halves pull the same way, which is what makes the join hard to find.

14 across ADAMANTINE

Very tough mother opposed to ibuprofen finally admitted to A&E (10)

Definition: Very tough · container + charade + last letter

  • A&E → AE A&E is Accident and Emergency, the British hospital department an American would call the ER, and its two letters open and close the word
  • mother → DAM a dam is an animal's mother, the breeder's word that sits beside sire
  • opposed to → ANTI someone opposed to a plan is anti it
  • ibuprofen finally → N the closing letter of the drug's name and nothing else of it

The whole surface is a casualty ward, which is what makes 'A&E' look like scenery rather than two letters with work to do.

18 across TURTLEDOVE

True love juggling with two donations - just the first of those? (10)

Definition: True love juggling with two donations - just the first of those? · &lit + anagram + first letters

  • True love → TRUELOVE eight of the ten letters come from these two words
  • two donations → TD the opening letter of each of these two words makes up the shortfall

An all-in-one: every word is wordplay, and the same words read straight are the definition. The dash is the hinge, and 'those' looks back rather than forward.

19 across PSST

Secret alert, with ship in port unloading gold (4)

Definition: Secret alert · container + deletion

  • gold → OR OR is gold in heraldry, the tincture named on a coat of arms — and it is what has to come out
  • port unloading gold → PT the harbour is PORT, and losing those two letters leaves a shell of two
  • ship → SS SS is the prefix on a steamship's name

The surface is a smuggling story, gold coming ashore at night. The definition is the noise you make to catch one person's attention without anyone else hearing.

21 across NOHOW

Recognise Thatcherite Chancellor on the radio? Not at all (5)

Definition: Not at all · homophone + charade

  • Recognise → NO to recognise something is to know it, and said aloud that word loses its silent K
  • Thatcherite Chancellor → HOW Geoffrey Howe was Margaret Thatcher's Chancellor of the Exchequer, Britain's finance minister, and his name is said exactly like the question word

Both halves are heard rather than spelled, and neither is written the way it sounds. The surface asks whether you would place the man's voice on air.

23 across GYMNASIUM

Space to work out my amusing anagrams (9)

Definition: Space to work out · anagram

  • my amusing → GYMNASIUM these two words hold all nine letters between them

'Work out' does double duty in the surface, where it means solving the puzzles. The definition needs the other sense, the one done in trainers.

24 across EMULATE

Fast runner passed rival (7)

Definition: rival · charade

  • Fast runner → EMU the Australian bird cannot fly but sprints at close to 50km/h
  • passed → LATE the late Mr So-and-so is the one who has passed away

'Fast runner passed' reads as one athlete overtaking another, which is the whole misdirection: 'passed' is the euphemism for died, not the move on the track.

25 across NAURUAN

Pacific Islander going east or west? (7)

Definition: Pacific Islander · palindrome

  • Pacific Islander → a person from one particular island republic — all the definition offers, since the wordplay has to supply the spelling
  • going east or west → start at the right-hand end and the letters arrive in exactly the order they do from the left, so the word is its own mirror

Nothing is built here and nothing is turned round to become something else — the clue simply says the word reads the same both ways, and the definition narrows the Pacific to one tiny republic.

26 across FEED ONE'S FACE

Charge fellow spreading faeces for pig (4,4,4)

Definition: pig · charade + anagram

  • Charge → FEE the charge made for a service is its fee
  • fellow → DON a don is a fellow of an Oxford or Cambridge college, which is where the crossword's 'fellow' usually comes from
  • faeces → ESFACE the six letters of the British spelling, reordered

The surface is the farmyard job nobody wants, and 'Charge fellow' reads as an accusation. The definition is the single verb at the end.

Down

1 down BLUE PETER

Peel tuber for cooking programme (4,5)

Definition: programme · anagram

  • Peel tuber → BLUEPETER these nine letters are the whole of the material; nothing else in the clue joins in

Definition by example: a programme in general, one particular show in fact. The BBC has run this children's magazine since 1958, named after the flag a ship flies when about to sail.

2 down NOTARY

Official witness to any Republican dodges (6)

Definition: Official witness · anagram

  • to any → TOANY five of the six letters come straight off the page, waiting to be reordered
  • Republican → R R is the standard abbreviation for a US Republican

The surface has a politician evading awkward questions. 'Official witness to any Republican' reads as one long job description, which is where the definition looks longer than it is — it stops after two words.

3 down FORWARDED

Cross journalist covering conflict sent on to another (9)

Definition: sent on to another · container + charade

  • Cross → FORD to cross a stream on foot is to ford it
  • journalist → ED ED is the crossword's standing journalist, short for editor
  • conflict → WAR a war is an armed conflict

'Cross' wants to be the adjective meaning annoyed, matching the angry journalist of the surface. It is the verb, and a ford is a place where you cross.

4 down CABLE

Long line of cars? (5)

Definition: Long line of cars? · cryptic definition

  • Long line of cars → the reading the surface offers is traffic: a tailback of vehicles stretching away up the road
  • of cars → the setter's cars are the ones that hang from a moving steel rope up a mountainside or a San Francisco hill, and it is the rope being described

No wordplay to separate out here — the whole clue is one description, and the joke is that a line of cars can mean a mountain lift rather than a traffic jam.

5 down REDSHANK

Dark hens fancy one with colourful legs (8)

Definition: one with colourful legs · anagram

  • Dark hens → REDSHANK these two words hold all eight letters, and nothing else in the clue is needed

The surface is a poultry keeper's note, so 'one with colourful legs' reads as another bird in the run. It is the definition, and the bird it describes is wild.

6 down SHEAR

Fleece as thin as tights, you'd say (5)

Definition: Fleece · homophone

  • as thin as tights → SHEAR tights thin enough to see through are sheer, and that word said aloud is written a second way

'Fleece' looks like the noun, a woolly thing being compared with tights. It is the verb, and the joke is that a fleece is exactly what comes off under the clippers.

7 down ACQUAINTANCE

Charming party held by great guy you hardly know (12)

Definition: guy you hardly know · container + charade

  • Charming → QUAINT quaint is charming in an old-fashioned, slightly odd way
  • party → ANC the ANC is South Africa's governing political party, the African National Congress
  • great → ACE ace is slang for great or first-rate, and it splits to take the others in

'Held by great guy' reads as a single phrase about one generous host, which is the trap: 'great' is doing its own separate job and the definition is only the last four words.

10 down PRESENTIMENT

Gift wrapping season, November's advance warning (12)

Definition: advance warning · container + charade

  • Gift → PRESENT a present is a gift, and only its final letter is left on the far side of the wrapping
  • season → TIME a season is a particular stretch of the year, and any stretch like that is a time
  • November → N N is November in the NATO alphabet; the possessive 's is only the sentence joining up

'Gift wrapping season' says December and 'November' says the month before it, so the whole surface is a Christmas picture. Neither month is what the wordplay wants.

15 down MOVEMENTS

Pieces' pieces? (9)

Definition: Pieces' pieces? · cryptic definition

  • Pieces' → the first word means whole works — a symphony, a sonata, a quartet — each written and performed as one piece of music
  • pieces? → the second means the self-contained sections such a work divides into, the numbered parts a concert programme lists with their tempo markings

There is nothing to take apart: the clue is one idea said twice, using the same word in two musical senses and letting the apostrophe do the joining.

16 down INSTITUTE

Start filling Aussie's truck, carrying melons to the north (9)

Definition: Start · container + reversal + charade

  • filling → IN whatever is filling something is in it
  • melons → STIT melons is slang for a woman's breasts, and in a down clue those four letters are climbed rather than read down
  • Aussie's truck → UTE a ute is an Australian pickup, short for utility vehicle

The loading-bay surface holds together so well that the definition at the front reads as part of it. 'Start filling' is two separate jobs sitting side by side.

17 down BLOWHARD

Thump insensitive bigmouth (8)

Definition: bigmouth · charade

  • Thump → BLOW a thump is a blow, the kind struck with a fist
  • insensitive → HARD a hard man is insensitive, unmoved by anything around him

Two plain synonyms laid end to end with nothing to mark the join, and a surface that reads as one continuous description of an unpleasant person.

20 down LACUNA

Break - nearly all want one in Rome (6)

Definition: Break · deletion + charade

  • want → LAC to want something is to lack it, and nearly all of that word is these three letters
  • one in Rome → UNA una is Italian for one, so what is wanted is the modern Roman's own word, not a Roman numeral

A lacuna is the gap left where something has dropped out, so the definition and the missing letter of the wordplay are telling the same joke.

22 down HOUSE

Where shoes may have to be removed, socks protecting you? (5)

Definition: Where shoes may have to be removed · container

  • socks → HOSE hose is the old collective word for socks and stockings, still alive in hosiery
  • you → U U is the textspeak short form of you, and crosswords use it freely

The surface is one tidy hallway scene. The question mark is the warning: 'socks protecting you' has stopped describing and started instructing.

23 down GLEAN

Pick up good tip (5)

Definition: Pick up · charade

  • good → G G is the standard abbreviation for good, as on a school report
  • tip → LEAN to tip is to lean, the way a ladder tips against a wall

'Pick up' looks like part of the same instruction as 'tip', as though something were being lifted and tilted. It is the definition, standing on its own.
