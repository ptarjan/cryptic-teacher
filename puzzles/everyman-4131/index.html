Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,131

Setter: Everyman · Published: Sunday 21 December 2025 · Difficulty: Brutal (harder than 96% of the puzzles here) · Grid: 15×15

Across

7 across GENEVA

Almond cake consumed in Jarbidge, Nevada (6)

Definition: Almond cake · hidden word

  • Jarbidge, Nevada → GENEVA six letters run straight on from the end of the town's name into the start of the state's, comma and all

Jarbidge is a real Nevada town, and it is in the clue for one reason: its last two letters and the state's first four sit side by side.

8 across RIESLING

Vino - cheese (not started) - then cocktail (8)

Definition: Vino · charade + deletion

  • cheese (not started) → RIE brie is the cheese, and lopping off the letter it opens with leaves three
  • cocktail → SLING a sling is a long mixed drink — gin sling, Singapore sling

The dashes make the clue look like three items on a wine list. Only the middle one is a word to be trimmed.

9 across TORTILLA

Everything I left over? An omelette and a cake (8)

Definition: An omelette and a cake · charade + reversal

  • Everything → ALL the everyday word for the whole of something
  • I → I the pronoun is taken at face value, as the single letter it is written with
  • left → TROT a Trot is a Trotskyist, British shorthand for someone well over on the political left

'Left' looks like the turning instruction an across clue would use; it is a person instead, and the little word after it does the turning.

10 across ASPICS

A small image's showing jellies (6)

Definition: jellies · charade

  • A → A the clue's own first word, taken as the letter it is
  • small → S S for small, as on a clothing label
  • image → PIC a pic is a picture, the short form used in every newspaper office
  • 's → S the apostrophe-s reads as a possessive on the surface and hands over a letter in the wordplay

Everyman is fond of letting an apostrophe-s do a letter's work; here it is the S that makes the answer plural, which the enumeration never warns you about.

11 across ADVOCAAT

Champion mostly having swallowed a liqueur (8)

Definition: liqueur · container + deletion

  • Champion mostly → ADVOCAT to champion a cause is to advocate it; docked of its last letter that leaves seven
  • a → A the article is swallowed whole, as the single letter it is written with

Wordplay and drink share a root: advocaat is Dutch for a lawyer, and the thick egg-and-brandy drink was reckoned good for a pleader's throat.

12 across TIPPLE

Before curt excuse, spill drink (6)

Definition: drink · charade + deletion

  • spill → TIP to tip a bucket over is to spill what is in it
  • curt excuse → PLE a plea is an excuse, shortened here by a letter

The surface has someone apologising and knocking a glass over. Read as instructions it is the excuse that gets cut short, not the glass.

13 across STEWED FRUIT

Drunk at Conference perhaps suggesting compote (6,5)

Definition: compote · charade

  • Drunk → STEWED stewed is long-standing slang for drunk
  • at Conference perhaps → FRUIT Conference is the commonest British pear variety, so a particular pear stands for the general kind

The surface is a delegate who has enjoyed the hospitality. The capital letter is the tell: Conference is a pear, not a venue.

18 across DIGS IN

Home, once more begins to eat (4,2)

Definition: begins to eat · charade

  • Home → DIGS digs are lodgings — the British word for rented rooms you go home to
  • once more → IN the words ask for home a second time, and someone who is in is at home

The comma makes 'Home, once more' read as a weary arrival, when it is really an instruction to give a word for home twice over.

20 across APPLE PIE

Yankiness yardstick? (5,3)

Definition: Yankiness yardstick? · cryptic definition

  • Yankiness → the invented noun asks what makes a thing American, which sends you hunting for a flag, a president or a state
  • yardstick? → a yardstick is what other things are measured against, and the phrase wanted is the one Americans use for something that could not be more American

'As American as apple pie' is the set phrase, so the yardstick turns out to be a pudding. There is no wordplay here to find — the whole clue is one definition, told slant.

22 across BICARB

Baking powder composed of bismuth, calcium and rubidium (6)

Definition: Baking powder · charade

  • bismuth → BI Bi is the chemical symbol for the element
  • calcium → CA Ca is the chemical symbol for the element
  • rubidium → RB Rb is the chemical symbol for the element

'Composed of' is doing double work: a compound is composed of elements, and so is this answer — three symbols off the periodic table, in order.

23 across LICORICE

Sweet bits of lardons in coulis on arborio, perhaps (8)

Definition: Sweet · charade + first letters

  • bits of lardons in coulis on → LICO the opening letters of the four words after the instruction, taken in the order they come
  • arborio, perhaps → RICE arborio is the short-grain Italian variety used for risotto, so the named type stands for the grain itself

The surface is a menu of French and Italian oddments, and 'on' looks like joinery holding it together. It is the fourth word to give up a letter.

24 across TURMERIC

New recruit, Miles, gets curry powder (8)

Definition: curry powder · anagram

  • recruit → RECRUIT all seven letters of the word go into the mix as they stand
  • Miles → M M is the standard abbreviation for miles, as on a road sign

Miles is a man on the surface and a unit of distance in the wordplay; the two commas are what make him look like a person being introduced.

25 across NIACIN

Necessity in alimentation; compound; indispensable nutrient, primarily! (6)

Definition: Necessity in alimentation; compound; indispensable nutrient, primarily! · &lit + first letters

  • Necessity in alimentation; compound; indispensable nutrient → NIACIN six words, six opening letters, read straight off in the order they are written

Every word is doing both jobs at once: read as a sentence the clue describes the vitamin, and read as an instruction each word gives up its initial.

Down

1 down SECONDS

Supports - ticks (7)

Definition: Supports · double definition

  • Supports → to second a motion is to back it, which is what a supporter at a meeting does
  • ticks → each tick of a clock marks off one unit of time, sixty of them to the minute

The dash is the whole disguise: two unrelated senses of one word sit either side of it, one from the committee room and one from the clock face.

2 down BEETROOT

Red insect with horn's noise entertaining King (8)

Definition: Red · charade + container

  • insect → BEE the three-letter insect a setter reaches for first
  • horn's noise → TOOT the noise a horn makes, written the way comics spell it
  • King → R R is the crossword king, from the Latin Rex stamped on coins and documents

The insect and the horn belong to a garden afternoon, and 'entertaining King' sounds like a royal visit — nothing in the surface admits that a colour is being built.

3 down PAELLA

Lapel splotched with a saffrony mixture (6)

Definition: saffrony mixture · anagram

  • Lapel splotched with a → PAELLA five letters from the garment and one stray article, six in all, re-sorted into a Valencian rice dish

The surface is a stained jacket, which is exactly what a plate of this balanced on your knee would produce.

4 down DECANTER

A little prude can't eradicate provider of vino (8)

Definition: provider of vino · hidden word

  • prude can't eradicate → DECANTER eight letters run without a break from the end of the first word, through the whole of the second and into the third; the apostrophe is no obstacle

'Can't eradicate' reads as a stubborn stain and the prude as the person complaining about it. The three words are only a letter run.

5 down SLAP-UP

'Lavish' starter for lunch covered in young insects sent back (4-2)

Definition: 'Lavish' · container + reversal

  • young insects → PUPAS a pupa is an insect at its second stage, between grub and adult
  • starter for lunch → L the opening letter of the meal, and nothing else of it

'Starter' pulls double duty: on the surface it is the first course of the lavish meal, in the wordplay the first letter of the word after it.

6 down KNUCKLE

Rotten luck, Ken: a horrible kind of 'sandwich' (7)

Definition: a horrible kind of 'sandwich' · anagram

  • luck, Ken → KNUCKLE seven letters from the two words, the K turning up twice, redealt

The surface is one friend commiserating with another, and the quotation marks are the only hint that the sandwich is not lunch.

8 down ROASTED GARLIC

Pungent ingredient presenting unfortunate gastric ordeal (7,6)

Definition: Pungent ingredient · anagram

  • gastric ordeal → ROASTED GARLIC thirteen letters across the two words, redealt to fill the seven and the six

Both halves of the clue describe the same dinner from opposite ends — what went into it, and what it did afterwards.

14 down WINE BARS

Catch intro to elegant music where one may drink elegantly (4,4)

Definition: where one may drink elegantly · charade + first letter

  • Catch → WIN to catch a prize is to win it
  • intro to elegant → E the first letter of the word, and no more of it
  • music → BARS written music is divided into bars

The word 'elegant' works twice over: once to give up its opening letter, and once, as an adverb, to say what sort of drinking place is meant.

15 down ICE CREAM

Dessert: the writer emits shrill laugh audibly (3,5)

Definition: Dessert · charade + homophone

  • the writer → I a setter referring to himself in a clue is 'the writer', which on paper is one letter
  • emits shrill laugh → SCREAM to let out a shrill cry of laughter is to scream
  • audibly → ICE CREAM said at speed the two words run together and a listener writes down something else entirely

The children's chant 'I scream, you scream, we all scream for ice cream' is built on exactly this: identical sounds, quite different spellings.

16 down BILIOUS

Every so often, Linus boils angrily - being this? (7)

Definition: being this? · anagram + alternate letters

  • Every so often, Linus → IU the second and fourth letters of the name, picked out in turn
  • boils → BOILS all five letters go into the mix, to be re-sorted along with the two above

'Boils angrily' looks like the definition and is really fodder plus its instruction. The definition is the two words after the dash, pointing back at the whole picture.

17 down BISCUIT

'Unfinished, Cubistic' - confused, perhaps - snap! (7)

Definition: snap! · anagram + deletion

  • Unfinished, Cubistic → BISCUIT the art word gives up its closing letter, and the seven that remain are redealt

The quotation marks and the dashes make the first two words look like a gallery label for a half-painted canvas. They are only letters.

19 down SPAMMY

Plans sent back: Everyman's persistently irritating online (6)

Definition: persistently irritating online · charade + reversal

  • Plans → MAPS plans in the draughtsman's sense — street plans are maps
  • Everyman's → MY the setter signs himself Everyman, so his own possessive is these two letters

Everyman writes himself into his own wordplay: whenever this puzzle says 'Everyman's', it means 'my'. Worth remembering — he does it most weeks.

21 down PECANS

Gutted: canapés - mixed nuts (6)

Definition: nuts · anagram + deletion

  • canapés → PECANS the seven letters lose the one at the dead centre, and the six left over are re-sorted

'Gutted' is also British for bitterly disappointed, which is what the surface wants: the canapés have gone and only the bowl of nuts is left.
