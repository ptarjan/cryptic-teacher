Across

1 across MATERNAL

Like a mother bird swallowed by heartless mammal (8)

Definition: Like a mother · deletion + container

Like a mother → MATERNAL: maternal is the adjective for anything belonging to a mother, from instinct to the grandmother on that side.

  • mammal → MAAL MAMMAL with the MM at its centre cut away
  • bird → TERN a tern is the sea bird every setter keeps in the drawer
  • swallowed by — To be swallowed is to go down inside the thing doing the swallowing, so one word sits in the middle of the other.
  • heartless — Heartless is without a heart, and a word's heart is the pair of letters at its dead centre.

The word being operated on is 'mammal' itself, which is why nothing in the clue names a particular beast — and the bird ends up inside the mammal rather than the other way round.

5 across CUSHY

Soft copper retiring (5)

Definition: Soft · charade

Soft → CUSHY: a cushy number is an easy, undemanding job, which is the colloquial sense of soft that means requiring no effort.

  • copper → CU Cu is copper on the periodic table
  • retiring → SHY a retiring person is a shy one

'Retiring' reads like an instruction to turn something round and is doing nothing of the sort — it is the plain adjective. 'Copper' is the metal, not the policeman.

9 across LURID

Sensational learner freed after university (5)

Definition: Sensational · charade

Sensational → LURID: lurid means shocking in a garish, tabloid way, the sense of sensational that means designed to shock rather than excellent.

  • learner → L L is the plate on a learner driver's car
  • university → U U is the standard abbreviation for university
  • freed → RID to be rid of something is to be freed of it
  • after — After is about position rather than time: what it introduces has already been written down when the next piece arrives.

L for learner and U for university are two of the abbreviations a solver meets weekly — worth learning once and keeping for good.

10 across PINK NOISE

Work on spike in sound machine's output (4,5)

Definition: sound machine's output · anagram

sound machine's output → PINK NOISE: sleep machines play white, brown or pink noise, pink being the deeper-toned hiss with the bass turned up.

  • on spike in → PINK NOISE the nine letters of ON SPIKE IN thoroughly worked over
  • Work — To work dough or clay is to push it into an entirely new shape, which is what the letters here are asked to do.

'Work on' reads as a single verb and is really an instruction plus the start of its own raw material, which is why the fodder is so hard to see.

11 across ELOPED

Last of hare sprang and ran off (6)

Definition: ran off · charade + last letter

ran off → ELOPED: to elope is to run off with someone to marry in secret, so the plain phrase covers the answer's narrower romantic sense.

  • hare → E the last letter of hare
  • sprang → LOPED to lope is to run in long easy bounds
  • Last of — Last of asks for the final letter alone, so the animal contributes its tail rather than the whole of itself.

A single letter taken off an animal and a past tense taken whole — and the definition is the pair of words at the far end, where 'off' looks like part of the picture.

12 across REVEREND

Republican first lady to rip into minister (8)

Definition: minister · charade

minister → REVEREND: used as a noun, a reverend is the clergyman who holds the title, so it names a minister of the church.

  • Republican → R R is the letter beside a Republican's name on a US ballot
  • first lady → EVE Eve was the first lady of all, long before the White House
  • to rip → REND to rend cloth is to rip it

'First lady' is the Book of Genesis, not the White House, and that single switch turns a story about American politics into one about the pulpit.

13 across BOARD SHORTS

Get on with photos, having secured right clothes (5,6)

Definition: clothes · charade + container

clothes → BOARD SHORTS: board shorts are the long surfing trunks worn in the water, so 'clothes' names them by the broad category they belong to.

  • Get on → BOARD to board a bus is to get on it
  • photos → SHOTS a photographer's shots are photos
  • right → R R is the map and stage abbreviation for right
  • secured — To secure something is to hold it fast, and a letter held fast is one trapped in the middle of a word.

Both long pieces are everyday words hiding shorter synonyms: 'get on' is boarding rather than making progress, and 'photos' are shots rather than pictures.

16 across MAC

Computer club loses backing (3)

Definition: Computer · deletion

Computer → MAC: Apple's Macintosh has been shortened to Mac for forty years, so the brand's nickname stands in for the machine itself.

  • club → MACE a mace is a spiked club, and also the one a mayor carries
  • loses backing — The backing of a word is its rear, so losing it means the final letter is dropped.

'Loses backing' sounds like a sponsor walking away and is really a letter walking off the end. 'Club' is the weapon, not the nightspot.

18 across ERA

Wild unclothed period (3)

Definition: period · deletion

period → ERA: an era is a long stretch of history with a character of its own, so 'period' names it as a span of time.

  • Wild → FERAL a feral cat is a wild one
  • unclothed — Unclothed means stripped of whatever covers it, so the letters at each end of the word come off.

In three letters there is nowhere to hide, so the whole solve is spotting that 'Wild' is a word to write down rather than an instruction to shuffle.

19 across FILET MIGNON

Spread, if melting over steak (5,6)

Definition: steak · anagram + charade

steak → FILET MIGNON: filet mignon is the small round cut taken from the end of the tenderloin, so 'steak' defines it by the kind of cut it is.

  • if melting → FILET MIGN the nine letters of IF MELTING in a new arrangement
  • over → ON butter spread over the toast is butter spread on it
  • Spread — Butter spread across toast has been pushed out of its neat shape, and so have these letters.

'Melting' looks like the instruction and is really the material; the word doing the shuffling is 'Spread', sitting at the front where it reads as a breakfast.

21 across CALLIOPE

Muse about work, boring friends endlessly (8)

Definition: Muse · charade + container + deletion

Muse → CALLIOPE: Calliope is the eldest of the nine Muses and the one who presides over epic poetry, so the general word is defined by one of them.

  • about → C c is circa, the abbreviation for about a date
  • friends → ALLIES allies are friends, and endlessly they give up their S
  • work → OP op is a composer's opus, a work
  • boring — Boring is drilling a hole through something, so whatever does it ends up buried in the middle.
  • endlessly — Endlessly means without an end, so the final letter of the word never gets written down.

'Boring friends' is the surface at its most inviting and neither word means what it seems: the friends are allies and the boring is a drill.

22 across RANKLE

To annoy deer, cut grass back (6)

Definition: To annoy · charade + deletion + reversal

To annoy → RANKLE: to rankle is to go on irritating, a slight that keeps stinging long after the moment it was given.

  • deer → ELK an elk is the largest of the deer
  • grass → NARK a nark is a police informer, one who grasses
  • cut — Something cut is made shorter, so the word gives up the letter at its end.
  • back — This entry runs across the grid, so back means reading the assembled letters from right to left.

'Grass' is the informer of a police drama rather than the lawn a deer would be eating — the one word that has to leave the surface picture entirely.

25 across MENAGERIE

Pennsylvania city pursuing absurd new mega zoo (9)

Definition: zoo · anagram + charade

zoo → MENAGERIE: a menagerie is a private collection of wild animals kept for show, the older travelling-show word for what we now call a zoo.

  • new mega → MENAG new shortens to N, and N with MEGA makes these five letters
  • Pennsylvania city → ERIE Erie sits on its lake in the north-west corner of the state
  • pursuing — To pursue is to come along behind, so the city's letters follow rather than lead.
  • absurd — Something absurd makes no sense in the order it arrives, so these letters are to be taken out of theirs.

The lake is the famous one, so the city is easy to miss — and 'new' is an abbreviation rather than an adjective, which is what makes the fodder the wrong length at first glance.

26 across IRONS

Decreases caddie's burden (5)

Definition: Decreases · double definition

To de-crease a shirt is to press it; and a golfer's irons are the numbered clubs weighing down the caddie's bag.

  • Decreases → split it after the two opening letters and it is de-creases, which is what a hot flat one does to a shirt
  • caddie's burden → the numbered clubs a golfer's caddie hauls round eighteen holes

Two definitions, no letters to assemble. The first is a word cut in an unexpected place, and once you see the cut the whole clue stops being about shrinking anything.

27 across CHESS OPENINGS

Starters of certain horizontal entries (seven) help around enclosing, striking the centre (5,8)

Definition: Starters of certain horizontal entries (seven) help around enclosing, striking the centre · &lit + first letters + charade + container + deletion

Every word does both jobs at once, and the straight reading is true of the grid: seven horizontal answers begin with a chess term.

  • certain horizontal entries (seven) → CHES the first letters of certain, horizontal, entries and seven
  • help → SOS an SOS is the distress call, the international shout for help
  • enclosing → PENNING penning sheep is enclosing them, and the -ing forms match
  • Starters of — A starter comes first, so each word in the list gives up nothing but the letter it begins with.
  • around — Around means wrapped about a thing, so one piece is split and laid on either side of another.
  • striking the centre — To strike something out is to delete it, and the centre of a word is the single letter at its middle.

Seven across answers open with a chess word: MATE, ELO, PIN, BOARD, FILE, RANK and MEN. Read straight, the clue also describes what these do on a board.

1 down MALLEABLE

Flexible English isle up to support shopping complex (9)

Definition: Flexible · charade + reversal

Flexible → MALLEABLE: a malleable metal can be hammered into a new shape, and the word carries the same sense of a person who bends under pressure.

  • shopping complex → MALL a mall is the covered shopping complex
  • English → E E is the usual one-letter abbreviation for English
  • isle → ABLE Elba, Napoleon's isle, climbing the grid
  • up — In a down entry up runs from the bottom of the grid towards the top, so the island is read the other way round.
  • support — To support a thing is to stand beneath it, so this piece is written under the one it holds up.

Elba is the crossword's standard small island, and it is doing double duty here: the surface wants a holiday brochure while the letters want a staircase.

2 down TURBO

Swimmer clipped part of motor (5)

Definition: part of motor · deletion

part of motor → TURBO: the turbo is the turbocharger bolted to an engine, so the definition names the answer as one component of the whole.

  • Swimmer → TURBOT a turbot is a large flatfish, and a fish is a swimmer
  • clipped — Something clipped has had its end trimmed off, so the last letter never reaches the grid.

'Swimmer' is one of those deliberately vague nouns — athlete, fish or bird — and the enumeration is what tells you which. Trim the fish and the engine part is left.

3 down REDHEAD

Soldiers finished eating hot duck (7)

Definition: duck · charade + container

duck → REDHEAD: the redhead is a diving duck of North America, named for the drake's coppery head, so the clue defines by species.

  • Soldiers → RE RE is the Royal Engineers, the army's sappers
  • finished → DEAD dead is finished in the most final sense
  • hot → H H is the hot tap and the hot setting on a dial
  • eating — Whatever is eaten goes inside the eater, so the single letter is swallowed by the word beside it.

'Duck' looks like the cricketing nought or the verb for stooping, and is neither: the redhead is a North American diving bird.

4 down ALPS

Alto records wide range (4)

Definition: wide range · charade

wide range → ALPS: the Alps are Europe's great mountain chain, so 'range' defines by example and 'wide' merely measures it.

  • Alto → A A is the score's abbreviation for the alto voice
  • records → LPS LPs are long-playing records

Both halves are music, which is what makes the mountains such a surprise — and LP for a record is a piece of vocabulary worth banking early.

5 down CONFESS

Admit company head's squeezed head of finance (7)

Definition: Admit · charade + container + first letter

Admit → CONFESS: to confess is to admit a fault or a crime, the sense of admitting that concedes guilt rather than letting somebody in.

  • company → CO Co is the abbreviation on every company's letterhead
  • head → NESS a ness is a headland — Loch Ness, Sheerness, Inverness
  • finance → F the first letter of finance
  • squeezed — Something squeezed is forced into a space that was not expecting it, so the letter goes in between the ones already written.
  • head of — The head of a word is the letter it begins with, so only finance's opening letter is taken.

'Head' is used twice over and means something different each time: once a headland, once simply the letter a word starts with. Spot that and the boardroom surface falls away.

6 down SPIDER-MAN

Mired in kerfuffle punching cross superhero (6-3)

Definition: superhero · anagram + container

superhero → SPIDER-MAN: Marvel's wall-crawler is one named member of the class, so the general word points at a single example of it.

  • cross → SPAN to cross a river is to span it
  • Mired → IDERM the five letters of MIRED in a fresh order
  • in kerfuffle — A kerfuffle is a disorderly commotion, and letters caught up in one come out in no particular order.
  • punching — To punch something is to drive straight into it, so the shuffled letters end up inside the word next to them.

'Cross' is the verb, not the adjective the surface wants, and 6-3 tells you the answer is hyphenated — a small piece of information the enumeration hands over for free.

7 down YIELD

Give up gym, stripping to exercise topless (5)

Definition: Give up · charade + middle letter + deletion

Give up → YIELD: to yield is to surrender ground or stop resisting, the sense of giving up a position rather than abandoning a habit.

  • gym → Y GYM stripped of the G and the M keeps only its middle letter
  • to exercise → WIELD to wield power is to exercise it
  • stripping — Stripping takes off what covers a thing, so the word is left with nothing but its middle.
  • topless — The top of a down entry is its first letter, and topless means that letter is missing.

Two shortenings in five letters, each cut a different way: one word loses both ends, the next loses only its head. Note 'exercise' as the verb wield, not a workout.

8 down UNLEARNT

Forgotten in Nice, a dilapidated rental (8)

Definition: Forgotten · charade + anagram

Forgotten → UNLEARNT: a lesson unlearnt is one that never stuck or has been let go again, which is what forgetting amounts to.

  • in Nice, a → UN in Nice they say un for a, and a French word is what 'in Nice' asks for
  • rental → LEARNT the six letters of RENTAL rebuilt
  • dilapidated — A dilapidated building has fallen apart, and so have these letters — not one of them stays where it was put.

Naming a French city is the standard signal to answer in French, and here it makes the tiniest of words: two letters that mean 'a'.

14 down AT A GLANCE

A children's game run through immediately (2,1,6)

Definition: immediately · charade

immediately → AT A GLANCE: something clear at a glance is taken in the instant you look at it, so the phrase means as soon as it is seen.

  • A → A the clue's opening word, written down as the letter it is
  • children's game → TAG tag is the playground chase every child knows
  • run through → LANCE to lance is to run something through with a point

'Run through' is a spear rather than a rehearsal, and the opening 'A' is a letter rather than an article — the two places this clue expects you to look straight past.

15 down HELIPORT

Base fellow essentially mainlining wine (8)

Definition: Base · charade + middle letters

Base → HELIPORT: a heliport is the base a helicopter operates from, so 'base' names it as the station where the aircraft is kept.

  • fellow → HE a fellow is a he, one of the shortest synonyms in the game
  • mainlining → LI the two letters at the middle of MAINLINING
  • wine → PORT port is the fortified wine passed to the left
  • essentially — Essentially asks for the essence of a word, the letters sitting at its dead centre and nothing on either side.

'Base fellow' reads as an insult, which is the trap — 'base' is a noun and belongs to the definition, while 'mainlining' is raw material rather than the drug reference it pretends to be.

17 down CONSENSUS

Scams nurse with suspicious agreement (9)

Definition: agreement · charade

agreement → CONSENSUS: a consensus is the general view a group settles on, the collective sense of agreement rather than a signed contract.

  • Scams → CONS a con is a scam and a con artist runs one
  • nurse → EN EN is the enrolled nurse, a standing crossword shortening
  • suspicious → SUS sus is the British slang clipping of suspicious

Three shortenings in a row, and the middle one is the one to bank: EN for enrolled nurse turns up in these puzzles far more often than any nurse does.

19 down FRINGES

Loud band with extremely egregious haircuts (7)

Definition: haircuts · charade + outer letters

haircuts → FRINGES: a fringe is hair cut straight across the forehead, what Americans call bangs, so the definition names a style of cut.

  • Loud → F f is forte, the score's instruction to play loud
  • band → RING a band round a finger or a barrel is a ring
  • egregious → ES the outermost letters of egregious
  • extremely — Extremely points at the extremes of the word, the letter at each far end and nothing in between.

'Loud band' paints a rock group and neither word is musical in that way: one is the dynamic marking f, the other a plain metal hoop.

20 down IRANIAN

Current translation of Narnia from the Middle East (7)

Definition: from the Middle East · charade + anagram

from the Middle East → IRANIAN: Iran is one country of that region, so a broad geographical phrase points at a single nationality within it.

  • Current → I I is the physicist's symbol for electric current
  • Narnia → RANIAN the six letters of NARNIA rearranged
  • translation of — A translation carries the same content across into a different form of words, and here the same letters come across in a different order.

'Current' is the electrical one, I, and not the adjective the surface offers. Narnia has a wardrobe rather than a border, which is the joke the geography sets up.

21 down COMIC

Performer to share amplifier with another? (5)

Definition: Performer · charade

Performer → COMIC: a comic is the stand-up who performs the act, the noun sense rather than the adjective meaning funny.

  • with another → CO doing a thing with another person is doing it co- — co-pilot, co-star, co-write
  • amplifier → MIC a mic is what a stand-up's voice goes through
  • to share → surface only: the verb that puts two acts at one microphone and makes the picture work

The joke is the hyphen you cannot see. Someone sharing a microphone would be a co-mic, and the question mark is the setter owning up to it.

23 down KNOWN

Popular first pair of knickers to have (5)

Definition: Popular · charade + first letters

Popular → KNOWN: a known name is one the public has heard of, and it is that public recognition, not being liked, that 'popular' means here.

  • knickers → KN the two letters knickers starts with
  • to have → OWN to own a thing is to have it
  • first pair of — A pair is two, so the word hands over only the two letters it begins with.

The underwear is there for the surface and for its first two letters only, which is a favourite Independent trick: the ruder the noun, the smaller its job.

24 down PEEP

Wee partridge's introduction? (4)

Definition: Wee partridge's introduction? · &lit + charade + first letter

The whole clue reads straight: the thin cheep a newly hatched partridge makes is its first announcement of itself.

  • Wee → PEE a wee is a pee, the nursery word for it
  • partridge's → P the letter Partridge begins with
  • introduction — An introduction comes at the front, so the bird's name contributes only the letter it opens with.

Read it twice. Once every word is wordplay, and once the same words are a plain description — which is what an &lit is, and why the question mark is there.
