Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,049

Across

1 across ANGELOU

Writer playing along with EU (7)

Definition: Writer · anagram

Writer → ANGELOU: definition by example — Maya Angelou, the American poet and memoirist who wrote I Know Why the Caged Bird Sings.

  • along → ALONG anagram fodder, five letters
  • EU → EU the European Union by its usual two initials
  • playing — An actor playing a part is being somebody other than themselves, and letters told to play will not stay as the clue sets them down.

The surface sounds like a musician joining in with Brussels, so 'playing' passes as something a person does rather than an instruction to the letters.

5 across LACED UP

As corsets are spiked all over (5,2)

Definition: As corsets are · charade

As corsets are → LACED UP: a corset closes by threading cord through its eyelets and hauling it tight, which is the everyday phrase for that fastening.

  • spiked → LACED a drink spiked with spirits has been laced
  • all over → UP when time is up it is all over — the sense in 'the game's up'

The surface dresses every word as one remark about uncomfortable corsetry; both halves of the answer are ordinary idioms wearing that costume.

9 across GRASP

Master goldsmith’s lead file (5)

Definition: Master · charade + first letter

Master → GRASP: to grasp a subject is to master it, the mental sense of taking firm hold rather than the hand's.

  • goldsmith’s lead → G the letter at the head of the word goldsmith
  • file → RASP a rasp is a coarse file for wood or metal
  • lead — A lead is what goes in front, so it asks for the letter standing at the front of the word before it.

'Master goldsmith' is a job title in the surface and a seam in the parse — the split falls in the middle of the phrase, which is the whole disguise.

10 across CAN-OPENER

Is cricketer able to provide a handy means of access for consumers? (3-6)

Definition: a handy means of access for consumers · charade

a handy means of access for consumers → CAN-OPENER: the little tool that lets a shopper get at tinned food, so 'access' means opening a tin.

  • able → CAN able to do a thing = can do it
  • cricketer → OPENER in cricket the opener is the batter who goes in first, at the start of an innings

The question mark licenses the leap from a sportsman's usefulness to something in the kitchen drawer, and 'handy' does double duty as praise and as description.

11 across BLINDFOLD CHESS

You won’t see board members taking part in this criminal bloc’s NHS fiddle (9,5)

Definition: You won’t see board members taking part in this · anagram

You won’t see board members taking part in this → BLINDFOLD CHESS: the players are given no sight of the board and call their moves from memory.

  • bloc’s → BLOCS a bloc is a group acting together, plus the S of the possessive
  • NHS → NHS the National Health Service, Britain's state health system, by its initials
  • fiddle → FIDDLE a fiddle is a swindle, which is what the surface is describing
  • criminal — A criminal breaks the rules and leaves things out of place, which is exactly what befalls the letters that follow.

'Board members' are chessmen rather than directors, and the answer runs on into 12 across. Playing without sight of the board is a real competitive discipline.

13 across AGLOW

Ruddy good to get in a depressed state! (5)

Definition: Ruddy · container

Ruddy → AGLOW: a ruddy face is flushed and warm with colour, which is how a face described as glowing looks.

  • a → A the clue's own 'a', taken as a letter
  • good → G g = good, the standard abbreviation
  • depressed state → LOW a low is a slump — a depressed state of mind or of the market
  • get in — Getting in means arriving inside something, so the letter named before it lands within the words that follow.

'Ruddy' is also the polite British stand-in for a stronger word, which is what the exclamation mark is selling, and the depressed state is a mood, not a country.

15 across NIGHTFALL

Evening: almost time to collapse (9)

Definition: Evening · charade

Evening → NIGHTFALL: the point at which the light goes and darkness arrives, which is what 'evening' names when it means dusk rather than the whole span.

  • almost → NIGH nigh is the old word for near — 'the end is nigh'
  • time → T t = time, as in the physics formulae
  • collapse → FALL a government or a wall that collapses is said to fall

The colon makes the clue read as a diary note about somebody flagging at the end of the day, while every word after it is quietly supplying letters.

18 across DISCREDIT

Department head is getting recognition? Rubbish! (9)

Definition: Rubbish! · charade + first letter

Rubbish! → DISCREDIT: 'to rubbish' is British informal for dismissing something as worthless, and destroying its standing is precisely that.

  • Department head → D the letter at the front of Department
  • is → IS the clue's own 'is', taken at face value as two letters
  • recognition → CREDIT credit is the recognition given for work done
  • head — The head of anything is its top or its front, so this asks for the letter the previous word starts with.

'Rubbish!' at the end reads as the setter's own snort about the department. It is the definition, and it is a verb.

19 across RATED

Right wants a familiar ex-PM judged (5)

Definition: judged · charade

judged → RATED: to rate something is to judge its quality and put a value on it, the way a critic rates a film.

  • Right → R r = right, the standard abbreviation
  • a → A the clue's own 'a', taken as a letter
  • familiar ex-PM → TED Edward Heath, prime minister from 1970 to 1974, was known to everyone as Ted

British prime ministers turn up in crosswords by their familiar first names as often as by their surnames, and 'Right wants' dresses the whole thing as a party dispute.

21 across PLATE TECTONICS

Police state can’t, without a new study of underground movement? (5,9)

Definition: study of underground movement · anagram + deletion

study of underground movement → PLATE TECTONICS: the geology of how the earth's crustal plates drift, collide and grind past one another far below the surface.

  • Police state → POLICESTATE anagram fodder, eleven letters
  • can’t, without a → CNT CAN'T gives up its A, leaving three letters for the mix
  • without a — To be without something is to lack it, so the letter named goes missing before anything else is done.
  • new — What is made new is made over out of the same material, and these letters are rebuilt into different words.

The surface is a political complaint and the question mark admits the definition is playful — the movement is geological, and the answer runs on into 23 across.

25 across SANATORIA

Top grade rations ordered by advanced health centres (9)

Definition: health centres · anagram

health centres → SANATORIA: the Latin plural of sanatorium, the residential clinics where patients were sent to convalesce, especially from tuberculosis.

  • Top grade → A the top grade awarded in an exam is an A
  • rations → RATIONS anagram fodder, seven letters
  • advanced → A a = advanced, as in the A-levels taken at eighteen in England and Wales
  • ordered — Something ordered has been put into an arrangement chosen by somebody else, not the one it arrived in.

Two separate As, one an exam grade and one the advanced level of those same exams, sit either side of the fodder — which is how the letter count reaches nine.

26 across FRILL

Gathering close to Gulf Stream (5)

Definition: Gathering · charade + last letter

Gathering → FRILL: in dressmaking a gathering is cloth drawn up on a thread into ruffles, which is exactly what edges a collar or cuff.

  • close to Gulf → F the final letter of Gulf
  • Stream → RILL a rill is a small stream or brook
  • close to — A close is an ending, so this asks for the letter that finishes the word after it.

'Gulf Stream' is one thing in the surface and two instructions in the parse, and the current in the Atlantic has nothing to do with either.

27 across TAX-FREE

A cross between tense and loose, as, say, kid’s clothing is (3-4)

Definition: as, say, kid’s clothing is · charade

as, say, kid’s clothing is → TAX-FREE: children's clothes carry no VAT in Britain, so they are sold free of the tax charged on most goods.

  • tense → T t = tense, the grammarian's abbreviation
  • A cross → AX a cross is the letter X, with the clue's own A in front of it
  • loose → FREE an animal that is loose has got free of its pen
  • between — What lies between two things has one on either side of it, so these letters take the middle position.

'A cross between' is the everyday phrase for a hybrid, and here it means precisely what it says: a cross, sitting between two other pieces.

28 across HERONRY

Old king protecting (or regressing) fledgling colony (7)

Definition: fledgling colony · container + reversal

fledgling colony → HERONRY: the wood or reed bed where herons nest together season after season, so the colony is full of young birds.

  • Old king → HENRY eight English kings bore the name, which makes it the crossword's default old king
  • (or regressing) → RO the little word 'or', read backwards
  • protecting — To protect something is to stand all round it, so the king's letters close over what comes next.
  • regressing — To regress is to go back the way you came, and in an across answer that means reading those letters right to left.

The brackets make '(or regressing)' look like an editorial aside, which is exactly where two of the letters are hiding.

Down

1 down ALGEBRA

In which X often appears with a broken leg support (7)

Definition: In which X often appears · charade + anagram

In which X often appears → ALGEBRA: the branch of mathematics that writes unknown quantities as letters, and x is the traditional first choice.

  • a → A the clue's own 'a', taken as a single letter
  • broken leg → LGE the three letters of LEG, reset in another order
  • support → BRA the undergarment is the crossword's standard support
  • broken — Something broken has come apart and been put back together wrongly, which is what happens to the three letters it sits next to.

X is the schoolroom's unknown, so the opening reads as a question about kisses or films. A support that turns out to be underwear is a joke this puzzle never tires of.

2 down GLADIOLUS

Happy soul dancing round Ireland’s top flower (9)

Definition: flower · charade + anagram + first letter

flower → GLADIOLUS: the tall spike of funnel-shaped blooms grown from a corm, named from the Latin for a little sword after its leaves.

  • Happy → GLAD glad = happy, and these four letters lead off unshuffled
  • soul → SOUL anagram fodder, four letters
  • Ireland’s top → I the letter Ireland begins with
  • dancing round — A dancer whirling round the floor finishes facing every way but the one they set off in, and so do these letters.
  • top — The top of a word printed down the page is where it starts, so only Ireland's opening letter is wanted.

'Flower' is the word solvers learn to distrust — in a cryptic it is often a river, something that flows — and this time it is honestly a bloom.

3 down LOPED

Was wolf-like lecturer against journalist’s opinion piece? (5)

Definition: Was wolf-like · charade

Was wolf-like → LOPED: to lope is to run with the long, loose, unhurried stride a wolf covers ground with, so the past tense answers 'was'.

  • lecturer → L l = lecturer, the academic abbreviation
  • journalist’s opinion piece → OPED an op-ed is the signed column a paper prints opposite its editorial page
  • against — To lean one thing against another is to set them side by side, so this letter simply sits in front of what follows.

The surface is a newsroom quarrel between a lecturer and a journalist, which is why nothing in it suggests an animal crossing open ground.

4 down UNCROWNED

Like a single draughts piece – 100 now under review (9)

Definition: Like a single draughts piece · anagram

Like a single draughts piece → UNCROWNED: in draughts, the British name for checkers, a piece is crowned into a king on reaching the far row.

  • 100 → C C is the Roman numeral for a hundred
  • now under → NOWUNDER anagram fodder, eight letters
  • review — A review looks at something again and hands it back rearranged, which is what becomes of the nine letters in front of it.

'Under review' looks like one phrase doing one job. Only 'review' is the instruction; 'under' has quietly been handed over as letters.

5 down LINED

Scored, though showing signs of age! (5)

Definition: Scored · double definition

Scored → LINED: a scored surface carries ruled marks and an aged face carries creases, so both senses come back to the same word for a mark.

  • Scored → to score a surface is to cut or rule straight marks across it
  • showing signs of age → a face that has weathered years is creased and furrowed

'Scored' pulls the surface towards football or sheet music, and the exclamation mark sells the sting: one word that can flatter and insult.

6 down CAPACITOR

Test player, international, opens another player’s electrical store (9)

Definition: electrical store · charade + container

electrical store → CAPACITOR: the circuit component that holds a charge between two plates and gives it back when the circuit needs it.

  • Test player → CAP a Test is an international cricket match, and a player who appears in one wins a cap
  • another player → ACTOR the other kind of player treads the boards, and this word parts after two letters
  • international → I i = international, the standard abbreviation
  • opens — To open something is to make a gap in it, and the letter named just before slips into that gap.

Two kinds of player stand side by side and the surface asks you to read both as sportsmen, which is what keeps the one on the stage out of sight.

7 down DANTE

Poet’s slowish to drop a name (5)

Definition: Poet · deletion

Poet → DANTE: definition by example — Dante Alighieri, whose Divine Comedy makes him the poet of medieval Florence.

  • slowish → ANDANTE the musical direction for a moderate, walking pace — on the slow side of the tempo markings
  • a → A the first letter to go is the clue's own 'a'
  • name → N n = name, the usual abbreviation
  • to drop — Dropping something lets it fall away, so the two letters named after this are simply lost from the word.

Tempo markings are worth collecting for crosswords, and 'slowish' is doing all the work here: the whole answer is sitting inside one of them.

8 down PERUSAL

Country’s introducing a Latin examination (7)

Definition: examination · charade

examination → PERUSAL: a perusal is a careful reading right through a document, examining it line by line rather than glancing at it.

  • Country’s → PERUS the South American country with the S of the possessive left on
  • a → A the clue's own 'a', taken as a letter
  • Latin → L l = Latin, as on a school timetable
  • introducing — To introduce is to bring something new in — used loosely here, since the letters brought in arrive at the end rather than inside.

The trick is the apostrophe: 'Country's' is not really possessive at all, just a country's name with an S left stuck to it.

14 down WORCESTER

China might be after this line-up – open secret (9)

Definition: China might be after this · reversal + anagram

China might be after this → WORCESTER: the English cathedral city whose potteries gave Royal Worcester its name, so the china is named after the place.

  • line-up → WOR a line is a row, and this one is read upwards
  • secret → CESTER the six letters of SECRET, opened out into another order
  • up — This is a down clue, so a piece told to go up is read from the bottom of the grid back towards the top.
  • open — To open something out is to spread it apart and lay it down differently, so the letters after it need not keep their order.

'Line-up' looks like one word for a squad or an identity parade, and it is two instructions. The city's porcelain works are what make the definition fair.

16 down GATECRASH

Billionaire accepts new car ‘hard to break in’ (9)

Definition: break in · container + anagram + charade

break in → GATECRASH: turning up at a party nobody invited you to is breaking in on it, and this is the verb for doing exactly that.

  • Billionaire → GATES the software billionaire Bill supplies his surname
  • car → CRA the three letters of CAR, reordered
  • hard → H h = hard, the grade stamped on a pencil
  • accepts — What you accept you take in, so the billionaire's letters open up and receive the ones named next.
  • new — Something made new is remade from the same material, so those three letters are rebuilt in a different order.

The quotation marks make 'hard to break in' sound like a verdict on a stiff new car. Only the first of those words is wordplay; the rest is the definition.

17 down ATTRITION

Maybe Titian or Turner’s main form of grinding (9)

Definition: form of grinding · anagram + first letter

form of grinding → ATTRITION: wearing something down by constant friction, which is what a war of attrition does to an enemy over time.

  • Titian or → TITIANOR anagram fodder — the painter's name and the little word after it, eight letters
  • Turner’s main → T the chief, and so the first, letter of Turner
  • Maybe — A loose anagram signal: it offers the letters that follow as only one possible arrangement, so you may settle them another way.
  • main — The main letter of a name is its chief one, which is a loose way of asking for the initial and nothing else.

Two painters side by side make this look like an art quiz, and neither is being defined: one lends his whole name to the letters, the other lends a single one.

18 down DEPOSIT

Pledge from would-be MP – dregs! (7)

Definition: Pledge from would-be MP · double definition

Pledge from would-be MP → DEPOSIT: the sum a candidate lodges with the returning officer; the same word names what a liquid lets fall, its dregs.

  • Pledge from would-be MP → a parliamentary candidate in Britain must lodge £500, forfeited unless they win a twentieth of the votes cast
  • dregs → what settles out of a liquid and lies at the bottom of the bottle

The dash makes the two halves read as one weary remark about politics, and both are the same noun: one from the returning officer's desk, one from the bottom of a bottle.

20 down DISPLAY

One’s place in Doris’s show? (7)

Definition: show · container + charade

show → DISPLAY: a display is a show mounted for people to look at — fireworks, shop windows, temper — and the verbs match too.

  • Doris’s → DAY Doris Day, the American singer and film star, supplies the surname
  • One’s → IS one is the Roman numeral I, and the clue's own 's follows it
  • place → PL pl is the abbreviation for Place in a street address
  • in — 'In' puts the letters named before it inside the word named after, rather than alongside it.

'Doris's show' reads as a programme with her name on it and 'One's place' as somebody's home; neither picture survives the parse.

22 down ANNEX

Seize one with leader ousted in a new vote (5)

Definition: Seize · container + deletion

Seize → ANNEX: a state that annexes territory seizes it and keeps it, the formal word for taking land by force.

  • a → A the clue's own 'a', taken as a letter
  • new → N n = new, the standard abbreviation
  • one with leader ousted → NE ONE without the letter at its head
  • vote → X a vote is cast by marking a cross on the ballot paper
  • leader ousted — A word's leader is the letter at its front, and ousting throws that letter out of office altogether.
  • in — 'In' drops what precedes it inside the little assembly that follows, between its pieces rather than after them.

The surface is a leadership challenge, so 'leader ousted' reads as political news rather than an instruction to knock a letter off the front of a word.

23 down TORTE

Wrong end of the dish (5)

Definition: dish · charade + last letter

dish → TORTE: a rich, many-layered cake from Germany and Austria, and any prepared item of food can be called a dish.

  • Wrong → TORT in law a tort is a civil wrong, the injury one person does another
  • end of the → E the last letter of the word 'the'
  • end of — The end of a word is the letter it finishes on, so only the final character of the next word is wanted.

'Wrong end of' is an idiom about grasping something the wrong way round, and it splits neatly into a legal noun and one letter of the next word.

24 down OFFER

Tender and even more sickly? (5)

Definition: Tender · charade

Tender → OFFER: a tender is the formal bid a contractor puts in for a job, and to tender something is to offer it.

  • sickly → OFF someone feeling off is out of sorts, and milk that has gone off has turned
  • even more → ER the comparative ending -er is what makes a word into more of itself

This is not a synonym hunt: the back half of the answer is a grammatical ending, and 'even more' is the instruction to add it. Comparatives are a rarer trick than they look.
