Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,092

Across

1 across FIRST

Maiden in reverse initially denting bumper? (5)

Definition: Maiden · container

  • reverse → R the initial letter of the word, and nothing else of it
  • bumper? → FIST a bumper is something that bumps, and the hand you bump is a fist — the question mark owns the stretch

The surface is a car-park prang: reversing, denting, bumpers. Underneath, every one of those words has been given a different job.

4 across BEL PAESE

Italian food with taste of cheese a plebeian’s thrown about (3,5)

Definition: Italian food · hidden word + reversal

  • cheese a plebeian’s → ESEAPLEB eight letters sit consecutively here, starting three into the first word and stopping short of the last two — then they are read the other way

The trap is that this looks like an anagram, with 'thrown about' begging you to shuffle. Nothing is shuffled: the letters are already in the clue, in order, running backwards.

8 across RUBBER SOLUTION

Sticky stuff, something to fill squares alongside something to remove it? (6,8)

Definition: Sticky stuff · charade

  • something to remove it? → RUBBER in British English the thing that takes pencil off paper is a rubber, what Americans call an eraser
  • something to fill squares → SOLUTION the completed answers to a crossword are its solution

The clue describes the object in your hands: pencil in the grid, rub it out again. Only 'Sticky stuff' is looking anywhere else.

10 across NON-RIGID

Elastic on equipment in racket pulled back (3-5)

Definition: Elastic · container + reversal

  • racket → NID a racket is a din, and the din is turned round to make the shell
  • on → ON two letters taken straight off the page
  • equipment → RIG a rig is equipment: a drilling rig, a lighting rig

Every noun here belongs to a tennis club — elastic, equipment, racket — and 'racket' is the one that changes sense, from the thing you swing to the noise you make.

11 across NEEDLE

Bait in hand? (6)

Definition: Bait · double definition

  • Bait → the verb, not the worm: to bait somebody is to keep poking at them until they rise to it
  • in hand? → the second half is a picture rather than a synonym — the thin sharp thing a knitter, a tailor or a nurse is holding — and the question mark admits as much

Three words, no wordplay, two runs at the same answer. Short clues like this are often the last ones in, because there is nothing to take apart.

12 across BOOKMAKER

Prize to catch shark detailed – one taking bets (9)

Definition: one taking bets · container + deletion

  • Prize → BOOKER the Booker is Britain's best-known literary prize, awarded each year to a novel
  • shark detailed → MAK a mako is a fast open-sea shark, and de-tailed it loses its closing O

'Prize to catch' reads as the start of a definition, something worth landing, and 'Prize' is really a proper noun with a hole cut in it.

15 across OSCAR

Award unblemished? (5)

Definition: Award · charade

  • unblemished? → O unblemished means there is not one on you, and that 'no' is written as the O that stands for nothing
  • unblemished? → SCAR the blemish being denied is the mark a healed wound leaves behind

One word of wordplay, and it has to be heard rather than dissected: 'unblemished' is being read as a two-word phrase meaning the same thing.

17 across AUDIO

Sound German wheels round (5)

Definition: Sound · charade

  • German wheels → AUDI the car marque from Ingolstadt, four letters of German engineering
  • round → O a round drawn on the page is the letter O

'Sound' at the front of a clue almost always asks you to listen. Here it is simply the definition, and nothing in this one is being said aloud.

18 across TIGER MOTH

Old plane flying right to me (5,4)

Definition: Old plane · anagram

  • right to me → RIGHTTOME anagram fodder, nine letters

The indicator is also the scenery. A plane flying is what the surface describes, so 'flying' reads as description rather than as the order it is.

19 across KINDLE

Empty lounge behind warm fire (6)

Definition: fire · charade + outer letters

  • warm → KIND the human sense of warm, said of a person rather than a temperature
  • lounge → LE the two outside letters of the word, with OUNG gone from the middle

Read straight it describes a room — an empty lounge, a warm fire — and every word of that picture is quietly carrying an instruction instead.

21 across TAKE A PEW

Was sad about having caged a parrot? So get a perch (4,1,3)

Definition: get a perch · container + reversal

  • Was sad → TPEW someone sad wept, and the word goes down back to front
  • a parrot? → AKEA the kea is a large green parrot from the New Zealand mountains, and the 'a' comes along with it

Parrot, caged, perch: the birds run right through to the last word, and the answer has nothing to do with birds at all.

24 across BANG OUT OF ORDER

Shot down, completely unacceptable (4,3,2,5)

Definition: completely unacceptable · charade

  • Shot → BANG the noise a gun makes, and a word for the shot itself
  • down → OUTOFORDER a lift or a machine that is down has stopped working

'Shot down' reads as one phrase, an argument demolished, and the two words belong to different halves of the build.

25 across WHITE LIE

With flexible back in exercise, stretch out – slight stretch? (5,3)

Definition: slight stretch? · charade + anagram + last letter

  • With → WHIT the clue's own opening word, its four letters put in a new order
  • in exercise → E the rear letter of the word 'exercise'
  • stretch out → LIE to stretch out on a sofa is to lie on it

'Stretch' appears twice and means something different each time: once the wordplay for the last chunk, once the definition. That is a setter's liberty, deliberately taken.

26 across EXPAT

After returning, record filled in by unknown emigrant (5)

Definition: emigrant · container + reversal

  • record → EPAT a tape is a recording, and it is laid down the other way round
  • unknown → X x is the unknown quantity in algebra

The surface is a traveller home again and filling in a form, which lets the definition sit at the very end looking like the last word of a sentence.

Down

1 down FIRING BLANKS

Doing this, not going to multiply 24? (6,6)

Definition: not going to multiply · cryptic definition

  • Doing this, not going to multiply → The surface offers arithmetic, a sum you are declining to do. The setter means the other multiplying, the biblical be-fruitful kind, and a cartridge with no bullet in it.
  • 24? → A number this far from any arithmetic is a cross-reference: solve 24 across and you have the second joke, because a cartridge like that makes a report that is doing none of a shot's work.

The cross-reference earns its keep twice. As a figure it belongs to the sum the surface pretends to set; as a signpost it sends you off to another entry for the punchline.

2 down ROBIN HOOD

Bird tough for Archer (5,4)

Definition: Archer · charade

  • Bird → ROBIN the red-breasted garden bird of every British Christmas card
  • tough → HOOD a hood is a hoodlum, a street heavy

'Archer' is the misdirection and the definition at the same time: the surface offers Jeffrey Archer the novelist, and the clue wants a man with a bow.

3 down TREVI

One south of flipping green district of Rome (5)

Definition: district of Rome · charade + reversal

  • green → VERT vert is the heraldic name for green, the colour as a coat of arms gives it
  • One → I the Roman numeral, which is where the surface has been standing all along

Heraldry is worth learning as a source of colours: vert, gules, azure and sable turn up constantly and almost never mean anything else.

4 down BUS TICKET

It’s rolled over, in something to kick – and pass (3,6)

Definition: pass · container + reversal

  • something to kick → BUCKET kick the bucket — the thing kicked is the one in the idiom, not the one on a football pitch
  • It’s → STI the two words shrink to three letters, then turn round

'Something to kick' pulls hard towards a ball, and the answer needs the dying idiom instead. That single swap is the whole difficulty of the clue.

5 down LULU

Opera, not entirely mirthful, ultimately uplifting (4)

Definition: Opera · hidden word + reversal

  • mirthful, ultimately → ULUL four letters run straight across the gap between these two words, comma and all

'Ultimately' reads as an order to take a last letter, and that is the trap. It is fodder: the answer is buried across it and the word in front of it.

6 down AT THE DOOR

Other toad in soup, here (2,3,4)

Definition: here · anagram

  • Other toad → OTHERTOAD anagram fodder, nine letters

The whole surface is a witch's cauldron and not one word of it suggests a house, which is why the small definition at the end looks like an afterthought.

7 down SWORD

Fight to draw up, through case in scabbard – this? (5)

Definition: this? · container + reversal + outer letters

  • scabbard → SD the outermost letters of the word, the casing with the middle thrown away
  • Fight → ROW a row is a fight, a shouting quarrel

'Draw' belongs to the fight on the surface and to the sheath underneath, since drawing is what you do to a blade. That double life is what hides the turnaround.

9 down FEAR THE WORST

Planet in stew, for troubled – don’t expect it to get better! (4,3,5)

Definition: don’t expect it to get better! · anagram

  • Planet → EARTH the planet we are standing on, spelled out in full
  • stew → STEW the word goes into the mix at face value, all four letters
  • for → FOR three more letters lifted straight off the page

'Planet in stew' reads as an insertion, something dropped into the pot, and it is nothing of the kind: the planet's five letters are shuffled along with the rest.

13 down MOONLIGHT

Flash activated, faint shiner (9)

Definition: shiner · charade

  • Flash → MO in a flash and in a mo both mean in a moment
  • activated → ON a switch that has been activated is on
  • faint → LIGHT light rain, a light breeze: light does duty for faint

'Faint shiner' looks like the definition and is one word too long. 'Faint' has already been spent on the last chunk of the build.

14 down RIGMAROLE

Part played by Baltic capital investing millions in bureaucracy (9)

Definition: bureaucracy · container + charade

  • Baltic capital → RIGA the capital of Latvia, one of the three Baltic states
  • millions → M m is the standard shortening for millions on any balance sheet
  • Part played → ROLE the part an actor plays is their role

The surface has a government pouring millions into red tape, and the answer is the red tape. A definition that agrees with the picture is the hardest kind to notice.

16 down CHOPPED UP

Tripped, breaking item of crockery – in little pieces? (7,2)

Definition: in little pieces? · container

  • item of crockery → CUP the everyday item of crockery, and here it gets split open
  • Tripped → HOPPED to trip along is to move in light quick steps, which is to hop

'Breaking ... crockery' is a picture and an instruction at once: the crockery really is broken in two, and the other word is put in the gap.

20 down NEATH

Under orderly, hospital (5)

Definition: Under · charade

  • orderly → NEAT an orderly desk is a tidy one: the adjective, not the hospital porter the surface offers
  • hospital → H H is the standard abbreviation on maps and road signs

'Orderly, hospital' reads as one idea, the man wheeling the trolleys, and is two pieces underneath. The answer is a poetic clipping that drops the be- from beneath.

22 down EMOTE

Little bit topped with last of chocolate, lay it on thick (5)

Definition: lay it on thick · charade + last letter

  • chocolate → E the closing letter of the word, sitting on top of the column
  • Little bit → MOTE a mote is a speck of dust, as in the mote in your eye

The surface is a pudding being decorated. The definition is a four-word idiom because English has no tidy single verb for overdoing a display of feeling.

23 down BULL

Elephant in bunk? (4)

Definition: Elephant · double definition

  • Elephant → the male of the species — the same word covers a male whale, seal or moose, so this is one example of a class
  • bunk? → bunk is nonsense, talk not worth listening to, and that is the sense the second definition wants

Two definitions dressed as one picture, joined by an 'in' that asserts nothing and only holds the sentence up.
