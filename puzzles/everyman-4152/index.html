Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,152

Setter: Everyman · Published: Saturday 16 May 2026 · Difficulty: Tough (harder than 50% of the puzzles here) · Grid: 15×15

Across

1 across TICS

Cosmetic surgeon suppresses mannerisms (4)

Definition: mannerisms · hidden word

  • Cosmetic surgeon → TICS four consecutive letters straddling the gap between the two words, three from the end of the first and one from the start of the second

The surface offers a plastic surgeon smoothing away a patient's twitches, which is why nobody looks at the two words as spelling rather than sense.

3 across PINOT BLANC

Bin ends in Ruislip, Southall and Acton shifting? (5,5)

Definition: Bin ends · anagram + last letters

  • Bin → BIN the wine trade's word for the rack a bottle is laid down in, taken whole for three of the letters
  • Ruislip, Southall → PL the letter each of these two west London suburbs finishes on
  • Acton → ACTON the third suburb is used entire, all five of its letters going into the mix

All three places are west London suburbs, which is what makes the surface read as a refuse round. Only two of them are wanted for their last letter; the third goes in whole.

9 across USED

Second-hand American edition (4)

Definition: Second-hand · charade

  • American → US US is the standard short form for American, and a favourite two letters in short clues
  • edition → ED ed. is how an edition is abbreviated on a title page

Two abbreviations laid end to end and nothing else, which is the shape a great many four-letter clues take once you learn to expect it.

10 across ASK ME LATER

Mark Steel, a comic? I don't have time for this (3,2,5)

Definition: I don't have time for this · anagram

  • Mark Steel, a → ASKMELATER the nine letters of the stand-up's name plus the article, rearranged

Mark Steel is a British stand-up and radio comedian, so the surface reads as a review; the question mark is the setter admitting the name is here for its letters.

12 across BESTIALISED

Made like an animal after changes to destabilise (11)

Definition: Made like an animal · anagram

  • destabilise → BESTIALISED the eleven letters of the verb, rearranged

The surface sounds like a policy going wrong, so nobody reads the last word as raw material. An answer ending -ISED very often comes out of a word ending -ISE.

15 across LEATHER

Belt and coat? (7)

Definition: Belt · double definition

  • Belt → the verb: to belt someone is to hit them hard, which is what a strap was traditionally for
  • coat → the noun: an animal's own covering, the hide that tanning turns into a material

Three words, and both halves point at the same corner of the world, which is what makes the split so hard to see. The question mark covers the second half being an example.

16 across SIGHTED

Seen, quoted, talked about (7)

Definition: Seen · homophone

  • quoted → SIGHTED to quote a source in an essay is to cite it, and that word said aloud is spelt a completely different way

Two words that sound identical and share barely a letter: one belongs to scholarship and the other to the eye, and a three-word clue gives you nothing else to go on.

17 across NO CAN DO

Lack of honesty you are heard missing in refusal (2,3,2)

Definition: refusal · charade + homophone + deletion

  • Lack → NO a lack of something is none of it, and this is the two-letter word for that
  • honesty → CANDOUR candour is plain honest speech, and it is the word the letters are quarried from
  • you are → UR the letters U and R read aloud say those two words, and they are the pair that leaves

Single letters standing for whole words by sound — U for you, R for are, Y for why — are worth collecting: a few of them turn up in any week of puzzles.

19 across ILL-WILL

Everyman is going to leave bitterness (3-4)

Definition: bitterness · charade

  • Everyman is going to → ILL the setter calls himself Everyman, so he is I, and 'is going to' shrinks to the contraction that follows it
  • leave → WILL what you leave behind when you die is set out in a document of that name

Everyman writes himself into his own puzzles constantly, and he is nearly always the first person singular. Apostrophes vanish in the grid, so a contraction is just letters.

20 across CUT AND PASTE

Perform digital operations by reinstalling updates? Can't! (3,3,5)

Definition: Perform digital operations · anagram

  • updates? Can't → CUTANDPASTE the eleven letters of these two words, punctuation ignored, rearranged

Fingers are digits, so the definition is a joke told twice: the operation is done on a computer and it is done with the hands.

23 across PADDINGTON

Bear fruit finally after waffling on (10)

Definition: Bear · charade + last letter

  • waffling → PADDING to waffle is to fill space with words that say nothing, and the noun for that filling is what the letters spell
  • fruit → T the closing letter of the word for what a tree bears
  • on → ON the small word is taken at face value and tacked to the end

'Bear' is a verb all through the surface — bearing fruit — and a noun in the answer, which is the switch the whole clue turns on.

24 across PISA

A taste that's repulsive somewhere in Italy (4)

Definition: somewhere in Italy · reversal

  • A taste → A SIP the article, then the word for a small mouthful of drink

The leaning tower is the only thing most solvers know the city for and the clue never mentions it. 'Repulsive' is not describing the taste; it says which way to read.

25 across CHEESINESS

His essence, weirdly, having broad appeal (10)

Definition: having broad appeal · anagram

  • His essence → CHEESINESS the ten letters of these two words, rearranged

The surface reads as a compliment about somebody's character, and 'essence' looks as though it is defining something when it is only supplying letters.

26 across STYE

Something taunting your eye, primarily? (4)

Definition: Something taunting your eye, primarily? · &lit + first letters

  • Something taunting your eye → STYE the opening letter of each of these four words, taken in the order they are written

An all-in-one: the same words define the answer and spell it out, and the question mark is the only signal that anything unusual is going on.

Down

1 down THUMBELINA

Tiny individual ain't humble, sadly (10)

Definition: Tiny individual · anagram

  • ain't humble → THUMBELINA the ten letters of these two words, apostrophe ignored, rearranged

Hans Christian Andersen's heroine was born inside a flower and stood no higher than a thumb, which is where her name comes from and why she can define smallness.

2 down CHESS MATCH

In which a monarch hopes not to find a mate (5,5)

Definition: In which a monarch hopes not to find a mate · cryptic definition

  • hopes not to find a mate → the reading the surface pushes: a lonely royal with no luck in romance, or none wanted
  • In which a monarch → the setter means the king on the board, and the mate he dreads is the move that finishes him

'Mate' is the crossword's favourite three-way word — a partner, a friend and the move that ends a game. Only the last of them is bad news for a king.

4 down INSULAR

Curtly malign a Republican that's xenophobic (7)

Definition: xenophobic · charade + deletion

  • malign → INSUL to malign someone is to insult them, and curt delivery clips the last letter off that word
  • a → A the article is taken at face value as a single letter
  • Republican → R R is the standard abbreviation for Republican, as printed beside an American politician's name

American party letters are staple filler in British cryptics — R for Republican, D for Democrat — and they turn up wherever a clue needs one more consonant.

5 down OSMOSIS

Part of cosmos isn't oozing (7)

Definition: oozing · hidden word

  • cosmos isn't → OSMOSIS seven consecutive letters that start inside the first word and finish inside the second

The surface promises astronomy and the answer delivers biology. An apostrophe counts for nothing in a letter run, so the two words read as one unbroken string.

6 down BULLDOG CLIPS

Films depicting icon of Britishness? They hold things together (7,5)

Definition: They hold things together · charade

  • icon of Britishness → BULLDOG the squat, jut-jawed breed Churchill was likened to, and the animal on wartime posters standing for national stubbornness
  • Films → CLIPS a short piece of film is a clip, and the plural is what the wordplay hands over

The question mark admits a bulldog is only one of the things a Briton might be represented by. 'Films' looks like the definition and is really the back half of the wordplay.

7 down ANTE

Might this prompt someone to give you a hand? (4)

Definition: Might this prompt someone to give you a hand? · cryptic definition

  • give you a hand → the everyday reading, and the one the surface wants first: somebody stepping in to help you out
  • Might this prompt → at the card table nothing is dealt until the stake is down, so the money is what makes the dealer move

In poker everyone puts a token stake in before the cards come out, and no stake means no deal. The hand you are given is therefore five cards rather than a favour.

8 down CARB

2 mostly rejected - 13 rejects it (4)

Definition: 13 rejects it · deletion + reversal

  • 2 → BRACE a brace is a pair of anything — two birds, two goals — so the figure is written out as that word before it is trimmed

Everyman numbers usually cross-refer, and 13 does. The 2 does not: it is just the number, and the word for two is what the wordplay is after.

11 down FISH AND CHIPS

Dish splitting loudly - tiny pieces (4,3,5)

Definition: Dish · homophone + charade

  • splitting → FISH AND splitting an atom is fission, and that word spoken aloud runs two short words together
  • tiny pieces → CHIPS a chip is a small fragment knocked off something larger

The surface is a plate breaking; the setter is thinking of an atom. The second half hides in plain sight because a chip is a fried potato and a fragment at once.

13 down ATKINS DIET

Regime in which, after a time, Parliament includes families (6,4)

Definition: Regime · charade + container

  • a time → AT the article stands as itself and T is the abbreviation for time
  • families → KINS your kin are your relatives, and the plural names more than one such group
  • Parliament → DIET the Japanese parliament is called the Diet, as the German and Hungarian assemblies once were

The surface promises a political dynasty and delivers an eating plan. The cross-reference runs both ways: 8 down is the food group this entry bans.

14 down ADULTERATE

Contaminate territory that will be accepted by fawn (10)

Definition: Contaminate · container

  • fawn → ADULATE to fawn on someone is to adulate them — flattery laid on thick
  • territory → TER ter. is the map-maker's short form of territory

'Fawn' looks like the young deer or the pale colour and is neither: setters use the grovelling verb far more often than either noun.

18 down OCTAGON

Figure can go to ... has gone to the dogs (7)

Definition: Figure · anagram

  • can go to → OCTAGON the seven letters of these three small words, rearranged

'Figure' is a shape here rather than a number, and the dots invite you to read a sentence about a dog going somewhere instead of three words waiting to be jumbled.

19 down IGNEOUS

Made from fire, rampant soldier with gumption and energy boxed (7)

Definition: Made from fire · charade + reversal + container

  • soldier → IG GI is the American private of countless war films, stood on his head by the entry running downwards
  • gumption → NOUS nous is practical common sense, exactly the quality gumption names
  • energy → E E is the physicist's symbol for energy

The definition is geology dressed as a battle report: rock formed out of molten magma is what a volcano leaves behind once it cools.

21 down EPIC

Big film cut: Pig in the City (4)

Definition: Big film · deletion + container

  • the City → EC EC is the postcode district covering the City of London, the financial square mile
  • Pig → PI the three-letter farm animal, docked of the letter it ends on

The italics dress the last four words up as the Babe sequel, and that is the whole trap: the only film in the clue is the one the definition names.

22 down IDLE

Not starting to walk like a crab? That's unimportant (4)

Definition: unimportant · deletion

  • to walk like a crab → SIDLE to sidle is to edge along sideways, which is how a crab gets from place to place

The surface dismisses somebody's odd walk as beneath notice, and the definition is the dismissal itself rather than anything to do with laziness.
