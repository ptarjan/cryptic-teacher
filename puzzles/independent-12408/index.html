Across

1 across SULPHATE

Heats up, after slip welcomes end of Phil Salt (8)

Definition: Salt · anagram + last letter

  • Heats up → HEATSUP anagram fodder, seven letters
  • end of Phil → L the closing letter of Phil

Phil Salt opens the batting for England, so the two words read as one cricketer's name while the clue wants the surname on its own.

5 across ARMPIT

One wearing confused tramp's sweater? (6)

Definition: sweater · anagram

  • tramp → TRAMP anagram fodder, five letters
  • One → I I is the Roman numeral one, worn inside the fodder

'Sweater' promises a jumper to go with the tramp's clothes, and the question mark is the setter admitting it means something that sweats.

10 across AFFLUENCE

Plenty of waste exposed in American church (9)

Definition: Plenty · charade + middle letters

  • American → A A stands for American, as in the A of USA
  • waste exposed → FFLUEN EFFLUENT is liquid waste, here without its first and last letters
  • church → CE CE is the Church of England, the crossword's standard church

Wealth and sewage are one letter apart in English, and this clue makes you build the pleasant word out of the unpleasant one.

11 across LENIN

Russian leader in square originally leading revolutionary (5)

Definition: Russian leader · charade + first letter + reversal

  • square → NINE nine is a square number, three times three
  • originally leading → L the first letter of leading

'Revolutionary' earns its keep twice over: it is the instruction to read the letters backwards, and it describes the man the definition points at.

12 across INSET

Perhaps Ant ignoring Conservative figure in the corner? (5)

Definition: figure in the corner · deletion

  • Perhaps Ant → INSECT an ant is one example of the class of small six-legged creatures
  • Conservative → C C is the standard abbreviation for the Conservative party

Ant of Ant & Dec, the British television double act, is what the surface is really about — a presenter snubbing a politician.

13 across ORIGINATE

Produce social security cheque sent back in haste regularly by Earl (9)

Definition: Produce · charade + reversal + alternate letters

  • social security cheque → ORIG a giro was the benefit cheque the British state paid out through its Girobank, written here the other way round
  • in → IN the word taken as itself
  • haste regularly → AT the second and fourth letters of hAsTe
  • Earl → E E is the abbreviation for earl in lists of the peerage

Four pieces in a row is a lot of joinery, and the giro is the one worth knowing: for decades 'the giro' simply meant the dole.

14 across LEMON SOLE

Swimmer in Moselle on mobile! (5,4)

Definition: Swimmer · anagram

  • Moselle on → MOSELLEON anagram fodder, nine letters

The exclamation mark sells a phone call from a river valley, and 'on' looks like part of that sentence rather than part of the fodder.

17 across DOGGO

Quietly hiding, Silvanus perhaps turned to leave (5)

Definition: Quietly hiding · reversal + charade

  • Silvanus perhaps → DOG Silvanus was the Roman god of woods and fields, and GOD is turned round here
  • leave → GO to leave is to go

The capital letter points hard at a person, and the wordplay only wants his job description — a deity, turned about.

18 across PESCI

Actor's school subjects, in brief (5)

Definition: Actor · charade

  • school subjects → PE PE is physical education on a school timetable
  • school subjects → SCI SCI is the timetable short form of science

One plural phrase covers two subjects, and the surname is assembled from them without a single letter to spare.

20 across EXTRAVERT

Outgoing person is greener? (9)

Definition: Outgoing person · charade

  • greener → EXTRA the -er ending is the comparative, asking for more, and more of a thing is extra
  • greener → VERT vert is green in heraldry and in French

One word of wordplay carries both pieces, and they come out in the opposite order to the way English says them. The -a- spelling is the psychologist's.

23 across IRISH STEW

Cook rises with traditional dish (5,4)

Definition: traditional dish · anagram

  • rises with → RISESWITH anagram fodder, nine letters

Bread rising beside a cook makes a smooth kitchen sentence, and that is what hides 'with' — it is fodder, not the little word joining two halves.

25 across GRAIL

Good public transport system is the ultimate aim (5)

Definition: the ultimate aim · charade

  • Good → G G is the standard abbreviation for good
  • public transport system → RAIL the rail network is a country's public transport system

The definition is the whole phrase at the end, and the surface spends its words on trains so that you read 'ultimate aim' as part of the sentence about them.

27 across BLAIR

Former leader, second-class, getting money from Iran returned (5)

Definition: Former leader · charade + reversal

  • second-class → B a second-class mark is a B
  • money from Iran → LAIR the rial is the Iranian currency, written backwards here

'Former leader' would fit hundreds of names, so the wordplay has to settle which one — and the surface's talk of Iranian money is the setter making that look like news.

28 across RESILIENT

Right to enter exam? It's tough (9)

Definition: tough · container

  • exam → RESIT a resit is the exam taken again after a failure
  • Right → LIEN a lien is the legal right to hold property until a debt is paid

'Right' is R in most cryptics, and the whole clue depends on it not being R here — it is the four-letter legal one instead.

29 across EVENTS

Happenings in flat extremely tedious (6)

Definition: Happenings · charade + outer letters

  • flat → EVEN a flat surface is an even one
  • tedious → TS the first and last letters of tedious

'In flat extremely tedious' reads as a grumble about a dull apartment, which hides both the adjective meaning level and the instruction beside it.

30 across CRUSADER

Campaigner is more vulgar about essay on the radio (8)

Definition: Campaigner · container + homophone

  • more vulgar → CRUDER crude means vulgar, and the comparative adds the -r
  • essay → SA a short piece of writing is an essay, and saying those two letter names aloud is saying the word

The letter-name homophone is worth collecting: 'essay', 'excel' and 'empty' are all just letters read aloud, and setters use them for short awkward chunks.

Down

1 down SPANIEL

Time that is left for King Charles? (7)

Definition: King Charles · charade

  • Time → SPAN a span of time is a stretch of it
  • that is → IE i.e. is Latin id est, 'that is'
  • left → L L is the abbreviation for left

Three tiny conventions laid end to end, dressed up as a question about the monarch — and the question mark is there because the definition names one breed by its royal owner.

2 down LIFTS

Steals fifty-one paper planes, ultimately (5)

Definition: Steals · charade + last letter

  • fifty-one → LI L is fifty and I is one in Roman numerals
  • paper → FT the Financial Times, Britain's business daily, is the FT
  • planes, ultimately → S the last letter of planes

The surface is a theft of stationery, and 'paper' is the crossword's favourite newspaper rather than a sheet of the stuff.

3 down HOUSTON

Passionate about entertaining American city (7)

Definition: city · container

  • Passionate about → HOTON someone hot on a subject is passionate about it
  • American → US US is the everyday short form of the United States

'About' sits next to 'Passionate' looking like a turning instruction; it belongs to the phrase instead, and the phrase is what supplies the letters.

4 down TANGO

Try following beat music (5)

Definition: music · charade

  • beat → TAN to tan somebody is to give them a beating
  • Try → GO a go is a try, an attempt at something

'Beat' is where the surface wants you to hear rhythm, and the wordplay wants the thrashing sense of the same word.

6 down RALLIED

At first, Rotherham United got better (7)

Definition: got better · charade + first letter

  • Rotherham → R the initial letter of the Yorkshire town
  • United → ALLIED nations that are united are allied

Rotherham United is a South Yorkshire football club, so the two words read as one team name — and the clue pulls them apart.

7 down PENTANGLE

Star writer supported by Ravel (9)

Definition: Star · charade

  • writer → PEN a pen does the writing, so it stands in for the writer
  • Ravel → TANGLE to ravel is to tangle — the capital letter is borrowed for the surface

Maurice Ravel is planted there to make you hunt for a composer. The lower-case verb 'ravel' means to tangle, which is where English gets 'unravel'.

8 down TANGELO

The Spanish probing 4 fruit (7)

Definition: fruit · container

  • 4 → TANGO the answer to 4 down, borrowed whole
  • The Spanish → EL 'the' in Spanish is el

A cross-reference: solve 4 down first, then split it open. The name of this citrus is a squash of its two parents, tangerine and pomelo.

9 down SEDIMENT

Broadcast about foreign cash settled matter (8)

Definition: settled matter · container

  • Broadcast → SENT a programme that was broadcast was sent out
  • foreign cash → DIME the dime is the American ten-cent coin, foreign currency from a British desk

'Broadcast' is a three-way trap — it can signal an anagram, or scattered seed, or, as here, plain past-tense 'sent'.

15 down MASTICATE

Friend drinking sparkling wine beginning to crave champ (9)

Definition: champ · container + first letter

  • Friend → MATE a mate is a friend
  • sparkling wine → ASTI Asti is the sparkling white wine of Piedmont
  • beginning to crave → C the opening letter of crave

'Champ' looks like the winner standing beside the wine that is being poured; it is the verb, to chew noisily.

16 down OVERTURN

Cancel patent on receptacle (8)

Definition: Cancel · charade

  • patent → OVERT a patent lie is an overt one, out in the open
  • receptacle → URN an urn is a receptacle for ashes or for tea

'Patent' looks like the document you file at the office; here it is the adjective meaning obvious, which is stressed on a different syllable.

18 down PLIABLE

Priest likely to be flexible (7)

Definition: flexible · charade

  • Priest → P P is the dictionary abbreviation for priest
  • likely → LIABLE liable to happen means likely to happen

'Liable' overlaps with 'likely' in only one sense — liable to rain, likely to rain — and that is the sense the wordplay leans on.

19 down INHERIT

Be left at home with husband with tax going up (7)

Definition: Be left · charade + reversal

  • at home → IN somebody at home is in
  • husband → H H is the abbreviation for husband, as on a family tree
  • tax → ERIT to tax someone is to tire them, and TIRE is set climbing

Inheritance tax is sitting in plain sight in the surface, which is what makes the seam between definition and wordplay so hard to find.

21 down ANGELUS

Prayer from setter in hole (7)

Definition: Prayer · container

  • hole → ANUS an opening in the body, wanted here as a plain hole
  • setter → GEL gel is the stuff that sets — hair gel — so the word for what sets is used loosely for the setting agent

In a cryptic, 'setter' almost always means the person who wrote the clue, so it takes some resisting to read it as a substance instead.

22 down TELSTAR

Satellite rattles around (7)

Definition: Satellite · anagram

  • rattles → RATTLES anagram fodder, seven letters

Telstar relayed the first live television pictures across the Atlantic in 1962, and the surface makes it sound like loose change in a drawer.

24 down WISER

More sage in group of women's English recipe (5)

Definition: More sage · charade

  • group of women's → WIS the Women's Institute, the British countrywomen's organisation, is the WI, and the possessive adds the S
  • English → E E is the abbreviation for English
  • recipe → R R is the old prescription and cookery abbreviation for recipe

Sage is a herb as well as an adjective, which is the only reason a recipe and some English cooking are in the surface at all.

26 down AHEAD

Ace boss is winning (5)

Definition: winning · charade

  • Ace → A the ace is the A of a suit of cards
  • boss → HEAD the head of a firm is its boss

Two short words that read as praise for a person — an ace boss — and neither is doing anything but handing over letters.
