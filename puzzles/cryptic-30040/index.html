Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,040

Across

8 across MUTINEER

Term in use possibly with no end of churches for Christian, say (8)

Definition: Christian, say · anagram + deletion

  • Term in use → TERMINUSE anagram fodder, before the deletion
  • no end of churches → drop S, the last letter of churcheS
  • possibly → anagram indicator

The payoff is the definition: Fletcher Christian led the mutiny on the Bounty, so 'Christian, say' is a fair example — and the S leaves before the shuffle, not after.

9 across NOUGHT

Duck in middle of canal looked for when sun is out (6)

Definition: Duck · charade + middle letter + deletion

  • middle of canal → N the central letter of caNal
  • looked for when sun is out → OUGHT SOUGHT (looked for) with S (sun) taken out

Duck for zero is cricket's gift to crosswords, and it is the definition here — not the bird the canal invites you to picture.

10 across SPECTRUM

Colourful band from special city snubbed president (8)

Definition: Colourful band · charade + deletion

  • special → SP sp = special, a standard abbreviation
  • city → EC the City of London's EC postcode
  • snubbed president → TRUM TRUMP cut short — 'snubbed' as in snub-nosed

'Snubbed' means cut short, as in a snub nose, rather than slighted — and the president loses his last letter for it. That double sense is the whole joke.

11 across NUMPTY

Dimwit put many off after scrubbing article (6)

Definition: Dimwit · anagram + deletion

  • put many → PUTMANY anagram fodder, before the deletion
  • after scrubbing article → remove A, the article
  • off → anagram indicator

Numpty is cheerfully Scottish. The surface — putting people off — sounds like a real complaint rather than a bag of letters waiting to be shaken.

12 across NATURAL DISASTER

Supermarket among restaurants stricken in flood, maybe (7,8)

Definition: flood, maybe · anagram

  • Supermarket → ALDI Aldi, the supermarket chain
  • restaurants → RESTAURANTS the rest of the fodder — ALDI goes 'among' it
  • stricken → anagram indicator

'Among' only tells you ALDI joins the other letters — in an anagram, where a piece goes makes no difference. And a stricken supermarket in a flood is very nearly the answer already.

15 across STATE

Declare condition (5)

Definition: Declare · double definition

  • Declare → STATE this half asks for a verb meaning to announce or assert something formally
  • condition → STATE this half is a plain noun definition: the condition or mode something currently is in

A two-word clue with no indicator and nothing to take apart is nearly always two definitions. The only work is finding the word that answers both.

16 across DIVER

Amusements (not half) for one working in a suit (5)

Definition: one working in a suit · deletion

  • Amusements (not half) → DIVER DIVERSIONS (amusements) keeping only its first half

'Not half' is a standard instruction: keep the first half, drop the rest. The joke is 'working in a suit' — a diving suit, not a pinstripe.

20 across BY TRIAL AND ERROR

Andorra terribly treated in an experimental way? (2,5,3,5)

Definition: in an experimental way? · anagram

  • Andorra terribly → ANDORRATERRIBLY anagram fodder, 15 letters
  • treated → anagram indicator

'Treated' is the anagram indicator — a sneaky choice, since 'terribly' looks like one and is actually fodder.

21 across TRAVEL

Insurable activity held in extra velodrome (6)

Definition: Insurable activity · hidden word

  • extra velodrome → TRAVEL hidden: exTRA VELodrome

Hidden words hide best inside long words, and 'velodrome' is long enough that nobody looks. 'Insurable activity' gives it away once you think of travel insurance.

23 across DISPATCH

Circling resort, abandon speed (8)

Definition: speed · container

  • resort → SPA a spa = a resort
  • abandon → DITCH to ditch = to abandon; it circles the SPA

The surface is all holiday, and the definition is the noun sense of 'speed' — to do something with dispatch is to do it quickly.

25 across POTTER

One engaged in break, perhaps, gets to move with little energy (6)

Definition: One engaged in break, perhaps · double definition

  • One engaged in break, perhaps → POTTER a snooker player making a break pots balls
  • move with little energy → POTTER this half asks for a verb meaning to move about without urgency, doing odd little tasks

'Break' sends you to snooker, and 'perhaps' concedes that a potter is only one example of someone making one. The two senses share nothing but their letters.

26 across SANCERRE

Anglican bishop into sound tipple (8)

Definition: tipple · charade + container

  • Anglican → CE CE = Church of England
  • bishop → RR RR = Right Reverend, a bishop's style
  • sound → SANE sound = sane; it takes the CE RR inside

CE for Church of England and RR for Right Reverend are both standard; the surface, an ecclesiastical wine tasting, is built to keep them looking like people.

Down

1 down OUTPLAY

Perform better than leader of party cutting expenditure (7)

Definition: Perform better than · container + first letter

  • leader of party → P the first letter of Party
  • expenditure → OUTLAY outlay = expenditure
  • cutting → insertion indicator — P cuts into OUTLAY

'Cutting expenditure' reads as a single piece of budget news, which is why 'cutting' never sounds like an instruction to slide a letter in.

2 down DISCOUNTER

Cheap outlet with 70s music and a French name losing money (10)

Definition: Cheap outlet · charade + deletion

  • 70s music → DISCO the sound of the seventies
  • a French → UN 'a' in French = un
  • name losing money → TER TERM (to name) losing M (money)

The hard step is 'name' as a verb — to name is to TERM — and only then does 'money' take its M away. Everything else is disco and schoolboy French.

3 down WEAR

Sport seen in Jarrow earlier (4)

Definition: Sport · hidden word

  • Jarrow earlier → WEAR hidden: jarroW EARlier

'Sport' reads as a noun — games, athletics — and it is really the verb: to sport a hat is to wear it. Jarrow sits near the River Wear, so even the hiding place fits.

4 down BROMIDE

Dim bore concocted placatory statement (7)

Definition: placatory statement · anagram

  • Dim bore → DIMBORE anagram fodder, 7 letters
  • concocted → anagram indicator

A bromide is a soothing platitude, named after the sedative — and the surface even sounds like the sort of dull person who would utter one.

5 down ON ONE'S MIND

Performing individual with singular object of concern? (2,4,4)

Definition: of concern? · charade

  • Performing → ON a performer who is on stage is 'on'
  • individual → ONE one = an individual
  • singular → S s = singular, the grammar abbreviation
  • object → MIND to mind = to object ('do you mind?')

'Object' looks like a noun and turns out to be a verb: do you mind? That switch is the clue's one real difficulty; everything before it is small dull abbreviations.

6 down GUAM

Revolutionary face encapsulating a US territory (4)

Definition: US territory · reversal + container

  • Revolutionary face → GUM MUG (face) turned round
  • a → A the letter A, tucked inside

'Revolutionary face' sounds like a poster of Che, and it means MUG turned round. Note that the 'a' is not a link word here — you actually use the letter.

7 down CHUTNEY

Husband in trim overturned longing for Indian food (7)

Definition: Indian food · container + reversal

  • Husband → H h = husband, as in family trees
  • trim → CUT to trim = to cut; it wraps around the H
  • longing → NEY YEN (longing) 'overturned' in this down clue

H for husband and YEN for longing are both worth filing away. In a down clue 'overturned' means read upwards, and the curry-house surface covers all three moves.

13 down ART GALLERY

Paul S’s partner in Paris to go over unknown site with a collection? (3,7)

Definition: site with a collection? · charade

  • Paul S’s partner → ARTG Paul Simon's partner Art Garfunkel — 'Art G'
  • in Paris to go → ALLER the French for 'to go'
  • unknown → Y y, an algebraic unknown

'Paul S's partner' is the pleasure of the clue — Simon and Garfunkel, reduced to ART G. After that it is French and algebra, and 'in Paris' is the standard signal to translate.

14 down SHEARWATER

Tar was here at sea to locate marine bird (10)

Definition: marine bird · anagram

  • Tar was here → TARWASHERE anagram fodder, 10 letters
  • at sea → anagram indicator

Lovely surface: a tar is a sailor, exactly the person you would expect to have scrawled 'was here' out on the ocean, and 'at sea' means both lost and jumbled.

17 down CYPRIOT

Islander clearly disheartened facing start of public disorder (7)

Definition: Islander · charade + deletion + first letter

  • clearly disheartened → CY CLEARLY loses its heart, leaving C…Y
  • start of public → P the first letter of Public
  • disorder → RIOT a riot = disorder

'Disheartened' always means scoop out the middle, leaving the two ends. The surface, unrest on a sunny island, keeps you reading news rather than instructions.

18 down FADDIST

Duke defending a theologian, one given to temporary obsession? (7)

Definition: one given to temporary obsession? · container

  • Duke → FIST dukes = fists, as in 'put up your dukes'
  • a theologian → ADD A + DD (Doctor of Divinity)

'Put up your dukes' is where the fists come from, and DD for Doctor of Divinity is standard shorthand. The surface, a duke defending a theologian, is period drama.

19 down CONCURS

Agrees with Italian and French priests (not English) (7)

Definition: Agrees · charade + deletion

  • with Italian → CON 'with' in Italian = con
  • French priests (not English) → CURS CURES (curés, French priests) minus E (English)

Two languages in seven letters: Italian con and French curés. The ecclesiastical surface is exactly what stops you hearing either as a foreign word.

22 down VETO

Sanction over Eton excluding outsiders (4)

Definition: Sanction · charade + middle letters

  • over → VE oVEr without its outer letters
  • Eton → TO eTOn without its outer letters

'Sanction' is its own opposite: it can mean approve or forbid, and here it is the blocking sense. Two words stripped of their outsides is an unusual way to build four letters.

24 down SINK

Decline feature in a kitchen (4)

Definition: Decline · double definition

  • Decline → SINK this half asks for a verb meaning to drop or fall in level, as an economy might
  • feature in a kitchen → SINK this half names the basin fixture built into every kitchen counter

Both senses are so ordinary that the clue barely looks cryptic, which is the trap. 'Decline' is the abstract sense while the kitchen keeps you thinking literally.
