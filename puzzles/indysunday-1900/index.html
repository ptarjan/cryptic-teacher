Across

1 across BUCKSHOT

Greens in US enjoying great popularity now lead (8)

Definition: lead · charade

lead → BUCKSHOT: buckshot is the coarse lead pellets a shotgun cartridge is loaded with, so the metal names the shot made of it.

  • Greens in US → BUCKS American money is green, and a greenback is a buck
  • enjoying great popularity now → HOT a hot ticket is the one everybody wants at the moment

Both halves are slang for something other than shooting: dollars and fashionability. 'Lead' is the metal, not the verb, which is why it can sit at the end looking like a job description.

5 across BUST

Piece made by sculptor completely out of dough (4)

Definition: Piece made by sculptor · double definition

A bust is the sculpted head and shoulders; the same word is slang for broke, so somebody entirely out of money is bust.

  • Piece made by sculptor → the head-and-shoulders portrait in stone or bronze
  • completely out of dough → broke — dough being money, and the slang for having none of it

'Dough' does two jobs at once: it belongs with the sculptor's modelling material on the surface, and it means cash in the definition that counts.

8 across MARS

Spoils of war? (4)

Definition: Spoils · double definition

To mar is to spoil, so the verb's third person is one reading; the other is the Roman god war itself was named after.

  • Spoils → the plain verb: to mar a thing is to spoil it, and this is the third person
  • of war → the Roman god, whose name the red planet also carries

Four letters and a famous phrase: the setter has written nothing but 'spoils of war' and split it in the one place that turns plunder into a deity.

9 across RHETORICAL

Using persuasive language that might make you a lot richer (10)

Definition: Using persuasive language · anagram

Using persuasive language → RHETORICAL: rhetoric is the ancient art of persuasion, so rhetorical describes speech shaped to win an audience round.

  • a lot richer → RHETORICAL the ten letters of A LOT RICHER, dealt again
  • make — 'Make' here means turn into: the letters are being turned into something they are not yet, so they cannot stay as they are.

Persuasive language really might make you richer, so the surface holds together perfectly and gives you no reason to count the letters of the last three words.

10 across ENVIRONS

Half grudgingly admired golf club's location (8)

Definition: location · charade + deletion

location → ENVIRONS: the environs of a town are the ground immediately round it, so the word names a place by the district it occupies.

  • Half grudgingly admired → ENV to admire grudgingly is to have ENVIED, and half of that six-letter word is its first three
  • golf club → IRON an iron is the metal-headed golf club
  • 's → S the apostrophe-s of 'club's' is taken as a plain letter
  • Half — Only the front portion of the word is wanted, three letters out of the six that grudging admiration spells.

A golf club's location reads as a course address, and the clue means nothing of the kind. Watch for a possessive being cashed in as a letter — setters do it constantly.

11 across GO-KART

Racer finally demonstrating good skill (2-4)

Definition: Racer · charade + last letter

Racer → GO-KART: a go-kart is a small open-framed racing car, so the definition names it by the only thing it is built for.

  • demonstrating → G its final letter, which is what 'finally' asks for
  • good → OK OK is the everyday word for good enough
  • skill → ART art in the sense of a mastered craft, as in the art of conversation
  • finally — It asks for the very end of the word beside it, one letter and no more.

The enumeration (2-4) tells you the answer is hyphenated, and the wordplay pays that hyphen no attention at all. Note too that 'finally' sits before its word rather than after it.

12 across BARREN

Producing no crops, to do with farm storage limits (6)

Definition: Producing no crops · container

Producing no crops → BARREN: barren land is soil too poor to yield a harvest, the farming sense of the word rather than the human one.

  • farm storage → BARN the barn is where a farm keeps its harvest, and it is the word that splits
  • to do with → RE re means concerning, the word at the head of a memo
  • limits — To set the limits of a thing is to stand at both ends of it, so the outer word opens and takes the other inside.

A barn stands for farm storage so readily that it never looks like a container, and 'limits' passes as part of the same phrase about capacity.

13 across WHEATEAR

Small layer you hear, little songbird (8)

Definition: little songbird · homophone

little songbird → WHEATEAR: the wheatear is a small chat of open moorland, and 'songbird' places it by family rather than naming it.

  • Small layer → WHEATEAR small is wee and a layer of a cake is a tier; run the two together and the sound is the bird's name
  • you hear — What you hear is a sound and not a spelling, so the words are to be taken by ear and written down differently.

'Layer' pulls hard towards a hen, which is the wrong kind of layer entirely — the setter wants the layer of a cake. The bird has nothing to do with wheat or with ears.

15 across PERSPIRE

Let out water through conical structure (8)

Definition: Let out water · charade

Let out water → PERSPIRE: perspiring is the body letting water out through the skin, which is simply sweating said politely.

  • through → PER per is Latin for through, the sense kept in 'per capita'
  • conical structure → SPIRE a spire is the tapering cone on top of a church tower

Water running through a conical structure paints a fountain, and the answer is about skin. PER for 'through' is worth banking — it turns up in dozens of charades.

18 across SIGNED

"Retreat," soldiers heading to the front communicated silently (6)

Definition: communicated silently · charade + reversal

communicated silently → SIGNED: to sign is to talk with the hands, in sign language or by gesture, which makes no sound at all.

  • Retreat → DEN a den is a private retreat, a bolt-hole rather than a withdrawal
  • soldiers → GIS GIs are American soldiers
  • heading to the front — In an across entry the front is the left-hand end, so letters that head there arrive in the opposite order.

'Retreat' in quotation marks looks exactly like the order to turn about, and it is a hiding place instead. The turning is done by the march to the front.

20 across NO-BALL

Toff completely foul at Lord's (2-4)

Definition: foul at Lord's · charade

foul at Lord's → NO-BALL: a no-ball is the bowler's illegal delivery, and naming the ground is the setter's way of saying the foul is a cricketing one.

  • Toff → NOB a nob is a swell, somebody of high social rank
  • completely → ALL all in the sense of wholly, as in 'all wrong'

Lord's is cricket's headquarters, so the foul in question is a bowling offence. 'Toff' and 'nob' are both Victorian words for the same well-dressed man.

22 across TEETOTAL

Always taking the soft option? (8)

Definition: Always taking the soft option? · cryptic definition

Always taking the soft option? → TEETOTAL: soft drinks are the ones without alcohol, so somebody who takes only those has sworn off drink altogether.

  • Always taking → the surface reading: somebody who never once risks the harder choice
  • the soft option? → at a bar the soft option is the drink with no alcohol in it, and taking it every time is a way of life

There is no wordplay to find here — the whole clue is one description, and the step is realising that 'soft' is a bar word rather than a word about courage.

23 across DECK QUOITS

Throwing loops of rope on board, pack square boxes first for once (4,6)

Definition: Throwing loops of rope on board · charade + container + first letter

Throwing loops of rope on board → DECK QUOITS: quoits are rings pitched at a peg, and the shipboard version uses rope rings thrown along the deck.

  • pack → DECK a pack of cards is a deck of cards
  • square → QUITS to be quits with somebody is to be square with them, all debts settled
  • once → O its opening letter alone
  • boxes — To box a thing is to shut it in, so the word doing the boxing opens up and takes the other inside.
  • first — Only the opening letter of the word beside it is wanted, not the word itself.

The whole surface is a packing job aboard ship, which is also where the game is played, so definition and wordplay are describing the same deck.

24 across INDY

Motor races affected by gales not starting (4)

Definition: Motor races · deletion

Motor races → INDY: the Indianapolis 500 is universally shortened to the Indy, and the name now covers the whole American open-wheel racing series.

  • affected by gales → INDY weather affected by gales is WINDY, which here never gets to start
  • not starting — A word that is not starting has lost its opening letter and begins one along.

A race that never starts is the joke. The letter goes missing from the weather rather than from the race, which is what makes the surface hold together.

25 across ANTE

German teacher keeps money in pot, initially (4)

Definition: money in pot, initially · hidden word

money in pot, initially → ANTE: the ante is the stake every player must put into the pot before a hand begins, so it is the pot's opening money.

  • German teacher → ANTE the run bridges the join between the two words, ending just past the start of the second
  • keeps — What a phrase keeps, it holds on to inside itself, so the letters are already there in the words as written.

'Initially' looks like an instruction to take a first letter and is part of the definition instead — the stake goes in before any cards are dealt.

26 across AT ANCHOR

Report of a Russian ghost ship stopped in the Channel? (2,6)

Definition: stopped in the Channel? · homophone

stopped in the Channel? → AT ANCHOR: a vessel at anchor is held fast to the seabed and going nowhere, which for a ship is what being stopped means.

  • a Russian ghost ship → AT ANCHOR Russia's sanctioned 'ghost fleet' is made of tankers, so a Russian ghost ship is a tanker — and 'a tanker' is what you hear
  • Report of — A report of something is the account you are given by ear, so what follows is to be heard rather than read.

The surface is a news bulletin about the shadow fleet. The trick is where the break falls in the sound: move it one letter along and the same syllables turn nautical.

Down

1 down BRAINWAVE

No chicken sandwiches in Washington? What an idea! (9)

Definition: What an idea! · container + charade

What an idea! → BRAINWAVE: a brainwave is the sudden bright idea that greets exactly that exclamation, not the electrical trace neurologists measure.

  • No chicken → BRAVE a chicken is a coward, so no chicken is brave
  • in Washington → INWA IN taken as itself, then WA, the postal abbreviation for Washington state
  • sandwiches — To sandwich a filling is to shut it between two slices, so the outer word splits and takes the rest inside.

Two American conventions in five words: the state abbreviation WA, and 'chicken' for a coward. The exclamation mark belongs to the definition, not to the surface's outrage.

2 down CASHIER

Dismiss employee always putting their hand in the till (7)

Definition: Dismiss · double definition

To cashier an officer is to dismiss them from the service in disgrace; a cashier in a shop is the worker whose hands belong in the till.

  • Dismiss → the military sense: to strip an officer of rank and turn them out
  • employee always putting their hand in the till → the shop sense, where a hand in the till is the job rather than the crime

The joke lives in 'always': a till-dipping employee sounds like a thief, and the second definition is describing somebody entirely honest doing their work.

3 down SIR JOHN GIELGUD

Old luvvie girl judges on all but the last of his plays (3,4,7)

Definition: Old luvvie · anagram + deletion

Old luvvie → SIR JOHN GIELGUD: 'luvvie' is British slang for a grandly theatrical actor, and he was the most celebrated of the knighted stage generation.

  • girl judges on → GIRLJUDGESON twelve of the fourteen letters, taken as they stand
  • all but the last of his → HI HIS gives up its S, adding the last two letters to the pile
  • plays — To play a part is to appear as somebody you are not, and these letters appear as a name that is not their own order.
  • all but the last of — It keeps everything except the closing letter, so the word joins the mix one short.

A fourteen-letter anagram hides best when its fodder reads as ordinary sense — a girl judging a playwright's work — and when the indicator is the theatrical word 'plays'.

4 down OVERSAW

Did boss make too deep cuts? (7)

Definition: Did boss · charade

Did boss → OVERSAW: to oversee work is to be in charge of it, and the clue's 'Did' asks for that verb in the past.

  • too deep → OVER English marks excess with the prefix over-, so going too far is going over
  • cuts → SAW a saw is what makes cuts, and the tool's name serves as the verb

The surface is a manager asked about redundancies; the wordplay is a carpenter's blunder. The answer only turns into a past tense once the two pieces are read as one word.

5 down BRISKET

Cut chance of losing in wager (7)

Definition: Cut · container

Cut → BRISKET: brisket is the butcher's cut from the animal's breast, so the general word defines it as one cut among many.

  • wager → BET a wager is a bet, and it is the word that opens up
  • chance of losing → RISK the chance of losing something is the risk you run
  • in — The small word does the placing: what comes before it goes inside what comes after, rather than beside it.

Every word of the surface is about gambling and none of it is about meat. The one butcher's word in the clue is sitting at the very front.

6 down STAIR

Steps taken by proletariat succeeded in revolution (5)

Definition: Steps · hidden word + reversal

Steps → STAIR: a stair is a flight of steps taken as one thing — 'up the stair' — rather than a single tread.

  • proletariat succeeded → RIATS the letters run across the join — proleta|RIAT S|ucceeded — and they arrive turned round
  • taken by — What a phrase takes is carried inside it, so the letters are lifted out of the words exactly as they stand.
  • in revolution — A revolution turns a thing right round, so the run of letters has to be read the other way about.

A revolution by the proletariat is such a natural phrase that nothing in it looks like an instruction, which is exactly the cover a buried answer needs.

7 down CONGRESSPERSON

Representative upset being limited by sex, so neutral? (14)

Definition: Representative · charade + cycling

Representative → CONGRESSPERSON: a member of the US House is formally styled a Representative, so the definition names the office by its holder's title.

  • being → PERSON a being is a person — 'human being' with the human left off
  • sex → CONGRESS congress is the formal word for sexual intercourse, as in carnal congress
  • so neutral? → no letters: the setter's nod to why this ending exists, standing in for the two gendered titles
  • upset — In a down entry, upsetting the pile sends what was written at the foot of it up to the top.
  • limited by — To be limited by a thing is to have it as your edge, so the two words finish flush against each other rather than one inside the other.

The clue is about sex twice over — the act, and the grammatical kind the answer is designed to avoid. Only one of those meanings puts letters in the grid.

14 down ALEXANDER

Military leader's old mate aboard American Eagle? (9)

Definition: Military leader · container

Military leader → ALEXANDER: it names a commander by example — Alexander the Great, and the Field Marshal Alexander of the Second World War.

  • American Eagle? → ALANDER Apollo 11's Eagle was a lunar module — a lander, and an American one; the question mark admits it is an example
  • old mate → EX an ex is the partner you used to have
  • aboard — Anything taken aboard travels inside the vessel, so these letters ride within the word around them.

'American Eagle' reads as a bird, an airline or a coin, and the setter means a spacecraft. Once the Eagle is a lander, the indefinite article in front of it is doing real work.

16 down SPARKLE

Deer charges around in charming animation (7)

Definition: charming animation · charade + reversal

charming animation → SPARKLE: sparkle is the liveliness of a person's manner, the same metaphor as calling a talker sparkling.

  • Deer → ELK the elk is the crossword's standard deer
  • charges → RAPS a rap is a criminal charge — 'take the rap' — so charges are raps
  • around — Sent around, a thing comes back the way it went, so the whole assembly is read from its far end.

Two little words carry the whole clue: 'charges' means accusations rather than a stampede, and the deer is not running anywhere. Turned round, the pair spell the answer.

17 down EAT DIRT

Suffer humiliation, turning red at it (3,4)

Definition: Suffer humiliation · anagram

Suffer humiliation → EAT DIRT: to eat dirt is the idiom for swallowing an insult without answering back, a whole phrase rather than a synonym.

  • red at it → EAT DIRT the seven letters of RED AT IT, dealt again
  • turning — Milk that is turning is no longer what it was, and letters in the act of turning have not settled into their order yet.

'Turning red' is a blush, which is what humiliation looks like, so the indicator and its fodder read as a single idea. The answer is two words where the fodder is three.

19 down GOODISH

Fair beauty burdened by sentiment (7)

Definition: Fair · charade

Fair → GOODISH: the -ish weakens good to 'reasonably good', and 'fair' is the same grudging half-praise in a school report.

  • sentiment → GOO goo is sloppy sentimentality, the stuff a gooey film is made of
  • beauty → DISH a dish is long-standing slang for a good-looking person
  • burdened by — A burden sits on top of whatever carries it, so in a down entry the load is written above the thing bearing it.

Both pieces are slang, which is what lets the surface read as a straight sentence about somebody weighed down by feeling. A down entry's geometry sets the order.

21 down OCEAN

Saving pence, Capone doctored the drink (5)

Definition: the drink · anagram + deletion

the drink → OCEAN: 'the drink' is sailors' and airmen's slang for the sea, as in a plane ditching in the drink.

  • pence → P p is the abbreviation for pence, and it is the letter set aside
  • Capone → OCEAN CAPONE without that p leaves five letters to be rearranged
  • Saving — Saving here means 'except for', so the coin named next is held back rather than used.
  • doctored — A doctored document has been tampered with, and tampered-with letters no longer sit where they were put.

Al Capone was a bootlegger, so a doctored drink is exactly the story the surface wants to tell, and none of it has anything to do with the sea.
