Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,066

Across

1 across FERMAT'S LAST THEOREM

Scientific fact maybe lacking substance – flat-earthers most stupid (7,4,7)

Definition: Scientific fact · anagram + deletion

  • maybe lacking substance → ME MAYBE 'lacking substance' loses its insides (AYB), leaving M…E
  • flat-earthers most → FLATEARTHERSMOST anagram fodder, 16 letters
  • stupid → anagram indicator

The surface is the joke — flat-earthers really are the people ignoring scientific fact — and the answer runs on into 9 across. 'Lacking substance' is worth learning: a word emptied of its middle.

10 across COLOGNE

Pass person inhaling good scent (7)

Definition: scent · charade + container

  • Pass → COL a col is a mountain pass
  • person → ONE 'one' = a person
  • good → G standard abbreviation

The surface is someone walking past and catching a whiff, which is why 'Pass' reads as a verb. It is a noun here — a col, the dip between two peaks.

11 across FECUNDATE

Withdraw sheath for contraceptive during enjoyable time to get pregnant (9)

Definition: to get pregnant · container + reversal + outer letters

  • sheath for contraceptive → CE the 'sheath' (outer letters) of C-ontraceptiv-E
  • Withdraw → reversal indicator: CE becomes EC
  • enjoyable time → FUN DATE a fun date is an enjoyable time

Tramp at his most risqué, and 'sheath' works in both readings at once — the outer letters of contraceptive, and what the surface means by it. Reversed to EC, it slips into FUN DATE after the F.

12 across CUT UP

Focus for rescuing sheep in distress (3,2)

Definition: in distress · charade + middle letters

  • Focus for rescuing → CU the focus (exact centre) of 'resCUing'
  • sheep → TUP a tup is a ram

'Focus for' is an unusual way of asking for the exact middle of a word. And the definition is idiom rather than dictionary: you are cut up when something has upset you.

13 across EXPO

Sexpot stripped for show (4)

Definition: show · deletion

  • Sexpot → EXPO SEXPOT 'stripped' of its outer letters S and T

The joke lives entirely in the surface. 'Stripped' takes the outside off, which is worth noting — elsewhere the same word can mean the insides go instead.

14 across STAY-AT-HOME

Intimate squeezing partner’s core, relaxed in armchair? (4-2-4)

Definition: in armchair? · charade + container + middle letter

  • Intimate → SAY to intimate = to hint at, to say
  • partner’s core → T the middle letter of parTner
  • relaxed → AT HOME at home = at ease, relaxed

'Intimate' looks like an adjective in a clue about a couple, and it is a verb: to intimate is to hint. The definition is the armchair, where a stay-at-home does his supporting.

16 across CULMINATES

Destroying climate, sun reaches highest point (10)

Definition: reaches highest point · anagram

  • climate, sun → CLIMATESUN anagram fodder, 10 letters
  • Destroying → anagram indicator

The surface is a headline about the sun destroying the climate, topical enough that you read it for meaning and never count the letters sitting in front of you.

19 across BEAT

Risk money accepting a flutter (4)

Definition: flutter · container

  • Risk money → BET to risk money = to bet
  • a → A used literally

The trap is a straight swap: 'flutter' looks like the betting word and 'risk money' like the definition, when it is the other way round. A flutter is what a heart does.

21 across MANGA

Form of comic, Mark, with one short joke (5)

Definition: Form of comic · charade + deletion

  • Mark → M M = mark (the old German currency)
  • one → AN 'an' = one
  • short joke → GA GA(g) — a gag cut short

Capitalised, Mark looks like a man being addressed; it is the old German currency, and M is its abbreviation. Setters dress abbreviations as proper names constantly.

22 across REPROBATE

Rake with money returned to pick up sex worker (9)

Definition: Rake · container

  • money returned → REBATE a rebate is money returned
  • sex worker → PRO pro = prostitute (crossword staple)

'Rake' is the trap: not the garden tool but the eighteenth-century wastrel, a sense you meet far more often in crosswords than in life.

24 across ROSSINI

He scored from ball into box, missing header during international (7)

Definition: He scored · charade + deletion

  • ball into box → CROSS in football, a ball into the penalty box is a cross
  • missing header → ROSS CROSS loses its first letter — a football pun on 'header'
  • during → IN during = in
  • international → I I = international

The whole clue reads as football and is really about opera, with 'missing header' the best pun in the puzzle: a header removed means the first letter goes.

25 across WRECKED

Drunk with wine drinking fizzy stuff out of round (7)

Definition: Drunk · container + deletion

  • with → W w = with
  • wine → RED a red is a wine
  • fizzy stuff out of round → CKE COKE ('fizzy stuff') minus O ('round')

The surface is an ordinary evening's drinking, and the two smallest words do the most work — 'with' is a single letter, and 'round' is the O taken out of the coke.

26 across ADULTERATOR

Polluter to run after dirty river checked by Environment Agency (11)

Definition: Polluter · charade + container

  • dirty → ADULT adult = dirty, as in adult movies
  • river → R standard abbreviation
  • Environment Agency → EA its initials, wrapped round the R: E(R)A
  • to run → TOR TO + R (r = run, as on a cricket scorecard)

A dirty river being checked by the Environment Agency is a complete and plausible news story, which is the red herring. 'Dirty' for adult, as in adult films, is the piece to see first.

Down

1 down FRENCH POLYNESIA

Foreign land line scary: phone mobile connected with female (6,9)

Definition: Foreign land · anagram

  • line scary: phone → LINESCARYPHONE anagram fodder, 14 letters
  • female → F f = female, 'connected with' the fodder
  • mobile → anagram indicator

The surface is all telephones — line, phone, mobile — and not one word of it suggests the South Pacific. That distance between surface and answer is what makes a long anagram hard to see.

2 down RERUN

Once more broadcast concerning series (5)

Definition: Once more broadcast · charade

  • concerning → RE re = concerning, as in memo headers
  • series → RUN a run = a series (of shows, results…)

The whole clue reads as a line from a television listing, which is how the definition and the wordplay manage to hide inside the same sentence about repeats.

3 down ARMY ANT

Host, part of TV duo, is a crawler (4,3)

Definition: a crawler · charade

  • Host → ARMY host = army, a great multitude
  • part of TV duo → ANT Ant of Ant & Dec

'Host' is placed to look like a presenter standing beside his co-star, and it means a multitude instead. ANT is worth knowing in its own right — the crossword's standard worker.

4 down SOCIETY

Club foot out of sock that is tiny without padding (7)

Definition: Club · charade + deletion

  • foot out of sock → SOC SOCK loses its 'foot' (last letter K)
  • that is → IE i.e. = that is
  • tiny without padding → TY TINY with no 'padding' (inner letters) leaves T…Y

Two deletions dressed up as clothing: the foot comes off a sock, and the padding is the soft middle of a word. Both sit so neatly in the surface that neither reads as an instruction.

5 down ALLOCATE

Order at local store, ultimately to get deal (8)

Definition: deal · anagram + last letter

  • at local → ATLOCAL anagram fodder
  • store, ultimately → E the last letter of storE
  • Order → anagram indicator

'Order' opens the clue looking like something you place at a shop counter, and it is the anagram instruction. Indicators like the first word, where they pass as ordinary nouns.

6 down TIGHTROPE WALKER

Act in theatre work: PG film essentially getting released (9,6)

Definition: Act · anagram + middle letters

  • theatre work: PG → THEATREWORKPG anagram fodder, 13 letters
  • film essentially → IL the essential middle of fILm
  • getting released → anagram indicator — the letters are set loose

'Act' on its own is the definition, which gives you very little to aim at, and the film-certificate surface hides fifteen letters of fodder. A short definition beside a long answer usually means an anagram.

7 down STIFLE

Squash flies all over the place with end of swat (6)

Definition: Squash · anagram + last letter

  • flies → FLIES anagram fodder
  • end of swat → T the last letter of swaT
  • all over the place → anagram indicator

Every word belongs to one picture of swatting flies, so nothing sticks out as fodder or as instruction. That is what a well-made anagram looks like.

8 down WEEPIE

Small place cleared to screen one sad film (6)

Definition: sad film · charade + container + deletion

  • Small → WEE wee = small (Scots)
  • place cleared → PE PLACE 'cleared' of its middle leaves P…E
  • one → I I = one

'To screen' does both jobs at once: it is the container instruction, meaning to conceal, and it belongs to the world of the answer. WEE for small is Scots, and a crossword regular.

15 down TIRAMISU

I squeeze into casual suit that could be taken in when not satisfied? (8)

Definition: that could be taken in when not satisfied? · container + anagram

  • I → I used literally
  • squeeze → RAM to ram = to squeeze, cram
  • casual suit → TISU SUIT anagrammed ('casual' = disarranged)

'Taken in' is tailoring on the surface — a suit altered because it does not satisfy — and eating underneath: the pudding you find room for when you are still not full.

16 down CAMERA

Apple device set up date feature on smartphone (6)

Definition: feature on smartphone · reversal + charade

  • Apple device → MAC the computer; 'set up' reverses it in a down clue
  • date → ERA a date = an era, a period

The tech surface never once mentions photography, and 'set up' is positional: in a down clue it lifts MAC back up the grid rather than telling you to arrange anything.

17 down AIRLIFT

Rescue from island – lifeboat team wanting number in back of boat (7)

Definition: Rescue · container + deletion

  • island → I I = island
  • lifeboat team wanting number → RLI the RNLI 'wanting' (lacking) N for number
  • back of boat → AFT aft = the back of a boat

A maritime surface hiding an answer that happens by air. The RNLI is the sort of British initialism worth having ready — setters reach for it whenever a clue goes near the sea.

18 down EMPOWER

Give authority? Those people might (7)

Definition: Give authority? · charade

  • Those people → EM 'em = them, those people
  • might → POWER might = power, strength

The second half of the clue winks at its own answer: those people might, indeed, em-power. And 'EM for them is apostrophe-dropped speech, a favourite two-letter piece.

20 down TRENDY

Stylish attempt to cover butt (6)

Definition: Stylish · container

  • attempt → TRY an attempt = a try
  • butt → END butt = end (of a cigarette, say)

'Butt' is a cigarette end, not the anatomy the surface invites you to picture. Hearing 'attempt' as a noun is the other small step.

23 down OVERT

Visible hole on green (5)

Definition: Visible · charade

  • hole → O a hole is O-shaped
  • green → VERT vert = green in heraldry (and French)

The golfing surface, hole and green, is entirely decorative. VERT is heraldry's green, and it sits alongside OR for gold as a colour a setter can turn into letters.
