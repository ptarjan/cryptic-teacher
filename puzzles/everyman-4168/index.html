Across

1 across FAVE

Pet's loud greeting (4)

Definition: Pet · charade

  • loud → F f is the musician's mark for forte, the instruction to play loudly
  • greeting → AVE ave is the Latin hail, the greeting of Ave Maria and Ave Caesar

A dog barking its hello at the door.

Nothing on the surface is about volume or Latin: an animal greets you noisily and that is the whole picture. The apostrophe-s is doing the work of an equals sign.

3 across MARGINALLY

Slightly spoil drink put before friend (10)

Definition: Slightly · charade

  • spoil → MAR to mar something is to spoil it, the way a scratch mars a table
  • drink → GIN gin is the crossword's default spirit, three letters and always to hand
  • friend → ALLY an ally is the friend who is on your side in a fight

A spoiled drink set down in front of a friend.

'Slightly spoil' reads as one phrase, and only the first word of it is the definition — the spoiling belongs to the wordplay.

9 across FACT

Falsehood's absolute converse; truth, primarily? (4)

Definition: Falsehood's absolute converse; truth, primarily? · &lit + first letters

  • Falsehood's → F its opening letter; a falsehood is a lie
  • absolute → A its opening letter; absolute means complete, with nothing held back
  • converse → C its opening letter; the converse of a thing is its opposite
  • truth → T its opening letter, taken like the three before it

Someone explaining, a shade pompously, what the opposite of a lie is.

'Truth' looks like the definition parked at the end, and it is really the fourth word being mined for its initial. The sentence describes what its own initials spell, which is what the question mark flags.

10 across GOING VIRAL

Popular online: dubiously 'original', vacuous vlogging (5,5)

Definition: Popular online · anagram + outer letters

  • 'original' → ORIGINAL the word in quotation marks is here for its eight letters, not for its meaning
  • vlogging → VG the letters at the two ends of the word, with everything between them emptied out

A vlogger's claim to be original is treated with suspicion and the content dismissed as hollow.

The quotation marks around 'original' do two jobs at once: sneering at the vlogger on the surface, and marking the word off as raw material.

12 across HABERDASHER

Sewer's outlet? (11)

Definition: Sewer's outlet? · cryptic definition

  • Sewer's → read as the drain under the street, which is what the surface is about, and the word is said quite differently in that sense
  • outlet? → an outlet is also a shop, so the question is where somebody at a sewing machine goes for thread, buttons and zips

A question about where the drains empty out.

The joke rests on one word with two pronunciations: the drain, and the person who sews. British high streets kept these shops for the small-wares a dressmaker needs.

15 across HALLWAY

At midpoint of entrance area, having change of heart (7)

Definition: entrance area · substitution

  • At midpoint → HALFWAY something at the midpoint of a journey is halfway along it
  • change of heart → L the F sitting at the exact centre of those seven letters gives way to a new one

Standing in the middle of the entrance and changing your mind.

'Change of heart' is the surface's second thoughts and the wordplay's instruction, and the two readings share the word 'heart': in one it is the mind, in the other the middle letter.

16 across TOO MUCH

Meditator's sound, in a sense, is more than is tolerable (3,4)

Definition: more than is tolerable · container

  • a sense → TOUCH touch is one of the five senses, and its five letters open up to take a passenger
  • Meditator's sound → OM om is the syllable chanted in meditation, hummed on one long note

A complaint that the chanting next door has gone beyond what anybody can stand.

'In a sense' is the phrase people use for 'loosely speaking', which is exactly what it is not doing here: the sense is one of the five, and it is wanted for its letters.

17 across EARTHLY

Most of the lining primitive material (7)

Definition: material · deletion + container

  • primitive → EARLY primitive man is early man, the first and simplest of his kind
  • Most of the → TH the definite article with its last letter left off

The bulk of a garment's lining turns out to be crude stuff.

Nothing announces itself as an instruction: 'lining' is a noun on the surface and a verb in the wordplay. 'Material' is the philosopher's sense, the opposite of spiritual, not a bolt of cloth.

19 across BUMPKIN

Protuberance on monarch, largely unsophisticated type (7)

Definition: unsophisticated type · charade + deletion

  • Protuberance → BUMP a bump is a small protuberance, the lump a knock leaves behind
  • monarch, largely → KIN a monarch is a king, and the greater part of that word is its first three letters

A swelling raised on the royal head.

In an across clue 'on' means no more than 'next to', so the two pieces are laid left to right in the order they are clued rather than one above the other.

20 across BELL-RINGING

Activity in church reminding you of something? (4-7)

Definition: Activity in church · double definition

  • Activity in church → the tower team hauling on ropes, a Sunday morning sound in every English village
  • reminding you of something? → the idiom for a faint memory: something sounds faintly at the back of the mind and you half recognise it

A churchgoer being asked whether the sound stirs a memory.

The same hyphenated word is defined twice, the second time through the idiom — a thing that rings a bell brings a memory back. Only the question mark warns that the second reading is playing.

23 across OXTAIL SOUP

Beefy broth and duck, served badly, spoil a tux (6,4)

Definition: Beefy broth · anagram

  • duck → O a duck in cricket is a score of nothing, written as a nought on the scorecard
  • spoil a tux → SPOIL A TUX nine letters taken exactly as they stand, for their spelling and not their sense

A badly served dinner of broth and duck ends up down the front of a dinner jacket.

The menu is the disguise: 'and duck' reads as the second course and is really one letter off a cricket scorecard. Everything after the comma is raw material, the tux included.

24 across ALSO

Further some characters in Royal Society (4)

Definition: Further · hidden word

  • Royal Society → ALSO four letters in a row that begin inside the first word and finish inside the second

Advancing the careers of one or two figures at the Royal Society.

'Further' is a verb on the surface — to advance somebody's career — and an adverb in the answer. The Royal Society is Britain's national academy of science, here only for the letters at the join.

25 across MACEDONIAN

Balkan spice introduced to academic Scotsman (10)

Definition: Balkan · charade

  • spice → MACE mace is the spice made from the lacy coat around a nutmeg
  • academic → DON a don is a senior member of an Oxford or Cambridge college
  • Scotsman → IAN Ian is the Scottish forename crosswords keep permanently on hand

Someone presenting a jar of spice to a Scottish academic.

Three ordinary things are named and not one of them is Balkan; the definition is the single word at the front, an adjective standing in for a nationality.

26 across IDLE

Workshy Everyman had leave (on vacation) (4)

Definition: Workshy · charade + outer letters

  • Everyman had → ID the setter signs himself Everyman, so this is 'I had' written short
  • leave → LE the first and last letters of the word, with the middle emptied out

A lazy setter who has just been away on holiday.

'Everyman' is the setter's own pen name, so it stands in for the first person: what Everyman had, I had. The bracketed phrase is holiday talk and an instruction at the same time.

Down

1 down FIFTH WHEEL

The whiffler mostly daft, useless individual (5,5)

Definition: useless individual · anagram + deletion

  • The whiffler mostly → FIFTH WHEEL eleven letters there, and dropping the final R leaves exactly the ten the grid wants

A parade official written off as half-witted and surplus to requirements.

A whiffler is the man who clears a path in front of a procession, a job with the air of being unnecessary, so the surface reads as an insult rather than as an instruction.

2 down VOCABULARY

That's all you have to say? (10)

Definition: That's all you have to say? · cryptic definition

  • That's all → the surface reading is a snub: is that really the whole of your argument, is that everything you can offer
  • you have to say → the setter's reading counts words rather than weighing them — every one you own and can produce

One person cutting another short for making a thin case.

Read 'have to' as an obligation and the clue is a put-down; read it as possession and it is an inventory of every word you keep.

4 down ANOMALY

Converted layman suppresses expression of wonder, showing inconsistency (7)

Definition: inconsistency · anagram + container

  • Converted layman → ANMALY the six letters of the noun rearranged, with a gap left after the second
  • expression of wonder → O the wondering exclamation written down is a single letter: O, look at that

A new convert holding back a gasp of amazement.

'Showing inconsistency' looks like a participle rounding off the sentence, and it is the definition: one word of it, with 'showing' doing nothing but joining.

5 down GUNSHOT

Weapon's warm: has this been discharged? (7)

Definition: Weapon's warm: has this been discharged? · charade + &lit

  • Weapon's → GUNS the weapon is a gun, and the possessive apostrophe hands over its S as well
  • warm → HOT hot is warm taken a good deal further

A detective at the scene, touching the barrel and drawing the obvious conclusion.

The colon is a dramatic pause on the surface and a joint in the wordplay. The whole sentence works as the definition too, which is what makes 'this' fair — it points at the answer rather than at the gun.

6 down NEVER YOU MIND

I've not saying I've done my run after workout (5,3,4)

Definition: I've not saying · anagram

  • I've done my run → NEVER YOU MIND twelve letters in that boast, exactly the twelve the three words of the entry need

A gym-goer insisting, a little defensively, that the run is already done.

The clue says 'I've' twice and only the second one is fodder; the first belongs to the definition. 'Workout' is the sort of everyday noun a setter can bury an instruction in, and nothing in the sentence points at it.

7 down LORD

God, that's almost flipping funny (4)

Definition: God · deletion + reversal

  • funny → DROL something funny is droll, and that word is written one letter short of its end

Somebody half-amused, swearing mildly at a joke that nearly works.

'God' opens the clue as an oath and is doing duty as the definition, while 'flipping' reads as the same kind of mild swearing. Both words are working harder than they look.

8 down YULE

We're told the solver's going to festival (4)

Definition: festival · homophone

  • the solver's going to → YULE the person solving the clue is 'you', and 'you will' contracts to one syllable

Word has got round that whoever is doing the crossword is off to a festival.

'The solver' is the one phrase in the clue pointing out of the puzzle at the reader, and 'going to' is the future tense rather than a direction of travel.

11 down DRAW THE BLIND

Part of bedtime routine: depict nursery-rhyme mice? (4,3,5)

Definition: Part of bedtime routine · charade

  • depict → DRAW to draw something is to depict it in pencil or ink
  • nursery-rhyme mice? → THE BLIND the rhyme counts three of them and says in its title what is wrong with all three

Bedtime asks for one more thing: a quick sketch of the mice from the rhyme.

'Three Blind Mice' is the rhyme, and the setter takes only the adjective from it — the number and the animals stay behind. The question mark admits you have to name the rhyme before you can borrow from it.

13 down DUCK-BILLED

Avoid having charged description of Drake? (4-6)

Definition: description of Drake? · charade

  • Avoid → DUCK to duck a punch or a question is to avoid it
  • charged → BILLED a customer who was charged for something was billed for it

Keeping clear of a highly charged account of Drake.

The capital D pulls hard towards Sir Francis, or towards the rapper, and the setter means neither: a drake is a male duck.

14 down CHINA GOOSE

Flighty type giving crockery to nitwit (5,5)

Definition: Flighty type · charade

  • crockery → CHINA china is the fine white ware a crockery cupboard is full of, named after the country that first exported it
  • nitwit → GOOSE a silly goose is an old and gentle way of calling somebody a nitwit

A frivolous sort handing plates over to an idiot.

'Flighty type' wants to be a scatty person and means a bird that flies. The breed is the big farmyard goose developed from the Asian swan goose, known for the knob above its bill.

18 down YELTSIN

Former president in style, fashionable (7)

Definition: Former president · anagram

  • in style → YELTSIN seven letters in that phrase, exactly the seven the entry wants and no others

Somebody arriving in style and thoroughly in vogue.

'In style' looks like the manner of the great man's arrival and is the raw material; the only instruction in the clue is the last word, dressed as a compliment.

19 down BERMUDA

Bare mud wrestling in Atlantic tourist destination (7)

Definition: Atlantic tourist destination · anagram

  • Bare mud → BERMUDA seven letters between those two words, exactly the seven the entry needs

A mud-wrestling bout, stripped off, laid on for the holidaymakers.

'Wrestling in' reads as a single phrase and only the first word of it is the instruction — the 'in' belongs to the definition that follows.

21 down NORM

As a rule, what's found in an extremity of neoliberalism (4)

Definition: As a rule · outer letters + charade

  • of neoliberalism → N the letter standing at the front end of that long word
  • an extremity → OR only one extremity is asked for, either of them, and the little word that offers a choice between two things is spelt out in full
  • neoliberalism → M the letter standing at the other end of the same word

The far edge of free-market politics, and what turns up out there.

Read 'an extremity' politically and the clue is about where an ideology ends up. The joke is that the wordplay refuses to choose which end it means and writes that refusal into the middle of the answer.

22 down OTIC

Describing ears somewhat idiotically (4)

Definition: Describing ears · hidden word

  • idiotically → OTIC four letters in a row, sitting well inside that long adverb

A silly way of talking about ears.

The hiding place is one long word rather than a run across a join, which is much harder to spot: no capital, no odd punctuation, and 'somewhat' reads as a throwaway.
