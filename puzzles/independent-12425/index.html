Independent Cryptic Crossword No 12,425

Across

1 across GENERATION

Age of Information speech starting late, after English ... (10)

Definition: Age · charade + deletion

  • Information → GEN gen is the word for the information you need — 'give me the gen on him'
  • English → E E is the standard abbreviation for English
  • speech → RATION a speech is an oration, and one starting late has lost its opening O

The dots run the sentence on into 6 across, which is what makes 'Age of Information' look like a set phrase when the definition stops at its first word.

6 across PLAY

... act's song on piano (4)

Definition: act · charade

  • piano → P p is the mark in a score for piano, meaning softly
  • song → LAY a lay is an old word for a song or ballad

The leading dots carry over from 1 across; ignore them and what is left is a two-piece build with the definition sitting first.

9 across MAINE

One of fifty children I amused somewhat when falling over (5)

Definition: One of fifty · hidden word + reversal

  • children I amused → ENIAM childrEN I AMused holds five letters in an unbroken run, waiting to be read the other way

Fifty is the American state count, and that is the whole of the definition — everything after it is a hiding place spread across three words.

10 across PALLADIUM

Cloud facing union's leader amid unruly element (9)

Definition: element · charade + anagram + first letter

  • Cloud → PALL a pall of smoke is a cloud hanging over something
  • union's leader → U the letter at the head of union
  • amid → AMID amid reads as a position and is really four letters of material

The trap is 'amid', which every solver first reads as an instruction about where something goes. Palladium is element 46, mined mostly for catalytic converters.

12 across MORSEL

Easy listening music? Promote short piece (6)

Definition: piece · charade + deletion

  • Easy listening music? → MOR MOR is the radio trade's label for middle-of-the-road, the easy listening slot
  • Promote → SEL to promote a product is to sell it, and SELL cut short is SEL

The question mark is the tell that easy listening music is being named by its trade abbreviation rather than defined.

14 across DODDERY

Faltering comedian ending on unintelligible line (7)

Definition: Faltering · charade + last letter

  • comedian → DODD Ken Dodd, the Liverpool comedian with the tickling stick
  • unintelligible → E the printed word unintelligible, closing letter only
  • line → RY RY is the map abbreviation for a railway line

'Ending on unintelligible' asks for a letter of the word as printed, not a synonym for it — a device that reads as ordinary English until you need it.

15 across SLINGERS

People who shy girls sent almost mad (8)

Definition: People who shy · anagram + deletion

  • girls → GIRLS the five letters of girls go into the pot whole
  • sent → SEN sent, taken almost to its end, joins them one letter short as SEN

The awkward part is the reach: 'almost' picks off 'sent' alone and leaves 'girls' untouched, which is why the letter count only works one way.

16 across UNUSED

Idle American bores classy new journalist (6)

Definition: Idle · container + charade

  • classy → U U is the label from U and non-U speech, the crossword's word for classy
  • new → N N is the standard abbreviation for new
  • journalist → ED ed, short for editor, is the crossword journalist, and the drilling happens just before it
  • American → US US is the American doing the boring

U for classy comes from Nancy Mitford's U and non-U speech, and it is the setter's standing shorthand for anything posh.

19 across SUPERB

"Give us a twirl!" Games with King Brucie at the front, terrific (6)

Definition: terrific · reversal + charade + first letter

  • us → US the setter's own 'us', two letters ready to be spun
  • Games → PE PE, physical education, is games on the school timetable
  • King → R R is Rex, the Latin king
  • Brucie → B the front letter of Bruce Forsyth's nickname

Bruce Forsyth's catchphrase is a whole quoted sentence doing one small job, turning two letters round, and then Brucie turns up again as an initial.

21 across STRICTLY

Case for Turandot lyrics being translated to the letter? (8)

Definition: to the letter · anagram + outer letters

  • Turandot → TT the opera's name keeps only its two end letters, one T at each
  • lyrics → LYRICS the six letters of lyrics join them in the pot

Turandot is italicised because it is a title, and those italics are the only hint that the word is being handled as letters rather than as an opera.

23 across ENAMOUR

Charm of hair swept back over curl in the centre (7)

Definition: Charm · reversal + charade + middle letters

  • hair → MANE a mane is a head of hair, here written from the back
  • over → O O is the cricket scorer's abbreviation for an over
  • curl → UR the middle two letters of cURl

The surface is all hairdressing and every word of it does a different job: one word to reverse, one cricket abbreviation, one word to take the middle out of.

25 across GLOOMY

German ladies say brother's dim (6)

Definition: dim · charade

  • German → G G is the abbreviation for German
  • ladies → LOO the Ladies is one of the two doors on a pub loo
  • brother → MY brother! and my! are the same mild exclamation of surprise

Two exclamations meeting is the joke — 'brother!' and 'my!' are both what people say when something is mildly too much.

27 across JOE BLOGGS

Book records for auditor, average kind of chap (3,6)

Definition: average kind of chap · charade + homophone

  • Book → JOB Job is the Old Testament book of suffering and patience
  • records → LOGS a ship's logs are its records
  • Book records → JOE BLOGGS run the two together and say them aloud: the B slides across the gap between the words

A biblical book and a shelf of logs are two flat nouns until you say them quickly, and what falls out is the name we give to nobody in particular.

28 across CARDS

Republican interrupting scoundrel's brag? (5)

Definition: brag · container

  • scoundrel's → CADS a cad is the crossword's scoundrel, and the 's supplies the closing letter
  • Republican → R R is the abbreviation for Republican in American election coverage

'Brag' reads as boasting right up to the question mark, which is the only signal that the word is a noun and the name of a game.

29 across YOUR

Solver's Yankee ball game in retirement (4)

Definition: Solver's · charade + reversal

  • Yankee → Y Yankee is Y in the phonetic alphabet
  • ball → O a ball drawn on paper is a plain round O
  • game → RU RU is rugby union, and it is the piece that withdraws

Only the game turns round, not the ball: 'in retirement' reaches back a single word, and testing how far an indicator reaches is half of solving one.

30 across STATEMENTS

Maybe 9 soldiers tense before special bulletins (10)

Definition: bulletins · charade

  • Maybe 9 → STATE 9 across turns out to be MAINE, which is maybe a state
  • soldiers → MEN soldiers are the men in the ranks
  • tense → T T is the grammarian's abbreviation for tense
  • special → S S is the abbreviation for special

A bare number is a cross-reference, so 9 across has to be solved first, and 'maybe' is then the flag that the answer there is one example of a wider word.

Down

1 down GAME

Heat possibly rising before Spain match (4)

Definition: match · reversal + charade

  • Heat possibly → MAG Heat is a celebrity weekly, so it is possibly a mag — and it climbs the entry
  • Spain → E E is the international vehicle code for Spain, from Espana

The capital H is the giveaway that is meant to be missed: this Heat is a title on the newsagent's shelf, not a temperature.

2 down NAIROBI

Show outside broadcast in Northern Ireland's capital (7)

Definition: capital · container + charade

  • Northern Ireland → NI NI is the everyday abbreviation for Northern Ireland, and it splits to take the rest
  • Show → AIR to air a programme is to show it
  • outside broadcast → OB OB is the broadcaster's shorthand for an outside broadcast

Northern Ireland's capital is Belfast, and the clue is counting on you reaching for it — the two words are letters to be split open, not a country to look up.

3 down RYE

Argyle periodically showing spirit (3)

Definition: spirit · alternate letters

  • Argyle → RYE aRgYlE hands over its second, fourth and sixth letters

Argyle is a sock pattern and Plymouth's football club, and both are decoys: the word is here only to be counted through.

4 down TIPPLERS

Dump student taken in by a society's regular drinkers (8)

Definition: regular drinkers · charade + container

  • Dump → TIP the tip is where you dump the rubbish
  • a → PER per means a — twice per day is twice a day — and it opens up to take the next letter
  • student → L L-plates mark a learner, the crossword's standing student
  • society → S S is the abbreviation for society

'A' for PER is the piece worth banking — pounds a head, miles an hour — and it does the hiding here, because nobody suspects an article.

5 down OGLED

Stared at Adele, upset and exposed after pitch error (5)

Definition: Stared at · charade + deletion + reversal

  • pitch error → OG an own goal, marked OG on the scoresheet, is the error you can only make on a pitch
  • Adele → DEL strip the singer's outer A and E and DEL stands exposed, then tips over

The comma is doing the misdirection: it sets Adele up as the person being stared at, when the clue wants only the middle of the name.

7 down LOITERS

Hangs around Liberal, going off Tories (7)

Definition: Hangs around · anagram

  • Liberal → L L is the abbreviation for Liberal on a ballot paper
  • Tories → TORIES the six letters of the party name are the material to be shuffled

A political surface is the setter's favourite cover for an anagram: 'going off Tories' reads as disillusionment and means only 'rearrange'.

8 down YUMMY

Attractive model's daughter becoming Yale's principal (5)

Definition: Attractive · substitution

  • model → DUMMY a tailor's dummy is the model that clothes are fitted on
  • daughter → D D is the family-tree abbreviation for daughter, and it is the letter that leaves
  • Yale's principal → Y the front letter of Yale steps into the vacancy

A substitution clue always names both letters, the one leaving and the one arriving, so 'becoming' promises a word you know already with one letter changed.

11 down ANDANTINO

Instruction from Madonna, losing head badly when covering metal? (9)

Definition: Instruction · anagram + deletion + container

  • Madonna → ADONNA take the M off the front and ADONNA is the letter stock to shuffle
  • metal → TIN tin is the crossword's handiest metal, and it is wrapped up inside

The question mark apologises for 'metal': tin is a metal by example, not a definition of one. Andantino is a shade quicker than andante.

13 down SUGAR BOWL

Small uniform, clothing that hurts rear of admiral in vessel (5,4)

Definition: vessel · charade + last letter

  • Small → S S is the clothing-label abbreviation for small
  • uniform → U U is uniform in the phonetic alphabet
  • clothing → GARB garb is what a person is clothed in
  • that hurts → OW ow is the noise that means exactly that
  • rear of admiral → L the letter at the back of admiral

'Clothing' looks like an instruction to wrap one piece round another and is really a plain noun — that is the whole trick in a five-piece build.

15 down SIS

Relative clears Chianti bottles last of all (3)

Definition: Relative · last letters

  • clears Chianti bottles → SIS the closing letters of clearS chiantI bottleS

'Bottles' looks like a container instruction, and a wine word in a wine surface is exactly where you would expect one — it is really the third word being trimmed.

17 down DAY

Robin perhaps lifted by hand now and then (3)

Definition: Robin perhaps · alternate letters + reversal

  • by hand → YAD bY hAnD gives up its second, fourth and sixth letters, which then climb the entry

Sir Robin Day was the BBC's great political interviewer, and 'perhaps' is the flag that a surname is being clued through one of the people who bore it.

18 down STAGE SET

Move seats and get props and scenery (5,3)

Definition: props and scenery · anagram

  • seats and get → SEATSGET the eight letters of the two words, with 'and' just holding them together

The clue reads as instructions to the stage crew, which is the joke: 'move seats and get' is the material rather than the job.

20 down PLATEAU

Under pressure formerly, are United level? (7)

Definition: level · charade

  • pressure → P P is the physicist's abbreviation for pressure
  • formerly → LATE the late king is the former one, so late does duty for formerly
  • are → A an are is a metric unit of area, written a, and a hundred of them make a hectare
  • United → U U is how United is written on a league table

A crossword 'are' is almost always the unit of area rather than the verb, and it is worth banking, because the verb is what the eye sees every time.

22 down TAMARIN

Primate thanks sailor without hesitation (7)

Definition: Primate · charade + deletion

  • thanks → TA ta is the informal thanks
  • sailor → MARINER a mariner is a sailor
  • hesitation → ER er is the noise of hesitation, and it is the pair of letters that comes out

'Primate' points at monkeys here rather than archbishops — the other sense the word carries, and the one the churchy surface would rather you tried.

23 down ENJOY

Like judge probing opera company? Initially, yes (5)

Definition: Like · container + charade + first letter

  • opera company → ENO ENO is English National Opera, and the probe goes in after its first two letters
  • judge → J J is how a judge is abbreviated in a law report, as in Smith J
  • yes → Y the opening letter of yes

The question mark warns that 'opera company' means one particular company rather than the general idea, and ENO is the set of initials you are expected to know.

24 down RIGHT

Just female getting away from the creeps, ... (5)

Definition: Just · deletion

  • the creeps → FRIGHT giving somebody the creeps is giving them a fright
  • female → F F is the standard abbreviation for female, and it is the letter that escapes

The ellipsis runs the sentence on into 26 down, so the comma and dots at the end are punctuation rather than anything to parse.

26 down ASPS

... vacuous admirers, posers and snakes (4)

Definition: snakes · outer letters + charade

  • admirers → AS hollow out admirers and its first and last letters are what remain
  • posers → PS posers emptied the same way leaves its outer pair

One 'vacuous' serves both words at once, which is why the comma is carrying more weight than it looks: it holds two identical operations together.

28 down CAM

Being cut off from papa affected banker (3)

Definition: banker · deletion

  • affected → CAMP affected manners are camp
  • papa → P papa is P in the phonetic alphabet, and P is the letter cut off here

'Banker' is the crossword's favourite lie: not somebody who works in a bank but a thing that has banks, which is to say a river.
