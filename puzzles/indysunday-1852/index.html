Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,852

Across

1 across FIREARM

Weapon provided in retirement to raise money (7)

Definition: Weapon · reversal + charade

A firearm is any gun that fires a shot with an explosive charge, so the definition names the wider class the answer belongs to.

  • provided in retirement → FI 'provided' in the sense of 'if', turned round
  • raise → REAR to rear a child is to raise one
  • money → M M is the standard abbreviation for money
  • in retirement — Somebody in retirement has withdrawn, gone back the way they came, and the word in front of the phrase is written out backwards to match.

A pensioner handed a gun as a way of bringing in some extra cash.

'Raise money' looks like one idea and is two separate pieces; 'provided' is the everyday 'if', as in 'provided that you come'.

5 across CONFORM

Accommodate prisoner with criminal record (7)

Definition: Accommodate · charade

To conform is to fall in with what is required, which is 'accommodate' in its sense of adapting yourself rather than finding somebody a bed.

  • prisoner → CON a con is a convict
  • criminal record → FORM form is British slang for a police record — somebody with form has previous convictions

Finding room for a convict who has previous convictions.

'Accommodate' is the trap: it reads as putting somebody up and means bending to fit. Racing gave English 'form' for a past record, and the police borrowed it.

9 across INGOT

Metal bar securing otter traps (5)

Definition: Metal bar · hidden word

An ingot is metal cast into a solid block for storage or transport, which is the bar shape gold bullion comes in.

  • securing otter → INGOT five consecutive letters run across the join between these two words
  • traps — Something trapped is held fast by whatever sits on either side of it, which is where the letters wanted here are caught.

A metal bar holding shut the traps set for an otter.

'Traps' reads as the noun an otter gets caught in and works as the verb doing the holding, which is the only instruction in the line.

10 across SALAD DAYS

Easy-peasy, add a lasagne, bring back entertaining - good times! (5,4)

Definition: good times · hidden word + reversal

Salad days are the carefree early years of a life, so 'good times' names them in the loose, nostalgic sense the exclamation mark points at.

  • Easy-peasy, add a lasagne → SALAD DAYS nine consecutive letters sit inside this stretch, running backwards
  • bring back — Bringing something back is fetching it the other way, so the run of letters reads right to left along this across answer.
  • entertaining — To entertain guests is to have them inside the house, so this stretch of the clue is holding something within it.

A breezy recipe note about throwing a lasagne together.

The phrase is Cleopatra's in Antony and Cleopatra — 'my salad days, when I was green in judgement' — where green means young and untried.

11 across ARTIFICIAL

With Earth unprotected I authorised releases of plastic (10)

Definition: plastic · charade + middle letters + deletion

Plastic used as an adjective means synthetic or insincere — a plastic smile — and that is the sense in which it matches the answer.

  • Earth unprotected → ART EARTH with the letters at each end stripped away
  • I → I the single letter is used just as it stands
  • authorised releases of → FICIAL something authorised is official, and OF is what it lets go
  • unprotected — A word left unprotected loses the letters standing guard at each end, and only the middle survives.
  • releases — To release something is to let it go, so the two letters named straight afterwards leave the word they were sitting in.

An official admitting they signed off plastic waste while the planet lay unprotected.

'Releases of plastic' is an environmental phrase in the surface and an instruction in the wordplay, where 'of' is a thing to be thrown away rather than a joining word.

12 across BOLD

Striking bachelor of long standing (4)

Definition: Striking · charade

Bold means standing out — a bold colour, bold type — which is what 'striking' describes when it is used of appearance.

  • bachelor → B B is the abbreviation for bachelor, as in BA and BSc
  • of long standing → OLD a friendship of long standing is an old one

An eye-catching man who has been unmarried for years.

'Of long standing' is an adjective phrase meaning long-established, not a comment on how long anybody has been waiting about.

14 across EVIL SPIRITS

Elvis trip is blighted with gremlins? (4,7)

Definition: gremlins · anagram

Gremlins are the mischievous imps blamed when machinery goes wrong, so they are one species of the answer and the question mark says so.

  • Elvis trip is → ELVISTRIPIS eleven letters of fodder, three small words taken whole
  • blighted → the shuffling instruction
  • blighted — A blighted crop is spoiled and misshapen, and letters blighted the same way come out in a shape they did not start in.

An Elvis-themed holiday ruined by things going mysteriously wrong.

'Is' belongs to the letters rather than to the grammar, which is the count most solvers get wrong here — eleven, not nine.

18 across BRONZE MEDAL

Face of net zero blamed wrongly for third-rate award (6,5)

Definition: third-rate award · anagram + first letter

The bronze medal goes to whoever finishes third, so 'third-rate' is being used literally here instead of as the insult it usually is.

  • Face of net → N the front letter of 'net'
  • zero blamed → ZEROBLAMED ten more letters of fodder, the two words taken whole
  • wrongly → the shuffling instruction
  • Face of — The face of a thing is its front, so only the opening letter of the word after this phrase is wanted.
  • wrongly — A job done wrongly comes out in the wrong order, which is what happens to the letters gathered before it.

Net zero policy being unfairly blamed for a poor showing.

'Third-rate' is an insult everywhere except on a podium, where third is simply a placing; 'net zero' is a policy in the surface and one letter in the wordplay.

21 across AMYL

Radical setter's imprisoned in Alabama (4)

Definition: Radical · container

In chemistry a radical is a group of atoms that travels between compounds intact, and amyl is the five-carbon group C5H11.

  • Alabama → AL AL is the two-letter postal abbreviation for the state
  • setter's → MY the setter speaks in the first person, so anything belonging to them is 'my'
  • imprisoned in — Being imprisoned in a place puts you within its walls, so this piece sits between the letters of whatever follows.

A political extremist locked up in an Alabama jail.

'Radical' is a political word everywhere but the laboratory, and the setter talking about themselves is one of the most-used tricks in the game.

22 across ADROITNESS

Confounded assertion about daughter's dexterity (10)

Definition: dexterity · anagram + container

Adroitness is skill with the hands or the wits — literally right-handedness, from the French a droit — which is what dexterity names.

  • assertion → AROITNESS the nine letters of ASSERTION, rebuilt
  • daughter → D D is the standard abbreviation for daughter
  • Confounded — Something confounded has been thrown into confusion, and letters in confusion do not keep the order they came in.
  • about — Putting one thing about another wraps it round, so the letter named next ends up inside the rebuilt letters.

A disputed claim about how nimble somebody's daughter is.

'Confounded' is an exclamation of annoyance in the surface — confounded nuisance — and an instruction in the wordplay, which is why it can sit at the front.

25 across IN ANY CASE

Upper or lower? Whatever! (2,3,4)

Definition: Whatever · double definition

The phrase is the shrug that means whatever happens, anyway; the second reading is printer's talk, since every letter is set in upper or lower case.

  • Upper or lower? → the two forms a printed letter can take, capital or small
  • Whatever! → the dismissive shrug: it makes no difference which one you pick

Somebody offered a choice between two things and refusing to care which.

Read as dialogue it is a question about berths or decks, answered with a shrug; the pun is on 'case', which printers use for capitals and small letters.

26 across EXUDE

From 6 November missing former partner's heading for discharge (5)

Definition: discharge · deletion + charade

To exude something is to let it ooze out through a surface, which is 'discharge' in its seeping sense rather than its sacking one.

  • former partner's → EX an ex is a former partner
  • From 6 November missing → UDE the answer at 6 down, less the N that November stands for in the phonetic alphabet
  • missing — What is missing has been taken out, so the letter named just before this word is gone from the word it sat in.
  • heading — Something heading for a place is on its way to the front of it, which is where this pair of letters ends up.

Since 6 November a missing former partner has been heading for a discharge.

A bare number sends you to another light for your letters, and 'November' is the phonetic alphabet's N — a whole word standing in for one letter.

27 across SPLODGE

Say, party records on the counter make a big splash (7)

Definition: a big splash · charade + reversal

A splodge is a big irregular blot of something wet, paint or mud landing all at once, which is a splash where it lands.

  • Say → EG eg is short for exempli gratia, the Latin for 'for example'
  • party → DO a do is a party or a social function
  • records → LPS LPs are long-playing records
  • on the counter — Counter means against, so everything assembled so far runs against the grid's direction — right to left in an across answer.

Records for a party, left out on the counter, causing quite a sensation.

'Make a big splash' is idiom for causing a sensation and is meant literally here, and 'on the counter' is a position in the surface and a direction in the wordplay.

28 across ARMREST

Starmer struggling for support? (7)

Definition: support · anagram

An armrest is the padded bar on a chair that takes the weight of your arm, so it supports you in the plainest physical sense.

  • Starmer → STARMER seven letters of fodder, the surname taken whole
  • struggling → the shuffling instruction
  • struggling — Somebody struggling is thrashing about, and letters thrashing about will not stay in the order they were handed over in.

The Prime Minister having a hard time of it and looking for backing.

Keir Starmer is the British prime minister, and the question mark admits 'support' is doing double duty — political backing in the surface, furniture in the answer.

Down

1 down FRIDAY

One died in fight just before the weekend (6)

Definition: just before the weekend · container

Friday is the last working day, sitting immediately before Saturday and Sunday, so the definition locates the day rather than naming it.

  • fight → FRAY a fray is a brawl or a fight
  • One → I one written as a Roman numeral
  • died → D d is the abbreviation used for 'died' on gravestones and in family trees
  • in — One thing described as in another is inside it, so the letters named first are dropped between the letters of the word that follows.

A single death in a brawl at the end of the working week.

'Just' here means immediately rather than merely, which is what turns the last four words into a definition instead of a remark about the fight.

2 down RIGHTS

Just claims R and R? (6)

Definition: Just claims · cryptic definition

Rights are the claims a person is entitled to press, and 'just' is doing the work of legitimate — the ones law or morality allows.

  • Just claims → entitlements: the things you are owed and may demand
  • R and R? → R is the standard abbreviation for right, and the clue hands you two of them; the surface wants rest and recreation

Somebody insisting that all they are owed is a little rest and recreation.

R and R is the soldier's rest and recreation in the surface. Read the two letters separately and each is an abbreviation, which makes the pair of them a plural — and the question mark carries the joke.

3 down ANTIFREEZE

In Manchester BBC releases broadcast essential for motorists in winter? (10)

Definition: essential for motorists in winter · homophone

Antifreeze is the additive poured into a car's radiator to stop the coolant icing up, which is what makes it a winter essential.

  • BBC → ANTI the BBC's long-standing nickname is Auntie, said here with a northern short A
  • releases → FREEZE to release someone is to free them, and the verb is said exactly like the winter one
  • In Manchester — A Manchester speaker uses a short flat A, and that northern vowel is what changes the sound of the first word being spoken here.
  • broadcast — Broadcasting is putting something out to be heard, so the words in front of it are wanted for their sound rather than their spelling.

A BBC news bulletin out of Manchester about something drivers cannot do without in the cold.

The BBC has been Auntie since the 1930s, a nickname for its prim manner, and the northern vowel is what closes the gap between the two sounds.

4 down MUSIC

Total revolutionary in command of country perhaps? (5)

Definition: country perhaps · reversal + charade

Country is one kind of music among many, so the definition works by example — which is what 'perhaps' and the question mark are admitting.

  • Total revolutionary → MUS a total is a sum, written out from the back
  • in command of → IC i/c is the services' short form for the officer in charge
  • revolutionary — A revolution turns a thing full circle, so the word in front of this one is read from its far end back.

A rebel leader running an entire country.

'Country' wants to be a nation and is a genre, which is the one step the surface works hardest to hide.

5 down COLLAPSED

Fall in freezing fell (9)

Definition: fell · container

To collapse is to fall down suddenly and completely, and the past tense of 'fall' matches the answer's own past tense exactly.

  • freezing → COLD freezing weather is cold
  • Fall → LAPSE a lapse is a fall — a lapse from grace
  • in — Falling in something puts you inside it, so the piece named first goes between the letters of the word named next.

A tumble taken on a freezing hillside.

A fell is a hill in the north of England, which is the picture the surface paints; the definition is the other 'fell', the past tense of fall.

6 down NUDE

Note stylish man dropping dead in Emperor's new clothes (4)

Definition: in Emperor's new clothes · charade + deletion

Andersen's emperor is paraded in clothes that do not exist, so anybody wearing them is wearing nothing at all.

  • Note → N N is the abbreviation for a musical note
  • stylish man dropping dead → UDE a dude is a stylish man, and D is the abbreviation for dead
  • dropping — Dropping something is letting it fall away, so the letter named next is taken out of the word in front of it.

A well-dressed man keeling over in the middle of a fairy tale.

'Dropping dead' reads as a man collapsing and instructs you to remove a single letter, the D that stands for dead.

7 down ORATORIO

Musical story with Hornblower possibly heading off to capture gold (8)

Definition: Musical story · deletion + container

An oratorio is a large sacred work for singers and orchestra that tells a story without staging it, so the definition names the form.

  • Hornblower possibly heading off → ORATIO Horatio Hornblower is C. S. Forester's Napoleonic naval officer, and his first name loses its head
  • gold → OR or is gold in heraldry, from the French
  • heading off — Heading off is leaving, and what leaves here is the head itself — the opening letter of the name.
  • to capture — Capturing somebody is holding them inside your own walls, so the letters named next go within the ones already assembled.

A seafaring musical whose hero slips away to seize some treasure.

'Possibly' is the flag for an example rather than a synonym, and OR is heraldry's gold — two conventions that turn up in every other puzzle.

8 down MUST DASH

Gathered facial hair as I have to leave quickly? (4,4)

Definition: I have to leave quickly · homophone

Dash means to move at speed, so the phrase is the apology you make on your way out of the door in a hurry.

  • facial hair → MUST DASH the growth on a man's upper lip, said out loud
  • Gathered — To have gathered something is to have picked it up by ear — I gathered from what was said — so the words after it are wanted as sound.

Somebody notices a moustache and makes an excuse to hurry away.

The question mark flags a definition that is a whole spoken sentence rather than a synonym, and 'as' is the hinge holding the two halves together.

13 down MILLSTREAM

Instruction for cryptic solvers to form master race (10)

Definition: race · charade + anagram

A race is the fast channel of water that drives a mill wheel — the mill race — so the definition is the technical term for the thing itself.

  • Instruction for cryptic solvers → MILL to mill is to move about in a confused mass, which is what setters use such words to ask you to do to letters
  • master → STREAM the six letters of MASTER, rebuilt
  • to form — Forming something is making a new shape out of material you already have, so the letters named next are built into a different order.

Crossword solvers being told to go away and breed a master race.

The first three words define one of the setter's own instruction words, and 'race' at the end is a channel of water rather than people.

15 down IMMEDIATE

Instant time off is restricting newspapers and magazines? (9)

Definition: Instant · anagram + container

An immediate reply comes with no delay at all, which is what 'instant' means when it describes an action rather than a moment in time.

  • time off → IMTE the four letters of TIME, put into a new order
  • newspapers and magazines → MEDIA newspapers and magazines together are the media
  • off — Milk that is off has gone bad, and a word that has gone off the same way no longer holds its letters in the right order.
  • restricting — Restricting something is closing round it, so the letters just rebuilt shut around whatever the clue names next.

A break from work being spoiled by the papers.

'Time off' reads as a holiday and is an instruction with its material: 'off' is the spoiling word, used the way milk goes off.

16 down ABBACIES

Reportedly seize backing singers from Sweden for offices of religious leadership (8)

Definition: offices of religious leadership · charade + homophone

An abbacy is the office and jurisdiction of an abbot, so the plural names exactly these posts at the head of a religious house.

  • backing singers from Sweden → ABBA Sweden's Eurovision-winning quartet; 'backing' belongs to the surface's picture of session singers and does nothing to the letters
  • seize → CIES the verb said out loud, then spelt the way the rest of the assembly needs
  • Reportedly — Something reported is passed on by word of mouth, so the word straight after it is wanted for its sound and not its spelling.

A report that Swedish backing singers are being grabbed to fill church posts.

The group's name is the initials of Agnetha, Bjorn, Benny and Anni-Frid, and the second half of the answer is the spelling of a noise rather than of a word.

17 down PONYTAIL

Toy with plain awful hairstyle (8)

Definition: hairstyle · anagram

A ponytail is hair gathered at the back and left to hang the way a horse's tail does, which is one named way of wearing it.

  • Toy with plain → TOYPLAIN eight letters of fodder, from the words on either side of 'with'
  • awful → the shuffling instruction
  • awful — An awful version of a thing is a badly made one, and letters made up badly are not left in the order they arrived in.

Somebody idly playing with a drab and dreadful haircut.

'Toy with' reads as a single verb meaning to fiddle, and prising the two words apart is what frees the three letters of the toy itself.

19 down REFUSE

Decline in official employment (6)

Definition: Decline · charade

To decline an invitation is to refuse it: both are the polite verb for saying no, so one stands in for the other exactly.

  • official → REF the referee, the official in charge of a match
  • employment → USE employing a thing is putting it to use

A drop in the number of people in public-sector jobs.

'Employment' wants to mean holding down a job and means use, the plain noun — and the answer here is the verb, stressed on its second syllable, not the rubbish you put out.

20 down ASPECT

Feature of distorted space-time (6)

Definition: Feature · anagram

An aspect is one of the faces a thing turns towards you — the most rewarding aspect of the job — which is what 'feature' names.

  • space → SPACE five letters of fodder
  • time → T t is the symbol physicists write for time
  • distorted → the shuffling instruction
  • distorted — A distorted shape has been pulled out of true, and letters pulled out of true no longer sit where they started.

Something worth noticing about the way gravity warps space-time.

The hyphenated physics phrase splits into a whole word and a single symbol, which is the join the clue is banking on you not seeing.

23 down OMEGA

Conclusion is nothing great (5)

Definition: Conclusion · charade

Omega is the last letter of the Greek alphabet, so it stands for the end of a thing — the alpha and the omega.

  • nothing → O the letter is a nought by its shape
  • great → MEGA mega is Greek for large or great

Somebody dismissing an ending as nothing special.

'Nothing great' reads as faint praise and is two separate pieces, one of them the Greek word that gave English megaphone and megabyte.

24 down DYED

Coloured the ancient religious academic houses (4)

Definition: Coloured · container

Dyed is the past participle of dye, and cloth that has been dyed has been given colour, which is what the definition says of it.

  • religious academic → DD a Doctor of Divinity is an academic in religion, and DD is the degree's letters
  • the ancient → YE ye is the antique spelling of 'the', as on a Ye Olde Tea Shoppe sign
  • houses — A house holds people within it, and used as a verb the word puts whatever came before between these letters.

Elderly divinity scholars living in college rooms.

That Y is a printer's substitute for the lost letter thorn, so the old spelling was always pronounced 'the' — the mock-medieval shop sign is a much later joke.
