Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,863

Across

1 across TIPTOP

A few coins left for parking? Excellent (6)

Definition: Excellent · charade

  • A few coins left → TIP the small change left on the table for the waiter is a tip
  • for → TO 'to' does the same job as 'for' when you name a purpose: an aid to sleep, an aid for sleep
  • parking → P P is the road-sign abbreviation for parking

'Left for parking?' reads like an instruction to swap an L for a P, and there is no swap here — three chunks simply sit end to end. The question mark marks the joke about how mean a tip can be.

5 across TENT CITY

Refugee camp's perseverance with area becoming tense (4,4)

Definition: Refugee camp · substitution

  • perseverance → TENACITY tenacity is dogged perseverance
  • area → A A is the standard abbreviation for area, and it is the letter that leaves
  • tense → T T is the grammarian's abbreviation for tense, and it takes the vacant place

One letter steps out and another steps in — the whole instruction is those four words. The surface hides it behind 'tense' in its emotional sense, when the clue wants the grammar-book one.

9 across ANTIMONARCHIST

Opponent of UK institution possibly giving nation its charm? (14)

Definition: Opponent of UK institution · anagram

  • nation its charm → NATIONITSCHARM fourteen letters, exactly the material the grid needs

The UK institution is the monarchy, and the joke is that the letters spelling its opponent are drawn entirely from the nation's own charm.

10 across BATHERS

Bondi Beach swimming costume that woman wears out to lunch (7)

Definition: Bondi Beach swimming costume · container

  • out to lunch → BATS someone out to lunch is bats — away with the fairies
  • that woman → HER 'that woman' is her, the object pronoun

Bondi Beach is in Sydney, and the beach is there to tell you which country's word is wanted: Australians call swimwear this, where the British say trunks or costume.

11 across ANSELM

Saintly Archbishop of Canterbury laments perverts avoiding time (6)

Definition: Saintly Archbishop of Canterbury · deletion + anagram

  • laments → LAMENS the T has gone from the word for mourning, leaving six letters to shuffle
  • time → T T is the standard abbreviation for time, and it is the letter that drops out

'Saintly' is not describing a temperament: it tells you the archbishop wanted is one of the canonised ones, which narrows eleven centuries of them to a handful.

12 across SELL

Betray clandestine group in report (4)

Definition: Betray · homophone

  • clandestine group → SELL a clandestine group is a cell — the small secret unit that activists and spies organise into

The surface is a spy story, where a secret group and a betrayal sit together happily. The whole trick is that the group is heard rather than written down, and C and S sound alike here.

13 across BROADSWORD

Promise from Pitt, say, involving old military equipment (10)

Definition: military equipment · container + charade

  • from Pitt, say → BRADS Brad Pitt is one Pitt you might name, 'say' flagging the example, and something from him is Brad's
  • old → O O is the standard abbreviation for old
  • Promise → WORD your word is your promise — 'I give you my word'

Read as the surface asks and this is a promise from a film star, Brad's word, with a letter let into it. 'Old' looks like the front of the definition and is really that letter.

15 across DOWNSTREAM

Daughter holds last bit of toilet paper as the water runs (10)

Definition: as the water runs · charade + last letter

  • Daughter → D D is the genealogist's abbreviation for daughter
  • holds → OWNS to hold property is to own it
  • toilet → T the closing letter of that word
  • paper → REAM a ream is five hundred sheets of paper

'Holds' looks like an instruction to put something inside; it is simply the verb owns. Four pieces in a row, and the plumbing surface falls away.

19 across ROPY

Unsatisfactory seats 16 back in the audience (4)

Definition: Unsatisfactory · homophone

  • seats → ROW a theatre's seats are counted in rows
  • 16 → P P is the sixteenth letter of the alphabet, and theatres letter their rows from the front
  • seats 16 back → ROPY said in one breath, that seat description runs together into a single word

The number is not a cross-reference to another clue, which is the trap — count sixteen letters into the alphabet instead, and say the result out loud.

21 across ESTEEM

Favour endless semester for revising (6)

Definition: Favour · deletion + anagram

  • semester → EMESTE take the S off the front and the R off the back and six letters remain

The count settles which reading of 'endless' is meant: strip only the last letter and there are seven letters for a six-letter grid entry, so both ends have to go.

23 across INHABIT

People soon bored by hospital (7)

Definition: People · container

  • soon → INABIT 'in a bit' means soon, and its three words run together
  • hospital → H H is the road-sign abbreviation for hospital

'People' is the verb, not the noun — to people a place. That single shift is the whole misdirection, and the surface's queue of bored patients keeps it up.

24 across ACHILLES TENDON

Body part wrongly put in a trunk lengthways (8,6)

Definition: Body part · container + charade

  • a trunk → ACHEST a trunk is a chest, the big box you pack for a voyage
  • wrongly → ILL ill means wrongly, as in ill-judged or ill-gotten
  • lengthways → ENDON end on means lengthways, with one end pointing towards you

'Body part' and 'trunk' belong to the same picture, and that is the trap: the trunk here is the box you pack, not the torso.

25 across EYESIGHT

Ability to spy certainly limited by double-0 following Bond? (8)

Definition: Ability to spy · container

  • double-0 following Bond? → EIGHT Bond is 007, and the number following seven is eight
  • certainly → YES 'certainly!' as a reply is simply yes

The Bond surface does two jobs at once: it makes 'spy' look like espionage, and it hides an ordinary number behind a licence to kill.

26 across ASH-KEY

Seed for tree that's dying back remains important (3-3)

Definition: Seed for tree that's dying back · charade

  • remains → ASH what remains in the grate after a fire is ash
  • important → KEY a key witness is an important one

Ash dieback is the fungal disease that has been killing Britain's ash trees since 2012, so 'dying back' names the tree without ever naming it.

2 down IGNOBLE

Common large cracks worried Boeing (7)

Definition: Common · anagram + container

  • Boeing → IGNOBE the plane-maker's six letters, resettled into a new order
  • large → L L is the clothing-label abbreviation for large

Two words in this surface read as damage to an aircraft, and only one of them shuffles. The shuffle is six letters long against a seven-letter grid entry, so the L has to be posted into the gap the crack opens.

3 down TRIATHLON

Sound of 'Go', then lake with hat for cycling and running around it? (9)

Definition: Sound of 'Go', then lake with hat for cycling and running around it? · &lit + charade + homophone + cycling + container

  • Sound of 'Go' → TRI a go at something is a try, and said aloud that is the prefix meaning three
  • hat → ATH send the H of HAT round to the back and the other two letters stay put
  • lake → L L is the map-maker's abbreviation for lake
  • running → ON a machine that is running is on

Read straight, the sentence is a race: a starting shout, a lake, then a bike and a run. Read again, the same words spell that race out, which is why nothing is left over for a separate definition.

4 down PROVERB

Bishop alongside demonstrator, moral in a line (7)

Definition: moral in a line · charade

  • demonstrator → PROVER someone who demonstrates a theorem is a prover
  • Bishop → B B is the chess notation for a bishop

The clue names the bishop first and the answer puts him last: 'alongside' is deliberately silent about which side, so the crossing letters decide it for you.

5 down THAT'S MORE LIKE IT

Now we're making progress which means computers are increasingly popular (5,4,4,2)

Definition: Now we're making progress · charade

  • which means → THAT'S 'which means' is 'that is', and that's is its contraction
  • are increasingly popular → MORE LIKE if a thing is catching on, more people like it
  • computers → IT IT is information technology, the trade's own name for computing

The wordplay is the phrase taken literally, with IT as the department rather than the pronoun. The definition is the same six syllables used as the exclamation you make when something finally starts going right.

6 down NICE AND

Butcher can dine pleasantly ... (4,3)

Definition: pleasantly · anagram

  • can dine → CANDINE seven letters of fodder, and every one of them is used

The butcher's shop and the trailing dots are surface. What you get is only half an idiom — nice and warm, nice and easy — which is why the definition is a single adverb.

7 down CHIPS

Tweets showing no Republican means to gamble (5)

Definition: means to gamble · deletion

  • Tweets → CHIRPS a small bird tweets, which is to say it chirps
  • Republican → R R is the abbreviation for Republican in American politics

The surface is a politician's timeline, and the wordplay wants the birdsong sense of 'tweets' instead. Take the R out of what a bird does and the casino counters are left.

8 down TATTLER

Murphy coming round starts on the latest gossip (7)

Definition: gossip · container + first letters

  • Murphy → TATER a murphy is British and Irish slang for a potato, and a tater is the same vegetable
  • the latest → TL the opening letters of those two words, in the order the clue gives them

Murphy reads as a surname turning up at a party; he is a potato. The slang is two centuries old and still standard crossword vocabulary.

14 down WORKBENCH

New WC broken by husband making table (9)

Definition: table · anagram

  • WC broken → WCBROKEN the two letters of WC and the six of 'broken', all of them raw material
  • husband → H H is the standard abbreviation for husband

'Broken' is not the instruction here, it is the material — its own six letters go into the mix. The word doing the shuffling is 'New', and for once it comes first.

16 down OSSUARY

Vault with bones broken in your ass (7)

Definition: Vault with bones · anagram

  • your ass → YOURASS seven letters, and every one of them is used

Filbert's surface is a joke about a broken tailbone, and the letters he needs are sitting in the last two words of it.

17 down TIME LAG

Maybe hours of delay wind university up (4,3)

Definition: Maybe hours of delay · reversal + charade

  • wind → GALE a gale is a strong wind
  • university → MIT MIT is the Massachusetts Institute of Technology

'Wind' invites you to twist something, and it is the weather noun instead. Assemble the two pieces, turn the whole thing round as 'up' asks, and it reads down the grid.

18 down MAHATMA

Sage beanie, say, pocketed by lesbian parents? (7)

Definition: Sage · container

  • lesbian parents? → MAMA two mothers between them, so a MA on each side
  • beanie, say → HAT a beanie is one example of the thing, which 'say' flags

'Sage' is the noun, a wise man, not the herb the kitchen-ish surface offers. Mahatma is the honorific Indians gave Gandhi, and it means great soul.

20 down POTHOLE

Danger for cyclists even if coming through icy zone (7)

Definition: Danger for cyclists · container

  • icy zone → POLE the icy zones at the ends of the globe are the poles
  • even if → THO 'even if' is 'though', which verse and crosswords alike shorten to THO

The surface is a winter warning to cyclists, which is what the answer is too — but the icy zone is the polar one, and 'even if' is a contraction rather than a condition.

22 down ETHOS

Spirit's teeth 16D contains (5)

Definition: Spirit · hidden word

  • teeth 16D → ETHOS the closing letters of one word run straight on into the opening letters of the entry at 16 down

The '16D' is not decoration: write out the answer to that clue after 'teeth' and the five letters are lying there in order, straddling the join between the two words.
