Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,063

Across

1 across ACADEMIC

Teacher of no practical use (8)

Definition: Teacher · double definition

  • Teacher → ACADEMIC a job title for someone who teaches at a university
  • of no practical use → ACADEMIC describes a question or argument that is purely theoretical, with no bearing on anything real

One word wearing two parts of speech, run together with nothing between them. The joke is that the second reading is a quiet insult aimed at the first.

6 across BLURBS

Obscure, extremely bumptious words on book jackets (6)

Definition: words on book jackets · charade + outer letters

  • Obscure → BLUR to obscure something is to blur it
  • bumptious → BS the outer letters of B-umptiou-S

'Obscure' is a verb here, not an adjective, and that switch is most of the difficulty. The surface pretends the whole clue is about a pompous author.

9 across ASSASSINATION

Two idiots initiating one state shooting, perhaps (13)

Definition: shooting, perhaps · charade + first letter

  • Two idiots → ASSASS an ass is an idiot, and the clue asks for two of them
  • one → I the initial letter of 'one'
  • state → NATION a nation is a state

The surface reads as a news bulletin and every word of it is doing something else: 'state' is an ordinary country and the idiots are donkeys.

10 across SCRAPE

Dispose of the last bit of homemade pickle (6)

Definition: pickle · charade + last letter

  • Dispose of → SCRAP to scrap a thing is to dispose of it
  • homemade → E the last letter of 'homemade'

'Pickle' is the definition and has nothing to do with food: a scrape is a spot of bother. The surface leans hard on the homemade-chutney reading to hide that.

11 across EYESIGHT

Perception absolutely possessed by rowing crew (8)

Definition: Perception · container

  • rowing crew → EIGHT an eight is a racing crew of eight rowers
  • absolutely → YES 'absolutely' is an emphatic yes

An eight for a rowing crew is one of the most reliable pieces of crossword vocabulary, and 'absolutely' as a word of agreement is the other half worth remembering.

13 across GLITTERATI

International clothing label about to bring on board rubbish celebrities (10)

Definition: celebrities · charade + reversal + container

  • International → I I = international, the standard abbreviation
  • clothing label → TAG the label sewn into a garment is its tag
  • rubbish → LITTER litter is rubbish dropped about the place

'Clothing label' invites you to name a fashion house and means the thing sewn inside a collar. 'On board' is the setter's usual way of saying something is taken inside.

15 across CODE

System of rules requiring constant, dedicated work? (4)

Definition: System of rules · charade

  • constant → C C = constant, the standard symbol in mathematics
  • dedicated work → ODE an ode is a poem written in someone's honour

An ode is dedicated to its subject, which is what makes 'dedicated work' fair rather than merely flattering. The question mark flags that stretch.

16 across NAFF

Trashy, somewhat lumpen affair (4)

Definition: Trashy · hidden word

  • lumpen affair → NAFF the answer sits unbroken across the join between these two words

Hidden answers love a join between two words like this, because the eye reads whole words and steps straight over the seam where the letters actually are.

18 across MANAGERIAL

Wild animal rage coming from the top dogs? (10)

Definition: coming from the top dogs · anagram

  • animal rage → ANIMALRAGE anagram fodder, ten letters

'Wild animal rage' is a complete picture in three words, which is what makes it so hard to see the last two as raw material rather than scenery.

21 across CALLISTO

Satellite phone? It’s crazily old (8)

Definition: Satellite · charade + anagram

  • phone → CALL to phone someone is to call them
  • It’s → ITS three letters, the apostrophe dropping out
  • old → O O = old, the standard abbreviation

'Satellite phone' is lovely misdirection: two words that belong together in life and to opposite halves of the clue. The answer is one of Jupiter's moons.

22 across CRAYON

Drawer primarily containing fabric (6)

Definition: Drawer · charade + first letter

  • containing → C the first letter of 'containing'
  • fabric → RAYON rayon is a manufactured fabric

'Containing' looks every inch like an instruction to put one thing inside another, and it is really a word being asked for its opening letter. That double bluff is the clue.

23 across AUTHORITARIAN

Domineering writer from Rome, maybe changing sides? (13)

Definition: Domineering · charade + substitution

  • writer → AUTHOR an author is a writer
  • from Rome, maybe → ITARIAN someone from Rome might be an ITALIAN, whose L (left) becomes R (right)

L for left and R for right is a swap worth memorising, and 'changing sides' is one of several ways a setter can ask for it. Rome supplies the nationality by example.

25 across AT EASE

A kid taking it easy (2,4)

Definition: taking it easy · charade

  • A → A the clue's own A, used as itself
  • kid → TEASE to kid someone is to tease them

A bare 'A' at the start of a clue is nearly always itself, which is why it disappears so easily. 'Kid' is a verb here, not a child.

26 across HEDONIST

Fun-loving sort of mafia boss involved in robbery (8)

Definition: Fun-loving sort · container

  • robbery → HEIST a heist is a planned robbery
  • mafia boss → DON don is the title of a mafia chief, borrowed from Sicilian usage

'Sort' means a person here rather than a kind of thing, which is what keeps the definition from looking like one. The surface stays entirely inside the crime film.

2 down CHANCEL

Opening above large part of church (7)

Definition: part of church · charade

  • Opening → CHANCE an opening is a chance, an opportunity
  • large → L L = large, the clothing-size abbreviation

'Opening' looks architectural in a clue about churches and turns out to mean a stroke of luck. L for large comes off a shirt collar and is worth filing away.

3 down DISTASTEFUL

Unpleasant fluid dilutes fast (11)

Definition: Unpleasant · anagram

  • dilutes fast → DILUTESFAST anagram fodder, eleven letters

The surface reads like the small print on a bottle, which is exactly why 'fluid' slips past as a description of the liquid rather than an instruction.

4 down MASSE

Bulk energy shot (5)

Definition: shot · charade

  • Bulk → MASS bulk and mass are both sheer quantity of matter
  • energy → E E = energy, the physicist's abbreviation

'Bulk energy' reads as one phrase about power supply and is two separate pieces. The billiards word is properly spelt with an accent, which the grid simply drops.

5 down CHIMERA

Fancy that guy being put into care, possibly (7)

Definition: Fancy · container + anagram

  • care → CERA the letters of CARE rearranged
  • that guy → HIM that guy is him

'Fancy that guy' reads as a swoon and is really the definition bumping straight into the wordplay. Short ordinary words like 'care' are the ones setters most like to jumble.

6 down BEAVERING

Person providing protection for state is working very hard (9)

Definition: working very hard · container

  • Person → BEING a being is a person, a living creature
  • state → AVER to aver is to state something as fact

Reading 'person' as a being rather than a named someone is the step that unlocks this. Beavering away is the idiom the definition leans on, from the animal's proverbial industry.

7 down UNI

Learning institution’s subdivision reduced in size by 25% (3)

Definition: Learning institution · deletion

  • subdivision → UNIT a unit is a subdivision of something larger
  • reduced in size by 25% → four letters minus a quarter leaves three: the closing T drops off

The percentage is the joke - read '25%' as arithmetic performed on the word, not as a description of a department being cut.

8 down BUNCHED

Gathered together and had late-morning meal right away (7)

Definition: Gathered together · deletion

  • had late-morning meal → BRUNCHED brunch is the meal between breakfast and lunch
  • right → R R = right, the standard abbreviation

Brunch is a portmanteau of breakfast and lunch, which is why 'late-morning meal' points at it so precisely. R for right pairs with L for left all over crosswords.

12 down INCARNATION

Popular pink avatar (11)

Definition: avatar · charade

  • Popular → IN what is popular is in, as in fashion
  • pink → CARNATION a carnation is a variety of pink, the garden flower

Three words, no indicator, and the whole difficulty is that 'pink' is a flower rather than a colour. 'Avatar' is wanted in its older Hindu sense, not the film.

14 down EMMA STONE

She acts single, following couple of males in certain direction (4,5)

Definition: She acts · container + charade

  • certain direction → EAST east is one of the four points of the compass
  • couple of males → MM M = male, and a couple of them is MM
  • single → ONE a single of anything is one

The clue never says 'actress' - two words do the same job while reading as gossip about someone's love life. 'Certain direction' stays vague until the letters force the compass point.

17 down ADAMANT

Determined 80s pop star (7)

Definition: Determined · double definition

  • Determined → ADAMANT describes someone who will not be moved from a position, whatever the pressure
  • 80s pop star → ADAM ANT an early-80s New Romantic frontman, best known for 'Stand and Deliver' — his stage name reads as two words

Respacing an ordinary word into a name is one of the oldest tricks in the book, and a two-word pop star is the perfect place to hide it.

19 down NEOLITH

Frost ultimately breaks hole in crumbling relic (7)

Definition: relic · anagram + last letter + container

  • hole in → NEOLIH the six letters of HOLE IN, rearranged
  • Frost ultimately → T the last letter of 'Frost'

Frost is capitalised so it reads as a surname, and it is only weather. The picture the surface paints - ice splitting a ruin - is the picture archaeology would give you too.

20 down AMOUNTS

Supplies of first-class horses (7)

Definition: Supplies · charade

  • first-class → A A1 is top grade, so first-class gives A
  • horses → MOUNTS a mount is a horse you ride

A mount is the rider's word for a horse rather than the animal itself, and A for first-class comes from A1, the shipping register's top rating.

22 down CHARD

Head of cabbage with tough green leaves (5)

Definition: green leaves · charade + first letter

  • cabbage → C the first letter of 'cabbage'
  • tough → HARD a tough customer is a hard one

A head of cabbage is a real thing you can buy, which is what lets the instruction pass as scenery. The surface never leaves the vegetable patch.

24 down TEA

Drink in the bar periodically (3)

Definition: Drink · alternate letters

  • the bar → TEA every second letter of T-h-E-b-A-r

'Bar' and 'drink' hold the surface together while the letters come from somewhere else entirely. Taking every other letter is one of the commonest extractions there is.
