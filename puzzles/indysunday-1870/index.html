Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,870

Across

1 across WINEGLASS

Temporary vintage carrier bag for one girl (9)

Definition: Temporary vintage carrier · charade

  • bag → WIN to bag a prize is to win it, the verb rather than the shopping bag of the surface
  • for one → EG 'for one' is how a clue says e.g., the abbreviation for 'for example'
  • girl → LASS a lass is a girl, chiefly in northern English

Three everyday words, none of them meaning what the sentence about shopping wants them to mean, and no instruction between them beyond laying them end to end.

6 across WIELD

Handle fuse with electric current through it (5)

Definition: Handle · container

  • fuse → WELD to fuse two pieces of metal is to weld them, and this word splits after its first letter
  • electric current → I I is the physicist's symbol for electric current, from the French intensité

'Handle' is the definition and it is a verb — to handle a sword — not the part you grip, which is what the fusebox picture wants you to think.

9 across RONDO

Movement Republican party guards against (5)

Definition: Movement · container + charade

  • Republican → R R is the standard abbreviation for a Republican in American politics
  • against → ON to lean on something is to lean against it, so the two share that sense
  • party → DO a do is a party — a works do, a birthday do

'Republican party' reads as one phrase and the clue needs it as two words doing separate jobs, which is the commonest way a surface hides a split.

10 across HAMSTRING

Lame man's right to jog (9)

Definition: Lame · anagram

  • man's right → MANSRIGHT nine letters of fodder, exactly the count the enumeration asks for

'Lame' is doing duty as a verb, and 'right' is not the crossword's usual single letter R here — every one of its letters is wanted.

11 across SCARFED

Mark US agent covered around his neck (7)

Definition: covered around his neck · charade

  • Mark → SCAR a scar is the mark an old wound leaves behind
  • US agent → FED a fed is an FBI or federal agent in American slang

The definition is the whole phrase at the end, and it means wearing a scarf, not the informal sense of eating something at speed.

12 across HOODIES

These house torsos with head hidden (7)

Definition: These · charade + deletion

  • house → HO HO is the standard abbreviation for house, the one estate agents use
  • torsos → ODIES torsos are BODIES, and this set arrives with its opening B left off

Read straight through, the sentence is also a fair description of the garment — it houses the torso and keeps the head out of sight — which is what makes 'These' enough of a definition.

13 across FORGET-ME-NOTS

Trademark on counterfeit jewel, back-to-front little flowers (6-2-4)

Definition: little flowers · charade + reversal

  • counterfeit → FORGE to forge is to counterfeit, and the verb hands over five letters whole
  • Trademark → TM TM is the symbol printed beside a trademarked name
  • jewel → ENOTS a jewel is a STONE, turned round here so that it reads from its last letter

The definition is the tail of the sentence even though it reads as part of it, and the comma after 'jewel' is punctuation the setter may put wherever the surface needs it.

17 across OBSTACLE RACE

Old couple going round ruined castle hampered event (8,4)

Definition: hampered event · container + anagram + charade

  • Old → O O is the standard abbreviation for old
  • couple → BRACE a brace is a couple — two of a thing, as in a brace of pheasants
  • castle → STACLE the castle's six letters, rebuilt in another order and dropped inside

'Hampered' is not saying the event was spoiled: it is the definition's own word for a course deliberately filled with things to climb through.

20 across ANODYNE

Dull Mondays regularly boring princess (7)

Definition: Dull · container + alternate letters

  • princess → ANNE Princess Anne, the Princess Royal and the King's sister, is the crossword's usual princess
  • Mondays regularly → ODY the second, fourth and sixth letters of Mondays, counted off in step

'Boring' reads as a second word for dull and is really the instruction, so the pun is the point — only one of its two senses hands over anything.

21 across DUE DATE

End of term decided on by start of last period (3,4)

Definition: End of term decided on by start of last period · cryptic definition

  • End of term → the surface is a school calendar breaking up for the holidays; the other reading is the end of a pregnancy, which is also called a term
  • decided on by start of last period → a timetable's last period is a lesson, but a midwife counts forty weeks from the first day of the previous monthly cycle

There is no wordplay to take apart here: it is one sentence read twice, once in a school and once in a maternity clinic, and every word changes job between them.

22 across PHOTOSHOP

Doctor visually very attractive, swanky surgery admits (9)

Definition: Doctor · container + charade

  • swanky → POSH posh is swanky — showily upmarket
  • visually very attractive → HOT calling someone hot is calling them very good-looking
  • surgery → OP an op is an operation, the surgery a patient goes in for

'Doctor' is the verb — to tamper with an image — rather than the person in the surgery, and the whole surface exists to keep you reading it the other way.

23 across ODOUR

Well-to-do urbanite keeps something to sniff (5)

Definition: something to sniff · hidden word

  • Well-to-do urbanite → ODOUR five letters in a row, running out of the hyphenated phrase and on into the word after it

An urbanite is only a city dweller, a stiff word chosen for the letters it begins with — that awkwardness is the tell worth learning.

24 across TENET

Principle superficially true and clear (5)

Definition: Principle · charade + outer letters

  • superficially true → TE the two outer letters of TRUE, one at each end, with its middle left behind
  • clear → NET to clear a sum is to net it — what is left once the deductions are done

'True and clear' reads as one description of a principle, which is the misdirection: the two words are doing quite separate mechanical jobs.

25 across NEODYMIUM

Metal zip's inside covered by plastic dummy one (9)

Definition: Metal · middle letter + container + anagram

  • zip's inside → I the middle letter of ZIP, and nothing else of the word is wanted
  • dummy one → DUMMYONE eight letters of fodder, which with the letter above make the nine the enumeration asks for

'Zip' is the crossword's usual word for nothing, which makes it a fine decoy: the clue wants a letter out of the middle of it, not its meaning.

Down

1 down WARDS OFF

Magnetism reversed like that very strongly repels (5,3)

Definition: repels · reversal + charade

  • Magnetism reversed → WARD magnetism is a DRAW, the pull one thing exerts on another, and it is turned round here
  • like that → SO 'so' means 'like that' in a sentence: he did it so, meaning in that manner
  • very strongly → FF FF is fortissimo, the score's instruction to play very loudly

Magnetism attracts, which is the trap the first word sets; the clue only wants that attraction so it can turn it round into its opposite.

2 down NON-DAIRY

Agree offhand about Norway suiting vegans (3-5)

Definition: suiting vegans · container + charade

  • Agree → NOD to nod is to agree without saying anything, and this word opens up to take a letter
  • Norway → N N is Norway's international vehicle code, the letter carried on cars registered there
  • offhand → AIRY an airy remark is offhand — light, casual and unbothered

Read as English, 'Agree offhand' is one idea; the clue needs it as two separate words doing separate jobs, which is the seam the surface hides.

3 down GEOFFREY BOYCOTT

English cricket side wearing grey refuse to use old bat (8,7)

Definition: old bat · container + charade

  • grey → GREY the plain word, opened up after its first letter to take what the clue puts inside
  • English → E E is the standard abbreviation for English
  • cricket side → OFF a cricket field divides into the off side and the leg side, and the off is the half the batter faces
  • refuse to use → BOYCOTT to boycott something is to refuse to use it, after an Irish land agent his neighbours cut off

'Old bat' is normally an insult aimed at an elderly woman, and here it means a batsman long retired, which is the whole joke of the clue.

4 down APHID

Pest turned up every year then disappeared (5)

Definition: Pest · reversal + charade

  • every year → AP per annum is Latin for every year and shortens to PA, which climbs the entry here
  • disappeared → HID something that hid has disappeared from view, and the past tense matches

'Turned up' reads as arriving, which is what makes the sentence about an insect work; in the wordplay it is an instruction about direction.

5 down SYMPHONIC

Bit of music in Psycho arranged for orchestra (9)

Definition: for orchestra · anagram + first letter

  • Bit of music → M the opening letter of music, and the only part of the word wanted
  • in Psycho → INPSYCHO eight more letters of fodder, taken from the film's title along with the word in front of it

Psycho is italicised because it is Hitchcock's title; those shrieking violins are a fine decoy, since all the clue wants from the film is its letters.

6 down WITHOUT CEREMONY

Informally, jam makers turning more to chutney (7,8)

Definition: Informally · charade + anagram

  • jam makers → WI the Women's Institute, the British village organisation famous for making and selling jam
  • more to chutney → THOUTCEREMONY thirteen letters of fodder, which with the two above make the fifteen the enumeration wants

Once the preserves stall stops being the subject, the definition turns out to be the single word at the front, easy to read as part of the sentence.

7 down ELIXIR

Obelix irreversibly imbued with magic potion (6)

Definition: magic potion · hidden word

  • Obelix irreversibly → ELIXIR six letters in a row, running out of the end of the name and on into the word beside it

Obelix fell into the cauldron as a baby in the Asterix books, so the surface is a joke about him and the letters need no more than a careful reading.

8 down DIGEST

Pass on joke you hear in summarised version (6)

Definition: summarised version · homophone

  • Pass on → DIE to pass on is to die, the gentle way of saying it
  • joke → JEST a jest is a joke, the older word for one
  • Pass on joke → DIGEST said at any speed the two short words run together into one, and the spelling shifts as they join

Nothing is shuffled or buried here: the clue asks you to say two words one after another and write down the single word you hear.

14 down MOTHER HEN

Fussy manager runs through ground then home (6,3)

Definition: Fussy manager · anagram

  • runs → R R is the scorer's abbreviation for runs on a cricket card
  • then home → THENHOME eight more letters, which with the one above make the nine the enumeration wants

The surface is a player crossing a pitch and both halves of it are wordplay: a cricketer's single letter, and seven words' worth of nothing but letters.

15 down MACARONI

Graduate arranged air-con for penguin (8)

Definition: penguin · charade + anagram

  • Graduate → MA an MA is a Master of Arts, the crossword's usual graduate
  • air-con → CARONI the six letters of the shortened air conditioning, rewritten in another order

The bird is named after the 18th-century macaronis, dandies with absurdly elaborate hair — the ones Yankee Doodle mocks — which its yellow crest resembles.

16 down TELEGRAM

Message shortening tenancy brought over (8)

Definition: Message · charade + reversal

  • shortening → MARGE marge is British shorthand for margarine, and margarine is a shortening — the fat rubbed into pastry
  • tenancy → LET a let is a tenancy, the flat or room you rent for a term

'Shortening' looks like an instruction to trim a word and is really a noun for baking fat, which is the whole trap the clue is built on.

18 down CARPET

Roast beef out of this world (6)

Definition: Roast · charade

  • beef → CARP to beef about something is to carp about it — both are verbs for complaining
  • out of this world → ET ET is Spielberg's extra-terrestrial, and an extra-terrestrial comes from beyond this world

'Roast beef' is a Sunday dinner in the surface and two separate jobs in the clue, and reading the pair as one dish is the trap.

19 down COCOON

Shelter for longer beneath palm tree (6)

Definition: Shelter · charade

  • palm tree → COCO the coco is the coconut palm, the tree itself rather than its fruit
  • for longer → ON to keep going for longer is to go on, so 'on' carries the sense of continuing

'Shelter' is a noun here rather than the verb: the case a caterpillar spins round itself, which is why the clue's picture of beach shade is a decoy.

21 down DIPSO

Drinker really after drop (5)

Definition: Drinker · charade

  • drop → DIP a drop in the road, or in prices, is a dip
  • really → SO 'so' does the same work as 'really' as an intensifier: it was so cold, it was really cold

'Drinker' is doing more than naming someone with a glass in hand; the answer is a clipped, faintly clinical word for one who cannot stop.
