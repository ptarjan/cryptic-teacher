Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,060

Across

1 across BLOATED

Shifting a lot into base that’s feeling full (7)

Definition: feeling full · anagram + container

  • base → BED a bed is the base a thing rests on — a road bed, a river bed — and it splits open to take the insertion
  • a lot → LOAT the four letters of A LOT, shifted into a new order

The surface sounds like chemistry — something tipped into a base. Crosswords lean on the other sense: a bed is what a road or a river is laid on.

5 across TEARS UP

Causes damage to and gets emotional (5,2)

Definition: Causes damage to · double definition

  • Causes damage to → TEARS UP to tear something up is to rip it to pieces
  • gets emotional → TEARS UP getting overcome with emotion is described with the same two words as ripping something apart

The two halves are spelt identically and said differently: the ripping 'tear' rhymes with air, the weeping one with ear. Only the sense tells you which you are holding.

9 across CHASM

Partially staunch a smoking fissure (5)

Definition: fissure · hidden word

  • staunch a smoking → CHASM staunCH A SMoking — the answer runs straight across the two word joins

A smoking fissure is a volcanic vent, so the definition is doing geology while the surface does first aid. Hidden answers are the one family whose letters are already in front of you.

10 across BARRICADE

Block local commercial involvement in staple food (9)

Definition: Block · charade + container

  • local → BAR the local is the pub, and the bar is what you drink at
  • staple food → RICE rice is the staple food of half the world; it opens up for the insertion
  • commercial → AD an ad is a commercial

Nothing here sounds like an instruction — a local firm's commercial involvement in food is an ordinary news sentence. 'Local' for BAR is the British pub sense.

11 across UNINSPIRED

Like church’s addition to wedding without ring? It’s so dull (10)

Definition: so dull · charade + deletion

  • wedding → UNION a wedding is a union of two people
  • ring → O a ring is a circle, written O — this is the letter that goes
  • Like church’s addition → SPIRED the addition a church carries on top is a spire, and a building with one is spired

The whole surface is a wedding — a church, an addition to it, a ring — and every piece of it is doing something else. O for 'ring' is currency you will meet weekly.

12 across ERE

Atmosphere that’s spoken of before in poetry (3)

Definition: before in poetry · homophone

  • Atmosphere → ERE the atmosphere is the air, and AIR said aloud is spelt this way in verse

Three letters, and the whole clue turns on hearing. Poets keep this word alive in 'ere long' and 'ere now', which is why the definition has to say where it lives.

14 across GRAPHOLOGIST

You must write to get her to analyse you (12)

Definition: You must write to get her to analyse you · cryptic definition

  • You must write → the surface reads as an order to send a letter; what is really wanted is a sample of your handwriting
  • to get her to analyse you → the analysis is not of your mind but of the way your letters lean, loop and press on the page

There is no separable wordplay here — the sentence simply describes the job. The joke is that this is one analyst whose couch you never have to lie on.

18 across ONE FOUR SEVEN

Maximum break adds up to 12 (3,4,5)

Definition: Maximum break adds up to 12 · cryptic definition

  • Maximum break → snooker's highest possible break: fifteen reds, each followed by a black, and then all the colours
  • adds up to 12 → the three digits total twelve — and so, as a second wink, do the three word lengths in the enumeration

Nothing here comes apart into letters: you need the snooker number, and the arithmetic is the setter's way of confirming you have the right one.

21 across GUT

Wind has no singular source from which it comes (3)

Definition: source from which it comes · deletion

  • Wind → GUST a gust is a wind
  • singular → S s = singular in the grammar books, and it is the letter that goes

Two kinds of wind in one clue: the gust the wordplay wants, and the sort the definition is being coy about. Only the second has a source worth naming.

22 across DOUBLE DATE

Repeat take out – it needs to feed two couples (6,4)

Definition: it needs to feed two couples · charade

  • Repeat → DOUBLE to double something is to repeat it
  • take out → DATE to take somebody out is to date them

The dash does the work of a full stop: everything before it builds the answer, everything after describes it. 'Take out' looks like a takeaway and is really the romantic verb.

25 across AMERICANO

On camera, I spilt coffee (9)

Definition: coffee · anagram

  • On camera, I → AMERICANO the nine letters of ON CAMERA I rearranged

An anagram that swallows the clue's first three words, comma and all, is fodder hiding in plain sight — the clumsy moment on film reads as pure scene-setting.

26 across RANCH

Farm starts to rear another new cattle herd (5)

Definition: Farm · first letters

  • rear another new cattle herd → RANCH the opening letters of Rear Another New Cattle Herd

Five words in a row give up their first letter and the sentence still reads as a plausible instruction about livestock — that smoothness is the craft in an acrostic clue.

27 across TOP HOLE

Spot on jumper? That’s just great (3,4)

Definition: just great · charade

  • jumper → TOP a jumper is a top, in the garment sense
  • Spot → HOLE a spot and a hole are both awkward situations — in a tight spot, in a hole

The question mark admits the picture is a stretch. Both halves are wearing disguises, and it is 'on' that quietly says which way round they go.

28 across FASTEST

Most secure force when cold war sides abandoned leaders (7)

Definition: Most secure · charade + deletion

  • force → F F is the physicist's symbol for force, as in F = ma
  • cold war sides abandoned leaders → ASTEST the two sides in the cold war were East and West; each drops its leading letter, leaving AST and EST

The whole surface is a history lesson, and nothing in it looks like an instruction until you notice that the cold war had exactly two sides to name.

Down

1 down BUCK UP

Become encouraged as dollar gaining value (4,2)

Definition: Become encouraged · charade

  • dollar → BUCK a buck is a dollar
  • gaining value → UP a currency gaining value is one that is going up

The second half is the harder half: 'gaining value' describes UP instead of naming it, which is how setters clue a bare preposition without making it obvious.

2 down ON A BIT

Performing one piece that’s old? (2,1,3)

Definition: old? · charade

  • Performing → ON a show being performed is on
  • one piece → A BIT a bit is one piece of something

'Performing one piece' reads as a recital, and the (2,1,3) enumeration is the tell: a one-letter middle word almost always means an everyday phrase rather than a word.

3 down TIME’S ARROW

A worm tries wriggling but it always goes forward (5,5)

Definition: it always goes forward · anagram

  • A worm tries → TIME’S ARROW the ten letters of A WORM TRIES rearranged

The picture the clue paints — a worm inching along, never backing up — is the very property the answer is named for, which is a rare case of fodder meaning what it makes.

4 down DUBAI

Name top class capital (5)

Definition: capital · charade

  • Name → DUB to dub is to name — 'I dub thee'
  • top class → AI A1 means first-rate, and the figure 1 is written as a capital I so it can join a word

A1 for 'top class' comes off Lloyd's shipping register, where A1 was a ship in perfect condition. It is one of the most reused scraps in cryptics — worth banking.

5 down THREESOME

See mother cavorting in erotic adventure (9)

Definition: erotic adventure · anagram

  • See mother → THREESOME the nine letters of SEE MOTHER rearranged

The surface is a dutiful family visit and the definition is anything but — that gap is the joke, and it is what stops you looking at 'See mother' as raw material.

6 down AKIN

Like coming around without clothing (4)

Definition: Like · deletion

  • coming around → WAKING to come around is to regain consciousness, which is waking
  • without clothing → AKIN strip WAKING of the W and the G at its ends and that is what is left standing

Every word of the surface is about undressing, so nothing in it sounds like an instruction. 'Coming around' is the hospital sense, not a visit to the neighbours.

7 down SNAKE OIL

Dodgy treatment leads to almost bare ground outside (5,3)

Definition: Dodgy treatment · container + deletion

  • ground → SOIL soil is ground, and it opens up to sit outside the rest
  • almost bare → NAKE bare is NAKED, stopped one letter short of its D

The surface is a lawn ruined by the wrong feed; the answer is the quack medicine sold off the back of a wagon, which is why 'treatment' can mean both.

8 down PRESENTS

Bushes, for example, I’d left as gifts (8)

Definition: gifts · deletion

  • Bushes, for example → PRESIDENTS George Bush father and son were both presidents, and 'for example' says they are only an instance of what is wanted
  • I’d → ID the contraction I'd, written out as two letters — this is what departs

The definition and the surface both stay in the garden while the wordplay is in the White House. 'For example' is the standard warning that a name stands in for its class.

13 down LOVE HEARTS

Fancy suit will send sweet messages of endearment (4,6)

Definition: sweet messages of endearment · charade

  • Fancy → LOVE to fancy someone is to love them
  • suit → HEARTS hearts is one of the four suits in a pack of cards

The surface dresses a card player in a fancy suit. The answer is the chalky sweets stamped with BE MINE and KISS ME, so the definition is being literal about both halves.

15 down PERFORATE

Bore winding up for repeat (9)

Definition: Bore · anagram

  • for repeat → PERFORATE the nine letters of FOR REPEAT rearranged

'Winding up' reads as needling somebody, which is what stops it looking like an instruction, and 'Bore' looks like a tedious person until you take it as a verb.

16 down POIGNANT

Moving location where gunman occasionally hides (8)

Definition: Moving · container + alternate letters

  • location → POINT a point is a location on a map, and it opens up to take the insertion
  • gunman → GNA gUnMaN read every other letter, starting at the front, gives GNA

'Location where gunman occasionally hides' reads as one phrase from a police briefing, which is what keeps you from splitting it into an instruction and its material.

17 down SETTLE UP

Pay to have seat lifted (6,2)

Definition: Pay · charade

  • seat → SETTLE a settle is a long wooden bench with a high back
  • lifted → UP anything lifted has been raised up

A settle is the high-backed bench you still find in old pubs — one of the few pieces of furniture that crosswords expect you to know by name.

19 down SARNIE

Snack, core part of unsafe carnal diet (6)

Definition: Snack · middle letters

  • unsafe → SA unSAfe: the middle pair, once two letters go from each end
  • carnal → RN caRNal is treated the same way, its middle pair being RN
  • diet → IE dIEt is shorter but built alike — one letter off each end leaves the middle pair

The surface warns you off a risky diet; the answer is the most ordinary lunch there is. Reading 'core' as an instruction rather than description is the whole step.

20 down RED HOT

Extremely rare dish, opulent and very popular (3,3)

Definition: very popular · outer letters

  • rare → RE the outer letters of RARE, one off each end
  • dish → DH the outer letters of DISH
  • opulent → OT the outer letters of OPULENT

Three words in a row all treated the same way, which is easy to miss because the surface — an extremely rare and opulent dish — reads as a single idea.

23 down BE OFF

Live abroad? Get lost (2,3)

Definition: Get lost · charade

  • Live → BE to live is to be, to exist
  • abroad → OFF somebody abroad is away, off somewhere else

Both ends of the clue are things you could shout: the surface asks a mild question about living overseas, and the definition tells somebody to clear off.

24 down LIFO

Initially it’s used to determine the order of firing (4)

Definition: used to determine the order of firing · first letters

  • Initially → LIFO the rule is 'last in, first out'; take the opening letter of each of its four words in order

'Firing' reads as gunnery and means redundancy: when jobs are cut, the newest recruits go first. You have to know the four-word rule before the initials mean anything.
