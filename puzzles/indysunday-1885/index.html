Across

1 across STRAW MAN

Nobody climbing from 12 to 7? (5,3)

Definition: Nobody · charade + reversal

Nobody → STRAW MAN: a straw man is an invented opponent, nobody real, set up so that knocking him over looks like winning the argument.

  • from 12 → STRAW 12 down opens with WARTS, and going up the column you meet those five letters the other way round
  • to 7 → MAN 7 down is NAM, read upwards from the foot of the same column
  • climbing — The two entries it names sit one above the other in a single column of the grid, so climbing them means reading their letters upwards.

12 down and 7 down share a column, and the answer is written up it: the top of one entry, then the whole of the other, read from the bottom of the grid towards the top.

6 across PUNISH

Correct wordplay is hard (6)

Definition: Correct · charade

Correct → PUNISH: the old schoolroom sense of correcting a child is disciplining them, so the verb means chastise rather than put right.

  • wordplay → PUN a pun is the commonest kind of wordplay
  • is → IS the clue's own linking verb, taken at face value for its two letters
  • hard → H H is hard, the grade stamped on a pencil

The surface is a solver grumbling that cryptics are difficult. A charade needs no instruction word, so there is nothing here that announces itself and the small pieces simply queue up.

9 across DAMP

Discourage power-crazy revolutionary (4)

Definition: Discourage · charade + reversal

Discourage → DAMP: to damp something is to deaden it, and damping someone's enthusiasm or spirits is exactly discouraging them.

  • power → P p = power, in physics and on a switch
  • crazy → MAD crazy is mad, three letters queueing behind the P
  • revolutionary — A revolution takes a thing full circle, so the assembled letters are read round the other way.

A power-crazy revolutionary is one person on the surface, which hides the join. The two pieces are assembled first and only then turned round.

10 across SINGLE MALT

Spooner's blend, basic flavour whiskey (6,4)

Definition: whiskey · spoonerism

whiskey → SINGLE MALT: a single malt is whisky made from the malted barley of one distillery, so the definition names the family it belongs to.

  • blend → SINGLE to blend is to mingle; it hands its opening sound to the second word and takes that word's in exchange
  • basic flavour → MALT salt is one of the basic flavours the tongue detects, and it receives the sound the first word gave up
  • Spooner's — The Reverend Spooner's name is the standing signal that two words trade their opening sounds.

The joke is that both halves of the swap are still drink words — a blend and a flavour — so the surface stays in the bar while the sounds change places.

11 across FLAMETHROWER

Weapon's elm haft troubled man wielding blade (12)

Definition: Weapon · anagram + charade

Weapon → FLAMETHROWER: a flamethrower is a weapon in the plainest sense, a device built and carried to attack with, so this is a straight category word.

  • elm haft → FLAMETH seven letters of fodder, shaken into a new order
  • man wielding blade → ROWER the flat end of an oar is its blade, so the man wielding one is at the oars
  • troubled — A troubled thing has been disturbed out of its settled state, so the seven letters beside it will not stay where they were put.

'Man wielding blade' wants to be a swordsman, and that is the trap — the blade belongs to an oar. The surface is assembling an axe from an elm handle while the answer assembles something else.

14 across REPAIR

Touching melody on piano, more than a tune? (6)

Definition: more than a tune? · charade

more than a tune? → REPAIR: a tune is the quick adjustment a mechanic makes, so mending what has actually broken goes well beyond one.

  • Touching → RE touching in the old prepositional sense of concerning, which is what re means at the head of a letter
  • piano → P p is piano in a score, the mark telling you to play softly
  • melody → AIR an air is a melody, the word Handel and the folk singers use

The tune here is the one a garage gives an engine, not a melody. In an across clue one thing 'on' another sits after it, so the melody follows the piano.

15 across PATERNAL

Pre-natal changes concerning dad? (8)

Definition: concerning dad? · anagram

concerning dad? → PATERNAL: paternal means of or from a father, and 'dad' is the everyday word for one, which is what the question mark excuses.

  • Pre-natal → PRENATAL eight letters of fodder, hyphen and all
  • changes — On the surface these are the alterations a pregnancy brings; as an instruction the word says the letters beside it change places.

'Pre-natal changes' is such a natural phrase that the instruction hides inside it, and the answer's moment is one step further on than the surface's — after the birth rather than before it.

17 across FAMISHED

Ravenous Shakers ate sandwiches (8)

Definition: Ravenous · container

Ravenous → FAMISHED: to be famished is to be weak with hunger, the same far end of the scale that ravenous describes.

  • ate → FED someone who has eaten has fed; these three letters open after the F
  • Shakers → AMISH the Amish are the plain-living American sect the setter is pointing at, though the Shakers were a different group
  • sandwiches — A sandwich holds its filling between two slices, so the word doing the sandwiching splits and closes round the other piece.

Sandwiches, eating and hunger make a single picture, and that is what hides the instruction inside it: the food word is the container, not the meal.

19 across EASTER

Leaving home, trainees worked extra long weekend (6)

Definition: extra long weekend · deletion + anagram

extra long weekend → EASTER: with Good Friday and Easter Monday as British bank holidays, the festival delivers four days off instead of the usual two.

  • home → IN someone at home is in, and that pair is what departs
  • trainees → TRAEES the eight letters of trainees less the pair above, ready to be pushed around
  • Leaving — Leaving says a piece goes: the two letters named beside it come out before anything else happens.
  • worked — Dough that is worked is pushed into a new shape, and letters worked over do not keep the order they arrived in.

Trainees leaving home to work reads as a job advert, so nothing announces itself. In Britain Good Friday and Easter Monday are both public holidays, which is what makes the weekend an extra long one.

20 across CONVALESCENT

Cold following track across glen, now recovering (12)

Definition: now recovering · charade + container

now recovering → CONVALESCENT: a convalescent patient is one getting their strength back after illness, so the word names the stage rather than the cure.

  • Cold → C c = cold, the letter on a tap
  • following → ON on carries the sense of coming after — from that day on — and hands over two letters
  • glen → VALE a glen is a narrow valley, a vale
  • track → SCENT the track a hound follows is a scent
  • across — Something laid across another thing spans it and lies within its width, so the valley goes into the middle of what the other pieces build.

The surface is a walk over cold hills, and the definition is the last two words — 'now' belongs to it, not to the wordplay, which is where most solvers put the break too early.

23 across CORDILLERA

Thin rope in bad condition, time for chain (10)

Definition: chain · charade

chain → CORDILLERA: a cordillera is a system of parallel mountain ranges, which is exactly what English means by a mountain chain.

  • Thin rope → CORD a cord is a thin rope, the sort on a dressing gown
  • in bad condition → ILL something in bad condition is ill
  • time → ERA an era is a stretch of time

Three plain synonyms laid end to end; the difficulty is all in the answer, a Spanish word English borrowed for the mountain systems of the Americas.

25 across AXEL

Winter Olympics aerial manoeuvre beginning for Lindsey on chopper (4)

Definition: Winter Olympics aerial manoeuvre · charade + first letter

Winter Olympics aerial manoeuvre → AXEL: the Axel is the figure-skating jump named after Axel Paulsen, the one entered from a forward edge.

  • chopper → AXE a chopper is an axe, the tool rather than the helicopter the surface offers
  • beginning for Lindsey → L the letter that name starts with
  • beginning — It asks for the front of the name that follows and nothing else — one letter, taken off the start.

In an across clue one piece 'on' another sits after it, so the single letter goes at the end. 'Chopper' pulls hard towards a helicopter, which is just what an aerial surface wants.

26 across CYGNET

New pen, say, Charlie gently agitated with line not appearing (6)

Definition: New pen, say · charade + deletion + anagram

New pen, say → CYGNET: a pen is a female swan and a cygnet is a swan's young, so 'say' marks the pen as one example of the parent bird.

  • Charlie → C Charlie is C in the phonetic alphabet used over the radio
  • line → L l = line, and this is the letter held back
  • gently → GENTY gently once the L above has gone, leaving five letters to be shaken up
  • agitated — Agitate a liquid and everything in it is thrown out of place, which is what becomes of the letters standing beside the word.
  • not appearing — What does not appear is missing from the finished word, so the letter named beside it never reaches the grid.

'New pen' is a stationery cupboard on the surface. A pen is a female swan, so the definition is about a bird, and 'say' is the warning that she is only an example.

27 across TOTALITY

Whole form replaced in ceremony by child (8)

Definition: Whole · substitution

Whole → TOTALITY: the totality of something is the whole of it taken together, the noun for completeness rather than the adjective.

  • ceremony → FORMALITY a ceremony gone through for the sake of appearances is a formality, nine letters
  • form → FORM the four letters at the front of the word above, and the ones being shown the door
  • child → TOT a tot is a small child, and it moves in where those four letters were
  • replaced in — One piece stands down and another takes its place inside the longer word, so nothing is added at either end.

'Whole form' reads as a school year group, and that is the trap: the definition is one word long and 'form' belongs to the wordplay.

Down

2 down THAW

Improvement in relationship seen during month away (4)

Definition: Improvement in relationship · hidden word

Improvement in relationship → THAW: a thaw is ice melting, and the same word covers hostility easing between two people or two countries.

  • month away → THAW four letters run straight on across the gap between these two words, already in the order you need
  • seen during — What is seen during something is inside it rather than beside it, so the letters run unbroken through the words that follow.

Nothing looks like an instruction: 'month away' reads as a holiday. Warmth is also what the definition is about, so the surface and the answer share one idea and the span is easy to walk past.

3 down APPELLATION

Name from range in eastern US, you hear (11)

Definition: Name · homophone

Name → APPELLATION: an appellation is the name or title something goes by, the formal word for what a person, a place or a wine is called.

  • range in eastern US → APPELLATION the Appalachians are the mountain chain running down the eastern United States; keep the sound of that name and change every letter that needs changing
  • you hear — It asks for the sound of the mountain range rather than its spelling, so what goes in the grid is a different word entirely.

Say the range's name and the entry one after the other and you hear how near they run. A setter is allowed a homophone this approximate when the pairing is the whole joke.

4 down MESOMORPH

Muscly type miles more posh after makeover (9)

Definition: Muscly type · charade + anagram

Muscly type → MESOMORPH: in the three-way classification of body builds the mesomorph is the solid, muscular one, so the definition names a category.

  • miles → M m is the short form of miles, as printed on a road sign
  • more posh → ESOMORPH these eight letters, given a new arrangement, follow the M
  • makeover — A makeover throws nothing away and rearranges everything, which is what the two words in front of it undergo.

'Muscly type' sounds like slang for a person and is in fact the technical term for one of the three body builds, which is what makes it a definition rather than a joke.

5 down NON-STOP

Express back to Waterloo departing midday's best (3-4)

Definition: Express · charade + deletion + last letter

Express → NON-STOP: an express is the service that runs through without calling anywhere, so the adjective and the train describe the same thing.

  • midday's → NOONS midday is noon, and the possessive brings an S with it
  • back to Waterloo → O the letter at the back of Waterloo, and the one that leaves
  • best → TOP best in the sense of top of the class
  • departing — In a clue full of trains it reads as a timetable word, but what it sends away is a letter of the wordplay.

Every word is station announcement — an express, a terminus, a departure, a time — and none of it is doing the job the surface claims.

6 down POLAR

Look upset, wearing standard alternative to brown or black? (5)

Definition: alternative to brown or black? · reversal + container

alternative to brown or black? → POLAR: the three familiar kinds of bear are brown, black and polar, so the question mark flags a definition by example.

  • standard → PAR par is the standard, the score a golf hole is expected to take
  • Look → OL lo is the old exclamation for look, tipped over here
  • upset — Something upset has been tipped over, so the two letters it applies to arrive the other way about.
  • wearing — What you wear goes round you, so the piece doing the wearing opens up and closes again over the other one.

'Look' as the archaic lo is a crossword staple worth owning. The definition is the third member of the bear family: brown, black and this one.

7 down NAM

American war, oh boy, on the rise (3)

Definition: American war · reversal

American war → NAM: Nam is what US soldiers and films call Vietnam, so the shortened place name stands in for the war fought there.

  • oh boy → MAN 'oh boy' and 'man!' are the same exclamation of surprise
  • on the rise — This is a down entry, so a piece on the rise is read from the bottom of the grid towards the top.

Both halves lean on American speech — the exclamation for the wordplay, the soldiers' shorthand for the definition — which is why the clue reads in one voice.

8 down SOLAR PANEL

New Orleans pal producing silent generator (5,5)

Definition: silent generator · anagram

silent generator → SOLAR PANEL: a panel makes electricity straight from sunlight, with no engine and no moving parts, so it generates without a sound.

  • Orleans pal → ORLEANSPAL ten letters of fodder, split between a city and a friend
  • New — It reads as half of the city's name, but the instruction is the ordinary adjective: make something new out of the letters beside it.

'New Orleans' is the trap: the city is so familiar that breaking the name in two feels wrong, and the half you would never separate is the anagram indicator.

12 down WARTS AND ALL

Design shoe during walk with regular gaps, showing what's better hidden (5,3,3)

Definition: showing what's better hidden · container + charade + alternate letters

showing what's better hidden → WARTS AND ALL: the phrase comes from Cromwell's order to paint him complete with blemishes, so it means the flaws left on show.

  • walk → W read at alternate letters this word keeps two, and this is the one it starts with
  • Design → ART design in the sense of decorative work — the art on a vase
  • shoe → SANDAL an open summer shoe, six letters
  • walk → L the other letter that same word keeps when it is read at alternate spacing
  • during — What happens during a walk happens inside it, so the other two pieces are dropped between the letters that survive.
  • with regular gaps — Leave a gap after each letter you keep and a four-letter word gives up only two of them, evenly spaced.

Two long pieces go inside a two-letter shell, which is back to front for a container — usually the big word is the one that opens up. Counting the gaps is what breaks it open.

13 down MELANCHOLY

Blue skirts from Amazon received by Sporty Spice, divine (10)

Definition: Blue · container + outer letters + charade

Blue → MELANCHOLY: feeling blue is feeling low, and melancholy is that same settled sadness rather than a passing mood.

  • Sporty Spice → MELC Melanie Chisholm was Sporty Spice of the Spice Girls and goes by Mel C; that short name splits in two here
  • from Amazon → AN the letters at either end of the river's name
  • divine → HOLY divine in the religious sense is holy
  • skirts — To skirt a thing is to keep to its outside edge, so what you take is the pair of letters at either end of the word.
  • received by — Whoever receives something has it inside them, so the pop star's name opens up and closes again round it.

The surface is online shopping, so 'skirts' reads as clothing and 'Amazon' as the shop; both are doing other work. Mel C is the piece a solver outside Britain may need told.

16 down TRENCHANT

Scathing daughter put off by fashion mantra (9)

Definition: Scathing · deletion + charade

Scathing → TRENCHANT: trenchant comes from the French for cutting, so trenchant criticism is incisive and severe rather than merely clever.

  • fashion → TREND a fashion is a trend, and it is about to be a letter shorter
  • daughter → D d = daughter in the family abbreviations, and this is the letter that goes
  • mantra → CHANT a mantra is a chant, said over and over
  • put off by — Being put off is being dropped, so the letter named in front of it leaves the word that comes after.

The surface is a teenager unimpressed by a slogan, and every word of it is doing a second job. Only the deletion needs spotting; the rest is two words laid end to end.

18 down DEEPEST

Most genuinely felt insect repellent reduced irritation (7)

Definition: Most genuinely felt · deletion + charade

Most genuinely felt → DEEPEST: your deepest sympathy or regret is the most heartfelt kind, so the superlative measures sincerity rather than distance.

  • insect repellent → DEET DEET is the active ingredient in almost every bottle of insect repellent, and it is about to lose its tail
  • irritation → PEST an irritation is a pest, four letters following on
  • reduced — Something reduced has been made shorter, so the word beside it loses its final letter before anything is added.

'Insect repellent reduced irritation' is a whole sentence about a camping trip, which is why none of it reads as instruction. DEET is the piece worth learning once.

21 down ALLOT

Divide a tax on motorists up (5)

Definition: Divide · charade + reversal

Divide → ALLOT: to allot is to divide something into shares and hand them out, the sense behind an allotted time or an allotment.

  • a → A the clue's own indefinite article, taken as a single letter
  • tax on motorists → TOLL a toll is what a driver pays to use a bridge or a road; this is the piece that climbs
  • up — This is a down entry, so the piece marked up is read from the bottom of the grid towards the top.

'A tax on motorists' reads as one phrase, and the trap is dragging the A into the climb with it. Only the tax is turned; the article stays where it is.

22 down HEAT

Round microwave? (4)

Definition: Round · double definition

Round → HEAT and microwave? → HEAT: a heat is a qualifying round, and to heat food is precisely what that appliance does to it.

  • Round → the qualifying round of a race or a contest, the one finalists come out of
  • microwave? → what the kitchen appliance does to last night's dinner, read as a verb

Two words, two definitions, no wordplay at all — the shortest kind of cryptic. The question mark admits that the appliance is only one way of doing the second one.

24 down DEN

Retreat without warning, in the second half (3)

Definition: Retreat · last letters

Retreat → DEN: a den is the private room someone retreats to for peace and quiet, rather than the animal's lair of the other sense.

  • without warning → DEN 'without warning' says sudden, and the half of that word you keep is the half at the end
  • in the second half — It asks for the back half of a six-letter word rather than the whole of it, so only three letters are kept.

'Retreat without warning' is one idea on the surface — bolting — and two on the page, because the definition stops after the first word.
