Across

1 across NICHE

Boutique hotel in Provence? (5)

Definition: Boutique · container

Boutique and niche are both adjectives for a small specialised corner of a market, as in a boutique hotel or a niche product.

  • Provence? → NICE the big city of the French Riviera sits at the eastern edge of Provence, and the question mark owns up to the looseness
  • hotel → H H is hotel in the radio alphabet that pilots and police use, alpha bravo charlie delta
  • in — 'In' drops the letter just named inside the word that follows it, so one piece ends up housed within the other.

A small stylish hotel somewhere in the south of France.

'Hotel' is a letter here rather than a building, and 'Boutique' is being used as an adjective — the sense in 'boutique brewery', not a shop with a window.

4 across GATEPOST

Our secret repository tapes got interfered with (8)

Definition: Our secret repository · anagram

Say a thing is between you, me and the gatepost and the gatepost is where the secret is lodged, so it stands as our confidential store.

  • tapes got → TAPESGOT eight letters of raw material, the two words run together
  • interfered with — Something interfered with has been meddled about until it is no longer as it was left, and letters left in that state do not keep their order.

A hiding place for our secrets, whose tapes somebody has tampered with.

'Between you, me and the gatepost' is the British phrase for saying something in confidence, which is what makes a gatepost the keeper of a secret.

8 across MORE TO THE POINT

At first, Harry Potter got embroiled with emotion of greater relevance (4,2,3,5)

Definition: of greater relevance · anagram + first letter

'More to the point' is the idiom for what bears harder on the matter under discussion, so it works as a whole phrase rather than word by word.

  • Harry → H the opening letter of the name, which is all the instruction before it asks for
  • Potter → POTTER six letters of raw material, the surname taken whole
  • emotion → EMOTION seven more letters of raw material, the word as it stands
  • At first — 'At first' takes only what stands at the front, so the name beside it hands over its opening letter and nothing else.
  • got embroiled with — To be embroiled is to be tangled up in something, and letters tangled together come apart in a new order.

Harry Potter dragged into a row that turns emotional.

'At first' looks as though it governs the whole name, and it takes only the H — the surname goes into the mixture entire, which is where the extra letters come from.

10 across NIHILIST

Sceptic rogue exposed Philistine (8)

Definition: Sceptic · anagram + deletion

A nihilist rejects every belief and value as groundless, which makes 'sceptic' a fair if understated word for the doubter it names.

  • Philistine → HILISTIN the setter's own pen name with its outermost letter taken off each end, leaving eight
  • exposed → the P and the E come away before any shuffling happens
  • rogue — A rogue element has broken ranks and gone its own way, so the letters beside it abandon the order they were in.
  • exposed — Someone exposed has had their covering stripped off, so the word loses the letter standing at each end.

A headline: a rogue doubter has shown somebody up as a boor.

Philistine is the setter's pseudonym, printed above the puzzle, and the clue makes fodder of it — a Guardian setter signing the grid from inside one of its answers.

11 across BO PEEP

Smell and look of a shepherd (2,4)

Definition: a shepherd · charade

Little Bo Peep of the rhyme keeps sheep and loses them, so she is a shepherd named as one individual rather than described as a type.

  • Smell → BO BO is body odour, the initials British advertising made unmentionable
  • look → PEEP a peep is a quick furtive look

The scent and the appearance of somebody who watches over sheep.

Little Bo Peep is the nursery-rhyme girl who lost her sheep, which is why the strays she mislaid are sitting two rows below her in this grid.

12 across LOST SHEEP

Strays see plot unravel, keeping quiet (4,5)

Definition: Strays · anagram

Strays are animals that have wandered from where they belong, and lost sheep are exactly that, both words in the plural.

  • see plot → SEEPLOT seven letters of raw material, the two words together
  • quiet → SH sh is the noise you make when asking for quiet
  • unravel — Something unravelling comes apart thread by thread, so what it touches does not stay in the order it was wound in.
  • keeping — What is kept is held on to, so the letters named next stay in the mixture instead of dropping out of it.

Watching a story fall apart and saying nothing at all about it.

A lost sheep is a person who has wandered off from the fold, the parable's sense, and the shepherd two rows above in this grid is the one who mislaid them.

15 across NACHO

Snack shop unwrapped snack (5)

Definition: snack · middle letters

A nacho is a single tortilla chip served with melted cheese or salsa, so 'snack' names it by the kind of thing it is.

  • Snack → NAC one letter off each end of the five, leaving the three in the middle
  • shop → HO the same treatment on the second word, its two middle letters left standing
  • unwrapped — Unwrapping takes the covering off both ends at once, so each word beside it gives up its outermost letters and keeps the middle.

Something bought at a snack shop and already out of its packet.

The same word opens and closes the clue doing two different jobs: the first is material to be trimmed, the last is the definition.

17 across TASER

Team swears regularly? That’s a shocker! (5)

Definition: a shocker · alternate letters

A taser fires darts that deliver an incapacitating electric shock, so 'a shocker' names it by the one thing it does.

  • Team swears → TASER the first, third, fifth, seventh and ninth letters of the two words read as one string
  • regularly — Doing a thing regularly is doing it at a fixed interval, so letters are picked at every other position instead of in a run.

A side whose players are forever cursing, which is quite a scandal.

'That's a shocker' reads as tabloid outrage at the language and means a thing that delivers a shock — the setter's electricity, not the newspaper's.

18 across PROSECUTE

Sue’s verse lacking charm, then? (9)

Definition: Sue · charade

To sue is to take somebody to law and to prosecute is to bring the case against them, both being the verb for hauling a person into court.

  • verse lacking → PROSE writing with the verse taken out of it is prose, a definition by opposite rather than by synonym
  • charm → CUTE cute means charming or appealing, the adjective beside the noun

Sue's poetry, it seems, has no charm about it at all.

'Sue' is a woman's name on the surface and a verb underneath, and the first half of the wordplay is defined by what it lacks, which is rarer than a straight synonym.

19 across REMOTE

Distant memory about author’s return (6)

Definition: Distant · container + reversal

Something remote is far off, whether in miles or in likelihood, and 'distant' is the plain adjective for it in either sense.

  • memory → ROTE learning by rote is memorising through repetition, so rote does duty for memory
  • author → EM the person writing the clue is ME, and here those two letters run backwards
  • about — To be about a thing is to be all round it, so the word before this one closes over the letters that follow.
  • return — What returns comes back the way it went, so the two letters named here are written in the opposite order.

A faint recollection of a writer making a comeback.

'Author' is the setter referring to themselves, one of the oldest conventions in the craft — writer, compiler and yours truly all mean the same two letters.

21 across ANCESTOR

Perhaps grandma to some extent dances to relax (8)

Definition: Perhaps grandma · hidden word

A grandmother is one of the forebears a person is descended from, so the clue defines the general word by handing over a single instance.

  • dances to relax → ANCESTOR eight letters running unbroken from one letter into the first word as far as the opening letter of the last
  • to some extent — 'To some extent' means part of what is there rather than all of it, and the part wanted is a run of letters lying inside the words that follow.

A grandmother who does a certain amount of dancing to unwind.

'Perhaps' is a fair-play flag rather than padding: a grandmother is one example of the class the answer names, not a synonym for the whole of it.

24 across KAKISTOCRACIES

Poor governments giving Socrates a kick, I fancy (14)

Definition: Poor governments · anagram

A kakistocracy is a state run by its worst citizens, so 'poor governments' names them in the plural by the quality of whoever governs.

  • Socrates → SOCRATES eight letters of raw material, the philosopher's name taken whole
  • a kick → AKICK five more letters, the article counted in with them
  • I → I the pronoun contributes itself, the fourteenth letter needed
  • fancy — 'Fancy' asks you to imagine the material as something other than what is written, so its order is not to be trusted.

Somebody picturing a good boot aimed at Socrates and at incompetent governments.

A kakistocracy is rule by the worst people, from the Greek kakistos meaning worst, which is why a Greek philosopher is standing in the fodder.

25 across STIGMATA

Marks floral parts (8)

Definition: Marks · double definition

One sense is the marks of Christ's wounds appearing on a body; the other is the plural of the flower's pollen-catching stigma.

  • Marks → the wounds said to appear on the hands and feet of certain saints, mirroring the crucifixion
  • floral parts → the sticky tips of a flower's pistil, where the pollen has to land

An examiner awarding grades to the various bits of a flower.

Both senses are the plural of stigma, one out of religion and one out of botany, and the clue sets them side by side so the first reads as a verb.

26 across TASKS

Finally grant requests for jobs (5)

Definition: jobs · charade + last letter

Tasks are pieces of work set for somebody to do, and 'jobs' is the everyday plural for the same things.

  • grant → T the closing letter of the word, which is all the instruction wants
  • requests → ASKS to request is to ask, and both words stay in the third person
  • Finally — 'Finally' points at what comes at the very end, so only the closing letter of the next word is taken.

An official giving way at last and approving applications for work.

'Finally grant requests' reads as a bureaucrat giving in after a long wait, which is what makes the first word look like an adverb of time rather than a letter-picking instruction.

Down

1 down NOMENCLATURE

Undersign leaders of climate legislation in environment terminology (12)

Definition: terminology · container + first letters

Nomenclature is the system of names a field uses for the things in it, which is what terminology means in any specialist subject.

  • environment → NATURE the natural world is nature, and environment is the everyday word for the same thing
  • sign → OMEN an omen is a sign of what is coming, the portent sense of the word
  • climate legislation → CL the front letter of each of these two words, taken in the order given
  • Undersign — One word doing two jobs: the 'sign' is a piece to be found, and 'under' puts what follows below it, which in a down entry means later in the answer.
  • leaders of — The leader of a word is the letter at its front, so two words named here surrender one letter each.
  • in — 'In' asks for the pieces already found to be dropped inside the word that comes after it.

Signing off on the first green bills, in the language environmental policy uses.

'Undersign' looks like one word about signatures and is really two instructions run together, and 'environment' is the natural world rather than the political one the surface is talking about.

2 down CIRRHOSIS

Disease can be horrific if ignored by family member (9)

Definition: Disease · charade + anagram + deletion

Cirrhosis is the scarring of the liver that follows long damage, usually from alcohol, so 'disease' names it by its plainest category.

  • horrific → CIRRHO eight letters of raw material with two struck out, and the six left over reshuffled
  • family member → SIS sis is the familiar short form of sister
  • if → the I and the F come out of the material before any shuffling starts
  • can be — 'Can be' says the material is capable of coming out as something else, which is a licence to move its letters around.
  • if ignored — What is ignored is passed over and never used, so the two letters spelling that little word leave the material.

A warning that an illness turns nasty when relatives take no notice of it.

'Horrific' reads as a comment on the disease and is the material itself, while the word sitting inside it is the one the clue tells you to throw away.

3 down EXTOL

Wax lyrical about the previous bunch getting a comeback (5)

Definition: Wax lyrical · charade + reversal

To extol is to praise something warmly and at length, which is exactly what waxing lyrical about it means.

  • the previous → EX an ex is the previous partner, and ex- is the prefix for anything former
  • bunch → TOL a bunch of something is a lot of it, here written back to front
  • getting a comeback — Something making a comeback arrives back the way it went, so the letters just found are written in the opposite order.

Praising a former band whose old records are selling again.

'About' looks like an instruction to wrap one piece around another, and it is only the 'concerning' of waxing lyrical about a thing.

4 down GO TO SLEEP

Conk out drunk, get up and use loo? Not us! (2,2,5)

Definition: Conk out · anagram + deletion

To conk out is to stop dead or drop off, and of a person it means dropping into sleep, usually all at once.

  • get up → GETP five letters with the U struck out, leaving four to work with
  • use loo → SELOO six letters with the second U struck out, leaving five
  • Not us → the two Us that go, one out of each of the phrases before it
  • drunk — A drunk cannot hold a straight line, and letters in that condition will not keep to the order they were handed over in.
  • Not us — Read 'us' as the plural of the letter U: both of them are struck out of the material before the rest is rearranged.

We are not the sort to pass out drunk and then stagger up to the loo.

'Us' is not the pronoun it looks like — it is the plural of the letter U, and the eye reads straight past that while the clue quietly removes two letters.

5 down TEEN

X÷e = 13-19 (4)

Definition: 13-19 · container

Thirteen to nineteen are the numbers whose names all finish with these four letters, so the range points at the ending they share.

  • X → TEN X is ten in Roman numerals
  • e → E the letter is taken at face value out of the equation
  • ÷ — The division sign is read as English rather than arithmetic: it cuts the numeral in two, and the letter named after it goes into the cut.

A scrap of algebra, X over e, coming out somewhere in a range of numbers.

Roman numerals are ordinary crossword words — X for ten, C for a hundred — and the mathematical symbols here are read as English, with 'divided by' meaning split open.

6 down PRONOUNCE

You or me on vacation, creative and articulate (9)

Definition: articulate · charade + outer letters

To pronounce a word is to sound it out distinctly, and 'articulate' as a verb means exactly that, saying a thing clearly.

  • You or me → PRONOUN both of these little words belong to that part of speech, so the clue names the class by handing over two of its members
  • creative → CE the letters at the two ends of the word, with the middle cleared out
  • on vacation — A house whose people are on vacation has nothing left but its shell, so the word keeps its first and last letters and loses everything between.

You and I away on holiday, inventive and clear-spoken with it.

'You or me on vacation' looks like one phrase about a holiday, and the emptying instruction actually reaches forward past the comma to work on the word after it.

7 down SINGE

Some fires, in general, burn (5)

Definition: burn · hidden word

To singe is to burn something only at its surface, so 'burn' defines it as the plain verb with the depth taken out.

  • fires, in general → SINGE the run starts on the last letter of the first word, swallows the two-letter word whole and stops two letters into the third
  • Some — 'Some' asks for a portion of what follows rather than the whole of it, and the portion wanted is an unbroken run of letters.

Fires burning here and there, spoken of in general terms.

The commas do the camouflage: they break the words into a remark about fires when the three of them are one continuous row of letters.

9 down OPTOMETRISTS

Those with charts choose to hide book? Stirs trouble (12)

Definition: Those with charts · container + anagram

Optometrists test eyesight for a living and the letter chart on the wall is the tool of the trade, so they are named by it.

  • choose → OPT to opt for something is to choose it
  • book → TOME a tome is a big heavy volume
  • Stirs → RISTS five letters of raw material, stirred into another order
  • to hide — To hide a thing is to put it out of sight inside something else, so one piece goes bodily within the word before it.
  • trouble — Trouble is disorder, and letters in disorder do not stay in the arrangement the clue gave them.

People with charts choosing to conceal a book, which stirs up bother.

The charts are the boards of shrinking letters you read out from across the room, not tables of figures, and 'Stirs trouble' reads as one phrase while working as material plus instruction.

13 down SURE THING

Of course their guns misfired (4,5)

Definition: Of course · anagram

'Sure thing' is the spoken agreement meaning yes, certainly, which is the job 'of course' does in the same conversation.

  • their guns → THEIRGUNS nine letters of raw material, the two words run together
  • misfired — A gun that misfires has not gone off the way it was loaded, so the letters come out other than as they went in.

A resigned account of a skirmish in which every weapon on their side failed.

Definition and answer are both things people say rather than things they mean, and 'a sure thing' carries a second sense the gambler uses: a certainty.

14 down PHOENICIA

Desperately, I phone agency in old Lebanon (9)

Definition: old Lebanon · anagram

Phoenicia was the ancient name for the coastal strip that is Lebanon today, so 'old Lebanon' names the same ground at an earlier date.

  • I phone → IPHONE six letters of raw material, the pronoun and the verb together
  • agency → CIA the CIA is the American foreign intelligence service
  • Desperately — Doing a thing desperately is doing it without order or plan, so the letters land in whatever arrangement comes.

A frantic call put through to the intelligence services.

Phoenicia was the seafaring civilisation of Tyre and Sidon on what is now the Lebanese coast, and its traders gave Europe the alphabet these letters are written in.

16 down COUNTRIES

Nations, My Lord, rise in revolution (9)

Definition: Nations · charade + anagram

Countries and nations are both plurals for sovereign states, though 'nation' leans on the people and 'country' on the territory they hold.

  • My Lord → COUNT a count is the continental nobleman, of the rank a lord is addressed by
  • rise → RIES four letters of raw material, turned about into another order
  • in revolution — A revolution overturns the established order, so the word beside it comes back in a different arrangement.

Somebody telling a judge that the nations are rising in revolt.

'Rise in revolution' reads as one phrase about an uprising and is material plus instruction, and 'My Lord' wants the rank rather than the person being addressed.

20 down MEANT

Cruel treatment essentially deliberate (5)

Definition: deliberate · charade + middle letter

Something meant is something intended rather than accidental, which is what calling an act deliberate says about it.

  • Cruel → MEAN a mean act is a cruel one, the unkind sense of the word rather than the average
  • treatment → T the middle letter of the nine, with four standing either side of it
  • essentially — What is essential sits at the heart of a thing, so the letter at the very centre of the word is the one taken.

Harsh handling that was entirely intended.

'Deliberate' is the adjective here and not the verb you hear first, and 'cruel treatment' reads as a single phrase while working as two separate pieces.

22 down EXACT

Precise demand (5)

Definition: Precise · double definition

One sense is the adjective for something correct to the last detail; the other is the verb meaning to wring a payment out of somebody.

  • Precise → the adjective for something correct down to the last detail
  • demand → the verb for wringing a payment or a promise out of somebody who would rather not give it

A demand worded down to the very last detail.

The two words look like an adjective describing a noun, and they are two independent senses standing side by side, one adjective and one verb.

23 down ETNA

This erupts frequently in Vietnam (4)

Definition: This erupts frequently · hidden word

Etna is the Sicilian volcano that erupts more often than any other in Europe, so the clue names it by its habit.

  • Vietnam → ETNA four consecutive letters beginning at the third letter of the country's name
  • in — 'In' says the letters wanted are already sitting inside the word that follows, in the order they are written there.

A report of something going off again and again in Vietnam.

The volcano is Sicilian, so 'in Vietnam' is geography for the surface only and the country's name is a row of letters rather than a place.
