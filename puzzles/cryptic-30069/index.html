Across

1 across SOUL-DESTROYING

Extremely boring oldster is young after treatment (4-10)

Definition: Extremely boring · anagram

  • oldster is young → SOULDESTROYING the fourteen letters of these three words, resettled

Fourteen letters of fodder for a fourteen-letter answer, which is a useful way to place a long anagram: the definition can only be the two words left over.

9 across ABIGAIL

Woman needs a large beer delivered (7)

Definition: Woman · charade + homophone

  • a → A the article taken straight off the page
  • large → BIG big is the plain synonym for large
  • beer → AIL the beer is ALE, and said aloud it can just as well be spelt the other way

'Delivered' is the giveaway that something here is meant for the ear. Once the beer has been heard, the woman's name is ordinary vocabulary.

10 across LUMPIER

Less smooth piece of sugar? That is right (7)

Definition: Less smooth · charade

  • piece of sugar? → LUMP a lump of sugar is the cube that goes in your tea
  • That is → IE i.e. is the Latin id est, which translates as that is
  • right → R R is the standard abbreviation for right

IE for 'that is' is one of the abbreviations worth owning outright — it appears in hundreds of grids. The sugar lump keeps the surface at the tea table.

11 across GROWL

Warning sound from half grey bird (5)

Definition: Warning sound · charade + first letters

  • grey → GR the first half of GREy
  • bird → OWL an owl is the bird

OWL is the crossword's default bird — short, vowel-rich, endlessly useful — so it is worth trying whenever a bare 'bird' turns up.

12 across IMPENDING

Threatening little devil with termination (9)

Definition: Threatening · charade

  • little devil → IMP an imp is the small mischievous devil of folklore
  • termination → ENDING a termination is an ending

IMP is the crossword's standard little devil. The surface reads as a threat of dismissal, which is exactly the misdirection 'termination' is there to supply.

13 across DEMAGOGUE

Democrat, very eager, occasionally pure rabble-rouser (9)

Definition: rabble-rouser · charade + alternate letters

  • Democrat → DEM Dem is the newspaper shorthand for a Democrat
  • very eager → AGOG agog means eagerly expectant
  • pure → UE the second and fourth letters of pUrE

The surface paints a party colleague being praised; the definition is the sting waiting in the last word. American politics supplies the opening three letters.

14 across MAYOR

PM, or less important office (5)

Definition: less important office · charade

  • PM → MAY Theresa May was Prime Minister, and PMs' surnames are standard fodder
  • or → OR the little conjunction is taken at face value, two letters straight off the page

Prime ministers' surnames are a standing resource for setters — May, Heath, Brown, Blair — and here one of them is bolted to the word that follows it.

15 across GRAFT

Tennis champion finally quit hard work (5)

Definition: hard work · charade + last letter

  • Tennis champion → GRAF Steffi Graf won twenty-two grand slam singles titles
  • quit → T the last letter of quiT

The surface pretends to be about a player retiring. Sporting surnames of exactly four letters — Graf, Borg, Hunt, Owen — are a setter's staple.

17 across LOUISIANA

US state’s old university is covered in creeper (9)

Definition: US state · container + charade

  • old → O O is the standard abbreviation for old
  • university → U U is the standard abbreviation for university
  • is → IS the little verb is taken at face value, two letters straight off the page
  • creeper → LIANA a liana is the woody creeper that climbs rainforest trees

The little word 'is' is not holding the sentence together — it is two letters of the answer, and spotting that is the hardest step in the clue.

20 across TREE HOUSE

Branch manager may put this up for children’s play (4,5)

Definition: Branch manager may put this up for children’s play · cryptic definition

  • Branch manager → The surface hands you a bank official; the setter means somebody whose branches want managing, which puts you in the garden looking upwards.
  • may put this up for children’s play → What gets erected among those branches is a small wooden platform with walls, built so the young have somewhere of their own.

There is no separable wordplay here: the clue is one picture, and it turns on reading 'branch manager' as a job in a bank before you read it as a job in a garden.

22 across LYRIC

What one can get from really rich poem (5)

Definition: poem · hidden word

  • really rich → LYRIC the answer sits across the join in reaLLY RICh

An adverb and an adjective sit side by side and the answer bridges them, so the trick is to stop reading words and start reading letters.

23 across READIER

Better prepared to fail in the end (7)

Definition: Better prepared · container

  • the end → REAR the rear of something is its end
  • fail → DIE a plan that fails is said to die

The surface is about giving up, and 'the end' is not an instruction about final letters — it is simply a word meaning the back of something.

24 across TITANIA

The last to leave doomed ship, a drama queen (7)

Definition: drama queen · deletion + charade

  • doomed ship → TITANI the Titanic is the doomed ship, and its closing C is the one that leaves
  • a → A the article taken straight off the page

'Drama queen' is an insult on the surface and a job description in the answer: a queen who lives in a play. Shakespeare's fairy monarch is the one wanted.

25 across VESTED INTEREST

Financial claim as tense test drive crashed (6,8)

Definition: Financial claim · anagram

  • tense test drive → VESTEDINTEREST the fourteen letters of these three words, resettled

The surface is a road accident and the answer is a legal one. 'Vested' is property-law language: an interest that has already come to you rather than one you might yet acquire.

1 down SHAGGY DOG STORY

Anticlimactic account of oversexed pet? (6,3,5)

Definition: Anticlimactic account · charade

  • oversexed → SHAGGY shaggy leans on the British slang sense of shag, and the question mark flags the liberty
  • pet → DOG a dog is the household pet the surface has in mind
  • account → STORY an account of events is a story, and this word is doing two jobs at once

'Account' pulls double duty: it closes the definition and it hands over the last word of the answer. Setters allow themselves that when the surface is this smooth.

2 down UNIFORM

What to wear in college class (7)

Definition: What to wear · charade

  • college → UNI uni is the everyday short form of university
  • class → FORM a form is a class in a British school

Two words for places of education sit side by side and neither is the definition. FORM for a school class is the piece worth remembering; it turns up constantly.

3 down DEADLIGHT

Cabin shutter so easy to pick up? (9)

Definition: Cabin shutter · charade

  • so → DEAD dead works as a flat intensifier the way so does — dead easy, dead right, dead certain
  • easy to pick up → LIGHT anything light is easy to lift, so the phrase describes the word by what it feels like in the hand

Both halves lean on loose everyday English rather than abbreviation, which makes them hard to spot. The nautical sense of the definition is the thing to look up and keep.

4 down SOLVING

What you are doing is very caring, nothing omitted (7)

Definition: What you are doing · charade + deletion

  • very → SO so is a plain intensifier, handed over whole
  • nothing → O a nought is written O, and this is the letter that gets taken out
  • caring → LVING LOVING is caring; lose that O and five letters remain

The definition is pointed straight at the reader, mid-puzzle, which is why it looks like part of the sentence rather than a synonym.

5 down RELAPSE

Sink back asleep, shattered after run (7)

Definition: Sink back · anagram + charade

  • run → R R is the cricket scoreboard's abbreviation for run
  • asleep → ELAPSE the six letters of asleep, resettled

'Sink back' is the definition and looks like part of the drowsy picture, which is the trap. The R hides in a single word doing service as a cricket score.

6 down YEMEN

The old folks land (5)

Definition: land · charade

  • The → YE YE is the antique spelling of the, as in ye olde shoppe
  • folks → MEN folks are people, and men will stand for them here

The YE spelling comes from a misread Old English letter, the thorn, which printers set as a Y. Setters flag it with 'old', 'once' or 'formerly'.

7 down NOISILY

Inquisitively touring island, while playing loud music? (7)

Definition: while playing loud music? · container

  • Inquisitively → NOSILY to be nosy is to be inquisitive, and the adverb follows
  • island → I I is the map abbreviation for island

'Nosy' and 'noisy' are one letter and one idea apart, which is the whole trick — the clue wants you to reach for the inquisitive word first.

8 down GREGORIAN CHANT

Grim from the start, hearing cantor messing up old music (9,5)

Definition: old music · anagram + first letter

  • Grim → G the first letter of Grim
  • hearing cantor → REGORIANCHANT thirteen letters resettled behind that G

The surface has a cantor being scolded at rehearsal. 'Hearing' is only more material to shuffle, despite looking every inch like an instruction of its own.

14 down MISTLETOE

Stole time for fixing Christmas decoration (9)

Definition: Christmas decoration · anagram

  • Stole time → MISTLETOE the nine letters of these two words, resettled

'Stole time' reads as theft and is really a bag of letters, which is the whole deception. The definition sits at the far end, plain as anything.

16 down AVERAGE

Declare period of history unexceptional (7)

Definition: unexceptional · charade

  • Declare → AVER to aver is to declare something as fact
  • period of history → AGE an age is a stretch of history, as in the Bronze Age

AVER is a courtroom verb that earns its keep in grids because of its vowels. The surface plays as a historian being dismissive about a whole century.

17 down LOUVRED

Museum cooled at last, given openings for air (7)

Definition: given openings for air · charade + last letter

  • Museum → LOUVRE the Louvre in Paris is the museum meant
  • cooled → D the last letter of cooleD

The Louvre is the museum setters reach for first, and here it is wanted as a string of letters rather than as a place in Paris.

18 down UNEATEN

Left you to tidy up (7)

Definition: Left · charade

  • you → U U is how a text message writes you, and setters adopted it long ago
  • tidy up → NEATEN to neaten is to tidy up

'Left' looks like a direction or a departure and is neither — it is food still on the plate when everyone has finished.

19 down ARRANGE

Missing some gear, rang emporium to order (7)

Definition: order · hidden word

  • Missing → nothing is taken away here — Missing only sets up the shopping story
  • gear, rang emporium → ARRANGE the answer runs straight through geARRANGEmporium

The emporium story is a decoy — nothing is being ordered. The answer spans the join between two words, which is why a comma is such good cover.

21 down HOIST

Raise anchor: I contributed (5)

Definition: Raise · container

  • anchor → HOST the anchor of a news programme is its host
  • I → I the letter I, taken as itself

'Anchor' is a television word here rather than a nautical one, which is a neat piece of misdirection in a clue that opens with 'Raise'.
