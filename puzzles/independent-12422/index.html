Across

8 across FAIRER

Suppressing anger is far more dispassionate (6)

Definition: more dispassionate · container

  • far → FAR the little word FAR is the container, and it comes apart between its A and its R
  • anger → IRE ire is anger, three letters that turn up in crosswords far more often than in speech

The word doing the swallowing is the one you would read straight past — a three-letter adverb that looks like part of the definition and is really the wrapper.

9 across TEA-CADDY

Source of drink and marijuana given to clubman? (3-5)

Definition: Source of drink · charade

  • marijuana → TEA tea has been slang for marijuana since the jazz age
  • clubman? → CADDY the caddy is the man who carries a golfer's clubs round the course

Both halves point at a gentleman's afternoon and mean something else: the drug rather than the leaf, the golf course rather than the club armchair.

10 across BERYLLIA

Oxide reliably breaking down (8)

Definition: Oxide · anagram

  • reliably → BERYLLIA the eight letters of RELIABLY, rearranged

A chemistry answer with a one-word definition gives you nothing to guess at, so this is a clue to solve from the letters inwards rather than the meaning outwards.

11 across INSANE

Cracked it, entering most of interior (6)

Definition: Cracked · container + deletion

  • interior → INNE what is interior is inner, and the word arrives without its final letter
  • it → SA 'it' is 1920s slang for sex appeal, which crosswords have written as SA ever since

SA for 'it' is worth learning outright: a person who had 'it' had sex appeal, and in a clue the pronoun almost never stands for the two letters I and T.

12 across STET

About to leave road? Keep in place (4)

Definition: Keep in place · deletion

  • road → STREET a street is a road, and it is the word the wordplay works on
  • About → RE RE is about, as in 'Re: your letter' at the head of a memo

RE for 'about' is one of the two most-used pairs of letters in the crossword, and here the road surface and the printed page it really belongs to never meet.

14 across BLAME

Book not generating interest or criticism (5)

Definition: criticism · charade

  • Book → B B is the standing abbreviation for book
  • not generating interest → LAME something lame falls flat and holds nobody's attention

'Not generating interest' looks like a phrase off the financial pages and is a plain synonym — the money surface has nothing at all behind it.

16 across AVER

Hesitate to oust leader in state (4)

Definition: state · deletion

  • Hesitate → WAVER to waver is to hesitate, swaying between two answers
  • leader → W the leader of WAVER is the letter at its head

AVER is a courtroom word — to assert something as fact — and it is one of that handful of short verbs that keep reappearing because their letters are so useful.

18 across BREATH

Suggestion engineers should be brought in by city (6)

Definition: Suggestion · container

  • city → BATH Bath is the Somerset spa city, and it comes apart straight after its B
  • engineers → RE the Royal Engineers are RE, the sappers of the British Army

'A breath of scandal' is the phrase the definition comes out of — a breath there is the faintest trace of a thing, nothing to do with lungs.

20 across MARINE

US state home to river touching the sea (6)

Definition: touching the sea · container

  • US state → MAINE Maine is the state at the north-east corner of the US, and it opens after MA
  • river → R R is the map abbreviation for river

The surface has a river reaching the coast and the wordplay has one letter inside a state, so 'home to' is doing hospitality above and plumbing below.

22 across SEED

Sailor escaping bottom of ocean for a start (4)

Definition: a start · deletion

  • bottom of ocean → SEABED the seabed is the floor of the ocean, one word and the whole of the raw material here
  • Sailor → AB an AB is an able seaman, the crossword's default sailor

'Bottom of ocean' is a definition of a word, not an instruction to take a last letter — spotting which of those it is decides whether the clue opens at all.

23 across PESTO

Sauce a part of canapés today? (5)

Definition: Sauce · hidden word

  • canapés today → PESTO canaPÉS TOday carries the answer straight across the gap between the two words

The join between the two words is where the eye slides past, and an accent sitting in the middle of the run makes it slide faster still.

25 across GATE

Kick-off of game upset spectators (4)

Definition: spectators · charade + first letter

  • game → G the first letter of GAME, an ordinary initial hidden in football language
  • upset → ATE what ate at you is what upset you — 'what's eating him?' asks what is wrong

The gate of a match is both the crowd and the money they paid, named after the turnstile they came through rather than anything they did.

26 across THREES

Crowds, perhaps, having hour in river (6)

Definition: Crowds, perhaps · container

  • river → TEES the Tees runs out to sea past Middlesbrough, and it opens after its first letter
  • hour → HR hr is how a timetable abbreviates hour

Two's company, three's a crowd: that proverb is the whole of the definition, and 'perhaps' is there because only the proverb makes the equation true.

28 across PLEASURE

Meadowlands contributing to unalloyed contentment (8)

Definition: contentment · container

  • unalloyed → PURE an unalloyed metal is pure, with nothing mixed into it, and the word splits after its P
  • Meadowlands → LEAS a lea is a meadow, so leas are meadowlands

A lea is the poets' and place-namers' word for a meadow, and capitalising it turns it into somewhere in New Jersey for as long as the surface needs.

30 across STRUTTER

Impediment bringing in right horse at dressage? (8)

Definition: horse at dressage? · container

  • Impediment → STUTTER a stutter is a speech impediment, and it opens up after its first two letters
  • right → R R is the standing abbreviation for right

Dressage horses are judged on an exaggerated high-stepping walk, so the definition is a description and a joke in the same three words.

31 across TWISTY

Charlie, slowly, finally absorbing section of convoluted story? (6)

Definition: convoluted story? · container + charade

  • Charlie → TWIT a right Charlie is a fool, and so is a twit
  • slowly, finally → Y slowlY ends on this letter
  • section → S S is the standing abbreviation for section

'Charlie' is the fool of 'a proper Charlie', not the NATO letter C the surface is hoping you reach for first.

Down

1 down LAMENTABLE

Label meant to be edited? That’s very unfortunate (10)

Definition: very unfortunate · anagram

  • Label meant → LAMENTABLE the ten letters of LABEL MEANT, resettled into a different word

The surface sells a printing job — a label with the wrong wording on it — and the sigh at the end is the only part of the sentence meant literally.

2 down ARMY

No opening for bubbly host (4)

Definition: host · deletion

  • bubbly → BARMY barm is the froth on fermenting beer, so barmy first meant frothy

Barmy has drifted a long way: it began as a brewer's word and ended up meaning daft, and the surface's lively party-giver is nothing like the multitude wanted.

3 down DRILL BIT

Component of toolbox routine took effect (5,3)

Definition: Component of toolbox · charade

  • routine → DRILL a drill is a fixed routine gone through again and again until it sticks
  • took effect → BIT sanctions that bite are sanctions that work, so what bit took effect

'Bit' as the past tense of bite is the step to spot: the clue never uses the word in its toolbox sense until the answer has already arrived.

4 down OTTAWA

A measure of power taken up under old Canadian government? (6)

Definition: Canadian government? · charade + reversal

  • old → O O is the standing abbreviation for old
  • A measure of power → TTAWA the watt is the measure of electrical power, and A WATT comes back the other way

Naming a capital for the government that sits in it is ordinary newspaper shorthand — Washington says, Ottawa announces — and the question mark is what licenses it.

5 down NATIVE

Oyster? Simple to swallow half of it (6)

Definition: Oyster? · container

  • Simple → NAIVE naive is simple in the unworldly sense, and the word opens up after its first two letters
  • it → T IT cut in half leaves a single T

Oysters are named for where they grew, so the home-grown ones are natives — the opening 'Oyster?' reads like an aside and is doing the whole job of the definition.

6 down NAUSEATING

Area amidst Northern university accommodation provoking illness (10)

Definition: provoking illness · charade

  • Northern → N N is the compass abbreviation for northern
  • Area → A A is the standing abbreviation for area
  • university → U U is the standing abbreviation for university
  • accommodation → SEATING seating is what a hall accommodates its audience with

Three single-letter abbreviations in a row is what makes a ten-letter answer feel unreachable, right up until the last word of the wordplay hands over seven letters at once.

7 down ADEN

Study supporting Australian port (4)

Definition: port · charade

  • Australian → A A is the standing abbreviation for Australian
  • Study → DEN the small private room you shut yourself in to work

'Study' is one of the crossword's hardest-worked words: DEN almost always, CON now and then, and hardly ever the act of studying.

13 down TWEEDLEDUM

One of a pair guided through river with hesitation (10)

Definition: One of a pair · container + charade

  • river → TWEED the Tweed runs along the Scottish border
  • guided → LED to guide someone is to lead them
  • hesitation → UM um is the noise of hesitating, and crosswords treat it as a word

Carroll's brothers are so alike that he needs both to make either one funny, which is what the definition is quietly telling you before you have a letter.

15 down MUM

Silent disease - no additional comment (3)

Definition: Silent · deletion

  • disease → MUMPS mumps is the swollen-glands illness of childhood
  • additional comment → PS a PS is the extra line added once a letter has been signed off

The trick is noticing that two of the letters on the end of a childhood illness are themselves a word, and a word this clue has a definition for.

17 down ELECTORATE

Voters cheer when Minister, ignoring leader, gets in (10)

Definition: Voters · container + deletion

  • cheer → ELATE to elate someone is to cheer them, and the word splits after its first two letters
  • Minister → ECTOR a rector is a parish minister, arriving here without the R he starts with

'Leader' means the letter at the head of a word rather than anybody in charge, and the election-night surface is there purely to stop you noticing that.

19 down HUE

Good to avoid excessive clamour (3)

Definition: clamour · deletion

  • excessive → HUGE huge is excessive in size
  • Good → G G is the standing abbreviation for good

Hue and cry was a legal duty once: the shout that obliged every neighbour to join the chase. The clue wants that shout, not the colour.

21 down AMORETTI

Lovers at it frenetically, increasingly under covers (8)

Definition: Lovers · anagram + container

  • at it → ATTI the four letters of AT IT, resettled and then opened up after the first
  • increasingly → MORE increasingly is more and more of a thing, so the word itself is more

Amoretti are the plump winged cupids of Italian painting, and the bedroom surface is the setter approaching the same subject from the least reverent end.

23 down PASHTO

Possibly Afghan remains? Read more about that (6)

Definition: Possibly Afghan · container

  • Read more → PTO PTO — please turn over — is the note at the foot of a page telling you to read on
  • remains → ASH ash is what remains once a fire has burnt out

PTO is worth banking: three letters for a whole instruction, and one of the few abbreviations a setter can clue without naming a word at all.

24 down SUPERB

Excellent US sports event not getting the bird? (6)

Definition: Excellent · deletion

  • US sports event → SUPERBOWL the Super Bowl is the NFL's championship game and the biggest American broadcast of the year
  • the bird → OWL the owl is the bird, and it happens to be the end of the event's name

Getting the bird also means being booed off, so the phrase reads two ways in the surface while doing one plain job underneath.

27 down HATE

Expedition leaving South - what’s not to like? (4)

Definition: not to like · deletion

  • Expedition → HASTE expedition is speed — 'with all expedition' means with all haste
  • South → S S is the compass abbreviation for south

The polar journey in the surface is built on the sense of 'expedition' the clue does not use; the sense it does use is the one that means despatch.

29 down SAIL

Mollusc spurning new trip at sea (4)

Definition: trip at sea · deletion

  • Mollusc → SNAIL the snail is the shell-carrying mollusc of the garden
  • new → N N is the standing abbreviation for new

'At sea' belongs to the definition here — a sail is an outing on the water, not a piece of canvas — so the words that look like an instruction are the plain half.
