Across

8 across HABITUAL

It’s normal for holiday at Legoland at the outset to involve a little something for everyone (8)

Definition: normal · charade + container + first letters

  • holiday at Legoland at the outset → HAL the opening letters of Holiday At Legoland
  • a little → ABIT a little of something is a bit of it
  • something for everyone → U a U certificate marks a film as universal, passed for everyone

The family outing is decoration — all the clue wants from Legoland is a letter. Bank the U: a U certificate film is the one anybody is allowed to watch.

10 across DEEP STATE

Spooner’s expensive rendezvous with The Blob? (4,5)

Definition: The Blob · spoonerism

  • expensive → DEEP expensive is steep, and Spooner takes its ST away in exchange for a D
  • rendezvous → STATE a rendezvous is a date, and it picks up the ST that steep gave up

'The Blob' is the political jibe for the permanent officialdom ministers say frustrates them; the question mark admits it is a nickname rather than a dictionary word.

11 across TIMBERLINE

I tremble in feverish place where the fuel runs out? (10)

Definition: place where the fuel runs out · anagram

  • I tremble in → TIMBERLINE ten letters of I TREMBLE IN, rearranged

The shivering and the mountain cold pull the same way, which is the misdirection. 'Fuel' here means firewood, and trees are what stop at that altitude.

12 across MURDER

Relish killing (6)

Definition: Relish · double definition

  • Relish → MURDER the slang sense of falling on something with great appetite, as in a familiar phrase about craving a curry
  • killing → MURDER the crime itself, one person unlawfully killing another

Two words, two senses, and a nastier surface than either deserves. It is also one of this grid's collective nouns — crows come in one.

14 across UNGLAZED

Open to the elements, caps missing, air processor burned (8)

Definition: Open to the elements · deletion + charade

  • air processor → UNG a lung processes air, and the L on top of it goes
  • burned → LAZED burned is blazed, and the B on top of it goes

'Caps' is plural, and that is the step people miss: one instruction beheads both words, which is why the second half looks nothing like the answer at first.

16 across DECRYPT

Unravel slam point (7)

Definition: Unravel · charade

  • slam → DECRY to slam a film in a review is to decry it
  • point → PT PT is the standard abbreviation for point, as on a chart

'Slam' invites you to think of doors and dunks; the wanted sense is the reviewer's. With no indicator anywhere, the two pieces simply sit end to end.

18 across IMMENSE

Mammoth setter’s crew stripped user (7)

Definition: Mammoth · charade + deletion

  • setter’s → IM the setter speaks as I, so the setter is becomes I'M
  • crew → MEN a ship's crew is its men
  • user → SE uSEr with its outside letters taken off

'Setter's' is the Guardian solver's daily bread — the setter is I, so the possessive is I'M. Mammoth is the adjective here, not the animal.

21 across STRIDDEN

Walked free in road house (8)

Definition: Walked · container + charade

  • road → ST street shortens to ST on any address
  • free → RID to be free of something is to be rid of it
  • house → DEN a den is a small private room in a house

The surface reads 'roadhouse' as one word, which is the trap — road and house are clued separately, and the participle is one solvers forget exists.

23 across SUTURE

Sew up ruptured uterus (6)

Definition: Sew up · anagram

  • uterus → SUTURE the six letters of UTERUS, rearranged

Surgical from end to end: what ruptures is the very thing you stitch, so fodder and definition belong to one scene rather than fighting each other.

24 across COLLECTIVE NOUNS

Ten solutions may provide tonic with novel clues (10,5)

Definition: Ten solutions · anagram

  • tonic with novel clues → COLLECTIVENOUNS the fifteen letters of TONIC, NOVEL and CLUES, rearranged

Ten of the other answers here are the crowd word for some creature — owls, crows, whales, wolves and more — which is why 'Ten solutions' is meant quite literally.

26 across PACE

Begging your permission, tread carefully (4)

Definition: Begging your permission · double definition

  • Begging your permission → PACE the bookish word used before disagreeing with someone, by their leave
  • tread carefully → PACE the plain English verb for walking in measured, deliberate steps

Two different words share one spelling — one straight out of Latin, one plain English — which is how the clue defines it twice without repeating itself.

28 across INNOCENT

Local, having no cash, in the clear (8)

Definition: in the clear · charade

  • Local → INN your local is the pub, an inn
  • no → O O is the round nothing, as written on a scoresheet
  • cash → CENT a cent is the smallest coin of American cash

Read the back half as a sum of money — nought cents, not a bean — and the penniless regular of the surface stops getting in the way.

Down

1 down MAKE SURE

Brand accepts plan backing guarantee (4,4)

Definition: guarantee · container + reversal

  • Brand → MAKE a car's brand is its make
  • plan backing → ESUR a crafty plan is a RUSE, written back to front

The surface is a company standing behind a scheme. The wordplay needs 'brand' as a noun, and that noun then splits open before its last letter to let the ruse in.

2 down WISP

Start to watch broadband supplier’s strand (4)

Definition: strand · charade + first letter

  • Start to watch → W the first letter of watch
  • broadband supplier → ISP an internet service provider is the firm that sells you broadband

ISP is worth banking: setters reach for it whenever broadband, the web or a connection turns up, and three of the four letters arrive in one go.

3 down MUSTER

Subsection of secret summons turned around rally (6)

Definition: rally · hidden word + reversal

  • secret summons → MUSTER secRET SUMmons carries RETSUM, which reads the other way as the answer

A hidden run almost always straddles a word boundary, because your eye reads whole words and skips the join — and here it is buried backwards as well.

4 down CLAMOUR

One who keeps quiet before the Guardian’s outcry (7)

Definition: outcry · charade

  • One who keeps quiet → CLAM someone who clams up gives nothing away
  • the Guardian’s → OUR the paper printing this puzzle calls what belongs to it ours

A paper naming itself in its own crossword is a Guardian regular — read the italicised title as the word the paper would use for its own property.

5 down ASHE

A female or male tennis icon? (4)

Definition: male tennis icon · charade

  • A → A the article is taken as it stands
  • female → SHE 'female' is a common stand-in for the three-letter subject pronoun

The joke is the sex change: two words for a woman spell a man. Arthur Ashe won Wimbledon in 1975 and has New York's main court named after him.

6 down PARLIAMENT

Chamber’s diet for those chosen (10)

Definition: Chamber · double definition

  • Chamber → PARLIAMENT the debating chamber where a country's laws are argued
  • diet for those chosen → PARLIAMENT a diet is a formal assembly — Japan still calls its own one — and its members are the elected

The trap is 'diet' as food. In politics a diet is an assembly, so both halves of the clue are describing the same institution from different angles.

7 down SEANCE

Late meeting of party in bishop’s office (6)

Definition: Late meeting · container

  • bishop’s office → SEE a bishop's office and district is his see
  • party → ANC the African National Congress, South Africa's governing party

'Late' is the obituary sense — the late Mr Jones — so this is a meeting with the dead, not one that runs past six o'clock.

13 down DERAILLEUR

Thwart fickle rule in gear shift? (10)

Definition: gear shift · charade + anagram

  • Thwart → DERAIL to derail a plan is to thwart it
  • fickle rule → LEUR the four letters of RULE, shuffled

The bicycle sense is the surprise: a derailleur is the arm that lifts the chain from one sprocket to the next, borrowed whole from French.

15 down GAM

Sport cut short leg (3)

Definition: leg · deletion

  • Sport → GAM a sport is a game, docked of its last letter

A gam is also a school of whales, so this one is quietly part of the grid's collection as well as being music-hall slang.

17 down POD

Hope to God every third letter appears in books made to order (3)

Definition: books made to order · regular letters

  • Hope to God → POD HOPETOGOD falls into HOP, ETO, GOD, and the third letter of each group is what is kept

Print on demand is the publishing trade's own abbreviation: a title is printed only once somebody has bought a copy, so no stock sits in a warehouse.

19 down SPRUCING

Smartening up University College in April and May (8)

Definition: Smartening up · container

  • April and May → SPRING both months fall in the same season
  • University → U U is the usual abbreviation, as in a degree from U of London
  • College → C C abbreviates college in the names of institutions

The capital letters make University College look like one place in London, which is the misdirection: the clue wants an initial from each.

20 down UNCIVIL

Offensive in the armed forces? (7)

Definition: Offensive · double definition

  • Offensive → UNCIVIL the everyday sense — rude, discourteous, giving offence
  • in the armed forces? → UNCIVIL a soldier is not a civilian and not in the civil service, so not civil

Everything hangs on which 'civil' you hear: polite in one half, civilian in the other. The question mark is the setter owning up to the second one.

22 down THORNY

Some leaderless after god proving hard to please (6)

Definition: hard to please · deletion + charade

  • god → THOR Thor, the Norse god of thunder
  • Some leaderless → NY some is any, which loses the A at its head

Norse gods are crossword furniture — THOR, ODIN, LOKI all fit tight spaces. Here the god's name is laid down first even though the clue mentions him second.

23 down SKEINS

Ask Einstein to include networks (6)

Definition: networks · hidden word

  • Ask Einstein → SKEINS aSK EINStein runs the letters straight through the gap between the words

A famous name is where setters like to bury things: you read Einstein as a person and never as six letters carried over from the word before.

25 down COSH

Whack the top guy and stay mum (4)

Definition: Whack · charade

  • the top guy → CO the commanding officer is the man in charge of a unit
  • stay mum → SH SH is the noise you make telling someone to keep quiet

CO for commanding officer is worth banking: it turns up whenever a clue mentions a boss, an officer or whoever is in charge.

26 down PACK

Wild Bunch get ready to hit the road (4)

Definition: Wild Bunch · double definition

  • Wild Bunch → PACK wolves and hounds run in one
  • get ready to hit the road → PACK filling a case is what you do before a journey

The capitals point at the western film, which is the misdirection; the wolves' word is also one of the collective nouns this grid keeps turning up.
