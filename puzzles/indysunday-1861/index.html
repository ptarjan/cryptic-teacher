Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,861

Across

8 across URBANELY

Bar in old city, one with a cathedral, charmingly (8)

Definition: charmingly · container + charade

  • old city → UR Ur was the great Sumerian city, and crosswords reach for it whenever an ancient one is wanted
  • Bar → BAN to bar something is to ban it
  • one with a cathedral → ELY Ely is a small Cambridgeshire city whose huge cathedral is known as the Ship of the Fens

The comma makes it look as though one place is being described twice over. They are two different places, ancient and English, and the bar drops into the seam between them.

9 across ALARM

Fear any injury except for left hand (5)

Definition: Fear · deletion

  • any → ALL in a phrase like 'without any damage', all does the same job as any
  • injury → HARM harm is the plain word for injury done to somebody
  • left hand → LH LH is the abbreviation a piano score uses for the left hand, and it is these two letters that go

Both letters come out of the middle of one seven-letter phrase. 'Left hand' looks like a body part or an instruction about which end to cut from, and it is neither.

10 across TWIN

Land on time just the same (4)

Definition: just the same · charade

  • time → T T is the physicist's abbreviation for time
  • Land → WIN to land a contract is to win it

'Land on time' is an aeroplane arriving punctually, and neither word is used that way. The definition is an adjective here, not the adverb it looks like.

11 across INHALATION

Island people taking mostly healthy breath (10)

Definition: breath · charade + container + deletion

  • Island → I I is the abbreviation for island on maps and charts
  • people → NATION a people held together by one country is a nation
  • mostly healthy → HAL hale means healthy — hale and hearty — and most of it stops short of the E

'Island people' looks like one phrase naming islanders and is really two separate pieces. The definition is the last word, doing no work at all in the surface picture.

12 across IBIDEM

One's bored by wait in the same place (6)

Definition: in the same place · container

  • One's → IM said of yourself, one's becomes I'm, and the apostrophe drops out
  • wait → BIDE to bide your time is to wait

'One's bored' sounds like a complaint about tedium; bored here is drilled through. The answer is the full Latin behind the footnote abbreviation ibid.

14 across LOOK UP TO

Admire search for explanation of atom's nucleus (4,2,2)

Definition: Admire · charade + middle letters

  • search for explanation of → LOOK UP when you want a word explained you go and look it up
  • atom's → TO the two letters at the middle of the word, with one letter either side of them

The surface is a physicist explaining what is inside an atom. The clue wants the middle of the printed word instead, which is a favourite trick with any short noun.

15 across TERCETS

Sets of triplets caught in street playing (7)

Definition: Sets of triplets · anagram + container

  • street → TERETS the six letters of the word resettled into a new order
  • caught → C C is how a cricket scorecard records a batter who was caught out

'Sets of triplets' pulls you towards prams and babies, which is what the street surface wants. The answer belongs to poetry: terza rima is built out of them.

17 across ADMIRAL

Sailor bringing Russian crate across border from the east (7)

Definition: Sailor · container + reversal

  • Russian crate → LADA a crate is a clapped-out old car, and the Lada is the Soviet-era Russian one
  • border → RIM the rim of a thing is its border

'Sailor' at the front is the definition, even though the surface makes him the subject of the sentence. Everything after it is machinery, and the lot is read right to left.

20 across CURATIVE

Healing evolved via truce (8)

Definition: Healing · anagram

  • via truce → VIATRUCE eight letters of fodder, and the two little words supply every one of them

Two short words hand over the whole grid entry, so the only instruction left is the one in the middle. The surface pretends the truce was what did the healing.

22 across DYNAMO

Too many dogs around keeping one constantly busy (6)

Definition: one constantly busy · hidden word + reversal

  • Too many dogs → DYNAMO six consecutive letters sit in this phrase, running backwards and straddling the gaps between the words

Every letter is sitting in the first three words, which is why no synonym is needed for any of them. The excess of dogs is scenery, and the run is printed the wrong way round.

23 across AIR HOSTESS

Old-fashioned steward from Rio hates jumping ship (3,7)

Definition: Old-fashioned steward · anagram

  • Rio hates → RIOHATES eight letters of fodder — count them against the (3,7) before you start
  • ship → SS SS is the prefix on a steamship's name, as in SS Great Britain

The surface is a fed-up steward deserting in Brazil, and none of it survives except the letters. 'Jumping ship' is split down the middle: one word shuffles, the other pays two letters.

24 across YEGG

US safe-cracker, Yankee with something to crack? (4)

Definition: US safe-cracker · charade

  • Yankee → Y Yankee is the international radio alphabet's word for Y
  • something to crack → EGG the thing you crack on the side of the bowl

'Crack' does two jobs at once, the safe in the definition and the breakfast in the wordplay, and the question mark is there to own up to the second one.

25 across ENNUI

Bad feeling having skinned bunnies for cooking (5)

Definition: Bad feeling · deletion + anagram

  • bunnies → UNNIE take the B off the front and the S off the back and five letters are left to work with

Skinned bunnies for the pot is a grim little picture, and a picture is all it is: what gets skinned is the word, and what gets cooked is the letters left over.

26 across SHANGHAI

City's quiet hotel filling again unexpectedly (8)

Definition: City · charade + anagram + container

  • quiet → SH sh is the noise you make when you are asking for quiet
  • again → ANGAI the five letters of the word settled into a different order
  • hotel → H Hotel is the international radio alphabet's word for H

The surface is a hotel filling up unexpectedly, and every word of it is quietly doing something else. The place also gave English a verb, for pressing a man into a ship's crew.

Down

1 down DRAWABLE

Inexperienced sailor shown moving back to front, possibly pulled (8)

Definition: possibly pulled · charade + cycling

  • Inexperienced → RAW a raw recruit is one with no experience behind him
  • sailor → AB AB is an able seaman, the Royal Navy's rating for a trained deckhand
  • shown → LED if you showed a guest to their table you led them to it

The surface pictures a green sailor marched up to the head of the parade. The instruction is aimed at the letters instead, and the D that ends the string goes round to start it.

2 down EARN

Win point with superior attention (4)

Definition: Win · charade

  • attention → EAR lend me your ear and you are lending me your attention
  • point → N N is north, one of the four points of the compass

'Point' is a compass bearing rather than a score or a full stop, and the clue names the small piece before the large one — which the position word quietly puts right.

3 down TEDIUM

Half-way through Spielberg film keeping you up, cause of 25? (6)

Definition: cause of 25? · container + charade + reversal

  • Half-way through → MID mid is the half-way point of anything — midsummer, midfield, midweek
  • you → U a single U is how a text message spells the word, and crosswords borrow the shorthand
  • Spielberg film → ET Spielberg's 1982 film about a stranded alien has a two-letter title

'Keeping you up' reads as a film so gripping you sat through the night. It is two instructions: swallow a letter, then read the whole assembly back up the column.

4 down EYEHOLE

Orbit I complete when told (7)

Definition: Orbit · charade + homophone

  • I → EYE the pronoun and the organ you see with are said exactly the same way
  • complete → HOLE something complete is whole, and the W of whole is silent, so the ear cannot tell it from the gap

'Orbit' looks astronomical and is anatomical: the bony socket an eyeball sits in. Say the other two words aloud and the spelling you want falls out.

5 down RAILROAD

American tracks rising lawyer, or 'user of puzzle's perimeter' (8)

Definition: American tracks · charade + reversal

  • lawyer → DA the DA is America's district attorney, the lawyer who prosecutes on the state's behalf
  • or → OR the little word is taken at face value and hands over its two letters
  • 'user of puzzle's perimeter' → LIAR the letters round the edge of this grid spell TERMINOLOGICAL INEXACTITUDE, Churchill's parliamentary euphemism for a lie

Filbert has hidden a message round the outside of the grid, and the quoted phrase points at that rather than at any dictionary sense. The small word 'or' is doing letter duty, not joining.

6 down MALTHUSIAN

Mother fifty, so Scot proposing birth control (10)

Definition: proposing birth control · charade

  • Mother → MA ma is the nursery word for a mother
  • fifty → L L is the Roman numeral for fifty
  • so → THUS thus and so both mean in that way
  • Scot → IAN Ian is the Scottish form of John, and crosswords use a typical first name to stand for a nationality

The surface offers a mother of fifty and a Scotsman, and neither survives the parse. Thomas Malthus argued in 1798 that population always outruns the food supply.

7 down IMPOST

Money due to HMRC, money Post Office's IT has pinched (6)

Definition: Money due to HMRC · container + charade

  • IT → IT the clue's own capitals are information technology, the department a Post Office is famous for
  • money → M M is the single letter money takes in figures, as in a salary quoted at £2m
  • Post Office's → POS PO is the Post Office on any high-street sign, and the possessive adds its S

The surface leans on the Post Office computer scandal, where money went missing; the clue wants the other sense of pinched, and 'money' is used once on each side of the comma.

13 down DICTAPHONE

Detectives turned up bug to sharpen recording device (10)

Definition: recording device · charade + reversal

  • Detectives → DIC CID is Britain's Criminal Investigation Department, the plain-clothes detective branch, stood on its head
  • bug → TAP to tap a phone is to bug it
  • to sharpen → HONE you hone a blade to sharpen it

Read straight, the surface is a police search that turns up a listening device — which is exactly what the wordplay is built from, three pieces borrowed out of the same scene.

16 down TAILSPIN

Dogs bite back in confusion (8)

Definition: confusion · charade + reversal

  • Dogs → TAILS a detective who dogs a suspect tails them
  • bite → PIN a bite is a nip, and this is that nip written the other way round

Read as a sentence about animals, 'Dogs bite' is a warning notice. Both words are doing quieter jobs here, and the whole surface picture is discarded.

18 down ARMAGNAC

Extract from prewar Magna Carta is something Gascon still produces (8)

Definition: something Gascon still produces · hidden word

  • prewar Magna Carta → ARMAGNAC eight letters run on without a break across the middle of these three words, ignoring where each one ends

The surface asks you to pull a quotation out of an old document, which is the instruction — just not aimed at the words it pretends to be. Gascony is the corner of France inland from Bordeaux.

19 down REVERSI

Flipping board game always limited by wrist pain? (7)

Definition: Flipping board game · container

  • wrist pain → RSI RSI is repetitive strain injury, the ache typists get from years at a keyboard
  • always → EVER ever is the older word for always — for ever and ever

'Flipping' looks like an instruction to turn something round and belongs to the definition instead: the game is played by flipping discs from one colour to the other.

21 down UNISEX

Academic outfits no longer meant to suit everyone (6)

Definition: meant to suit everyone · charade

  • Academic outfits → UNIS an outfit is an organisation, and universities are shortened to unis
  • no longer → EX an ex-anything is what it no longer is

'Academic outfits' pulls hard towards gowns and mortar boards, which is the trap. An outfit here is a firm or an institution, and the two chunks simply sit end to end.

22 down DISMAL

Sergeant major cuts key in dark (6)

Definition: dark · container

  • key in → DIAL to key in a phone number is to dial it
  • Sergeant major → SM SM is the army's abbreviation for a sergeant major

The surface offers a locksmith cutting a key. 'Key in' is really the verb you do to a telephone, and the definition is the single word at the end.

24 down YOGA

Spanish painter cycles to the top for exercise (4)

Definition: exercise · cycling + reversal

  • Spanish painter → GOYA Francisco Goya painted the Spanish court and, later, the horrors of its war

Two instructions land on one short name: the last letter goes round to the front, and what is left is then read up the column rather than down it.
