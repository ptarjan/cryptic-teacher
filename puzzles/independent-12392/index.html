Across

1 across BOWER

Hat Liberal discarded in recess (5)

Definition: recess · deletion

A bower is a leafy alcove or arbour in a garden, which is a recess in the sense of a sheltered hollow.

  • Hat → BOWLER The bowler is the hard round hat of City clerks and Charlie Chaplin.
  • Liberal → L L is the letter British ballot papers and crosswords use for Liberal.
  • discarded — Something discarded is thrown out and gone for good, so the letter it labels leaves the hat's name behind.

A hat a Liberal has thrown away, lying in an alcove.

'Recess' offers itself as a break in the working day, and the sense wanted is the architectural one — a shaded nook you can stand in.

4 across VITICIDES

Problems for wine-makers: six detectives probing links (9)

Definition: Problems for wine-makers · charade + container

Viticides are vine-killers — the diseases and chemicals that destroy grapevines — which is precisely what troubles anyone making wine.

  • six → VI Six in Roman numerals.
  • links → TIES Links between people are ties.
  • detectives → CID The CID is the Criminal Investigation Department, the plain-clothes detective branch of a British police force.
  • probing — A probe pushes into whatever it examines, so the piece doing the probing ends up inside the other.

Vineyard owners are in trouble as six detectives look into their connections.

Latin vitis is the grapevine and -cide is the killing suffix of pesticide and homicide, so the answer is a vine and its killer stuck together.

9 across MINIMALISTS

Top celebrities enthralled by notes from some contemporary composers … (11)

Definition: some contemporary composers · container

Minimalists are the composers — Glass, Reich, Adams — who build pieces from short repeating cells, so the school is defined by its members.

  • notes → MINIMS A minim is the half-note of written music, and the plural is what the clue asks for.
  • Top celebrities → ALIST The A-list is where the biggest names sit.
  • enthralled by — To be enthralled is to be held captive by something, so the celebrities are the ones shut inside.

The biggest names are captivated by the notes some of today's composers are writing.

'Notes' has half a dozen crossword senses — banknotes, memos, the sol-fa syllables — and the one wanted is the written half-note of a score.

10 across NOR

… and not enthralled by countertenors (3)

Definition: and not · hidden word

Nor is the conjunction meaning 'and not', the negative partner of 'or' in neither/nor.

  • countertenors → NOR Three consecutive letters towards the end of the singers' name.
  • enthralled by — Held captive by the word that follows, the letters are already in order and only need picking out.

Countertenors are not captivated either.

The ellipsis carries the sentence on from 9 across, and 'enthralled by' is reused for the same job in both clues.

11 across HOSEPIPE

It may dampen expectation about cooked pies (8)

Definition: It may dampen · container + anagram

A hosepipe is the garden tube that carries water under pressure, so whatever it is pointed at gets wet.

  • expectation → HOPE Expectation of something good is hope.
  • pies → PIES anagram fodder, four letters
  • about — Something placed about another thing goes round the outside of it.
  • cooked — Pies come out of the oven changed from what went in, so their letters do not keep their raw order.

Baked pies fall short of the expectation built up around them.

'It may dampen' works as a definition only as a whole phrase: the answer is the thing that does the dampening, not the dampening itself.

13 across POTEEN

Youngster pursuing work recalled making illegal booze (6)

Definition: illegal booze · charade + reversal

Poteen is Irish moonshine, distilled at home from barley or potatoes and illegal precisely because no duty is paid on it.

  • work recalled → PO Work is OP, the composer's opus, sent back on itself.
  • Youngster → TEEN A youngster of that age is a teen.
  • pursuing — Anything in pursuit comes along behind, so the youngster's letters follow rather than lead.
  • recalled — Recalled goods are sent straight back the way they came, and so are these two letters.

A youngster trails along behind a job that has been called back.

OP for 'work' is the composer's opus, printed on every concert programme, and it is one of the abbreviations that repays learning once.

15 across SERVITUDE

Bondage TV series not entirely scrapped, receiving backing of the French (9)

Definition: Bondage · anagram + deletion + container + reversal

Servitude is the condition of being bound to serve another — slavery, forced labour — which is what bondage means at its plainest.

  • TV series not entirely → TVSERIE TV series offers eight letters and the last of them is dropped, leaving seven to be shuffled.
  • of the French → UD The French for 'of the' is du, here written backwards.
  • not entirely — Taken not entirely, the phrase comes up short and its last letter is left behind.
  • scrapped — Scrapped machinery is broken up for parts, and letters scrapped are broken out of their order.
  • receiving — Whatever receives something takes it in, so the short piece lands inside the long one.
  • backing — Backing means moving backwards, so the French word is written the other way round.

A television series about bondage is partly scrapped, with French money coming in behind it.

'Bondage' opens the clue as though it were the subject of the programme, which is exactly what a definition standing first can get away with.

16 across OCTAL

Sources in Oxford and Cambridge chat endlessly regarding eights (5)

Definition: regarding eights · first letters + charade + deletion

Octal is the base-eight number system, so anything octal is by definition a matter of eights.

  • Oxford and Cambridge → OC The opening letters of the two university cities.
  • chat → TALK To chat is to talk.
  • Sources in — A source is where a thing starts, so only the letters the words start with are taken.
  • endlessly — Chat that runs on endlessly never gets to its end, so the closing letter is missing.

Insiders at the two old universities chat on and on about the Eights, the college rowing races.

The Eights of the surface are the bumping races Oxford and Cambridge crews row each summer; the eights of the definition are a counting base.

17 across ENEMA

Blokes in A&E returned for medical intervention (5)

Definition: medical intervention · container + reversal

An enema is fluid introduced into the bowel to clear it, a procedure carried out on a patient — hence a medical intervention.

  • A&E → AE A&E is the accident and emergency department of a British hospital, and the ampersand is not a letter.
  • Blokes → MEN Blokes are men.
  • in — Blokes in a place are inside it, so their letters sit within the other piece.
  • returned — An across answer that returns runs back the way it came, right to left along the row.

The men are back down at casualty again.

A&E is what Britain calls the casualty department, so the surface reads as a night shift and gives away nothing about the treatment.

18 across ROOMINESS

Aussie creatures surrounding excavations? Lots of space (9)

Definition: Lots of space · container

Roominess is the quality of having plenty of room, so 'lots of space' is the same noun idea in plainer words.

  • Aussie creatures → ROOS Roos is the Australian short form of kangaroos.
  • excavations → MINES Excavations dug for coal or gold are mines.
  • surrounding — What surrounds a thing closes round it on both sides, leaving the diggings in the middle.

Kangaroos standing round a set of diggings in the outback.

Nothing in the picture is about space at all — it is pure outback, and the definition is the three words tacked on at the end.

19 across COWBOY

Rancher wary, suppressing expression of pain in front of buckaroo, initially (6)

Definition: Rancher · container + charade + first letter

A cowboy is the hand who works cattle on a ranch, which is what 'rancher' names in loose everyday use.

  • wary → COY Someone wary of attention is coy.
  • expression of pain → OW Ow is the noise you make when something hurts.
  • buckaroo, initially → B The opening letter of buckaroo.
  • suppressing — To suppress a cry is to hold it down inside you, which buries those letters in the middle.
  • in front of — One piece stands ahead of the other, which settles the order the two go in.
  • initially — Only the letter the word begins with is wanted.

A cautious rancher swallows a yelp of pain in front of a young bronco-rider.

A buckaroo is a cowpuncher himself, so the surface's rider and the definition are the same man — and the word is there for one letter only.

20 across DIRTY DOG

Yard probing juicy gossip over Government scoundrel (5,3)

Definition: scoundrel · container + charade

A dirty dog is a contemptible, underhand person — the old-fashioned phrase you would use of a scoundrel or a cad.

  • juicy gossip → DIRT Dirt is the scandal people dig up on each other.
  • Yard → YD Yd is the written abbreviation for the imperial yard.
  • over → O O is the cricket scorer's abbreviation for an over.
  • Government → G G is the standard abbreviation for Government.
  • probing — A probe pushes into what it is examining, so these letters go inside the phrase the others build.

Scotland Yard digs into juicy rumours about the Government.

'Yard' means Scotland Yard in the surface and the imperial measure in the wordplay, and the insertion lands exactly on the join between the answer's two words.

23 across IMP

Independent politician, a source of funny business? (3)

Definition: a source of funny business · charade

An imp is a small mischievous demon, so it is a source of funny business in the sense of pranks rather than fraud.

  • Independent → I I is how Independent is marked against a candidate's name.
  • politician → MP An MP is a Member of Parliament.

An MP who sits for no party turns out to be behind some mischief.

The Independent is the paper the puzzle appears in and an independent MP sits for no party, so both readings are live and only one letter is used.

24 across NETHERLANDS

Country swamping the river in neon lights (11)

Definition: Country · container + charade

The Netherlands is the low-lying country on the North Sea coast, which many people know instead by the name of two of its provinces, Holland.

  • neon → NE Ne is the chemical symbol for neon.
  • the river → THER The word 'the' followed by R, the map-maker's abbreviation for river.
  • lights → LANDS A bird lights on a branch when it lands.
  • swamping — To swamp something is to flood right over it, leaving it submerged in the middle.

A country drowning its river under neon signs.

'Lights' is a verb here, not a noun — a bird lights on a branch — and the phrase 'neon lights' is what the whole misdirection hangs on.

26 across YORKSHIRE

Factory at first trimmed wages after year in part of Northern England (9)

Definition: part of Northern England · deletion + charade

Yorkshire is the largest of the historic counties of northern England, so it is one part of that region rather than the whole of it.

  • year → Y Y is the standard abbreviation for year.
  • Factory → WORKS A factory is a works, as in the steelworks up the road.
  • wages → HIRE Hire is payment for labour, the sense alive in 'the labourer is worthy of his hire'.
  • at first trimmed — Trimmed at its front, the word loses the letter it begins with.
  • after — What follows something goes behind it, so the year is at the head of the assembly.

A factory has cut its wages after a year, somewhere up north.

'At first' looks like a time word carried over from the surface's bad year, and it is really the pointer to which end of the factory gets trimmed.

27 across WEEDY

Went quietly, finally exhibiting little strength (5)

Definition: exhibiting little strength · charade + last letter

A weedy person is thin and feeble, like a plant grown spindly in the shade, so they exhibit little strength.

  • Went → WEED To wee is British nursery language for urinating, and 'went' is that verb in the past tense.
  • quietly, finally → Y The closing letter of quietly.
  • finally — The letter wanted is the one the word finishes on, and nothing before it.

Someone slipped away quietly at the end, showing no great strength of will.

'Went' is the nursery euphemism — went to the loo — which is a long way from the quiet departure the surface offers.

1 down BUMPH

Dreadful pub documentation (5)

Definition: documentation · charade

Bumph is the useless printed matter that arrives in bulk — forms, leaflets, circulars — so 'documentation' is its polite name.

  • Dreadful → BUM A bum deal or a bum note is a dreadful one.
  • pub → PH The map symbol for a public house.

The paperwork handed out by a truly dreadful pub.

PH marks a public house on an Ordnance Survey map, a convention British walkers know from the map key and crosswords borrow constantly.

2 down WINDSCREEN WIPER

Twist unsettling re: new prices for car accessory (10,5)

Definition: car accessory · charade + anagram

A windscreen wiper is the blade bolted to a car to sweep rain off the glass — a fitting rather than part of the machine.

  • Twist → WIND To wind is to twist, as a lane winds through hills.
  • re: new prices → RENEWPRICES anagram fodder, 11 letters — the colon counts for nothing
  • unsettling — To unsettle something is to knock it out of its settled arrangement, so these letters will not stay in the order given.

A memo headed 're: new prices' takes an unsettling twist.

'Twist' sits at the front where a definition usually sits, and the colon does its best to make 're:' look like a memo heading rather than fodder.

3 down RIM

Border in order, though not at first (3)

Definition: Border · deletion

A rim is the border running round the edge of a wheel, a cup or a crater.

  • in order → TRIM A ship in good order is in trim, and something tidy is trim.
  • though → Surface only — the concession holds the sentence up and contributes no letters.
  • not at first — What is missing is specifically whatever stood at the front, so the word turns up without its opening letter.

An edging that was eventually put in order, though not from the outset.

'Border in order' reads as one phrase about a tidy edge; the split falls after the first word, and everything following it is instruction.

4 down VOLUPTUARY

Hedonist elevated endless desire, elevated attempt to seize gold, on reflection (10)

Definition: Hedonist · charade + reversal + deletion + container

A voluptuary is someone given over to luxury and sensual pleasure, which is a hedonist under a grander name.

  • endless desire → VOL Desire is LOVE; with no end it is LOV, and it is then stood on its head.
  • elevated → UP The second 'elevated' is not an instruction at all, just the plain word for it.
  • attempt → TRY An attempt at something is a try.
  • gold → UA Gold is AU, the chemical symbol, arriving in mirror image.
  • elevated — Lifted up a down entry, letters read from the bottom of the grid upwards, so the word arrives back to front.
  • endless — A desire with no end is one letter shorter, losing the one it finishes on.
  • seize — To seize something is to close your hands round it, which leaves what is seized in the middle.
  • on reflection — Seen in a mirror the two letters swap places, so the metal's symbol arrives the other way round.

A pleasure-seeker, high on desire, makes a lofty grab for gold.

'Elevated' turns up twice and does two different jobs: once as the instruction to turn letters upwards, once as the word UP itself.

5 down TOSS

Cast will turn up in Cats, so there! (4)

Definition: Cast · hidden word + reversal

To cast a line or a net is to toss it, the same verb for throwing something with a flick of the wrist.

  • Cats, so there! → SSOT Four consecutive letters straddle the comma here; read up the entry they come out the other way round.
  • will turn up — Turning up a down entry sends the letters back the way they came, from the foot of the answer to its top.
  • in — The letters are already sitting inside the words that follow rather than waiting to be built from them.

Someone insists the company will show up in the musical Cats.

'Cats' is italicised because it is the musical, so 'cast will turn up' reads as theatre gossip about the company arriving.

6 down COSMOLOGIST

One considering the Universe dispersing stoic’s gloom (11)

Definition: One considering the Universe · anagram

A cosmologist studies the origin and structure of the universe as a whole, so the phrase is the job description.

  • stoic’s gloom → STOICSGLOOM anagram fodder, 11 letters — the apostrophe counts for nothing
  • dispersing — Gloom that disperses breaks up and drifts apart, and letters told to disperse scatter out of their order.

Thinking about the universe drives away a stoic's gloom.

'Dispersing' sits between definition and fodder and could belong to either — a universe that disperses is a real thing to consider, which is the trap.

7 down DANCE ATTENDANCE

Wait at a late point of the evening, being between turns on the floor? (5,10)

Definition: Wait · charade + container

To dance attendance on someone is to wait on them and run to their every want, which is 'wait' in its old transitive sense.

  • turns on the floor → DANCE A turn on the floor is a dance, and the plural asks for two of them.
  • at → AT The word itself, taken at face value.
  • a late point of the evening → TEN Ten o'clock is late in the evening, and the hour goes in as a word.
  • turns on the floor → DANCE The second of the pair, closing the sandwich.
  • being between — Whatever is between two things has one of them on each side, so the floor work is needed twice, once either side.

Hanging about late in the evening, stuck between two spins round the floor.

'Wait' is the servant's sense — waiting on someone, attending them — and not the standing-about that the rest of the sentence pretends it means.

8 down SPRINKLES

Small place initially without facilities for skating? Not quite - touches here and there (9)

Definition: touches here and there · first letters + charade + deletion

Sprinkles are light scattered touches — of rain, of sugar — landing here and there instead of covering evenly.

  • Small place initially → SP The opening letters of the first two words.
  • without facilities for skating → RINKLESS A place with no rinks is rinkless, a rink being the sheet of ice skaters use.
  • initially — Only the letters the words begin with are wanted, and nothing that follows them.
  • Not quite — Something not quite complete stops just short, so the final letter never arrives.

A little town that looks at first to have nowhere at all to skate.

'Without' is not the container instruction it looks like but the ordinary sense of lacking, and the coined word it produces is most of the answer.

12 down PRIMA DONNAS

Publicity group is taking care of pop singer and other divas (5,6)

Definition: other divas · container + charade

A prima donna is the leading female singer in an opera and, by extension, any temperamental star — which is what diva means too.

  • Publicity group → PR PR is the public relations outfit that handles publicity.
  • is → IS The word itself, taken at face value.
  • pop singer → MADONNA The Queen of Pop, still the first name most people reach for.
  • taking care of — Taking care of someone means keeping them inside your charge, which is where the singer's letters end up.

A publicity team is looking after a pop star and the other divas on the bill.

'Pop singer' belongs to the wordplay and 'other divas' to the definition, so the answer is everyone in the dressing room except the one actually named.

14 down VELOCIPEDE

With Charlie and I involved, excitedly develop European road transport? (10)

Definition: road transport · anagram

A velocipede is the early pedal cycle that ran on roads, so the definition names one example of road transport rather than the class.

  • Charlie → C Charlie is C in the NATO alphabet.
  • I → I The letter the speaker stands for.
  • develop → DEVELOP anagram fodder, seven letters
  • European → E E is the standard abbreviation for European.
  • involved — Letters involved in something are mixed into it, so these two join the pool instead of standing apart.
  • excitedly — Doing something excitedly means doing it in a rush and out of order, which is what becomes of the letters.

With Charlie and me caught up in it, a European road scheme develops fast.

'Involved' looks like part of the story about Charlie and me, and it is really the instruction that pulls two stray letters into the mix.

15 down SPEECHIFY

Deliver sermon: quick to dismiss God, capturing Church uncertainty (9)

Definition: Deliver sermon · deletion + container + charade

To speechify is to hold forth at length and self-importantly, which is what delivering a sermon amounts to outside church.

  • quick → SPEEDY Quick means speedy.
  • God → D D stands for the Latin Deus, the abbreviation behind Dei gratia on British coins.
  • Church → CH CH is how Church is shortened on maps and signposts.
  • uncertainty → IF An if is a doubt — no ifs, no buts.
  • to dismiss — Dismissing someone sends them away, so the letter standing for the deity leaves the word entirely.
  • capturing — A captor keeps what it takes shut inside, so both short pieces are held in the middle.

A hasty preacher drops God from the service while the Church wrestles with doubt.

'Quick to dismiss God' reads as one phrase about a hasty unbeliever, and the wordplay needs it broken in two before anything works.

21 down GUSTY

Chap clutching stone, feeling effects of gale (5)

Definition: feeling effects of gale · container

Gusty weather arrives in sudden hard blasts, so anywhere feeling the effects of a gale is gusty.

  • Chap → GUY A chap is a guy.
  • stone → ST St is the abbreviation for the stone, the British fourteen-pound unit used for body weight.
  • clutching — To clutch something is to grip it in your fist, closing right round it.

A man hangs on to a rock while the wind does its worst.

'Feeling effects of gale' is a whole-phrase definition — no single word in it is a synonym, which is why it reads as more of the picture.

22 down ETUI

Case ends in outcome against you and I (4)

Definition: Case · last letters

An etui is a small ornamental case for needles, scissors and other little tools, so 'Case' is its plain sense.

  • outcome → E The last letter of the word.
  • against → T The last letter of the word.
  • you and I → UI The last letters of the two pronouns; a one-letter word is its own ending.
  • ends in — The end of each word is where its letter comes from, taken in the order the words appear.

The outcome of a lawsuit has gone against the two of us.

An etui is a small stitched box for needles and scissors, a Victorian lady's accessory that survives almost entirely inside crosswords.

25 down LOW

Water movement lacking force, lacking strength (3)

Definition: lacking strength · deletion

Someone low is weak or run down — low on energy, feeling low — rather than short in height.

  • Water movement → FLOW Water on the move is a flow.
  • force → F F is the physicist's symbol for force, and it is the letter that goes.
  • lacking force — A word that lacks something has it taken out, and what goes is the single letter physics uses for force.

A current with no push behind it and no strength left in it.

'Lacking' does two different jobs in one short sentence: an instruction the first time, part of the definition the second.
