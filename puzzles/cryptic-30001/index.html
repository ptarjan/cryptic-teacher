Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,001

Across

8 across GOAL AREA

Indian resort put on a real spread for poacher’s hunting ground? (4,4)

Definition: poacher’s hunting ground? · charade + anagram

In football a poacher is a striker who feeds on scraps close in, and his hunting ground is the marked rectangle in front of the keeper.

  • Indian resort → GOA the beach state on India's west coast, a byword for a holiday
  • a real → AREAL anagram fodder, five letters
  • put on — 'Put on' stacks one piece on top of the next, which is how a charade announces the order of its parts.
  • spread — A spread lays things out anyhow across a table, so the letters beside it end up in a fresh arrangement.

An Indian holiday spot lays on a proper feast for a man who shoots game illegally.

A poacher has two lives here — the countryside thief of the surface, and the striker who scores from a yard out — and only the second one takes up the enumeration's two words.

9 across HEATH

Scrub part of moustache at hairdresser (5)

Definition: Scrub · hidden word

'Scrub' is rough, low, uncultivated growth, and the answer names open sandy waste covered in exactly that — gorse, ling and little else.

  • moustache at hairdresser → HEATH the run starts inside 'moustache' and finishes one letter into 'hairdresser', crossing both gaps
  • part of — 'Part of' promises you a piece of the words beside it, and the piece wanted is an unbroken run of their letters.

Somebody at the barber's is having part of his moustache trimmed off.

'Scrub' looks like a verb — cancel it, wipe it out — and it is a noun, rough open country. Edward Heath was prime minister from 1970 to 1974.

10 across BALD

Left in spa, smooth as a baby’s bottom (4)

Definition: smooth as a baby’s bottom · container

The idiom for skin with nothing on it at all is exactly the state of a hairless head, so the simile stands in for the adjective.

  • spa → BAD Bad is German for bath, which is why German spa towns are called Bad-something
  • Left → L L is the standard abbreviation for left, as on a pair of boots
  • in — 'in' puts the letter named before it inside the word named after it, the simplest insertion there is.

A session at the spa leaves the skin as smooth as a baby's bottom.

Stanley Baldwin, prime minister three separate times, turns up when this answer is set beside 15 down — several of the grid's answers pair off like that.

11 across FUNCTIONAL

Following rubdown with oil – everything not quite working (10)

Definition: working · charade + deletion

'Working' describes a thing that does its job — a machine in order, a design that performs — and the answer is the adjective for that state.

  • Following → F f = following in a reference, as in 'see p. 4f'
  • rubdown with oil → UNCTION unction is the ceremonial anointing with oil, as in extreme unction
  • everything not quite → AL everything is ALL, and stopping short of the end loses the second L
  • not quite — 'Not quite' stops you just short of the whole word, so its final letter never arrives.

After the oil rub, everything is not quite working as it should.

Unction is a church word — the anointing at a coronation or a deathbed — and the surface quietly turns it into a massage parlour.

12 across CLEAVE

Quit following Cameron’s first split (6)

Definition: split · charade + first letter

To split something is to cut it apart along a line, which is the answer's older sense — what a butcher's heavy blade does to a carcass.

  • Cameron’s first → C the opening letter of the surname
  • Quit → LEAVE to quit a job or a room is to leave it
  • following — 'Following' means coming after, so the piece it labels takes its place behind the letter already down.
  • first — 'first' after a possessive asks for the opening letter of the name, not for the name itself.

Somebody walks out in the wake of Cameron's first falling-out.

The answer is its own opposite: it means to split apart and also to cling faithfully to. David Cameron, prime minister from 2010 to 2016, joins the grid's line of them.

14 across NEWSROOM

Owners developed mantra for those who produce history’s first draft (8)

Definition: those who produce history’s first draft · anagram + charade

Journalism is traditionally called history's first rough draft, so the ones producing it are a paper's editorial floor and everybody on it.

  • Owners → OWNERS anagram fodder, six letters
  • mantra → OM om is the syllable chanted in meditation, the mantra of the surface
  • developed — 'Developed' is what happens to a plan that changes shape as it goes, and letters changing shape come out in another order.

The owners of a paper have worked up a new company mantra for the staff.

Proprietors do not write the paper, which is what lets 'Owners developed mantra' read as a boardroom memo instead of as eight letters waiting to be shuffled.

16 across AWESOME

How lovely! Cute! At last, it follows that setter’s wonderful (7)

Definition: wonderful · charade + last letter

'Wonderful' and the answer are interchangeable adjectives of praise; the answer is the modern enthusiastic one, weakened from a sense of inspiring dread.

  • How lovely! → AW the noise people make over something sweet
  • Cute! At last → E the final letter of 'Cute'
  • it follows that → SO 'so' is the word that introduces what follows from what went before
  • setter’s → ME the setter calls himself ME, a standing crossword habit
  • At last — 'At last' takes the letter standing at the end of the word beside it, the one that arrives last of all.

A string of coos over something adorable ends with a compliment to the person who set the puzzle.

Setters refer to themselves as 'me' or 'I' whenever they step into their own clues, and that is the convention to learn here; the exclamation marks do no work at all.

18 across INSTILS

Promotes risk of unprotected sex in one library in Edinburgh (7)

Definition: Promotes · container

To promote an idea is to work it into people gradually, and the answer is the verb for introducing a value drop by drop.

  • one → I one written as the figure 1, which doubles as the letter
  • library in Edinburgh → NLS the National Library of Scotland, the copyright library on Edinburgh's George IV Bridge
  • risk of unprotected sex → STI a sexually transmitted infection, STI on every clinic leaflet
  • in — 'in' places the letters just named inside the ones named next, splitting them open partway.

A public-health warning about unsafe sex is posted in an Edinburgh reading room.

Reading 'library in Edinburgh' as three letters rather than as a building is where the whole clue turns, and the clinic vocabulary keeps you looking somewhere else.

21 across BEHEMOTH

It’s monstrous if that man is preceding one drawn to light (8)

Definition: It’s monstrous · charade

The name comes from the giant beast of the Book of Job, so anything monstrous in sheer size can be given the word.

  • if that man is → BEHE put into the subjunctive, 'if that man is' becomes 'be he', the old form kept alive in 'be he friend or foe'
  • one drawn to light → MOTH the creature that cannot leave a lamp alone
  • preceding — 'Preceding' says the piece just built goes in front of the piece named next.

It is an outrage, apparently, that he should be standing ahead of the creature circling the lamp.

Hearing 'if that man is' as 'be he' takes the subjunctive, a form English has almost lost outside phrases like 'be that as it may'.

23 across RESIZE

Make bigger or smaller resolution? Checks out on the radio (6)

Definition: Make bigger or smaller · charade + homophone

Changing a thing's dimensions in either direction is what the verb does — it is the button you press when a photo is wrong for the page.

  • resolution? → RES res is the standard short form of resolution, as in screen res
  • Checks out → IZE to eye something up is to check it out, and that verb said aloud is written here with a z
  • on the radio — 'On the radio' means you hear the word rather than see it, so its sound matters and its spelling does not.

Someone confirms over the airwaves that a screen resolution checks out.

Resolution is a screen setting, a New Year vow and a formal motion; the clue wants only the photographer's sense, shortened the way a camera menu shortens it.

24 across TOOTHCOMBS

The first hot combos bent picks (10)

Definition: picks · anagram + first letter

A pick is the long-toothed comb used to lift Afro hair, so the plural noun names the same implements from the other end of the trade.

  • The first → T the opening letter of 'The'
  • hot combos → HOTCOMBOS anagram fodder, nine letters
  • first — 'first' takes the opening letter of the word in front of it, here the little article itself.
  • bent — Something bent has been pushed out of its proper shape, and letters out of shape come out in another order.

The earliest of the hot jazz combos have bent their plectrums out of shape.

A combo is a small jazz or dance band, which is what holds the surface together; the answer is what you go through hair with when you mean to miss nothing.

26 across LYRE

Harp on after lady loses heart (4)

Definition: Harp · charade + deletion

A harp defines it by kind: both are frames of strings plucked with the fingers, the answer being the small U-shaped one carried by Greek poets.

  • lady loses heart → LY take the middle two letters out of 'lady' and the outer two close up
  • on → RE re means on or about, as at the head of a memo
  • after — 'after' tells you the piece named before it goes second, behind the one named next.
  • loses heart — Losing heart takes out the middle of the word, since the heart of anything is what sits at its centre.

Somebody keeps harping on about a heartbroken woman.

'Harp on' is one phrase in the surface and two separate jobs in the parse: the definition takes 'Harp' and the wordplay takes 'on' by itself.

27 across STONE

Symbol of intention to marry sent back by the-love-that-dare-not-speak-its-name (5)

Definition: Symbol of intention to marry · hidden word + reversal

The gem in an engagement ring is the visible sign a couple mean to marry, so the word wanted is the rock rather than the ring.

  • the-love-that-dare-not-speak-its-name → ENOTS five consecutive letters straddle two hyphens in the middle of the phrase, and they run the wrong way
  • sent back by — 'Sent back by' does two jobs at once: the letters come out of the long hyphenated phrase, and they arrive in reverse.

An engagement present is returned by a lover whose kind of love could not be named aloud.

The phrase is Lord Alfred Douglas's line about homosexual love, quoted at Oscar Wilde's trial, and the setter has hyphenated it into one long word for letters to hide in.

28 across EARLIEST

First-rate novel includes things that are untrue (8)

Definition: First · anagram + container

'First' in its time sense, coming before all the others, matches the superlative; 'first' meaning best is the sense the answer never has.

  • rate → RATE anagram fodder, four letters
  • things that are untrue → LIES untrue things are lies
  • novel — A novel arrangement is a new one, so the letters beside it are set out in an order they have not been in before.
  • includes — 'Includes' says the shuffled letters close around the next piece rather than sitting alongside it.

A top-class novel is said to contain a number of falsehoods.

'First-rate' is one word in the surface and has to be broken at the hyphen, the definition taking 'First' and the wordplay taking 'rate' — with no warning that it splits.

Down

1 down BONAR LAW

Andrew, once at Number 10, managed comeback after box office passed bill (5,3)

Definition: Andrew, once at Number 10 · charade + reversal

The definition names a man by his first name and his address: the Conservative who took Number 10 in 1922, the only prime minister born outside the British Isles.

  • box office → BO the trade's short form for box office, as in 'BO takings'
  • managed → NAR managed a shop or a stable = RAN, then turned round
  • passed bill → LAW a bill becomes this once Parliament has passed it
  • comeback — A comeback is a return along the road you came by, so the word beside it is written out back to front.
  • after — 'after' puts the piece it introduces later in the line, so the turned-round word settles behind the takings.

Someone called Andrew who used to run the country makes a comeback once the box-office takings have settled the bill.

Number 10 is 10 Downing Street, the prime minister's London house, so 'once at Number 10' means a past occupant — and this grid is full of them, some whole and some split across two answers.

2 down GLAD

Happy to gallivant, swallowing drop of LSD (4)

Definition: Happy · container + first letter

A plain synonym: both are adjectives meaning pleased, though the answer is the warmer one you use when agreeing to do somebody a favour.

  • to gallivant → GAD to gad about is to gallivant — roam around looking for fun
  • drop of LSD → L one letter taken off the front of the drug's three
  • swallowing — Swallowing something takes it inside you, so the word before it closes around what comes next.
  • drop of — A drop is the smallest amount you can take of anything, and of a word that is one letter.

A party-goer out on the town swallows a tab of acid.

LSD is lysergic acid diethylamide; in older crosswords the same three letters are pounds, shillings and pence, so a drop of LSD can be chemistry or money.

3 down TRIFLE

Zuppa inglese non è niente? (6)

Definition: Zuppa inglese · double definition

Zuppa inglese is Italy's sponge-and-custard pudding, named 'English soup' after ours; and niente, nothing, gives the other sense — a mere nothing.

  • Zuppa inglese → the Italian dessert: layered sponge soaked in liqueur under custard
  • niente → Italian for nothing, and a thing of no consequence in English too

An Italian dismisses the local pudding as nothing at all.

The whole clue is Italian: 'non è niente' means 'it isn't nothing', and the setter's wink is that a trifle is very nearly nothing — but not quite.

4 down CANNING

Firing last of women in hiding … (7)

Definition: Firing · container + last letter

'Firing' is the sacking sense: to can an employee is slang for dismissing them, and both words are the -ing form of that act.

  • hiding → CANING a hiding is a beating, and a beating with a stick is a caning
  • last of women → N the final letter of 'women'
  • last of — 'Last of' asks for the final letter of the word it points at rather than for the word itself.
  • in — 'in' is the plain instruction to drop one piece inside another, and what follows it is the container.

The last of the women in hiding is dismissed, and the sentence runs on into the next clue.

A hiding in the schoolroom sense is a thrashing, not a place of concealment, and nobody in the surface is concealed at all. George Canning was prime minister for 119 days in 1827.

5 down THAT

… which leads to her arrest and transportation (4)

Definition: which · first letters

Both are relative pronouns and swap freely in a defining clause — 'the arrest which followed' says the same either way.

  • to her arrest and transportation → THAT the opening letters of 'to', 'her', 'arrest' and 'transportation', with 'and' only holding the phrase up
  • leads — 'leads' asks for the letter heading each word, the way a headline strips a phrase down to initials.

A woman is arrested and shipped off to the colonies.

The row of dots carries straight on from 4 down, so the sentence you are reading began in the clue above. Transportation is the old punishment of shipping convicts to Australia.

6 down FAVOURITES

Chosen ones regrettably sour at five (10)

Definition: Chosen ones · anagram

Plain synonym in the plural: the chosen ones are the people or horses picked out above the rest, the ones expected to come first.

  • sour at five → SOURATFIVE anagram fodder, ten letters — exactly the length wanted
  • regrettably — 'Regrettably' is a sigh at how things have turned out, and things turning out badly have not stayed in the order they were given.

The ones picked out have all turned sour by five o'clock, which is a shame.

Ten letters of fodder sitting in plain sight are the hardest sort to spot, because 'sour at five' reads as a remark about wine rather than as a bag of letters.

7 down CHEAPO

That which raises pitch overwhelms ambassador in budget (6)

Definition: budget · container

'Budget' as an adjective — a budget airline, a budget hotel — means done on the cheap, which is what the answer says with a sneer.

  • That which raises pitch → CAPO the clamp a guitarist fits across the fretboard to raise the pitch of every string
  • ambassador → HE an ambassador is addressed as His or Her Excellency, HE for short
  • overwhelms — To overwhelm something is to close right over it, so the outer word engulfs the two letters named next.

Whatever it is that lifts the pitch drowns out the ambassador at a cut-price event.

'Pitch' has a football sense and a musical one, and only the guitar reading yields letters. HE for an ambassador is worth pocketing: it turns up constantly.

13 down ABSTENTION

Refusal to choose teetotaller’s way out (10)

Definition: Refusal to choose · double definition

In a vote it is refusing to come down on either side; for a non-drinker it is the same act aimed at the bottle.

  • Refusal to choose → the voting sense: sitting on your hands when the ayes and noes are called
  • teetotaller’s way out → the drinker's sense: the escape route for anyone who will not take a glass

A teetotaller finds a polite exit from the round at the bar.

Both halves are the same noun in different clothes, one from the ballot and one from the bar, so nothing in the clue needs to spell anything out.

15 down WIN

Competition’s finale to follow Test team’s victory (3)

Definition: victory · charade + last letter

'Victory' is the noun for coming out on top of a contest, and the answer is the everyday sporting word for the same result.

  • Test team’s → WI WI is the West Indies, one of the sides that plays Test cricket
  • Competition’s finale → N the last letter of 'competition'
  • finale — A finale is the last thing to happen, so it asks for the last letter of the word in front of it.
  • to follow — 'To follow' sends that letter to the back of the assembly instead of the front.

The closing stage of a tournament comes after the touring side's success.

Test cricket is the five-day international format and the West Indies are one of the dozen nations who play it, which is the outside knowledge this clue leans on.

17 down MOO

Bellow’s return in part of Bloomsbury (3)

Definition: Bellow · hidden word + reversal

A bellow is the long deep noise a cow makes, so the word for that animal's call answers a general word for a loud low cry.

  • part of Bloomsbury → OOM three consecutive letters near the front of the district's name, waiting to be turned round
  • return — A return is a going back the way you came, so the letters are read from their far end towards the near one.
  • part of — 'Part of' asks for a piece of the word beside it — a run of its letters, never its meaning.

Saul Bellow is back on the shelves in a corner of literary Bloomsbury.

Saul Bellow the novelist is the surface's man and Bloomsbury is both a London district and a publishing house, and neither has anything to do with a cowshed.

19 down LIZ TRUSS

Would-be Iron Lady rusts badly after Soviet limo reverses (3,5)

Definition: Would-be Iron Lady · charade + reversal + anagram

The Iron Lady was Margaret Thatcher, and the definition names the later prime minister who cast herself as her heir — the would-be version.

  • Soviet limo → LIZ ZIL was the Moscow marque that built the state limousines for the Politburo, and it is turned round
  • rusts → RUSTS anagram fodder, five letters
  • badly — 'Badly' is how a thing is done when it comes out wrong, and letters done wrong come out in the wrong order.
  • reverses — A reversing car goes back along its own tracks, so the marque's letters are read from the end.
  • after — 'after' places the piece named before it behind the other one, so the shuffled letters finish second.

An aspiring Thatcher goes rusty behind a reversing Soviet limousine.

ZIL limousines carried the Soviet leadership, which is the general knowledge this clue is built on. Liz Truss held office for 49 days in 2022, the shortest premiership on record.

20 down CHAMBER

Put 9’s head (or tail) in bank vault (7)

Definition: vault · container + first letter

A vault is an arched room, usually underground, and the answer is the general word for such an enclosed room — a burial one, or the heart's.

  • bank → CAMBER the camber of a road is the bank or slope built into a bend
  • 9’s head (or tail) → H 9 across begins and ends on the same letter, so head and tail come to the same thing
  • Put — 'Put' opens an instruction to place something somewhere, and the rest of the clue says where it goes.
  • head (or tail) — The joke is that the cross-referenced answer starts and ends with the same letter, so either end hands you the same one.
  • in — 'in' is the insertion half of that instruction: the letter just taken goes inside the word that follows.

Something is dropped, head or tail up, into a strongroom at the bank.

Chamberlain arrives when this is read with 26 down, another of the grid's prime ministers split in two, and 'bank' has to become the tilt of a road before any of it works.

22 down EGOIST

One as vain as 19? Last to arrive, first to depart (6)

Definition: One as vain as 19? · charade + last letter

It defines by example, offering 19 down as a specimen: the noun means a person wholly wrapped up in themselves.

  • arrive → E the closing letter of 'arrive'
  • depart → GO to depart is to go
  • first → IST first written as a numeral is 1st, and the letters of that are read straight off
  • Last — 'Last' asks for the closing letter of the word beside it rather than for its meaning.

The guest who turns up last and leaves first is as vain as a certain politician.

'1st' for first is the move to learn here: setters take the numeral's tail whenever they want those three letters. The cross-reference is a joke at a politician's expense.

23 down ROSARY

Martyr – a sorry reflection – choked prayers (6)

Definition: prayers · hidden word + reversal

The word names the sequence of prayers Catholics count off on a string of beads, so it covers the whole devotion at once.

  • Martyr – a sorry → YRASOR the run starts four letters into 'Martyr' and stops two short of the end of 'sorry', straddling the dash
  • reflection — A reflection gives you the same thing the other way round, so the run of letters is read backwards.
  • choked — Something choked has been swallowed and held inside, which is where these letters are — buried across two words.

A martyr's story makes sorry reading and the prayers stick in the throat.

A hidden answer that also has to be turned round is the hardest sort to see, because the letters read as nonsense until you try them backwards; the martyr is pure surface.

25 down CHER

Dear revolutionary – and its leader (4)

Definition: Dear · charade + first letter

'Dear' is the English of the French adjective the answer spells — the word at the top of a letter written in French.

  • revolutionary → CHE Che Guevara is the crossword's standing revolutionary
  • its leader → R 'its' points back at the word 'revolutionary', whose front letter is taken
  • leader — 'leader' asks for the front letter of the word it refers back to, which here is a word already used in the clue.

A beloved revolutionary is named alongside whoever leads him.

'Its leader' sends you back to a word the clue has already used, which is a trick worth knowing: the pronoun points at the text rather than at anything in the story.

26 down LAIN

Set down in language without first thought (4)

Definition: Set down · deletion

Both describe having come to rest in a place: the answer is the past participle of 'lie', as in 'the dust had settled where it fell'.

  • language → LATIN the language of Rome, and the one crosswords mean when they leave it unnamed
  • first thought → T the opening letter of 'thought' is the piece taken away
  • without — 'Without' here means lacking, so the letter it names is taken out of the word rather than wrapped around it.
  • first — 'first' picks the opening letter of the word beside it, and that is the letter to be removed.

Something is written down in a foreign language without a moment's thought.

Chamberlain appears when this answer is read after 20 down, another prime minister split across the grid, and 'without' is doing subtraction here rather than enclosure.
