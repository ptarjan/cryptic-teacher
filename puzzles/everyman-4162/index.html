Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,162

Setter: Everyman · Published: Saturday 25 July 2026 · Difficulty: Tough (harder than 81% of the puzzles here) · Grid: 15×15

Across

1 across VICTOR HUGO

Who wrote fantastic Gothic oeuvre, never wanting energy? (6,4)

Definition: Who wrote fantastic Gothic oeuvre, never wanting energy? · anagram + deletion + &lit

  • energy → EE E is the physicist's symbol for energy, and the French word holds two of them — both are what leave
  • Gothic oeuvre → VICTORHUGO the ten letters left once the E's have gone — G, O, T, H, I, C, O, U, V, R — rearranged into the novelist

Notre-Dame de Paris is the Gothic novel behind the surface, and its author was famously prolific. Italics in a clue usually flag a foreign word.

6 across OPTS

Chooses, therefore, to go back to grasp point (4)

Definition: Chooses · container + reversal

  • therefore → SO therefore is so, and it is the word that gets turned round
  • point → PT pt is the standard abbreviation for point, on a map and on a scoreboard alike

Four squares and two abbreviations. Collecting the small ones — so, pt, v, n — is most of what makes short clues quick.

9 across SHAKE HANDS

Agree to unnerve crew (5,5)

Definition: Agree · charade

  • unnerve → SHAKE to shake somebody is to unnerve them, to leave them rattled
  • crew → HANDS hands are a ship's crew — all hands on deck

'Hands' for a crew is worth keeping: it is one of the sea words setters never let go of, and here it lands on a gesture instead of a boat.

10 across DELI

Finally obtained some delightful salami - here? (4)

Definition: here · last letters

  • obtained some delightful salami → DELI the last letter of each word in turn: obtaineD, somE, delightfuL, salamI

One indicator can govern a whole run of words, not just the one beside it. The dash before the definition is the setter pointing at the shop.

11 across PRETTY PLEASE

Prepay and settle rent, prithee (6,6)

Definition: prithee · anagram

  • Prepay → PREPAY six letters of the mixture
  • settle → SETTLE six more, and a second word that sounds like paying a bill

The surface is a landlord's demand and the answer is a child's wheedle, which is about as far apart as two readings of five words get.

15 across UNLEARN

Completely forget number Lauren arranged (7)

Definition: Completely forget · anagram

  • number → N n is the standard abbreviation for a number, as in n items
  • Lauren → LAUREN the name hands over all six of its letters

A first name in a clue is usually raw material rather than a person. Notice how much work 'completely' is doing in the definition.

16 across PEELERS

Detectives who'll prepare potatoes? (7)

Definition: Detectives · double definition

  • Detectives → Victorian slang for the police, coined from the name of the man who founded them
  • who'll prepare potatoes → the literal reading: the little blades that take the skin off before boiling

Sir Robert Peel set up the Metropolitan Police in 1829 and his men were peelers and bobbies from the start. The question mark admits the kitchen sense is the joke.

17 across NON-STOP

Section of canon's to play on a loop (3-4)

Definition: on a loop · hidden word

  • canon's to play → NONSTOP the run starts inside the first word and stops one letter into the last: ca(NON'S TO P)lay

Hidden answers love a possessive: the apostrophe in 'canon's' is invisible to the letters, so the run carries straight across it.

19 across OBSERVE

Old boy to wait at table and make remarks? (7)

Definition: make remarks · charade

  • Old boy → OB OB is the school register's shorthand for an old boy
  • wait at table → SERVE to serve is what waiting at table amounts to

The definition looks like the tail of the same restaurant scene, which is why it is easy to read past it and keep hunting.

20 across UGLY DUCKLING

Subject of a swan song? (4,8)

Definition: Subject of a swan song? · cryptic definition

  • swan song → the surface offers a farewell performance, an artist's last work; the setter means a tale that finishes with a swan in it
  • Subject of → the creature the story follows, jeered at in the farmyard until it grows into what it was all along

There are no letters to assemble here, only a phrase read the other way round. Knowing the Andersen story is the whole solve.

23 across TSAR

Title socialists avidly revoked, primarily! (4)

Definition: Title socialists avidly revoked · first letters + &lit

  • Title socialists avidly revoked → TSAR the initials of the four words in order: Title Socialists Avidly Revoked

An initial-letter clue that also reads as plain history is the prize of the genre, and the history here is right: the last holder was deposed in 1917.

24 across PICKET LINE

We come to work? Yes and no (6,4)

Definition: We come to work? Yes and no · cryptic definition

  • We come to work? → as an answer to a question it sounds shifty; said by strikers it is plain fact — they are at the gate at the usual hour
  • Yes and no → the qualification is the joke: they turn up at the place of work without doing any of it

No wordplay to find — 'we' is the strikers speaking. A clue in the first person nearly always means the answer is the speaker or the spot they stand on.

25 across EASE

Principally, Everyman, more than once, pronouncedly moderate (4)

Definition: moderate · first letter + homophone

  • Everyman, more than once → EASE the letter at the head of Everyman is E, and more than one E is E's — say that aloud and you have spelt the answer

Everyman puts his own name into a clue most weeks, and it is worth only its first letter here. The commas make a pile of instructions look like a description.

26 across PHONED IT IN

Made little effort: piano sharp (that's one element) (6,2,2)

Definition: Made little effort · charade

  • piano → P p is piano in a score, the musician's mark for soft
  • sharp → HONED a honed blade is a sharp one
  • one → I I is one in Roman numerals
  • element → TIN tin is element 50, and the bracket is what points at the periodic table

'Piano' and 'sharp' both look musical and only one of them is. P for soft is among the most-used single letters in cryptics, so it is worth banking early.

Down

1 down VAST

Very big and very secure, but lacking function (4)

Definition: Very big · charade + deletion

  • function → F f(x) — f is the mathematician's letter for a function, and it is the letter that goes
  • very → V v is the everyday abbreviation for very, as in v. good
  • secure → AST secure is fast, as in held fast; take the f off the front and these three letters are what remain

Two single letters standing for whole words in four squares. V for very and F for function are both worth banking — short clues are mostly built out of them.

2 down CRAB

Sour-tempered type, conservative, upset drinking establishment (4)

Definition: Sour-tempered type · charade + reversal

  • conservative → C C is the ballot-paper abbreviation for the Conservative party
  • drinking establishment → BAR a bar is where the drinking is done, and it is what gets tipped over

The comma after 'conservative' looks like punctuation in a list of insults and is really the join between two pieces.

3 down OVER-REACTED

Actor veered off and got upset about nothing (4-7)

Definition: got upset about nothing · anagram

  • Actor veered → OVERREACTED the eleven letters of ACTOR VEERED, taken in a new order

The surface sells you a temperamental actor, and the definition hides at the far end of the sentence still sounding like more of the anecdote.

4 down HEARTEN

Take courage and judicially try X (7)

Definition: Take courage · charade

  • judicially try → HEAR a court hears a case, which is what trying it judicially means
  • X → TEN X is ten in Roman numerals, which is why the setter has set it in italics

An italicised single letter is nearly always a numeral or a symbol rather than the letter itself, and X is the one you meet most.

5 down GIDDY-UP

Exhortation to make a nag depart? (5-2)

Definition: Exhortation · double definition

  • Exhortation → the general sense: a cry urging somebody to get moving, used now far away from any stable
  • to make a nag depart? → the original job of that cry — what a driver calls to a horse to set it going

A nag is a horse and also a person who goes on at you, and the clue is happy for you to hear the second one first.

7 down PIED-A-TERRE

Red tea and ripe bananas in second home (4-1-5)

Definition: second home · anagram

  • Red tea → REDTEA six letters of the mixture, hiding in a plausible drink
  • ripe → RIPE four more, and the word that makes the fruit of the surface work

The surface is a fruit bowl and the definition is two flat words at the end. The French phrase means literally 'foot on the ground'.

8 down SPIDER'S WEB

Bird sweeps up structure containing insects (7,3)

Definition: structure containing insects · anagram

  • Bird sweeps → SPIDERSWEB the ten letters of BIRD SWEEPS, resettled

Spiders are not insects, which is what makes the definition exact rather than sloppy: the maker is one thing and the catch is another.

12 down LEEDS UNITED

Players in starring roles the reader ennobled, we hear (5,6)

Definition: Players · homophone

  • starring roles → LEEDS the starring roles in a production are the LEADS, and said aloud that spells a Yorkshire city
  • the reader ennobled → UNITED the reader is YOU, and to ennoble is to knight — YOU KNIGHTED said aloud is a football suffix

Setters address the solver as 'the reader' or 'you' constantly, and spotting that is often the whole step.

13 down TURN TURTLE

Flip over and unwisely flutter runt that's not strong (4,6)

Definition: Flip over · anagram + deletion

  • that's not strong → F f is forte in a score, the mark for strong, so denying strength takes that letter away
  • flutter runt → TURNTURTLE with the f gone, the ten letters left are shuffled

'Strong' at the end of a clue is very often a single letter leaving rather than an adjective doing any describing.

14 down PLANELOADS

In announcement, manifest seams of ore as can be aerially transported (10)

Definition: as can be aerially transported · homophone

  • manifest → PLANE manifest means plain, obvious; PLAIN said aloud is spelt as an aircraft
  • seams of ore → LOADS a lode is a vein of ore in rock, so seams of ore are LODES, spelt as cargo

'Manifest' is doing double duty on the surface, since a manifest is also the list of what an aircraft is carrying. 'Mother lode' is where the ore word survives.

18 down PUCKISH

Mischievous, like element of ice-hockey game? (7)

Definition: Mischievous · charade

  • element of ice-hockey game → PUCK the hard rubber disc the whole game is played with
  • like → ISH -ish is the ending that turns a noun into 'somewhat like it'

The wordplay points at the rink and the meaning points at Shakespeare — Puck is the sprite of A Midsummer Night's Dream, and the two are unrelated.

19 down OILSKIN

Partially uncoils kinks in outerwear (7)

Definition: outerwear · hidden word

  • uncoils kinks → OILSKIN the run straddles the gap between the two words: unc(OILS KIN)ks

A hidden answer that crosses a word boundary is the hardest kind to see, because the eye reads whole words before it reads letters.

21 down GIST

General idea is tucking into G&T (4)

Definition: General idea · container

  • G&T → GT a G&T is a gin and tonic, and its two initials are what sit outside
  • is → IS the little verb goes in whole, letters and all

A drink written as initials is two capitals with a gap in the middle, which is exactly the shape a container clue wants.

22 down KEEN

Eager to wail (4)

Definition: Eager · double definition

  • Eager → the everyday adjective — keen on an idea, keen to start
  • to wail → a quite separate verb, borrowed from Irish, for the lament raised over a body

Four letters and two words that share nothing but their spelling. The Irish verb comes from 'caoinim', and it is the one crosswords lean on.
