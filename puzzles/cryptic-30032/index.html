Across

1 across TRIUMPHED

Current President assumed he had succeeded (9)

Definition: succeeded · container + charade

succeeded → TRIUMPHED: both are past-tense verbs for coming out on top, though 'succeeded' covers merely managing it where the answer claims an outright win.

  • President → TRUMP the surname of the sitting US president, five letters straight off the front page
  • Current → I I is the physics symbol for electric current and the crossword's standard way of clueing it
  • he had → HED he had contracts to he'd, and the grid has no room for an apostrophe
  • assumed — To assume something is to take it on and absorb it, so the word doing the assuming closes over the letter named beside it.

A head of state taking it as read that a man had already got where he wanted to be.

'Current President' reads as one phrase naming whoever is in office now, which is the whole of the misdirection: the two words belong to different pieces.

6 across SMUT

Soot – small amount oddly disappears (4)

Definition: Soot · charade + alternate letters

Soot → SMUT: a smut is a flake of soot or the black mark it leaves, so the two nouns name the same speck of chimney dirt.

  • small → S S is the size in a shirt collar and the standard abbreviation for small
  • amount → MUT a, o and n stand at positions one, three and five; take them out and three letters remain
  • oddly disappears — The odd ones are the letters going missing, so strike out the first, third and fifth and read off whatever survives.

Soot, with a tiny quantity of it going missing here and there.

A smut is a single flake of soot, the sort that settles on washing near a chimney; the word's other sense, dirty talk, is what 15 down leans on.

10 across TOKEN

Representation of crypto Musk finally invested in X (5)

Definition: Representation · container + last letters

Representation → TOKEN: a token stands in for something else, a bus fare or a promise, which is what a representation does; the crypto sense is the newest branch.

  • X → TEN X is the Roman numeral for ten, spelled out in letters here
  • crypto Musk → OK the closing letters of crypto and of Musk, taken in that order
  • finally — Finally asks for the ends of the words in front of it, so each one gives up its closing letter and nothing else.
  • invested in — Money invested in something is put inside it, and these letters go bodily into the middle of the piece that follows.

Elon Musk sinking the last of his money into a cryptocurrency on his own platform.

X does three jobs on the surface — Musk's platform, the unknown quantity, the kiss — and the wordplay wants none of them, only the Roman numeral.

11 across CONCUSSED

Stunned prisoner swore (9)

Definition: Stunned · charade

Stunned → CONCUSSED: both past participles describe somebody knocked senseless by a blow, concussed being the medical version of the same daze.

  • prisoner → CON a con is a convict, the crossword's usual prisoner
  • swore → CUSSED to cuss is to swear, so its past tense is six letters long

A prisoner still reeling from a knock on the head, letting fly with language.

CON for prisoner is the most-worked abbreviation in the British cryptic, and 'cuss' is the American softening of 'curse' that setters here borrow freely.

12 across NODULES

Nintendo console installing game after turning knobs (7)

Definition: knobs · container + reversal

knobs → NODULES: both plural nouns for small rounded lumps standing up from a surface, nodules being the word a doctor or a geologist reaches for.

  • Nintendo console → NES the Nintendo Entertainment System, the 1980s machine everybody called by its initials
  • game → ODUL ludo, the counters-and-dice race game, written backwards
  • installing — Installing a thing means fitting it inside whatever will hold it, so this piece drops into the middle of the one before.
  • after turning — The game is turned round before it goes in, so its letters are written out from the back end forwards.

A Nintendo machine with a game slotted into it and its control knobs given a twist.

'Nintendo console' looks like one indivisible phrase naming a box under the telly, and what the clue wants is the machine's own initials.

13 across DOORMAT

One looked down on party leader abandoning make-up (7)

Definition: One looked down on · charade + deletion

One looked down on → DOORMAT: it lies at the threshold and is literally looked down on, and the same word names a person everybody walks over.

  • party → DO a do is a party — 'a bit of a do' — and it is the crossword's standard one
  • make-up → ORMAT the make-up of a thing is its FORMAT, which here gives up its opening F
  • leader abandoning — A word's leader is the letter at its head, and here that letter is the one walking out, so what remains begins one letter in.

Contempt for a party leader who has stopped bothering with make-up.

'Party leader' reads as one phrase naming a politician, and the two words belong to different pieces of wordplay: the party is the knees-up.

14 across UNITED WE STAND DIVIDED WE FALL

Slogan – one used by Kanye and GAP before silly flawed deal’s conclusion (6,2,5,7,2,4)

Definition: Slogan · charade + anagram + last letter

Slogan → UNITED WE STAND, DIVIDED WE FALL: the proverb has been a rallying motto since the American Revolution and is the state motto of Kentucky.

  • one → UNITED an adjective here: a party or a country that acts as one has joined into a single body
  • used by Kanye → WEST the surname Kanye goes by
  • and → AND lifted straight out of the clue, letter for letter
  • GAP → DIVIDE a gap between two camps is what a newspaper calls a rift, and the capitals are the shop on the surface
  • flawed → DWEFAL six letters handed over to be shaken up
  • deal’s → L the letter the word deal stops on
  • before — What comes before something else is written ahead of it, so the gap's letters land in front of the shuffled ones.
  • silly — Something silly has no sense left in the way it is arranged, and letters called silly will not be standing in the order they arrived in.
  • conclusion — A word's conclusion is where it stops, so only the last letter of the one beside it is being handed over.

Kanye West's clothing tie-up with Gap, and the daft, botched way that deal came to an end.

The two entries tell the story in order: the first is the collaboration, the second the collapse. Kanye supplies his surname, not his music.

21 across HECKLES

Spots French disappearing behind hospital barracks (7)

Definition: barracks · deletion + charade

barracks → HECKLES: to barrack somebody is to shout them down from the crowd, and both verbs are the third-person singular of that same nuisance.

  • hospital → H H is the standard abbreviation, the letter on the road sign
  • Spots → ECKLES the spots are FRECKLES, and the two letters standing for French come away from the front
  • French disappearing — French is FR in wordplay, and disappearing says those two letters are the ones taken out of the word beside it.
  • behind — This entry runs left to right, so a piece placed behind another comes after it and fills the tail of the answer.

Spots on a French visitor fading from view behind the barracks by the hospital.

'Barracks' is placed to read as army buildings next to the hospital, and it is a verb: to barrack is British and Australian for jeering at a player or speaker.

22 across STEAMER

Ship’s pilot eats before noon (7)

Definition: Ship · container

Ship → STEAMER: definition by example — a steamer is one kind of ship, the steam-driven sort, so the general noun stands for the particular.

  • pilot → STEER to pilot a boat is to steer it — the verb, not the man on the bridge
  • before noon → AM the hours before noon are the am ones on a clock face
  • eats — What a word eats it takes inside itself, so the letters named next sit in the middle of it rather than alongside.

A ship's pilot having a bite to eat before midday.

'Ship's pilot' reads as one job title and then splits: 'Ship' is the definition on its own, and the apostrophe-s does the work of an equals sign.

24 across MENOPAUSE

The change made on vacation without delay … (9)

Definition: The change · charade + outer letters

The change → MENOPAUSE: 'the change', or 'the change of life', is the long-standing British euphemism for it, so the two nouns name one event.

  • made → ME the outside letters of made, with the middle two cleared out
  • without delay → NOPAUSE doing a thing without delay is doing it with no pause
  • on vacation — A word sent on vacation has been emptied of its occupants, so the middle letters go and only the two ends stay behind.

Alterations carried out while the family is away on holiday, and done straight away.

'The change' is the euphemism a doctor's waiting room would use, and the trailing dots hand the word 'change' straight on to 25 across, where it does a different job.

25 across TAMED

… change made on time – now under control (5)

Definition: now under control · anagram

now under control → TAMED: a tamed animal, river or temper has been brought to heel, and the past participle carries the 'now' of a finished change.

  • made on time → MADET the four letters of made plus T, the standard abbreviation for time — five to rearrange
  • change → anagram indicator
  • change — Letters told to change do not keep the arrangement they came in, and this is the same word 24 across used as its definition.

An alteration delivered on schedule, with everything now running smoothly.

The dots carry 'change' over from 24 across, where it was the definition; here the very same word is the instruction to rearrange.

26 across YO-YO

Type of dieting you endlessly repeated (2-2)

Definition: Type of dieting · deletion + charade

Type of dieting → YO-YO: the word attaches to dieting to name the up-and-down pattern, losing the same weight and putting it back on repeatedly.

  • you endlessly → YO the word you with its last letter taken off
  • repeated → YO the same two letters come round a second time
  • endlessly — A word taken endlessly has had its end removed, so the final letter of the one beside it is dropped before anything else happens.

The kind of dieting somebody keeps going back to, over and over again.

Yo-yo dieting is weight lost and regained in cycles, named for the toy that comes straight back up the string the moment you drop it.

27 across BATTLE-CRY

Book new cattery to accommodate Lambert and Geronimo? (6-3)

Definition: Geronimo · charade + anagram + container

Geronimo → BATTLE-CRY: 'Geronimo!' is what American paratroopers yelled leaving the aircraft, so the Apache leader's name became one particular battle-cry.

  • Book → B B is the standard abbreviation for book
  • cattery → ATTECRY seven letters rebuilt into a shell with a gap left in the middle of it
  • Lambert → L the lambert is the old unit of brightness, and its symbol is a single L
  • new — Something made new has been put together again out of the same parts, and letters rebuilt that way come out in a different order.
  • to accommodate — To accommodate a guest is to find room for them inside, so the letter named next is fitted into the middle of what precedes it.

Reserving space in a newly built cattery for two arrivals called Lambert and Geronimo.

The question mark flags a definition by example. Only one of the two names defines anything — the other is a unit of measurement dressed up as a pet.

1 down TITANIUM

Corporation covering it in a molten metal (8)

Definition: metal · container + anagram

metal → TITANIUM: definition by example, since titanium is one metal among dozens and the plain noun is standing in for the particular element.

  • Corporation → TUM a corporation is an old word for a paunch, the belly a well-fed alderman carries in front of him
  • it in a → ITANI five small words' worth of letters, run together and melted into a new order
  • covering — A cover goes over a thing on every side, so the word doing the covering wraps right round what comes next.
  • molten — Metal gone molten has lost its shape completely, and letters in that state will not hold the order they were handed over in.

A firm quietly hiding something small by dropping it into a bath of molten metal.

'Corporation' is planted to look like a company and it is the Victorian sense, a man's belly — the same joke Dickens would have made.

2 down INKED

Find keen lads in prime locations getting tattooed (5)

Definition: tattooed · prime letters

tattooed → INKED: ink is what a tattooist works in, so the past participle has become the ordinary word for having had the work done.

  • Find keen lads → INKED twelve letters run together, with the ones at prime positions picked off in order
  • in prime locations — The locations wanted are the prime-numbered ones: number the letters straight through and keep those standing at 2, 3, 5, 7 and 11.

Advice on where to look for the keenest lads, all of them covered in tattoos.

Prime numbers are a rare device and the giveaway is that 'prime locations' is estate-agent talk for the best spots, doing surface duty at the same time.

3 down MANILA ENVELOPE

Bill might arrive in this capital before eleven, worried about work (6,8)

Definition: Bill might arrive in this · charade + anagram

Bill might arrive in this → MANILA ENVELOPE: the buff office envelope, named for the Manila hemp it was first made from, is what an invoice comes in.

  • capital → MANILA the capital of the Philippines
  • eleven → ELEVEN the number spelled out in full, handing over its six letters to be shuffled
  • work → OP OP is a work — opus — the label printed on every piece of classical music
  • before — What stands before something else is written first, so the capital's six letters open the answer and the rest follows.
  • worried — To worry a thing is to shake it about the way a dog worries a rag, and letters shaken like that come back out of order.

Somebody fretting over a bill that might reach them in a far-off capital before eleven o'clock.

'Capital before eleven' reads as an appointment in a city, and 'about' is a plain joining word here rather than an instruction to go round anything.

4 down HACKSAW

Means to cut journalist on Maxim (7)

Definition: Means to cut · charade

Means to cut → HACKSAW: the phrase names an instrument, and a hacksaw is the fine-toothed blade kept for cutting through metal and pipe.

  • journalist → HACK a hack is a jobbing reporter, and it is not a compliment
  • Maxim → SAW a saw is an old proverb — Shakespeare's 'wise saws and modern instances' — and the capital M is the magazine on the surface
  • on — This entry runs down the grid, so a piece sitting on another is the one written above it and takes the front of the answer.

A way of cutting down to size a journalist who writes for a lads' magazine.

The italics are the trap: they make Maxim a title, and the word wanted is the ordinary noun for a pithy old saying.

5 down DENUDES

Dundee’s comic strips (7)

Definition: strips · anagram

strips → DENUDES: third-person verbs both, for taking the covering off something, whether that is a person, a hillside or a papered wall.

  • Dundee’s → DUNDEES anagram fodder, seven letters, the apostrophe ignored as punctuation always is
  • comic → anagram indicator
  • comic — Comic is doing service as 'funny' in the sense of odd, and letters called odd have stopped standing in their proper order.

The comic strips that come out of Dundee, home of the Beano and the Dandy.

DC Thomson has published the Beano and the Dandy from Dundee for a century, so 'Dundee's comic strips' reads as one solid phrase — and only 'strips' is the definition.

7 down MASS MEDIA

Graduate, 22 aimed to work in TV, radio etc (4,5)

Definition: TV, radio etc · charade + anagram

TV, radio etc → MASS MEDIA: the plural noun covers the channels that reach a whole population at once, of which broadcasting is the everyday example.

  • Graduate → MA Master of Arts, the letters a graduate puts after their name
  • 22 → SS 22 across is a steamship, and SS is the pair of letters painted before such a vessel's name
  • aimed → MEDIA five letters handed over to be rearranged
  • to work — Working a thing means kneading it about, the way a baker works dough, and letters worked over like that end up in a new arrangement.

A graduate with ambitions to land a job in broadcasting.

The 22 sends you across the grid: solve that entry first and it hands back two letters, which is why a cross-reference is worth doing early.

8 down TO DATE

Thus far, ultimately not, old fruit (2,4)

Definition: Thus far · charade + last letter

Thus far → TO DATE: both adverbial phrases mean up to the present moment, and either can end a sentence about how much has happened so far.

  • not → T the last letter of not
  • old → O O is the standard abbreviation for old
  • fruit → DATE the sticky brown fruit of the palm
  • ultimately — What happens ultimately happens last, so only the final letter of the word beside it is being offered.

Someone reporting that the answer is still no, and addressing a friend as 'old fruit'.

'Old fruit' is dated British chumminess, roughly 'old chap', and the surface leans on it hard while the wordplay quietly splits the phrase in two.

9 down PUT ON A PEDESTAL

Fix up 8 panels illustrating hero-worship (3,2,1,8)

Definition: hero-worship · anagram

hero-worship → PUT ON A PEDESTAL: to hero-worship someone is to set them above ordinary judgement, which is what the idiom's raised plinth pictures.

  • up 8 panels → UPTODATEPANELS 8 down is TO DATE, so this reads as up-to-date panels — fourteen letters in all
  • Fix → anagram indicator
  • Fix — Fixing something means pulling it apart and putting the parts back another way, which is exactly what happens to these fourteen letters.

An instruction to repair some modern display boards showing somebody being idolised.

'Fix up' wants to be read as one phrasal verb, and that is what buries the cross-reference: the 'up' belongs with the 8, not with the fixing.

15 down INDECENCY

Good taste, home before 6? (9)

Definition: 6 · charade

6 → INDECENCY: 6 across is SMUT, and indecency is the abstract noun for the same thing, material that offends against public decency.

  • home → IN somebody at home is in, the way a caller finds them
  • Good taste → DECENCY not taste in food or furniture but in the moral sense, the propriety that keeps people from giving offence
  • before — Whatever goes before goes first, so the two letters meaning at home are written ahead of the longer piece.

Good manners, and getting back to the house before six o'clock.

The 6 is a cross-reference and not a time: solve 6 across and you are holding the definition, which is what the question mark is apologising for.

16 down FLORIDLY

State of Mar-a-Lago after detailing walls of lavatory in a rococo style (8)

Definition: in a rococo style · charade + deletion + outer letters

in a rococo style → FLORIDLY: the adverb means ornately, in a manner loaded with flourish, which is exactly how rococo decoration is applied.

  • State of Mar-a-Lago → FLORID Mar-a-Lago is Trump's Palm Beach club, so the state is Florida, arriving here one letter short
  • lavatory → LY the two outermost letters of lavatory, one from each end
  • after detailing — De-tailing a word is docking its tail, so the state named beside it arrives with its final letter cut off.
  • walls of — The walls of a building are its outermost surfaces, so take the first and last letters of the word that follows and nothing between.

The state a Florida resort is in once it has been stripped back and its lavatory done out in gilt curlicues.

'State' reads as condition and means the US state, and 'detailing' reads as car valeting when it is de-tailing, docking a final letter.

18 down DISTURB

Worry ad-libs occasionally dry up (7)

Definition: Worry · charade + alternate letters + reversal

Worry → DISTURB: as verbs both mean to unsettle somebody's peace of mind, and 'do not disturb' asks the same thing as 'do not trouble'.

  • ad-libs → DIS the second, fourth and sixth letters of the word, the hyphen counting for nothing
  • dry → TURB brut is the dry style printed on a champagne label, and here it is climbing the grid
  • occasionally — Something done occasionally happens every so often rather than every time, so only every second letter of the word beside it is picked up.
  • up — This entry is written from the top downwards, so a piece told to go up is read from its bottom end back to its top.

An improviser's off-the-cuff lines drying up every now and again.

'Dry up' is one phrase on the surface, the thing that happens to a performer who loses their lines, and the wordplay takes the two words apart.

19 down DISSENT

Object in stock, we hear (7)

Definition: Object · homophone

Object → DISSENT: both verbs mean registering that you do not agree, with dissent carrying the formal note of a minority voting the other way.

  • stock → DISSENT stock in the sense of ancestry — 'of good stock' — is descent, and said aloud it comes out spelled another way
  • we hear — What we hear is the sound and not the spelling, so the answer is written the way a different word is said aloud.

An objection raised about goods sitting in the storeroom.

'In stock' wants to be one phrase about a warehouse; the wordplay needs 'stock' alone, and in the breeding sense rather than the inventory one.

20 down WHIMSY

Question of motive consumes one writing fantasy (6)

Definition: fantasy · container + charade

fantasy → WHIMSY: whimsy is playful fancy, the light end of fantasy, invention followed for its own oddity rather than a whole imagined world.

  • Question of motive → WHY the question you ask when what you want is a motive
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one, the crossword's usual way of writing it
  • writing → MS MS is a manuscript, a piece of writing before anyone sets it in type
  • consumes — Whatever a word consumes it swallows whole, so the pieces named after it end up held inside it.

A detective's question about motive swallowing up a novelist of fantasy.

'One writing' reads as a person who writes, which is the trap — it is two separate scraps, a numeral and an abbreviation, laid end to end.

23 down MIMIC

Case of Maserati on motorway going over 100, makes Mirror (5)

Definition: Mirror · charade + outer letters

Mirror → MIMIC: both are verbs for copying somebody's movements straight back at them, one from the glass and one from the impressionist.

  • Maserati → MI the first and last letters of Maserati
  • motorway → MI the M1 is Britain's first motorway, and in a grid of capitals its numeral is read as a letter I
  • 100 → C C is the Roman numeral for a hundred
  • Case of — A case is what encloses a thing, so what is wanted is the outside letters of the word and not its contents.
  • on — This entry runs down the grid, so a piece sitting on another is written above it.
  • going over — Going over something is passing above it, so whatever this piece goes over is written underneath it.

A Maserati flying up the motorway past a hundred, caught in the rear-view mirror.

The italics make Mirror the newspaper, which is the whole misdirection — it is the plain verb. Reading the M1's digit as a capital I is the step to spot.
