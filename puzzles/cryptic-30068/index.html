Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,068

Across

1 across PREDAWN

Early man planting tomato, cherry or chestnut (7)

Definition: Early · container

  • man → PAWN a chess man is a pawn
  • tomato, cherry or chestnut → RED all three name shades of red

The surface is a gardener at work; the man is a chess piece and the three fruity words are only paint colours. Chestnut and cherry are reds long before they are anything edible.

5 across GORILLA

Pass small runner, a thug (7)

Definition: thug · charade

  • Pass → GO let something pass and you let it go — the verbs share that sense
  • small runner → RILL a rill is a little stream, and running water is a runner
  • a → A the article taken as it stands

A runner made of water rather than legs is the step to spot; a rill is one of the small streams crosswords keep on hand for exactly this.

10 across TUTU

Old archbishop in tie, reportedly? (4)

Definition: Old archbishop · homophone

  • tie → TUTU a match finishing level at two goals each is a 2-2 tie, said aloud as 'two two'

Desmond Tutu died in 2021, which is what 'old' is doing. The ballet skirt never surfaces, and the tie is a scoreline rather than neckwear.

11 across GOODFELLOW

Puck, wicked soul (10)

Definition: Puck · charade

  • wicked → GOOD in slang, wicked means excellent — good
  • soul → FELLOW a poor soul is a poor fellow; both mean a person

'Wicked' meaning its own opposite is the joke, and it suits Shakespeare's mischievous sprite exactly. Slang that inverts a word — wicked, sick, bad — is a setter's standby.

13 across HEREUPON

Hydrogen pure, one gathered, as of now (8)

Definition: as of now · anagram

  • Hydrogen → H H is the chemical symbol for hydrogen
  • pure, one → PUREONE anagram fodder, seven letters
  • gathered → anagram indicator

Chemical symbols are worth learning as a set — H, O, N, FE — because setters reach for them constantly. Here the symbol joins the fodder instead of standing apart from it.

14 across TEA FOR TWO

Wrote to couple in cafe needing a brew – their request? (3,3,3)

Definition: their request? · anagram + middle letters

  • Wrote to → WROTETO anagram fodder, seven letters
  • cafe → AF the middle pair of letters of c-AF-e
  • needing a brew → anagram indicator

The definition is the couple themselves — what two people at a cafe table ask for. It is a song title too, which is why the whole sentence reads so smoothly.

16 across JOLLY

Really cheerful (5)

Definition: Really · double definition

  • Really → the intensifier: a very British way of saying 'very' before an adjective
  • cheerful → the everyday adjective — in good spirits

Two words, two definitions and no wordplay at all: the shortest clue in the puzzle is the one that tells you least. The answer is also wanted at 28 across.

17 across SPACE

Void, step beyond limit of consciousness (5)

Definition: Void · charade + last letter

  • consciousness → S the last letter of consciousnesS
  • step → PACE a step measured out is a pace

Read straight the clue is about fainting; read cryptically only two ordinary words do any work. 'Limit of' for a final letter is a formula worth filing away.

19 across SOUR CREAM

Guardian readers’ piercing cry, food dropped on jacket? (4,5)

Definition: food dropped on jacket? · container

  • cry → SCREAM a scream is a cry
  • Guardian readers’ → OUR the paper's readers, speaking of themselves, say 'our'

'Piercing cry' is one phrase doing two jobs — the sound, and the instruction to go inside it. The jacket is a potato, and the definition is what gets spooned over it.

23 across PINOCHLE

Help! Coin lost in card game (8)

Definition: card game · anagram

  • Help! Coin → HELPCOIN anagram fodder, eight letters
  • lost → anagram indicator

The exclamation mark is punctuation the surface needs and the wordplay ignores. Read past it and the eight letters are simply sitting there waiting.

24 across LARDER

Cool place for a cocktail if after 23, reportedly? (6)

Definition: Cool place · homophone

  • if after 23 → 23 across is the card game; this answer is to be said straight after it
  • a cocktail → LARDER run pinochle and this answer together and you hear the rum and pineapple cocktail

The cross-reference is a sound joke: two answers spoken in order become a drink neither of them mentions. The definition is the sober half of the clue.

26 across STRIKE ZONE

Area for swingers in action, number observed after opening of zip (6,4)

Definition: Area for swingers · charade + first letter

  • action → STRIKE industrial action is a strike
  • zip → Z the first letter of zip
  • number → ONE one is a number, spelt out

The swingers are batters rather than partygoers, and the action is the union sort. Baseball turns up far less often here than cricket, which is what makes the definition hard.

27 across GRIT

Steel crushed stones (4)

Definition: Steel · double definition

  • Steel → the figurative sense: nerve, the stuff you steel yourself with
  • crushed stones → the literal sense: the coarse material spread on icy roads

'Crushed' looks like an instruction to shuffle letters and is nothing of the kind — it belongs to the second definition, describing stone broken small.

28 across FREEZER

Cool place for a song if before 16 11, reportedly? (7)

Definition: Cool place · homophone

  • if before 16 11 → 16 and 11 across together give 'jolly good fellow', which this answer runs into
  • a song → FREEZER with the aitch dropped, 'for 'e's a' is how the celebration song begins

This clue and 24 across work the same way: an answer that means one thing on the page and something else entirely when you say it next to its neighbour.

29 across LET THAT SINK IN

Really think on it, and open the door to a new basin? (3,4,4,2)

Definition: Really think on it · double definition

  • Really think on it → the everyday idiom: give the news a moment to register
  • open the door to a new basin? → read word by word it is an instruction to the plumber — admit the basin

The question mark is the setter owning up to the second reading: fit a new basin and you really do let that sink in. One phrase doing two jobs, with no letters to shuffle at all.

2 down ROUTINE

Boring, boring affair out in Europe (7)

Definition: Boring · hidden word

  • affair out in Europe → ROUTINE the letters run consecutively across the boundary between 'affair' and 'Europe'

Paul writes the word twice so the second one can do a different job: the first is the definition, the second the instruction. A repeated word in a clue is nearly always two jobs.

3 down DRUNK

Lush dip crossing river (5)

Definition: Lush · container

  • dip → DUNK to dip a biscuit in tea is to dunk it
  • river → R R is the map abbreviation for river

'Lush' is the trap: it reads as green and leafy and it means a soak. American slang originally, long since at home in British crosswords.

4 down WAGONER

Romantic broken by love for driver (7)

Definition: driver · container

  • Romantic → WAGNER Wagner composed in the Romantic era
  • love → O love is a nil score at tennis, written as a zero

'Romantic' names a period in music rather than a temperament, and that shift is the clue's whole trick. Love for nothing is tennis arithmetic, and worth filing away.

6 down OXFORD

Eight, third of those with two crosses? (6)

Definition: Eight · charade + middle letter

  • those → O the third letter of THOSE, which is also its middle one
  • two crosses → XFORD two words for a cross: the written X, then a ford, the shallow place where you cross a river

'Eight' means a rowing crew, and the Boat Race is why one university comes to mind ahead of any other. The name really does record a ford where oxen crossed.

7 down ILL-HUMOUR

Bile in funny jokes? (3-6)

Definition: Bile · charade

  • funny → ILL feeling funny is feeling ill — off colour, not amusing
  • jokes → HUMOUR jokes are humour

Both halves hide behind comedy, and 'funny' is the unwell sense rather than the amusing one. Bile is the old bodily humour that gave the word its meaning of temper.

8 down LOO ROLL

Jumper turned up in lounge, sheets in lavatory (3,4)

Definition: sheets in lavatory · container + reversal

  • Jumper → ROO a kangaroo, the jumper, is a roo
  • lounge → LOLL to lounge about is to loll

Jumper looks woollen and is an animal; lounge looks like a room and is a verb. Both misdirections come from the same trick of choosing the other part of speech.

9 down GO THE WHOLE HOG

Fully commit, having found form before opening corner (2,3,5,3)

Definition: Fully commit · charade

  • having found → GOT having found something is having got it
  • form → HEW to hew stone is to form or shape it
  • opening → HOLE an opening in a wall is a hole
  • corner → HOG to corner the supply of a thing is to hog it

The surface sounds like football — form, an opening, a corner — and not one of those words keeps that meaning. Four plain synonyms in a row hide a long phrase very well.

15 down FACTORISE

Upset I forecast, list those contributing to result (9)

Definition: list those contributing to result · anagram

  • I forecast → IFORECAST anagram fodder, nine letters
  • Upset → anagram indicator

Factors are the numbers that multiply together to make a product, so the definition is a mathematician's sentence dressed up as an ordinary one.

18 down PAINTER

Bacon, say, a plant-based protein, initially (7)

Definition: Bacon, say · charade + first letter

  • protein → P the opening letter of protein
  • a → A the article taken as it stands
  • plant → INTER to plant a body is to inter it

The surface has you thinking of rashers and vegan substitutes. 'Say' is the little word that marks a definition by example, and it is always worth hunting for.

20 down RELIEVE

Cover for ever, unfortunately having buried high priest (7)

Definition: Cover for · anagram + container

  • ever → EVER anagram fodder, four letters
  • high priest → ELI Eli, the high priest who raised Samuel

Two mechanisms at once: four letters are shuffled, and the priest is dropped into the middle of the result. Eli is crosswordland's standing high priest, three handy letters.

21 down AMERICA

Setter in a state over ‘mouth’ in Cockney, a North and South, perhaps? (7)

Definition: North and South, perhaps? · container + charade + first letter

  • a → A the article, taken as a letter
  • Setter → ME the setter calls himself me — a standing crossword convention
  • state → RI RI is the abbreviation for Rhode Island
  • Cockney → C the opening letter of Cockney
  • a → A the second article, also taken as a letter

The Cockney bait is deliberate: rhyming slang really does make a mouth a north and south, so the eye goes there. The setter means the other sort of mouth, an opening.

22 down WHEEZE

Clever scheme taking more than one minute, we hear? (6)

Definition: Clever scheme · homophone

  • minute → WEE minute is the adjective here — tiny — and a tiny thing is wee
  • more than one → WHEEZE more than one wee makes WEES, and that sound is spelt out as the answer

'Minute' is the trap: the noun of sixty seconds is the wrong word, and its adjective twin, stressed on the second syllable, is the right one.

25 down RIGHT

Blue in the pink (5)

Definition: Blue · double definition

  • Blue → blue is the colour tied to the Conservative party, which sits on one side of the political spectrum
  • in the pink → in the pink means in good health, i.e. with nothing wrong

Two colours in five words and neither is about colour: one is party politics, the other is health. Colour words in a cryptic almost always stand for something else.
