Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,080

Across

1 across POPULAR FRONT

Left in van (7,5)

Definition: Left · charade

  • in → POPULAR the in thing is the one everybody likes, so in = popular
  • van → FRONT the van is the vanguard, the front of an army or a movement

Three words, no indicator and no join: the whole trick is that both halves are dictionary synonyms while the surface reads as a position on a road.

9 across AGAIN

With Adams’ header, win a second time (5)

Definition: a second time · charade + first letter

  • Adams → A the letter at the front of Adams
  • win → GAIN to win something is to gain it

Tony Adams heading one in is the surface; the wordplay wants nothing of the man but his initial, which is what 'header' is quietly asking for.

10 across COR BLIMEY

Wow! Old political leader pointlessly filmed Harry entering (3.6)

Definition: Wow! · container + deletion

  • Old political leader → CORBYN Jeremy Corbyn, who led Labour until 2020
  • pointlessly → N is the compass point, and taking it away leaves CORBY
  • filmed Harry → LIME Harry Lime is the man everyone is hunting in The Third Man

'Cor blimey' is 'God blind me' worn smooth by a century of use, which is why it means nothing in particular and everything as an exclamation.

11 across PUERILE

Trivial Pursuit’s first two getting egghead upset (7)

Definition: Trivial · charade + first letters

  • Pursuit’s first two → PU the opening pair of letters of Pursuit
  • egghead → E the head of egg is the letter at the top of it
  • upset → RILE to upset someone is to rile them

'Upset' looks like an instruction to turn something over and is a straight synonym instead, which is the whole trap in the clue.

12 across ECUADOR

Expert fisherman, one might say, takes you round country (7)

Definition: country · anagram

  • Expert → ACE an ace at anything is an expert at it
  • fisherman, one might say → ROD a river beat is let to so many rods, the angler named for his tackle
  • you → U you is U, the letter that says the whole word

'One might say' is Vlad owning how loose a rod for an angler is, and the fishing holiday it paints has nothing to do with South America.

13 across WEASEL WORD

Wives receiving ‘support’? No way, it’s a misleading term (6,4)

Definition: a misleading term · container + charade

  • Wives → WW W is the standard shortening of wife, so two wives are two Ws
  • ‘support’ → EASEL an easel is what supports a painter's canvas
  • No → O no score at all is nothing, written as O
  • way → RD RD is road, the way on a street sign

'No way' is a flat refusal in the surface and nothing-plus-road in the wordplay, and the quotation marks round support are the setter grinning.

15 across ABED

President departs, lying (4)

Definition: lying · charade

  • President → ABE Abe Lincoln, the president crosswords keep nearest to hand
  • departs → D D is departs on a timetable board

A first name and a station-board letter, wrapped in a surface about a president leaving office and telling untruths — neither of which is in the parse.

18 across SEEK

Try to get religious believer talking (4)

Definition: Try to get · homophone

  • religious believer → SEEK a Sikh is a believer in Sikhism, and his name is said exactly like the word wanted

Vlad is relying on you knowing how the religion is spelt: what you hear and what you write share not a single letter in the same place.

19 across HIGHTAIL IT

Run up to end something special (8,2)

Definition: Run · charade

  • up → HIGH up in the air is high
  • end → TAIL the tail is the end of a thing — the tail of a queue, the tail of a plane
  • something special → IT 'it' was Clara Bow's word for the something extra a star has

The idiom is American and never lets go of its 'it' — you cannot hightail anything else — which is why the last two letters need clueing separately.

22 across RAPPORT

Music and Liverpool? It’s a good relationship (7)

Definition: a good relationship · charade

  • Music → RAP rap is a kind of music
  • Liverpool → PORT Liverpool is a port, and one of the great ones

The question mark is covering a definition by example: any of Hull, Cork or Dover would have done the same job for those four letters.

24 across EMPTIES

‘Dead soldiers ultimately losers’ – Mr President regularly awful (7)

Definition: Dead soldiers · anagram + alternate letters + last letter

  • losers → S the final letter of losers
  • Mr President → MPEIET every other letter of Mr President, starting at the M

Two different letter-picking tricks feed a single anagram, so the order matters: choose the letters first, jumble them afterwards.

25 across BLADDERED

Wasted chance to go higher when retired? (9)

Definition: Wasted · container

  • retired → BED to retire for the night is to go to bed
  • chance to go higher → LADDER a ladder is a chance to get higher, up a wall or up a career

No word here tells you to put one piece inside the other — the single image of a ladder standing in the bedroom does that job, and its absence is the difficulty.

26 across ICENI

Old people’s home rejected jewellery originally (5)

Definition: Old people · charade + reversal

  • jewellery → ICE ice is the jeweller's slang for diamonds
  • home → IN someone at home is in

'Old people' is literal — a people, meaning a nation, rather than pensioners — and the surface's care home is there to stop you reading it that way.

27 across PORCH PIRATES

Thieves disposed of mostly property and cash around island (5,7)

Definition: Thieves · anagram + deletion + container

  • property → PROPERT property with its final letter dropped
  • cash → CASH the word's own four letters
  • around island → I I is island on a map, and the other letters close around it

The answer is a very new coinage and the wordplay is as old-fashioned as it comes, which is the joke: modern crime, Victorian machinery.

Down

1 down PLACE NAME

Could be Nepal one accepted – also separately Malta? (5,4)

Definition: Could be Nepal one accepted – also separately Malta? · anagram + &lit

  • Nepal → NEPAL the country's own five letters, waiting to be re-sorted
  • one accepted → ACE the card accepted as one, high or low as the game decides, is the ace
  • separately Malta → M taken on its own Malta is M, the letter its cars and aircraft carry

Every word works twice: the two countries are examples of the answer and, with the ace and Malta's own letter, they are the letters you re-sort.

2 down PANTILES

‘They’re on the house’ – worker comes in a lot (8)

Definition: ‘They’re on the house’ · container

  • a lot → PILES a lot of something is piles of it
  • worker → ANT the crossword's worker is almost always the ant

The quotation marks are the tell: they mark a phrase being quoted for its everyday meaning, and the everyday meaning is the wrong one.

3 down LUCRE

Decoy pocketing Charlie’s money (5)

Definition: money · container

  • Decoy → LURE a decoy set out to draw something in is a lure
  • Charlie → C C is Charlie in the NATO alphabet, the same trick that makes Oscar an O

The surface is a drugs bust, where Charlie is cocaine; the wordplay only ever wanted the alphabet's third letter.

4 down RARE EARTH

Element from Red Planet (4,5)

Definition: Element · charade

  • Red → RARE a steak still red in the middle is a rare one
  • Planet → EARTH the planet we are standing on

'Red Planet' is Mars while you read it as a phrase and two unrelated words the moment you stop, which is why nothing here is about space.

5 down ROLL UP

Arrive in drag – men bowled over (4,2)

Definition: Arrive · charade + reversal

  • drag → PULL to drag something along is to pull it
  • men → OR OR is other ranks, the army's men who are not officers

OR for other ranks is worth banking — it turns up far more often in cryptics than in life — and here it hides behind a picture of men in frocks.

6 down NAMED

Titled gent upset by journalist (5)

Definition: Titled · reversal + charade

  • gent → MAN a gent is a man
  • journalist → ED ED is the editor, the journalist crosswords keep on the payroll

The aristocratic ring of 'titled' is pure decoy, and 'upset' has to be read as an instruction rather than as the gent's mood.

7 down PAWPAW

Greedily handles fruit (6)

Definition: fruit · charade

  • handles → PAW to paw at something is to handle it clumsily
  • handles → PAW and again, because one grab was not enough

A two-word clue with a doubling in it is Vlad at his most compact: the picture of grabby hands is also the instruction to take the word twice.

8 down HYBRID

Through Henry about to get free modern car (6)

Definition: modern car · charade + reversal

  • Through → BY to go through a place is to go by way of it
  • Henry → H H is the henry, the unit of electrical inductance
  • to get free → RID to get free of something is to be rid of it

Henry is a man in the surface and a unit of inductance in the wordplay — one of physics' quieter gifts to the crossword, alongside amp and ohm.

14 down WRIST-DROP

Powder, not drug – it’s right for treating medical condition (5-4)

Definition: medical condition · anagram + deletion

  • Powder → POWDER the word's own letters, before anything is done to them
  • drug → E is the drug ecstasy, and it comes out of POWDER
  • it’s → ITS the clue's own word, taken as its three letters
  • right → R R is the standard shortening of right

The medical surface does double work: 'treating' is the instruction and 'condition' the definition, and they sit side by side pretending to be one phrase.

16 down BALMINESS

Playful lambs in SE showing soft and gentle nature (9)

Definition: soft and gentle nature · anagram

  • lambs → LAMBS the word's own five letters
  • in → IN the clue's own word, taken as two letters rather than as a position
  • SE → SE the compass point exactly as written

The surface is a spring field and the only instruction in it is the first word; everything after it, small words included, is fodder.

17 down WAR PAINT

Cunning hiding effect of injury – won leading decoration in battle (8)

Definition: decoration in battle · container + charade + first letter

  • won → W the letter at the front of won
  • Cunning → ART art is cunning — the artful dodger's kind rather than the gallery's
  • effect of injury → PAIN what an injury leaves behind is pain

The dash tempts you to read 'won' with the first half of the clue; it belongs to the second, and until it moves there is no letter to start from.

18 down SCRIBE

Writer’s copy almost ready for wrapping (6)

Definition: Writer · container + deletion

  • ready → SE ready is set — ready, steady, go — with the last letter gone
  • copy → CRIB a crib is a copy to work from, the schoolboy's cheat sheet

'Ready' for SET is worth banking, and it explains a clue that otherwise looks as though it is about a manuscript nearly finished.

20 down TESTIS

Iceland ordeal over, having a ball (6)

Definition: ball · charade

  • ordeal → TEST an ordeal is a test, a trial you have to get through
  • Iceland → IS IS is Iceland's international code, the letters on its cars and web addresses

Country codes are a small vocabulary that pays off often — E for Spain, D for Germany, IS here — and 'having a ball' sounds like a good holiday until the last word turns.

21 down NORDIC

Against describing one medic standing as blue-eyed boy (6)

Definition: blue-eyed boy · container + reversal

  • Against → CON the pros and cons are the fors and againsts
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one
  • medic → DR DR is the doctor, the crossword's standard medic

Two instructions sit next to each other here, one putting a piece inside and one turning the lot upright, and they have to be done in that order.

23 down PRADO

Where to see pictures causing trouble with publicists? (5)

Definition: Where to see pictures · charade

  • publicists → PR PR is public relations, the publicists' trade
  • trouble → ADO much ado is much trouble, as Shakespeare has it

The wordplay describes a publicity disaster and the definition points at Madrid, and only the question mark warns that the two halves share no subject.

24 down ELDER

The bilingual church official (5)

Definition: church official · charade

  • The → EL el is Spanish for the
  • The → DER der is German for the

Crosswords borrow the definite article from whichever language is handy — LE, LA, EL, DER, IL — and here one small word supplies the whole build.
