Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,050

Across

1 across CRITERIA

Spies admitting author’s missing the first tests (8)

Definition: tests · container + deletion

  • Spies → CIA the American intelligence agency, the crossword's standard shorthand for spies
  • author’s → RITER an author is a WRITER, and the word turns up here without its opening W

'Tests' looks like a verb closing a sentence about spies. It is a plural noun — the standards a thing is judged against — and the answer has to be plural to match it.

5 across UNITED STATES

Country dictator’s honoured after Posh and principally Scary Spice’s gallery opening (6,6)

Definition: Country · charade + homophone + first letters + container

  • Posh → U U is the old British class marker for upper-class speech and manners, as in U and non-U
  • honoured → NITED an honoured man has been knighted, and said aloud that word carries neither its K nor its GH
  • Scary Spice’s → SS the letters those two names start with
  • gallery → TATE the Tate is Britain's national gallery of British and modern art

The pop group is the trap: only 'Scary Spice' is treated as a name, and then only for its initials. 'Posh' is the class marker, and the honour is heard rather than spelled.

9 across NEARSIDE

See drain broken facing the kerb (8)

Definition: facing the kerb · anagram

  • See drain → SEEDRAIN eight letters of fodder, exactly the length wanted

'See drain' reads as an instruction to look at the gutter, which is where a kerb is too, so surface and definition point at the same bit of street while the letters do something else entirely.

12 across EATING PLACE

Bats get in palace restaurant perhaps (6,5)

Definition: restaurant perhaps · anagram

  • get in palace → GETINPALACE eleven letters of fodder, both words of the answer together

'Get in' looks like an insertion instruction and 'palace' like the thing to insert into. Both are simply fodder, and the first word is the only instruction in the clue.

15 across NOVEL

Book Christmas drinks – vodka at the start (5)

Definition: Book · container + first letter

  • Christmas → NOEL Noel is Christmas, the word the carols use
  • vodka → V the leading letter of the spirit

'Book Christmas drinks' reads as an instruction to reserve a table for the office party, which is the wrong way round: the first word is the definition and the drinking is the wordplay.

17 across NULLIPARA

One with no issue is invalid sitting next to one short diver (9)

Definition: One with no issue · charade

  • invalid → NULL a null contract is one that is not valid, void in law
  • one → I one in Roman numerals
  • diver → PARA para is the clipped form of parachutist, who dives out of an aeroplane

The definition is a medical term dressed as ordinary English: 'no issue' means no children, not no problem. Reading it the other way costs you the first four words of the clue.

18 across ART MUSEUM

Old are with mother, describing operation where hangings occur (3,6)

Definition: where hangings occur · charade + container

  • Old are → ART the archaic second-person form of the verb: thou art
  • mother → MUM the everyday British word for mother
  • operation → USE a machine in operation is in use

'Where hangings occur' wants to be a gallows or a prison yard, and the things hung here are pictures. 'Old are' is the archaic verb, not an elderly person.

19 across GRAPH

Chart Viagra Pharmaceuticals supplies (5)

Definition: Chart · hidden word

  • Viagra Pharmaceuticals → GRAPH the letters run on without a break from inside the first word, across the space, into the second

Two long invented brand names next to each other are worth reading letter by letter. A run that crosses the space between words is the hardest kind to spot.

20 across DECLARATION

Month with City helping to make announcement (11)

Definition: announcement · charade

  • Month → DEC the calendar's abbreviation for December
  • City → LA LA is Los Angeles
  • helping → RATION a ration is the portion you are allowed

'Helping' is the noun, not the verb — a helping is a portion — and 'City' wants a particular abbreviated one rather than cities in general.

24 across ELUDED

Avoided crazy duel with journalist (6)

Definition: Avoided · anagram + charade

  • duel → ELUD four letters of fodder
  • journalist → ED ED is the editor, the journalist crosswords always reach for

A four-letter anagram is easy to miss because there is so little to shuffle. The giveaway is that 'crazy' has nothing else in the clue it could be working on.

25 across COLONIES

Possessions: last of batteries including that is … (8)

Definition: Possessions · container + last letter

  • : → COLON the punctuation mark printed in the clue is itself a piece of the wordplay — name it
  • batteries → S the final letter of the word
  • that is → IE i.e. is Latin id est, which is what 'that is' translates

The colon after the first word looks like ordinary punctuation introducing a list. It is a piece of the wordplay, and you are meant to name the mark itself.

26 across IONISE

… 11 ones easy to charge (6)

Definition: to charge · anagram

  • 11 → II the figure is two ones side by side, and one is I in Roman numerals
  • ones → ONES the word joins the fodder whole, all four letters of it

The clue runs on from the one before, so the batteries are still in view and charging here is electrical. The '11' is a shape on the page rather than a number to count with.

27 across THIRTEEN

Their ten bananas or bakers’ 12 (8)

Definition: bakers’ 12 · anagram

  • Their ten → THEIRTEN eight letters of fodder

The definition is the joke. A dozen from a baker has always been one more than a dozen, so the number at the end of the clue is not the number it says.

2 down INACTIVATE

To stop from working during start of play, 5 brew tea (10)

Definition: To stop from working · charade + anagram

  • during → IN during something is in it — the two words swap freely
  • start of play → ACTI a play opens with Act I, the number written as a Roman numeral
  • 5 → V five in Roman numerals
  • tea → ATE three letters of fodder

The whole surface is one stage picture — someone making tea in the wings during the first act — and every phrase in it is a separate piece. The '5' is the Roman numeral, not a pointer to another clue.

3 down ESSEN

Directions for European city (5)

Definition: European city · charade

  • Directions → ESSEN five compass points in a row, each shrunk to its single letter: east, south, south, east, north

One plural noun supplies every letter, which is why there is so little clue to work with. Compass points are the commonest way a setter buys single letters wholesale.

4 down INDEPENDENCE

Trendy state with former vice-president accepting objective is home rule (12)

Definition: home rule · charade + container

  • Trendy → IN whatever is fashionable is in
  • state → DE the postal abbreviation for Delaware
  • former vice-president → PENCE Mike Pence held the office under Donald Trump
  • objective → END the end you have in view is your aim, not your finish

'State' is not a condition here but a two-letter US abbreviation, and the surname doubles as British small change — the politics of the surface is hiding two American pieces.

6 down NATTERING

Smashing knight, as bachelor, is having a chat (9)

Definition: having a chat · substitution

  • Smashing → BATTERING a battering ram smashes doors in, so this hands you a nine-letter word to tamper with
  • knight → N N is the knight in chess notation, K being taken by the king

The surface makes 'Smashing' an adjective of praise for the knight. It is the verb a ram does to a door, and one letter of it changes hands.

7 down TOTS

Babies’ fingers (4)

Definition: Babies · double definition

  • Babies → the affectionate word for very small children, the ones still in a highchair
  • fingers → a finger of whisky is a small measure poured into a glass

Two definitions with nothing between them, and the apostrophe glues them into one phrase so they read as a single picture of a baby's hand.

8 down DASH

Depressed about Charles the Second ruin (4)

Definition: ruin · reversal + second letter

  • Depressed → DAS depressed is SAD, and here it is written the other way round
  • Charles → H the second letter along in the name

'Charles the Second' wants to be the king, and it is a counting instruction. The definition is a verb: to dash someone's hopes is to ruin them.

11 down BALLYMEANOCH

Bloody selfish Officer Commanding hospital seen in ancient Scottish settlement (12)

Definition: ancient Scottish settlement · charade

  • Bloody → BALLY bally is the polite old British stand-in for 'bloody', the way 'blooming' is
  • selfish → MEAN a mean person keeps everything for themselves
  • Officer Commanding → OC the army's rank abbreviation for the officer in charge of a unit
  • hospital → H H is hospital on British road signs and maps

Every piece is a common British word or abbreviation, which is the only fair way to clue a name this obscure: the bloody-minded officer of the surface does all the concealing.

13 down FANATICISE

Cool, a jerk I almost recognise gets to become obsessive (10)

Definition: become obsessive · charade + deletion

  • Cool → FAN the thing you switch on to cool a room
  • a → A the letter is lifted straight out of the clue
  • jerk → TIC an involuntary jerk of the face is a tic
  • I → I the letter is lifted straight out of the clue
  • recognise → SE to recognise somebody is to SEE them, and the word stops a letter short

The comma after the first word is doing a lot of work: it stops you reading 'cool a jerk' as one phrase, and five separate little pieces are clued one after another.

14 down LAY HANDS ON

Seize and confirm (3,5,2)

Definition: Seize · double definition

  • Seize → grabbing hold of something bodily, or managing to find it at last
  • confirm → in church a bishop places both hands on the head of each candidate being confirmed

Three words and no wordplay at all. The two senses sit either side of 'and', and the churchy one is the half a solver is least likely to reach for.

16 down LAUNDRESS

Lamela discontented with strip’s cleaner (9)

Definition: cleaner · charade + outer letters

  • Lamela → LA the outer letters of the name, with everything between them gone
  • strip → UNDRESS to strip is to undress

'Discontented' looks like the mood of the person named at the front of the clue. It is an instruction to hollow that name out, and the two halves then meet with nothing added.

21 down AMOUR

Fling mail that’s not readable on the front (5)

Definition: Fling · deletion + first letter

  • mail → ARMOUR mail is the linked metal sort of body protection a knight wore
  • readable → R the opening letter of the word, and the one that goes

'Fling' is not a throw here but a brief romance, and 'mail' is the chain sort rather than the post — two everyday words in disguise before a letter has even moved.

22 down SEMIQUINCENTENNIAL

Quiet men in line can initially start getting excited for significant anniversary (18)

Definition: significant anniversary · anagram + first letter

  • Quiet men in line can → QUIETMENINLINECAN seventeen letters of fodder, taken just as they stand
  • initially start → S the first letter of the word that follows

Eighteen letters is the giveaway: nothing that long is built piece by piece, and the clue hands you seventeen of them in plain sight before asking for one more.
