Across

1 across LAVABO

Bowl of hot stuff with foul smell (6)

Definition: Bowl · charade

  • hot stuff → LAVA molten rock out of a volcano is about as hot as stuff gets
  • foul smell → BO BO is body odour, two polite initials standing in for the smell

BO is the crossword's stock foul smell, and 'hot stuff' reads as innuendo until the volcano turns up. The bowl being defined is a church fitting, which is why the surface can ignore it.

4 across PICADOR

Outspoken choice to be in love with ring combatant (7)

Definition: ring combatant · homophone

  • choice → PIC your pick is your choice, and the K vanishes once you write down only the sound
  • to be in love with → ADOR to adore is to be in love with, and the closing E is not sounded

Two ordinary English words said quickly run together into a Spanish one. 'Ring' is the trap — boxing first, bullfighting second — and only the answer settles which.

9 across WEBMASTER

Techie wordsmith holds advanced degree (9)

Definition: Techie · container

  • wordsmith → WEBSTER Noah Webster wrote the American dictionary that still carries his name
  • advanced degree → MA MA is a Master of Arts, one step past a first degree

Every word of the surface belongs to a CV — techie, holds, advanced degree — and none of it is doing what it appears to. The lexicographer behind 'wordsmith' is the whole solve.

10 across IONIC

Having special powers but uncrowned, like ancient Greeks (5)

Definition: like ancient Greeks · deletion

  • Having special powers → BIONIC bionic limbs give their owner powers no ordinary body has

Losing a crown sounds like abdication, which is why the word sits so comfortably next to the ancient world. It is only the first letter going.

11 across ANGUS

Mr. Young’s beef? (5)

Definition: Mr. Young · double definition

  • Mr. Young → the guitarist in school uniform, Australian by upbringing and Scottish by name
  • beef → the black cattle from the old Scottish county, and the steak they are raised for

The full stop after Mr marks the setter as American. Both halves are Scottish: a common first name that goes with the surname, and a breed named after the county.

12 across LATE LATIN

Language of sections of Rio eschewing contrived alliteration (4,5)

Definition: Language · deletion + anagram

  • alliteration → ALLITERATION twelve letters, and the raw material for everything that follows
  • Rio → RIO R, I and O come out, each one taken from a different place in the twelve

'Sections of Rio' reads as districts of a Brazilian city and 'contrived alliteration' as a complaint about the clue's own writing. Both are instructions, and the long word at the end is the material.

13 across E-WALLET

Renewal letter includes payment aid (1-6)

Definition: payment aid · hidden word

  • Renewal letter → EWALLET seven letters in a row, starting partway into the first word and stopping partway through the second

A renewal letter is such an ordinary piece of post that nothing about it asks to be read letter by letter, which is exactly the cover a hidden word wants.

15 across DEBRIS

Remnants of reversal of Jewish ritual? (6)

Definition: Remnants · charade

  • reversal → DE de- is the prefix that undoes a process: de-ice, de-frost, de-camp
  • Jewish ritual → BRIS a bris is the circumcision ceremony held on a boy's eighth day

'Reversal' looks like an instruction to turn letters round, and obeying it gets you nowhere. The word is being defined rather than obeyed: the setter wants the prefix meaning undo.

17 across NORDIC

Blonde, blue-eyed detectives and Harry’s pal lying about (6)

Definition: Blonde, blue-eyed · charade + reversal

  • detectives → CID the CID is the Criminal Investigation Department, the plain-clothes branch of a British police force
  • Harry’s pal → RON Ron Weasley, Harry Potter's best friend

The surface sounds like Scandinavian television — blond detectives, something nasty in the snow — which is the genre the answer names. Harry Potter has given crosswords a reliable three-letter friend.

19 across SWINGER

Promiscuous sort of small bird? (7)

Definition: Promiscuous sort · charade

  • small → S S is the clothing-label abbreviation for small
  • bird → WINGER one that has wings, coined for the occasion — the question mark owns up to it

A winger is normally a footballer out on the flank. Here the word is taken back to its parts and handed to anything with wings, which is what the question mark apologises for.

22 across TROUSSEAU

Thick-headed Genevan philosopher’s collection of bride’s clothing (9)

Definition: collection of bride’s clothing · charade + first letter

  • Thick-headed → T the letter standing at the head of THICK
  • Genevan philosopher → ROUSSEAU Jean-Jacques Rousseau was born in Geneva in 1712 and signed himself a citizen of it

Because Rousseau called himself a citizen of Geneva, the description is his own words. The insult at the front is aimed at nobody: it is there to hide a single letter.

24 across DECAF

Java, lacking kick, stood up to West (5)

Definition: Java, lacking kick · reversal

  • stood up to → FACED to face someone down is to stand up to them

The definition is longer than it looks. 'Java' on its own would be coffee with all its caffeine present, so the comma and everything after it are doing necessary work.

26 across RHINO

Beast with one horn that’s deformed (5)

Definition: Beast · anagram

  • one → I the Roman numeral for one
  • horn → HORN four letters of fodder, printed as they stand

The surface paints the animal it defines, which is why the fodder is so hard to see. 'One' is the Roman numeral rather than a count of horns.

27 across INSTAGRAM

A migrant’s developed popular app (9)

Definition: popular app · anagram

  • A migrant’s → AMIGRANTS nine letters of fodder, apostrophe and all

A migrant's story and a photo-sharing app have nothing to do with each other, which is the disguise. The count is exact, so the only work is spotting that 'developed' is the instruction.

28 across RATHOLE

Spa location uncovered in part of squalid digs (7)

Definition: squalid digs · container + deletion

  • part → ROLE an actor's part is a role
  • Spa location → ATH Bath, the Somerset city the Romans built their baths in, with its cover taken off

Two ordinary words are used in their less obvious senses at once: 'part' as a stage role, and 'Spa location' as an English city rather than a room in a hotel.

29 across GELATO

Faction of Minnesota legislature recalled dessert (6)

Definition: dessert · hidden word + reversal

  • Minnesota legislature → OTALEG six letters in a row spanning the space between the two words, waiting to be read from the far end

State politics is a good place to hide an Italian pudding: nothing in a legislature asks to be read letter by letter, still less read from the wrong end.

Down

1 down LAWFARE

Underhand tactic, fudging flaw before time’s up (7)

Definition: Underhand tactic · charade + anagram + reversal

  • flaw → LAWF the four letters of FLAW, shuffled
  • time’s → ARE an ERA is a stretch of time, climbing back up the column here

ERA is one of the crossword's workhorses for time, and it turns up climbing as often as the right way up. The word defined is a recent coinage: courts used the way weapons are.

2 down VW BUG

Early Porsche development: frontiers of UV waves on spyware (2,3)

Definition: Early Porsche development · charade + last letter + first letter

  • UV → V the letter standing at that word's border
  • waves → W the letter that word starts with, on the far side of the same border
  • spyware → BUG a bug is the hidden microphone a spy plants

Nothing in the surface admits to being about letters — 'frontiers of UV waves' reads as physics. The car is the one Ferdinand Porsche drew up in the 1930s and the world bought by the million.

3 down BEAN SALAD

Bane: where kidneys may be found? (4,5)

Definition: where kidneys may be found? · anagram

  • Bane → BEAN the same four letters in a different order — the clue prints the jumbled version and wants the tidy one

The clue never says shuffle, because the answer says it: a salad is a mixture, so 'BEAN, mixed' is exactly the word the clue opens with. Setters call this reverse wordplay.

4 down PIRATED

Breathily said: ‘Arsenic’s taken and bootlegged’ (7)

Definition: bootlegged · deletion

  • Breathily said → ASPIRATED a consonant let out with a puff of breath is aspirated — the p at the start of 'pin'
  • Arsenic → AS As is the chemical symbol for arsenic, straight off the periodic table

The quotation marks make it look like reported speech. It is really one long word about pronunciation with a poison lifted out of the front of it.

5 down CHILL

Stick ice cubes in prime minister’s rear? (5)

Definition: Stick ice cubes in · deletion

  • prime minister → CHURCHILL Winston Churchill, Britain's wartime PM, whose name is long enough to carry a short word at its back

The question mark apologises for the liberty: no particular prime minister is named, so you try the famous ones until one of them ends in something meaning cool.

6 down DO-NOTHING

Deadbeat, leaderless knight errant to pursue mobster? Duck! (2-7)

Definition: Deadbeat · charade + deletion + anagram

  • mobster → DON the head of a Mafia family is a don
  • Duck → O a duck in cricket is a score of nought, written on the scoreboard as a zero
  • knight → THING KNIGHT gives up its leading letter and the five that remain are jumbled

Cricket's duck for zero comes up constantly and is worth learning. The surface has a knight chasing a gangster, a picture from two different centuries that belongs to neither.

7 down RECANT

Withdraw from drunken trance (6)

Definition: Withdraw · anagram

  • trance → TRANCE six letters of fodder, exactly as the clue prints them

'Withdraw from' reads as one phrase and wants to be the definition; only the first word is. The drink and the trance are scenery, and the letters were in plain sight.

8 down STYLET

Probe small hovel? (6)

Definition: Probe · charade

  • hovel → STY a pigsty, and by insult any filthy dwelling
  • small → LET -let is the ending that shrinks a thing: booklet, piglet, droplet

The joke is that the two halves say the same thing twice — a small sty — and the question mark admits it. The medical instrument has nothing to do with pigs.

14 down APOLOGIST

Defender in a game point (9)

Definition: Defender · charade

  • a → A the clue's own indefinite article, taken at face value
  • game → POLO the four-letter game played on horseback, a crossword regular
  • point → GIST the gist of an argument is its point

The modern sense of apology pulls the whole clue towards regret; the older sense, a reasoned defence of a position, is the one being defined. At four letters, try POLO for any game.

16 down BLIND DATE

Three vitamins removed from balanced diet, unwise fix-up (5,4)

Definition: fix-up · deletion + anagram

  • balanced diet → BALANCEDDIET twelve letters to start from, and the surface's picture of healthy eating
  • Three vitamins → ACE A, C and E are all vitamins, and all three come out

Take the vitamins out of a balanced diet and what is left spells, pleasingly, a bland one. Those nine letters then rearrange into the evening the definition describes.

18 down CHEMISE

Drug dealer shortened Eastern undergarment (7)

Definition: undergarment · charade + deletion

  • Drug dealer → CHEMIS a chemist in Britain is the pharmacist who dispenses drugs, docked of his last letter here
  • Eastern → E E is the compass abbreviation for eastern

'Drug dealer' is a British chemist's shop rather than a street corner. It is the same joke every setter makes with the word, and worth expecting on sight.

19 down SQUASH

Obliterate vegetable (6)

Definition: Obliterate · double definition

  • Obliterate → to flatten a thing out of existence, under a boot or a thumb
  • vegetable → the gourd family, summer or winter, on every American table in autumn

Two words with nothing in common but their spelling, and the shortest clue in the puzzle. British solvers meet the vegetable sense less often than the drink of the same name.

20 down ROFLMAO

Moral of deviant: ‘That’s hilarious!’ (7)

Definition: That’s hilarious! · anagram

  • Moral of → MORALOF seven letters of fodder — the little 'of' is part of the material, not a joining word

'Of' looks like joinery and is really part of the fodder, which is what makes the count come out. A clue about morals delivering internet slang is the joke.

21 down STARER

PM expels head of monarchy, a rude sort (6)

Definition: a rude sort · deletion

  • PM → STARMER Keir Starmer, the Labour leader who became British prime minister in 2024
  • head of monarchy → M the first letter of the word monarchy, its head

The surface is a joke about a republican prime minister throwing out the crown. The mechanics are quieter: one name with a single letter lifted out of the middle.

23 down SLO-MO

Looms spinning at reduced speed (3-2)

Definition: reduced speed · anagram

  • Looms → LOOMS five letters of fodder, and a weaving shed in the surface

'Looms' wants to be a verb, something looming out of the fog. It is the weaver's machine, and it is the fodder: a plural noun doing an excellent impression of a threat.

25 down CIRCA

Around 5/9ths of what biorhythms are (5)

Definition: Around · deletion

  • what biorhythms are → CIRCADIAN body clocks run on a circadian rhythm, one turn of roughly a day

The fraction does arithmetic on a word the clue never prints: you have to supply the adjective for body clocks yourself, and only then start counting.
