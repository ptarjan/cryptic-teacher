Across

1 across DOORPOST

One stands by swinger cross over something seen on X (8)

Definition: One stands by swinger · charade + reversal

One stands by swinger → DOORPOST: the swinger is a door on its hinges, and the answer is the upright it swings against.

  • cross over → DOOR a rood is a cross, the one mounted on a church's rood screen, and it is turned round here
  • something seen on X → POST X is the site that used to be Twitter, and what people write on it is a post
  • over — Turned over means read the other way about, so the word in front of it is written back to front.

Someone stands beside a swinger who is furious about something on Twitter.

'Cross' reads as the swinger's temper and is really ROOD, the church cross, turned round. The swinger of the definition is not a party guest but something on hinges.

5 across HELIUM

He's "Hot Priest"? I'm not so sure (6)

Definition: He · charade

He → HELIUM: He is the element's chemical symbol, so the clue defines the gas by the two letters the periodic table gives it.

  • Hot → H H is the letter on the hot tap
  • Priest → ELI Eli is the Old Testament priest who brought up Samuel, and the crossword's stock clergyman
  • I'm not so sure → UM the noise you make while you hesitate

Someone doubts that the man in front of them is really television's Hot Priest.

'He' opens the clue looking like a pronoun and is really the two letters chemistry writes for an element, so the whole definition is one symbol long. The Hot Priest belongs to Fleabag.

9 across GRANULAR

Gritty queen and spare king finally moving forward (8)

Definition: Gritty · charade + anagram

Gritty → GRANULAR: gritty means made of grains, and granular is the technical word for that texture, sand rather than courage.

  • queen → R R is Regina, the Latin a queen signs with
  • and spare → ANGULAR a spare frame is a bony, angular one
  • king finally — 'Finally' takes only the last letter of the word in front of it, so 'king' is being read for its G alone.
  • moving forward — Moving forward means shifting towards the front, so the letter it picks up travels to the start of what has been built.

On the chessboard a determined queen and a spare king at last advance.

'Spare' is not the royal understudy but a build: lean, bony, angular. The G that 'king finally' picks out is already sitting inside that word, and it is the only letter that moves.

10 across CASSIS

Erica's sister nurses fruit drink (6)

Definition: fruit drink · hidden word

fruit drink → CASSIS: blackcurrant liqueur or syrup, the fruit cordial that turns a glass of white wine into a Kir.

  • Erica's sister → CASSIS six consecutive letters, starting inside the name and running across the apostrophe into the next word
  • nurses — To nurse something is to hold it close against you, and these words are holding letters that way.

Erica's sister looking after somebody, fruit drink to hand.

Names are where hidden words like to sit, because a solver reads a name as a label rather than as letters. Cassis is blackcurrant, the syrup that colours a Kir.

11 across A BIT MUCH

Hard to tolerate rather dirty husband squeezing out KY (1,3,4)

Definition: Hard to tolerate · charade + deletion

Hard to tolerate → A BIT MUCH: the English understatement for something that has gone past what anyone should have to put up with.

  • rather → ABIT 'rather cold' and 'a bit cold' say the same thing
  • dirty husband → MUCKYH mucky is dirty, and h is the husband of a family tree
  • KY → KY two letters named outright, and they are the ones that go
  • squeezing out — Squeezing something out forces it clear of whatever held it, so the letters named leave the word they were sitting in.

A less than clean husband squeezing the last out of the tube.

Letters are named outright here rather than clued, which is rarer than it looks: KY is not Kentucky but two letters to be got rid of. The tube in the surface does the misleading.

12 across BEIRUT

Capital tour ruined by regularly being knocked back (6)

Definition: Capital · alternate letters + reversal

Capital → BEIRUT: the capital city of Lebanon, so the definition names it only by the class it belongs to.

  • tour ruined by → TURIEB the first, third, fifth and every other letter of these three words, taken in the order they come
  • regularly — Something done regularly happens at a fixed interval, and the letters are picked at a fixed interval too, every other one.
  • being knocked back — A drink knocked back goes the way it came, so the letters just picked out are read from the far end.

A city tour spoiled by drinks being knocked back at every stop.

'Ruined' looks like an anagram instruction and is really fodder: the three words are there to be picked over, not shuffled. Knocking one back is the surface's joke about drinking.

14 across MISSEL TREE

Large plant third son leaves film studio's beauty queen? (6,4)

Definition: Large plant · deletion

Large plant → MISSEL TREE: 'missel' is the old name for mistletoe, and the missel tree is the tree that carries it.

  • film studio's beauty queen? → MISSELSTREE Elstree is the studio north of London, and its pageant winner would be Miss Elstree
  • third son → S s is the abbreviation for son, and there are three of them in that name to choose from
  • leaves — Something that leaves goes away, so the letter attached to it walks out of the words that follow.

A third son walking out on the film studio's beauty queen.

Elstree is where British films have been shot since the 1920s, so a setter can invent a Miss Elstree and expect the name to be recognised. Three S's sit in it, and the third one goes.

18 across AS PER USUAL

Like a drink just like always? (2,3,5)

Definition: just like always · charade

just like always → AS PER USUAL: the slightly weary idiom for something happening the way it always does.

  • Like → AS 'like a lamb' and 'as a lamb' do the same job
  • a → PER five pounds a head is five pounds per head
  • drink → USUAL in a British pub a regular asks for 'the usual' and the barman knows what to pour

A regular at the bar ordering exactly what they always order.

The 'a' is a word to translate rather than an article to skip past, which is the piece solvers walk straight over. The question mark admits how close the definition sits to the wordplay.

22 across HANG ON

Listen very carefully to Remain (4,2)

Definition: Listen very carefully to · double definition

Listen very carefully to → hanging on every word; Remain → staying where you are. One phrasal verb, figurative in the first sense and literal in the second.

  • Listen very carefully to → the phrase for catching every word somebody says
  • Remain → the sense of staying put rather than moving off

Paying very close attention to the Remain campaign.

The capital R on 'Remain' is surface work only: it puts a referendum campaign in the sentence where the parse simply wants a verb meaning to stay put.

23 across PASADENA

City pad Sean organised close to LA (8)

Definition: City · anagram + last letter

City → PASADENA: the city in Los Angeles County that stages the Rose Parade, so 'City' names it by its class alone.

  • pad Sean → PADSEAN seven letters of fodder
  • LA → A the letter that closes the abbreviation
  • organised — Organising something puts it into a new arrangement, so the letters come out in an order the clue never gave them.
  • close to — The close of a word is where it ends, so 'close to LA' asks for the letter that finishes it.

Sean's flat, tidied up, somewhere just outside Los Angeles.

The city really is close to Los Angeles, which is the setter's joke: words that look like a note on where it stands are telling you to take the last letter of LA.

24 across RENOIR

Where one might find better Irish impressionist (6)

Definition: impressionist · charade

impressionist → RENOIR: definition by example, one named painter of the Impressionist school standing in for the whole school.

  • Where one might find better → RENO Reno is the Nevada gambling town, and a better is somebody laying a bet
  • Irish → IR IR is the standard abbreviation for Irish

Somewhere to find a better class of Irish impressionist.

'Better' is not the comparative but a person placing a bet, which is what puts a Nevada gambling town in the clue. 'Impressionist' swaps the comedian for the painter.

25 across DIALOGUE

Europeans try laid back conversation (8)

Definition: conversation · charade + reversal

conversation → DIALOGUE: talk between two sides, the word also used for the formal sort of conversation between governments.

  • Europeans → EU EU is the European Union's own two letters
  • try → GO having a go at something is trying it
  • laid → LAID the word goes in as it stands
  • back — Back sends the letters the other way, and in an across entry that means reading them from the right.

A relaxed attempt by Europeans to get talking.

'Laid back' looks like one word for relaxed, and the clue splits it in two: 'laid' is fodder, and 'back' is the instruction that turns the lot round.

26 across ENSIGN

Tennis big guns periodically flag (6)

Definition: flag · alternate letters

flag → ENSIGN: an ensign is the flag a ship flies to show her nationality, so it is a flag of one particular kind.

  • Tennis big guns → ENSIGN every second letter of these three words, starting with the second
  • periodically — Something done periodically happens at a fixed interval, so the letters are picked at a fixed interval, every second one.

The big hitters of tennis flagging as the season wears on.

'Flag' reads as the verb, to tire, right up until you need it as a noun. The big guns of a sport are its hardest hitters, which is what keeps the sentence upright.

27 across INFANTRY

Abrupt Iberian princess readily discharged soldiers (8)

Definition: soldiers · deletion + charade + outer letters

soldiers → INFANTRY: the foot soldiers of an army, the branch that fights on foot rather than mounted or in armour.

  • Iberian princess → INFANTA the infanta is a daughter of the Spanish or Portuguese king
  • readily → RY the outer letters of READILY, with the middle emptied out
  • Abrupt — An abrupt ending is one cut short, so the word it describes loses its last letter.
  • discharged — Discharging something empties it out, so the word keeps only its outer letters and loses everything between them.

A curt Spanish princess dismissing her guard on the spot.

The title survives in English almost only in crosswords, so 'Iberian princess' is the setter's way of pointing at it. 'Discharged' is a soldier's word doing a setter's job.

Down

1 down DOG-EAR

Turn back and take drugs (3-3)

Definition: Turn back · charade

Turn back → DOG-EAR: folding the corner of a page turns it back on itself, which is exactly what the verb describes.

  • take → DO you do drugs the way you take them, same verb in street dress
  • drugs → GEAR gear is British street slang for drugs

An invitation to turn round and get high.

The hyphen printed in the answer falls after three letters, while the join the wordplay wants comes one letter earlier. Turning back is what you do to a page corner to keep your place.

2 down OKAPIS

American power is supporting fine residents of DRC (6)

Definition: residents of DRC · charade

residents of DRC → OKAPIS: the forest giraffe lives wild in that country's rainforest and nowhere else, so the country alone identifies the animal.

  • fine → OK 'fine' as the answer to 'how are you?' is OK
  • American → A A is the standard abbreviation for American
  • power → P p is power in physics and on the back of a plug
  • is → IS the clue's own word goes straight in, both letters as they stand
  • supporting — To support something is to prop it up from underneath, and in a down clue that puts these letters below the ones already written.

American might throwing its weight behind decent people in the Congo.

DRC is the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and the residents wanted are not people but the striped forest relatives of the giraffe that live in its rainforest.

3 down PLUMMY

First-class setter's rich! (6)

Definition: rich · charade

rich → PLUMMY: of a voice or a taste, deep and over-ripe, which is the sense of rich that has nothing to do with money.

  • First-class → PLUM a plum job or a plum role is the choice, first-class one
  • setter's → MY the setter is the person writing the clue, so what belongs to them is 'my'

The setter boasting of being top of the tree and loaded with it.

Crosswords let the setter speak as 'I', so 'setter's' is simply the possessive of that. The rich being defined is a voice or a pudding, never a bank balance.

4 down STARCHIEST

Most prim actress hit drunk (10)

Definition: Most prim · anagram

Most prim → STARCHIEST: starchy describes somebody stiff and correct, so the superlative is the primmest of the lot.

  • actress hit → ACTRESSHIT ten letters of fodder, exactly the length wanted
  • drunk — A drunk cannot hold a straight line, and these letters do not hold the line they were given either.

An actress the worse for drink takes a swing at somebody.

Starchy means stiff and formal, the way a starched collar is, so its superlative answers 'most prim'. The tabloid brawl in the surface has nothing to do with it.

6 down ELATEDLY

Bishop departs behind schedule in a joyful manner (8)

Definition: in a joyful manner · deletion

in a joyful manner → ELATEDLY: elated is the lifted, high-spirited feeling, and the -ly makes it the manner of doing something.

  • behind schedule → BELATEDLY the nine-letter adverb for doing something after the appointed time
  • Bishop → B B is the bishop on a chess scoresheet
  • departs — A bishop who departs walks out, and his letter walks out of the word in the same way.

A bishop setting off late and in high spirits.

Losing one opening letter turns an adverb of delay into an adverb of joy, and the two words differ by nothing else at all. The B is the chessboard's bishop, not the cathedral's.

7 down INSURERS

Those covering popular and confident Republican succeeded (8)

Definition: Those covering · charade

Those covering → INSURERS: cover is the trade's own word for the protection a policy buys, so the ones who provide it are the coverers.

  • popular → IN whatever is popular this season is in
  • confident → SURE somebody confident of a fact is sure of it
  • Republican → R R is the letter printed beside a Republican's name on a US ballot
  • succeeded → S s is how a peerage table marks who succeeded whom

The popular, confident Republican who took over.

'Covering' is what a policy does, not what a blanket does: the definition is the insurance sense, and the candidate of the surface is there to hide it.

8 down MUSETTES

Dances with character like M on film locations back to back (8)

Definition: Dances · charade + reversal

Dances → MUSETTES: a musette is the bagpipe tune and the rustic dance stepped to it, so the plural names dances.

  • character like M → MU mu is the Greek letter that stands where our M does
  • film locations → SET a set is where a film is shot, and the plural asks for two of them
  • film locations → TES the second one of those, turned round so that the two meet tail to tail
  • on — In a down clue one thing sits on another, so what comes first is written above what follows.
  • back to back — Back to back means the two are turned to meet at their tails, so the second one is written in reverse.

Dancing with a Bond-style spymaster across one film set after another.

A musette is a small French bagpipe and the country dance played on it, which is where the plural comes from. 'Back to back' is doing literal work rather than describing the dancers.

13 down ESCALATION

Build-up of lactose in athlete's initial runs (10)

Definition: Build-up · anagram + first letter

Build-up → ESCALATION: the steady increase of something, a conflict above all, which is a build-up rather than a single jump.

  • lactose in → LACTOSEIN nine letters of fodder
  • athlete's → A the letter this word starts with
  • initial — An initial is the letter a name starts with, so only the first letter of the word before it is wanted.
  • runs — Ink that runs has spread out of shape, and letters told to run will not keep the shape they were given.

Lactose building up in a runner during their first outings on the track.

Lactic acid is what really builds up in a runner's legs; lactose is the milk sugar, and the near-miss between them is what makes the sentence sound medical.

15 down SAWHORSE

One helps the chippy wash cod or skate's wings (8)

Definition: One helps the chippy · anagram + charade + outer letters

One helps the chippy → SAWHORSE: the trestle a carpenter lays timber across to saw it, so it is the chippy's assistant.

  • wash → SAWH the four letters of WASH in a false order
  • or → OR the conjunction is written in as it stands
  • skate's → SE the outermost letters of SKATE
  • cod — Cod means fake, as a cod accent is a put-on one, so what it labels is not the genuine article, letter order included.
  • wings — A bird's wings are on its outside edges, so 'wings' asks for the first and last letters and nothing between.

Getting the fish ready to fry: wash the cod, or the wings off a skate.

A chippy in British slang is a carpenter as well as a fish-and-chip shop, and the definition wants the carpenter and the trestle his timber rests on. 'Cod' is the fake, not the fish.

16 down OPENINGS

Bendy pens go in holes (8)

Definition: holes · anagram

holes → OPENINGS: an opening is a gap through something, which is what a hole is, and the same word covers a vacancy too.

  • pens go in → PENSGOIN eight letters of fodder, the length of the entry
  • Bendy — Something bendy will not hold its shape, and letters that will not hold their shape will not hold their order.

Flexible pens being posted into holes.

'Go in' looks like an instruction to put one word inside another; both words are fodder, and nothing goes inside anything here.

17 down GROOMING

First to grab opportunity in golf cleaning up (8)

Definition: cleaning up · charade + first letter

cleaning up → GROOMING: grooming is the brushing and washing that makes a horse or a person tidy, cleaning up in the literal sense.

  • grab → G the letter this word starts with
  • opportunity → ROOM room for improvement is opportunity for it
  • in → IN the preposition is written in as it stands
  • golf → G golf is G in the radio alphabet that runs alpha, bravo, charlie
  • First to — 'First to' asks only for the letter that leads the word after it, the way an initial does.

The first golfer to seize a chance ends up cleaning up.

'Cleaning up' reads as winning everything going on the course, and the definition wants the other sense, the brushing and washing kind.

19 down DAHLIA

One's blooming trouble had returned (6)

Definition: One's blooming · charade + reversal

One's blooming → DAHLIA: a garden flower is one that blooms, so the definition names it by what it is doing rather than what it is.

  • trouble → AIL to ail somebody is to trouble them
  • had → HAD the word goes in as it stands
  • returned — Something returned comes back the way it went, and in a down clue that means reading the letters upwards.

A complaint that somebody has been nothing but blooming trouble.

'Blooming' does both jobs at once: in the surface it is the mild British oath in 'blooming trouble', and in the parse it is a plant in flower.

20 down HEIGHT

Elevated position where one of four rooks sits (6)

Definition: Elevated position · charade

Elevated position → HEIGHT: a height is a high place, the noun for the elevated spot itself rather than a measurement.

  • where one of four rooks sits → H chess labels its files a to h, and a corner square stands on the h-file
  • where one of four rooks sits → EIGHT the rank of that same corner square, spelled out as a word

A high perch where one of a colony's four rooks settles.

Rooks are birds in the surface and chess pieces in the parse: four of them start a game, one to each corner, and the far corner's square is written out in words.

21 down JAPERY

Judge Judy ultimately accepts mimic runs pranks (6)

Definition: pranks · container

pranks → JAPERY: japery is the business of playing jokes, the activity that a run of pranks adds up to.

  • Judge → J J is how a law report abbreviates a judge's name
  • mimic → APE to ape somebody is to mimic them
  • runs → R r is runs on a cricket scorecard
  • Judy ultimately → Y the last letter of Judy
  • accepts — To accept something is to take it in, so the letters on either side open up and let the rest sit inside.

Judge Judy finally putting up with an impressionist's antics.

Judge Judy is a real television courtroom, which is what lets two letters from two separate jobs read as one person's name. R for runs comes off a cricket scorecard.
