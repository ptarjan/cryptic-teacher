Across

1 across SIMILES

Modified missile they compare (not literally) (7)

Definition: they compare (not literally) · anagram

  • missile → MISSILE anagram fodder, seven letters

The surface reads like a line from an arms report, and the joke is that a weapon gets turned into a figure of speech.

5 across DIAMOND

For Americans, field that’s extremely hard (7)

Definition: For Americans, field · double definition

  • For Americans, field → DIAMOND the baseball infield, named for the shape the four bases mark out
  • extremely hard → DIAMOND the hardest natural substance there is, right at the top of the mineral scale

Both halves are straight definitions, one geographic and one material, and the only misdirection is letting 'hard' sound like a remark about the field.

9 across INDEX

Last part of book, perhaps, some find exciting (5)

Definition: Last part of book, perhaps · hidden word

  • find exciting → INDEX five letters in a row bridge the gap between these two words

Nothing in the surface suggests concealment except 'some', which is carrying a whole instruction while looking like ordinary vagueness.

10 across ANSWERERS

Respondents, as can be seen, covering all points about right (9)

Definition: Respondents · container + charade

  • as can be seen → AS the word AS used exactly as printed, then parted
  • all points → NSWE the four points of the compass, N, S, W and E
  • about → RE re heads a memo and means about, concerning
  • right → R r is the usual abbreviation for right

Compass points are a standard supply of letters and 'all points' asks for the whole set; the order they come out in is fixed by what has to be spelled.

11 across ALLERGENIC

Recalling upset about drug producing bad reaction (10)

Definition: producing bad reaction · anagram

  • Recalling → RECALLING anagram fodder, nine letters
  • drug → E E is the tabloid shorthand for ecstasy, one letter joined to the fodder

The surface is a memory that provokes somebody, which is very nearly the shape of the definition — but the reaction wanted here is a medical one.

12 across CLUB

Arsenal’s example of this weapon (4)

Definition: weapon · double definition

  • weapon → CLUB a heavy stick, the oldest weapon of the lot
  • Arsenal’s example of this → CLUB the north London football side is one instance of the sporting sense

An arsenal is a weapons store, so the sentence holds together perfectly — while the name it is really pointing at belongs to a football ground in north London.

14 across ACCESSORIES

They help thieves steal handbags etc. (11)

Definition: They help thieves steal · double definition

  • They help thieves steal → ACCESSORIES in law, people who assist a crime without carrying it out themselves
  • handbags etc. → ACCESSORIES the bags, belts and scarves that finish an outfit off

One word, two worlds — the criminal courts and the fashion pages — and the clue lays them end to end so that the join disappears.

18 across UNICELLULAR

Like life as simple as possible? I’ll run a clue that’s convoluted (11)

Definition: Like life as simple as possible? · anagram

  • I’ll run a clue → ILLRUNACLUE anagram fodder, eleven letters

The setter's aside about writing a twisty clue is the clue's own raw material, which is the joke: the fodder describes what is being done to it.

21 across ERGO

Soccer goalkeeper goofed thus (once in each half) (4)

Definition: thus · hidden word

  • Soccer goalkeeper → ERGO four consecutive letters bridge the end of one word and the start of the next
  • goofed → the same four letters run again across the join before this word, which is what makes the bracketed promise true

The bracketed aside reads as a match report — a goal let in during each half — and it is really telling you the letters are there twice over.

22 across QUARANTINE

Volume in English about an isolated regime (10)

Definition: isolated regime · container + charade

  • Volume → QUART a quart is a measure of volume, two pints of it
  • an → AN the word an, lifted straight out of the clue
  • in → IN the word in, also used as itself
  • English → E E is the standard abbreviation for English

'Volume' looks like a book, especially standing next to 'English', and it is a liquid measure instead — the swap that makes an unrelated set of pieces read smoothly.

25 across IMITATING

Following one action of union, embracing it (9)

Definition: Following · container + charade

  • one → I the Roman numeral for one
  • action of union → MATING union in the biological sense, and the act of it
  • it → IT the word it, dropped in whole

'Union' reads like a trade union standing next to 'action', and that is the misdirection — the union wanted is the biological one.

26 across AFTER

Succeeding with a more stern position? (5)

Definition: Succeeding · charade

  • stern position → AFT aft is the position towards a ship's stern
  • more → ER the ending that makes an adjective comparative, as in 'sterner'

The question mark flags a nautical joke: at sea this really is a comparative, meaning further towards the stern of the ship.

27 across GHERKIN

Last of aging female’s family that may be prepared to help in a pickle (7)

Definition: may be prepared to help in a pickle · charade + last letter

  • Last of aging → G the final letter of aginG
  • female’s → HER what belongs to a female is hers, three letters of possessive
  • family → KIN kin is family, in the collective sense

'In a pickle' is the idiom for being in trouble, and the definition takes it at face value instead — what goes into the jar.

28 across GODDESS

Beautiful woman performs without love in theatre part (7)

Definition: Beautiful woman · container + deletion

  • performs → DOES performs in the sense of does — she does a fine job
  • love → O love is a score of nothing at tennis, written as a nought, and it is the letter that leaves
  • theatre part → GODS the gods are the highest and cheapest gallery in a theatre

'Theatre part' looks like a role and turns out to be a place in the building — the gallery up under the roof that everybody calls the gods.

Down

1 down SPINAL

Bias added to a line in personal kind of column (6)

Definition: personal kind of column · charade

  • Bias → SPIN spin is the bias a press officer puts on a story
  • a → A the word a, taken exactly as it stands
  • line → L l is the usual abbreviation for line, as on a map or in a script

Three kinds of column are in play at once: the newspaper sort the surface is hinting at, the architectural sort, and the one every person carries about inside them.

2 down MIDDLE

Waist hit when batting – precisely (6)

Definition: Waist · double definition

  • Waist → MIDDLE the part of the body a belt goes round
  • hit when batting – precisely → MIDDLE a cricketer who strikes the ball off the dead centre of the bat has done this to it

The surface has a batter taking a blow on the body, which lets two unrelated senses sit in one sentence: one anatomical, one straight off the bat.

3 down LUXURIATED

Heard you and I ruled tax out of order, had it very easy (10)

Definition: had it very easy · anagram + homophone

  • Heard you and I → UI said aloud, the letters U and I are exactly those two pronouns
  • ruled tax → RULEDTAX anagram fodder, eight letters, joined by the two letters heard above

'Heard' is doing the work that makes the sums add up: eight letters of fodder plus the two you can only get by saying the pronouns aloud.

4 down SPADE

Tool that’s sharp but may be said to be blunt (5)

Definition: Tool that’s sharp but may be said to be blunt · cryptic definition

  • Tool that’s sharp → the surface promises a digging implement with a keen edge, and that much is plainly true of the thing
  • may be said to be blunt → 'said' is the hinge — there is a stock phrase about naming a thing by its plainest name, and using it is what counts as speaking bluntly

A card suit runs right through this puzzle, and the pointed black symbol on the cards is sharp in a second way the clue never quite admits to.

5 down DISMISSAL

Firing careless shot might result in this (9)

Definition: Firing · double definition

  • Firing → DISMISSAL the formal word for being let go from a job
  • careless shot might result in this → DISMISSAL in cricket a rash stroke gets a batter out, and this is how the scorecard records it

'Firing' and 'shot' both pull the surface towards a gun, and neither sense of the answer has anything to do with one — the second belongs to cricket.

6 down ACED

Perfectly performed in most social classes (4)

Definition: Perfectly performed · charade + deletion

  • social classes → ACED Britain's social grades are lettered A, B, C, D and E, and four of those five letters are what get strung together

Social grade is one of the crossword's quieter conventions — A to E, straight off a market researcher's form — and here it supplies every letter of the answer.

7 down OVERLAID

Covered as a learner, I drove erratically (8)

Definition: Covered · anagram

  • a learner → AL a stays as it is, and a learner driver shows an L plate
  • I drove → IDROVE the remaining six letters of the fodder

The L-plate is the piece to spot: 'learner' is one of the reliable single-letter conventions, hiding here inside a sentence that is entirely about driving.

8 down DISABUSE

A coach secured by side in disarray? Correct (8)

Definition: Correct · anagram + container

  • side → DISE the four letters of SIDE shaken up, then opened out to make room
  • A → A the first word of the clue, used as itself
  • coach → BUS a coach is a bus — the vehicle, not the trainer the surface offers

'Correct' is a verb here, the sort you do to a person rather than to a proof, and 'coach' is the pivot the whole sporting surface turns on.

13 down FOUR-IN-HAND

It takes several horses to move it inches? (4-2-4)

Definition: It takes several horses to move it inches? · cryptic definition

  • It takes several horses to move it → the surface offers something too heavy to shift; the setter means a carriage pulled by a team, and the enumeration counts the team
  • inches? → horses are measured in hands, and the question is how many inches make one of those units — read as a plain sentence, the answer replies

Read as an ordinary phrase the answer is arithmetic, so many inches to a hand; read as a noun it is a carriage and its team. The clue wants both at once.

15 down COLLUSION

Complicity in conflict, having change of heart (9)

Definition: Complicity · substitution

  • conflict → COLLISION a conflict between two things is a collision
  • having change of heart → U the fifth of those nine letters steps aside and U takes the vacant place

The surface is a peace-talks headline, where a change of heart would be a change of mind; here it is strictly the letter sitting in the middle.

16 down QUEENING

Being promoted on board – for French, that upset good number (8)

Definition: Being promoted on board · charade + reversal

  • for French, that → QUE the French word for 'that'
  • good → G g is the standard abbreviation for good, as on a school report
  • number → NINE the number is left unnamed, and the count of letters still to fill settles which one it is

'On board' is the chessboard: a pawn reaching the far rank is promoted, and the piece almost everyone chooses gives the manoeuvre its name.

17 down RINGSIDE

Faction supporting gang in area close to fighting (8)

Definition: area close to fighting · charade

  • gang → RING a ring is an organised gang — a drugs ring, a betting ring
  • Faction → SIDE a side is a faction in a dispute

Boxing is nowhere in the surface, which is all street gangs, and the ring that supplies the first half is a criminal one rather than the roped kind.

19 down LITTLE

Short rhyme for it will, when spoken quickly (6)

Definition: Short · homophone

  • it will, when spoken quickly → LITTLE run together in speech those two words become a single contraction, and the answer is the word that chimes with it

This is a rhyme rather than a strict soundalike: the clue hands over the ending and leaves you to supply the letter that starts the word off.

20 down HEARTS

Emoji used by wooer in suit (6)

Definition: suit · double definition

  • suit → HEARTS one of the two red suits in a pack of cards
  • Emoji used by wooer → HEARTS the red symbol a suitor sends by text

The suit and the emoji are the same picture, which is the whole point: a romantic text and a hand of cards get by on one symbol between them.

23 down REGAG

Once more restrict speech, for example in newspaper (5)

Definition: Once more restrict speech · container

  • newspaper → RAG a rag is a slighting word for a newspaper, and it opens between its first and second letters
  • for example → EG e.g. is the everyday shorthand for 'for example'

The definition runs to four words before any wordplay starts, so the sentence about jokes in the press is doing nothing but hiding two short pieces.

24 down JACK

Device for lifting injured hill-climber (4)

Definition: Device for lifting · double definition

  • Device for lifting → JACK the screw device that raises a car off the ground to change a wheel
  • injured hill-climber → JACK the boy of the nursery rhyme who fell down and broke his crown

The second definition is a person rather than a thing: the nursery rhyme supplies the name, and the tumble down the slope supplies the injury.
