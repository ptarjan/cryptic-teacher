Across

1 across ADORABLE

Lovely lady more fabled when bare (8)

Definition: Lovely · charade + middle letters

  • lady → AD lADy with its outside letters taken off
  • more → OR mORe stripped down to its middle pair
  • fabled → ABLE fABLEd loses the f at the front and the d at the back

One instruction is doing the same job three times over, which is what lets the sentence read as praise for a lady rather than as an order.

5 across ABLOOM

A surge in activity catches learner out (6)

Definition: out · container + charade

  • A → A the clue's opening word joins in as a single letter
  • surge in activity → BOOM a boom is a sudden rush of growth or business
  • learner → L L is the plate a learner driver carries

The surface is a business report, with a boom and a learner sent packing, and none of it survives into an answer that is about flowers.

10 across TREAT

Negotiate withdrawal after soldiers go AWOL (5)

Definition: Negotiate · deletion

  • withdrawal → RETREAT a withdrawal of troops is a retreat
  • soldiers → RE RE is the Royal Engineers, the crossword's standing army

The letters that march off are the army's own, which is the joke: no retreat is possible once the soldiers have already gone.

11 across A GOOD DEAL

Much value for money (1,4,4)

Definition: Much · double definition

  • Much → the quantity idiom — a good deal of trouble, a good deal older
  • value for money → the shopper's sense: a bargain is a deal that is good

Four words and no seam to look for: the whole clue is one phrase read twice, once about quantity and once about buying.

12 across CLAMSHELL

Within enclosed chamber, metres inside, tie down stateside digging equipment (9)

Definition: stateside digging equipment · container

  • enclosed chamber → CELL a cell is a small shut room
  • tie down → LASH to lash a load down is to tie it fast
  • metres → M m is the symbol for metres

Two insertions, one nested in the other, which is why the clue reads like a stack of orders; 'stateside' is the warning that this bucket is American usage.

14 across VIALS

By means of leaks, drained bottles (5)

Definition: bottles · charade + outer letters

  • By means of → VIA via means by way of — travelling via Paris
  • leaks → LS the outside letters of LeakS

Small glass bottles for medicine are what is wanted, not wine bottles; the leak in the surface is there to make 'drained' look like scenery.

15 across AS THE CROW FLIES

Direct reorganisation of Welsh factories (2,3,4,5)

Definition: Direct · anagram

  • Welsh factories → WELSHFACTORIES the fourteen letters of the two words are the whole of the raw material

The enumeration is the way in: fourteen letters are too many to shuffle by eye, so let 2,3,4,5 suggest the shape of the phrase first.

18 across WORDS PER MINUTE

Crazily, Trump worsened international speed of data capture (5,3,6)

Definition: speed of data capture · anagram

  • Trump worsened → TRUMPWORSENED the thirteen letters of the two words are the fodder
  • international → I I is the standard abbreviation for international

'Data capture' is the office phrase for getting text into a computer, and typing has been timed this way since the typewriter.

22 across LEPER

Charitable person accommodates pariah (5)

Definition: pariah · hidden word

  • Charitable person → LEPER the run bridges charitabLE and PERson

A charitable person is exactly who would take one in, so surface and mechanism agree for once — which is what makes the join so hard to spot.

23 across BILL GATES

Big US donor's attendance restricted by what he calls checks (4,5)

Definition: Big US donor · container

  • attendance → GATE the gate is the crowd counted through the turnstiles at a match
  • what he calls checks → BILLS an American asks the waiter for the check where a Briton asks for the bill

'Big US donor' names one man rather than a type, and the 'he' in the wordplay quietly confirms that a person is wanted.

25 across INSTAGRAM

Platform One at northern station - note crush (9)

Definition: Platform · charade

  • One → I one is the Roman numeral I
  • northern → N N is the compass abbreviation for northern
  • station → STA STA is how a timetable shortens station
  • note → G G is a note of the musical scale
  • crush → RAM to ram is to force or crush together

The surface is a railway announcement built out of the abbreviations a timetable really uses, which is why a very modern answer hides so well in it.

26 across ID EST

That's the thing about poor grades, son (2,3)

Definition: That's · container + charade

  • the thing → IT 'the thing' is simply it
  • poor grades → DE a D and an E are the poor marks on a school report
  • son → S s is the standard abbreviation for son

Latin, and the enumeration 2,3 is the tell: two short words, which in English we usually write as the abbreviation i.e.

27 across HARASS

Fergie returns to confront start of soccer trouble (6)

Definition: trouble · reversal + charade + first letter

  • Fergie → HARAS Fergie is Sarah Ferguson, and SARAH comes back the other way
  • start of soccer → S the opening letter of Soccer

'Fergie' is two people on the sports pages, the Duchess and the football manager, and the soccer in the surface points firmly at the wrong one.

28 across ASWAN DAM

First man to bring in flier for viewing African landmark (5,3)

Definition: African landmark · container

  • First man → ADAM Adam is the first man of Genesis
  • flier → SWAN a swan is heavy but a perfectly good flier

The surface is a holiday snapshot, complete with a leaflet, and that is what makes the biblical reading so easy to walk past.

Down

1 down AUTOCRAT

Supremo's order to a curate finally cancelled (8)

Definition: Supremo · anagram + deletion

  • to a → TOA the two small words go into the mix exactly as they stand
  • curate finally cancelled → CURAT CURATE with its closing E struck out

The surface is a boss barking an instruction; the wordplay wants 'order' in the other sense, the one a librarian means.

2 down OVERACT

Trace very old chopped ham (7)

Definition: ham · anagram

  • Trace → TRACE the word itself is the raw material
  • very → V V is the standard short form of very
  • old → O O is old in the abbreviation book, as in OE for Old English

Everything before the definition reads like a shopping list — trace amounts, aged, diced — and none of it is about food at all.

3 down APT

School organisation cycling to get fit (3)

Definition: fit · cycling

  • School organisation → PTA the PTA, the parent-teacher association every school has

The surface has the committee out on a bike to keep in shape, while the wordplay only wants its initials rolled round one place.

4 down LEAVE

Part from grille a vent covers (5)

Definition: Part from · hidden word

  • grille a vent → LEAVE the run starts inside grilLE and finishes in A VEnt

The airbrick in the surface is there to hide a join: the letters straddle the gap between two words, which is where the eye slides past them.

6 down BEDEVILLING

Cockney chap was flipping covered in pearly tat, maybe being a nuisance (11)

Definition: being a nuisance · container + charade + reversal

  • Cockney chap → E a Cockney drops the aitch, so 'he' comes out as 'e
  • was → DEVIL he was is he LIVED, and that word turned round is the fiend
  • pearly tat, maybe → BLING bling is showy jewellery, and pearls are one example of it — 'maybe' flags the example

Pearly kings and queens are Cockneys themselves, so the sentence hangs together beautifully while each of its words quietly does a different job.

7 down OVERATE

Took too much on at once, ultimately (7)

Definition: Took too much · charade + last letter

  • on → OVER a row on money is a row over money — the two prepositions swap freely
  • at → AT the little word is dropped in whole
  • once → E the closing letter of oncE

'On' for 'over' looks unfair until you try the pair in a sentence: an argument on pay is an argument over pay.

8 down MOLEST

Attack using half of bullets, perhaps, in case (6)

Definition: Attack · charade + deletion

  • half of bullets, perhaps → MO bullets are an example of AMMO, and the back half of that is MO
  • in case → LEST lest means in case, as in 'lest we forget'

'Perhaps' flags that bullets are only one example of what is wanted, and 'in case' is a whole phrase rather than the insertion instruction it looks like.

9 down FOLLOWER

Most of religious community let down disciple (8)

Definition: disciple · charade + deletion

  • Most of religious community → FOL a religious community is a FOLD, and most of it is FOL
  • let down → LOWER to let a bucket down a well is to lower it

'Let down' is doing its literal work here — lowering on a rope, not disappointing — and that swap is what the surface is banking on.

13 down SPEED FREAKS

Drug users mistakenly freed in talks (5,6)

Definition: Drug users · anagram

  • freed → FREED the word goes into the mix as it stands
  • talks → SPEAKS talks and speaks are the same verb in the same person

The surface is a courtroom, with prisoners let go after negotiations, and 'talks' has to become a synonym before any of its letters are useful.

16 down ROPE BURN

Borne up bed of glacier, suffering climbing injury (4,4)

Definition: climbing injury · anagram + last letter

  • Borne up → BORNEUP both words go into the mix as plain letters
  • bed of glacier → R the bottom letter of glacieR

In a down clue 'climbing' looks exactly like an instruction to write something upwards, and here it belongs to the definition instead.

17 down NEWS ITEM

Piece of paper? (4,4)

Definition: Piece of paper? · cryptic definition

  • Piece of paper? → the surface offers a sheet or a scrap you could write on, and that is the picture to get out of your head
  • Piece → in a newsroom a piece is an article — the thing a reporter files
  • paper → the paper is the daily one, so the pieces in it are the stories it prints

There is no fodder to find and nothing to take apart: the whole clue is one description read two ways, and the question mark is the setter owning up.

19 down OPPOSER

One facing problem, weighed down by work (7)

Definition: One facing · charade

  • work → OP OP is a composer's opus, the crossword's standard short 'work'
  • problem → POSER a poser is a question that stumps you

The surface is an overworked employee with a problem, and the answer has nothing to do with tiredness — 'work' is doing an abbreviation's job.

20 down UPTREND

Wrong turn oddly heralds finish of economic improvement (7)

Definition: economic improvement · charade + alternate letters

  • Wrong → UP when something is up, something is wrong — 'what's up?'
  • turn oddly → TR the first and third letters of TuRn
  • finish → END the end of anything is its finish

'Wrong' for UP is the best trick here — it is the 'something's up' sense, not the direction, which a down entry makes very tempting.

21 down BLUISH

A bit raunchy? I show embarrassment outwardly (6)

Definition: A bit raunchy? · container

  • I → I the pronoun is taken as the letter it is written with
  • show embarrassment → BLUSH to blush is to show embarrassment in the face

Blue is the colour of the smutty joke, so a shade of it is a shade of rudeness, which is the sense the definition is after.

24 down LIMES

Trees in locations bisected by motorway (5)

Definition: Trees · container

  • locations → LIES where a thing lies is where it is — the lie of the land
  • motorway → M M is the prefix on every British motorway number

These are the tall avenue trees of a park, not the fruit, which is how the clue gets away with an unqualified 'trees'.

26 down IDA

Reportedly, you can get down from this mountain (3)

Definition: mountain · homophone

  • you can get down from this → IDA the bird whose breast feathers make eiderdown is the eider, and saying that name gives the peak

Mount Ida overlooks Crete, and the 'down' of the clue is a duck's soft under-feathers rather than a direction of travel.
