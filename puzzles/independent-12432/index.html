Across

1 across ROLLING STONES

Rock group going downhill? (7,6)

Definition: Rock group going downhill? · cryptic definition

  • Rock group → the surface sells a band: musicians who play rock
  • going downhill? → read the first two words literally instead and you have lumps of stone on a slope, which can only be doing one thing

There is no wordplay to find — the whole clue is one picture. A group of rocks heading down a slope is doing exactly what the band's name says.

8 across BLAG

Scrounge from bishop prior to arrest (4)

Definition: Scrounge · charade

  • bishop → B B is the bishop on a chessboard
  • arrest → LAG to lag someone is old slang for having them put away

'Lag' is prison slang from the days of transportation — a lag is a convict — and the surface hides it behind a churchman being arrested.

9 across INTERTWINE

Blend still Champagne, say, around back of restaurant (10)

Definition: Blend · container + charade + last letter

  • still → INERT still = inert, going nowhere
  • back of restaurant → T the last letter of restauranT, which goes inside
  • Champagne, say → WINE champagne is a wine — 'say' marks it as one example

'Say' is the standard flag for a definition by example. The surface is a barman's recipe, which is what lets 'still' pass as a word about the drink.

10 across APOLLO

God is a chicken in Spain (6)

Definition: God · charade

  • a → A the clue's own a, taken as it stands
  • chicken in Spain → POLLO pollo is Spanish for chicken

The surface accuses a deity of cowardice — 'chicken' in the English sense — while the wordplay wants the Spanish word for the bird on the menu.

11 across ESTEEMED

Renowned revolutionary has an encounter in river (8)

Definition: Renowned · container + reversal

  • river → DEE the Dee, the crossword's handiest short river — it holds the meeting
  • has an encounter → MEETS to have an encounter with somebody is to meet them

'Revolutionary' is the instruction to turn the whole assembly round, not a Che or a Red — and that misreading is the trap the surface sets.

12 across PERTUSSIS

Lively American relative's dangerous illness (9)

Definition: dangerous illness · charade

  • Lively → PERT pert = lively, a touch cheeky with it
  • American → US US is the usual abbreviation
  • relative → SIS sis is a sister, informally

The apostrophe-s is joinery standing for 'is', not a possessive. Pertussis is whooping cough, so the definition is not being dramatic.

14 across DIVE

Beginning to document setter's fall (4)

Definition: fall · charade + first letter

  • document → D the first letter of Document
  • setter's → IVE the setter speaks as I, so 'setter's' contracts to I've

'Setter' is the person who wrote the puzzle, so it means I — a first-person trick that recurs constantly and is worth learning early.

15 across BLEW

Failed to take down broadcast (4)

Definition: Failed to take · homophone

  • down → BLEW down in the sense of gloomy is BLUE

'Broadcast' is the sound signal here rather than anything to do with television, and 'down' wants the mood, not the direction.

16 across AGINCOURT

A drink date that leads to conflict (9)

Definition: conflict · charade

  • A → A the clue's own opening A
  • drink → GIN gin is the crossword's default drink
  • date → COURT to court someone is the old-fashioned way of dating them

'Date' wants the verb, not the diary entry. The surface promises a night out and delivers a battlefield in Picardy in 1415.

20 across GOALPOST

Supporter on football pitch to attempt a record most initially ignored (8)

Definition: Supporter on football pitch · charade + deletion

  • attempt → GO a go is an attempt
  • a → A the clue's own a
  • record → LP an LP is a long-playing record
  • most → OST MOST with its own initial letter ignored

LP for 'record' is worth banking — the long-playing vinyl album. The surface pretends to be somebody chasing a record on the pitch.

21 across MINUTE

Tiny amphibian belonging to me picked up (6)

Definition: Tiny · homophone

  • amphibian belonging to me → MINUTE the amphibian is a NEWT and it belongs to ME, so 'my newt' — which run together is the answer

'Picked up' means overheard, not lifted. The join is where the trick lives: two words of wordplay collapse into one word of answer.

23 across LAUNDRETTE

Dicky learnt duet in cleaner's (10)

Definition: cleaner's · anagram

  • learnt duet → LEARNTDUET anagram fodder — ten letters
  • Dicky → anagram indicator: dicky is British for shaky or unsound

'Dicky' means unsound — a dicky heart, a dicky knee — and here it is the shuffling instruction wearing a man's name.

24 across CALM

Local man keeps cool (4)

Definition: cool · hidden word

  • Local man → CALM loCAL Man, running across the gap between the words

'Keeps' is the concealment word — what keeps a thing holds it inside — and the answer straddles two words rather than sitting inside one.

25 across SADDAM HUSSEIN

Old tyrant uses handmaid's pants (6,7)

Definition: Old tyrant · anagram

  • uses handmaid's → USESHANDMAIDS anagram fodder — thirteen letters, apostrophe and all
  • pants → anagram indicator: pants is British slang for rubbish

'Pants' means rubbish in modern British slang, and rubbished letters are letters out of order. The Handmaid's Tale in the surface is fine misdirection.

Down

1 down RELAPSE

Returning more ashen, starts to suffer extreme setback (7)

Definition: setback · charade + reversal + first letters

  • more ashen → RELAP more ashen is PALER, written the other way round
  • suffer extreme → SE the starting letters of both words

The surface is a patient looking worse, which is the very world a relapse belongs to — so the definition hides in plain sight at the end.

2 down LEGAL

Stage naked ramble? It's allowed (5)

Definition: allowed · charade + middle letters

  • Stage → LEG a leg is a stage of a journey or a race
  • ramble → AL a ramble is a WALK, and a naked one has lost its outside letters

'Naked' is a stripping instruction: a word with its clothes off keeps only its middle. The surface is Britain's naked rambler and whether the law could touch him.

3 down IN IRONS

Popular English actor is heading directly into the wind (2,5)

Definition: heading directly into the wind · charade

  • Popular → IN in = fashionable, what everyone likes
  • English actor → IRONS Jeremy Irons, so the surname alone does the work

A surname standing in for its owner is a staple of the Independent. The definition sounds like weather and is in fact sailing jargon.

4 down GATHERING STITCH

Sewers sometimes require this decongestant, ultimately, long after parties (9,6)

Definition: Sewers sometimes require this · charade + last letter

  • parties → GATHERINGS a gathering is a party, and the plural is wanted
  • decongestant, ultimately → T the last letter of decongestanT
  • long → ITCH to long for something is to itch for it

'Sewers' here are people who sew, not drains, and anyone picturing plumbing has been caught. 'Long' is doing duty as a verb, which is where the tail of the answer hides.

5 down TURN TO

Result in wife abandoning second shot? (4,2)

Definition: Result in · deletion

  • second shot? → TURNTWO if a shot is a turn, the shot that comes second is turn two
  • wife → W W is the standard abbreviation for wife — and it is the letter that leaves

The question mark is the setter conceding that numbering a go this way is a liberty. The surface reads as a golfer whose partner has walked off.

6 down NEW MEXICO

Ox mince we fancy in part of America (3,6)

Definition: part of America · anagram

  • Ox mince we → OXMINCEWE anagram fodder — nine letters to rearrange
  • fancy → anagram indicator: fancy = elaborate, dressed up differently

'Fancy' sits where an adjective describing the mince would go, which is what keeps it from looking like an instruction to shuffle.

7 down SINCERE

Frank is following on (7)

Definition: Frank · charade

  • following → SINCE following the war, since the war — the same stretch of time
  • on → RE RE means on, as in a memo re expenses

'Frank' looks like a man's name in the surface, which is the whole disguise. RE for 'on' is a workhorse abbreviation worth banking.

13 down TREE-LINED

Mistakenly enter deli with limes on either side? (4-5)

Definition: with limes on either side? · anagram

  • enter deli → ENTERDELI anagram fodder — nine letters, in plain sight
  • Mistakenly → anagram indicator: the letters have been entered wrongly

Limes here are trees, not fruit — the lime avenues of an English park — and seeing that is the whole clue. The 4-5 enumeration means a hyphen.

15 down BIOMASS

Source of energy from Isle of Man fish stocks (7)

Definition: Source of energy · container

  • fish → BASS a bass is a fish, and it takes the island inside
  • Isle of Man → IOM IOM is the usual short form of the Isle of Man

'Stocks' is the container word — the old wooden frame that held a prisoner fast — while the surface offers the fish stocks a trawlerman worries about.

17 down NUMBERS

Book anaesthetists (7)

Definition: Book · double definition

  • Book → the fourth book of the Old Testament, after Leviticus
  • anaesthetists → split the word after the fourth letter and you have people who make you numb

Two words, no letters to assemble. One is straight out of the Pentateuch; the other is a joke about how the answer divides.

18 down RITALIN

Drug Republican from Rome, possibly, ignoring American (7)

Definition: Drug · charade + deletion

  • Republican → R R is the party's letter in American politics
  • from Rome, possibly → ITALIAN somebody from Rome is an Italian — 'possibly' concedes it is one example among many
  • American → A A for American, and it is the letter that is ignored

Both political words are abbreviations in disguise: one arrives as a letter, the other leaves as one, and neither means what the surface implies.

19 down CORREA

Blimey! Arsenal regularly making bloomer (6)

Definition: bloomer · charade + alternate letters

  • Blimey! → COR cor is the exclamation blimey usually follows
  • Arsenal → REA the regular letters of aRsEnAl

The surface is a fan groaning at a howler, which is the other sense of 'bloomer'. Here the word wants a plant that does the blooming.

22 down NICHE

Hearts blocking French football club's corner (5)

Definition: corner · container

  • French football club → NICE Nice, the Ligue 1 side from the Riviera
  • Hearts → H H is hearts on a bridge scorecard

Hearts is a Scottish football club as well, which is the misdirection — here the word wants the card suit, and only its letter.
