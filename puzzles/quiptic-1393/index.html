Across

1 across SPANISH

Resort name is hard in European language (7)

Definition: European language · charade

  • Resort → SPA a spa = a resort town
  • name → N n = name, as on a form
  • is → IS lifted straight into the answer
  • hard → H H = hard, the pencil grade

Two single-letter abbreviations do the quiet work: N for 'name' and H for 'hard', from pencil grading. 'Is' rides into the answer unchanged — a move worth learning to spot.

5 across SATCHEL

Bag for books put in stores at Cheltenham (7)

Definition: Bag for books · hidden word

  • stores at Cheltenham → SATCHEL hidden inside: storeS AT CHELtenham

'Put in' flags a hidden word: the bag is packed inside the shopping phrase, which is pure camouflage.

10 across PAIR

Mention of orchard fruit for couple (4)

Definition: couple · homophone

  • orchard fruit → PAIR a pear is the orchard fruit

'Mention of' asks for the sound rather than the spelling: PEAR and PAIR are perfect homophones, and the grid wants the one meaning two.

11 across BELL-RINGER

One regularly making changes? (4-6)

Definition: One regularly making changes? · cryptic definition

  • making changes → not alterations, which is what the surface offers: in campanology a 'change' is one fixed order in which a set of church bells is struck
  • One regularly → the changes come round in sequence, over and over — so this is a person with a weekly practice night, not someone reorganising an office

A cryptic definition: 'changes' sounds like alterations, but in campanology a change is a sequence rung on church bells, so this person makes changes every practice night.

12 across DERAIL

Throw off course dishonest type with journalist when backsliding (6)

Definition: Throw off course · charade + reversal

  • dishonest type → RAIL LIAR, turned around in the answer
  • journalist → DE ED (editor), turned around in the answer

Build LIAR + ED — the abbreviated editor is the crossword's stock journalist — then 'backsliding' turns the whole thing tail-first to send it off the rails.

13 across IMPROPER

This person’s to thrive after leaving society that’s not acceptable (8)

Definition: not acceptable · charade + deletion

  • This person’s → IM 'this person is' — the setter speaking — reads I'M
  • to thrive after leaving society → PROPER PROSPER ('to thrive') with S for 'society' taken out

'This person' is the setter, so 'this person's' compresses to I'M; S is the standard abbreviation for society, and PROSPER loses it.

14 across ENCOURAGE

Rally those heading each new company before this period? (9)

Definition: Rally · charade + first letters

  • each new company → ENC the heading (first) letters of Each New Company
  • this period → OURAGE read as two words: OUR AGE, the time we live in

'Those heading' harvests three initials at once, and 'this period' has to be re-spaced as OUR AGE — the era we are living through.

16 across GRATE

Reportedly, substantial framework of bars (5)

Definition: framework of bars · homophone

  • substantial → GRATE great means substantial

'Reportedly' asks for the sound: say GREAT aloud and you hear the fireplace fitting.

17 across SPITE

First of police in place showing deliberate nastiness (5)

Definition: deliberate nastiness · container + first letter

  • police → P its first letter
  • place → SITE a place = a site

'First of' takes the single P, and 'in' does the placing: it goes inside SITE, not after it.

19 across DOUBLE TOP

Score in darts led pub too to get excited (6,3)

Definition: Score in darts · anagram

  • led pub too → LEDPUBTOO anagram fodder, nine letters
  • to get excited → anagram indicator

The pub surface is a wink at where darts actually gets played, and it swallows the fodder whole — three innocent words in a row.

23 across NON-EVENT

Drab outcome at work still in area around Darwin (3-5)

Definition: Drab outcome · charade + container

  • at work → ON at work = on, operating
  • still → EVEN still = even, as in 'still more'
  • area around Darwin → NT the Northern Territory, whose capital is Darwin — it wraps the rest

Darwin is the misdirection: the naturalist springs to mind, but it's the Australian city, and NT — the area around it — encloses everything else.

24 across FABLED

Fine sailor was ahead of others, being legendary (6)

Definition: legendary · charade

  • Fine → F F = fine, the pencil grade
  • sailor → AB AB = able seaman, the crossword's stock sailor
  • was ahead of others → LED led the field

Two abbreviations every solver ends up learning: F for fine, from pencil grading, and AB for the able-bodied seaman.

26 across PARANORMAL

Old man managed by soldiers badly in France, defying rational explanation? (10)

Definition: defying rational explanation? · charade

  • Old man → PA pa = father
  • managed → RAN ran the show
  • soldiers → OR OR = other ranks, ordinary soldiers
  • badly in France → MAL the French for 'badly'

A four-part charade built from crossword old faithfuls — PA, OR for other ranks, and a scrap of schoolroom French to finish.

27 across NEED

Requirement once ready at intervals (4)

Definition: Requirement · alternate letters

  • once → NE the even-placed letters of oNcE
  • ready → ED the even-placed letters of rEaDy

'At intervals' means take every other letter — here the second and fourth of each word.

28 across RELEASE

Put out note before rental agreement (7)

Definition: Put out · charade

  • note → RE re, the second note of the sol-fa scale
  • rental agreement → LEASE a lease = a rental agreement

'Note' in a cryptic almost always means the sol-fa scale — do, re, mi — or the A to G of music. It is one of the most reliable two-letter sources there is.

29 across ADULATE

Flatter American scheduled to tour Californian city with tons (7)

Definition: Flatter · charade + container

  • American → A A = American
  • scheduled → DUE opens up to admit LA and T
  • Californian city → LA Los Angeles
  • tons → T t = tons

'To tour' asks one word to travel around others, and the traveller splits in half to do it — a container that opens in the middle rather than at one end. The surface is a straightforward itinerary.

2 down PHASE IN

Gradually introduce heaps for repair at home (5,2)

Definition: Gradually introduce · anagram + charade

  • heaps → PHASE anagram of HEAPS, 'for repair'
  • at home → IN at home = in

'For repair' is the anagram signal — broken things need repairing — and 'at home' = IN is one of the commonest small fillers in cryptic wordplay.

3 down NORMA

Woman found in Oregon or Maine (5)

Definition: Woman · hidden word

  • Oregon or Maine → NORMA hidden inside: OregoN OR MAine

'Found in' says the answer is sitting in plain sight; the two American states exist only to carry the hidden letters.

4 down SUBTLER

More delicate sort of troubles, nothing less (7)

Definition: More delicate · anagram + deletion

  • troubles, nothing less → TRUBLES TROUBLES with the O removed — 'nothing' is the letter O, 'less' takes it away
  • sort of → anagram indicator

'Nothing' meaning the letter O is a staple of cryptic subtraction: TROUBLES loses its O, and 'sort of' rearranges the seven letters that remain.

6 down ABRUPT

Fancy bar elevated front in Torquay? That’s unexpected (6)

Definition: unexpected · anagram + first letter

  • bar → BAR anagram fodder
  • elevated → UP elevated = up — the word itself joins the fodder
  • Torquay → T the 'front' (first letter) of Torquay
  • Fancy → anagram indicator

'Fancy' opens the clue looking like a cry of surprise and is really the anagram order; 'elevated' doesn't move anything — it simply contributes the word UP to the mix.

7 down CONCOURSE

Charlie attending programme of study in large open space (9)

Definition: large open space · charade

  • Charlie → C C = Charlie in the NATO alphabet
  • attending → ON attending a course = being ON it
  • programme of study → COURSE a course of study

Charlie for C comes from the NATO phonetic alphabet, a crossword favourite; 'attending' becomes ON because you are on a course while you attend it.

8 down ELEMENT

Perhaps, lead factor (7)

Definition: Perhaps, lead · double definition

  • Perhaps, lead → ELEMENT lead is one example of a chemical element — 'perhaps' flags the definition by example
  • factor → ELEMENT an element = a factor, one part of a situation

'Perhaps' is the courteous flag that lead is only an example of the answer, not a synonym for it — the mark of definition by example.

9 down ALL IN GOOD TIME

Leading limo to move in due course (3,2,4,4)

Definition: in due course · anagram

  • Leading limo to → LEADINGLIMOTO anagram fodder, 13 letters
  • move → anagram indicator

The sneaky part is that 'to' belongs to the fodder — count and the thirteen letters are all there before 'move' gives the order.

15 down ON THE CASE

Dealing with a problem, like an attentive lawyer? (2,3,4)

Definition: Dealing with a problem · double definition

  • Dealing with a problem → ONTHECASE the everyday idiom for tackling something
  • like an attentive lawyer? → ONTHECASE a lawyer at work is literally on the case

The split comes after 'problem'. The question mark concedes the second half is playful: a lawyer minding their brief really is on the case.

18 down PROFANE

Crude advertising by individual around flag intermittently (7)

Definition: Crude · charade + container + alternate letters

  • advertising → PR PR = public relations, i.e. advertising
  • individual → ONE wrapped 'around' the FA
  • flag → FA 'intermittently' — every other letter of FlAg

Three devices stacked up: PR for publicity, then ONE opens to admit FA, the alternate letters of 'flag'.

20 down BAFFLED

British brace of fellows probing complicated deal in a confused state (7)

Definition: in a confused state · charade + container + anagram

  • British → B B = British, as in BBC
  • brace of fellows → FF a brace = two, and F = fellow, so FF
  • deal → ALED anagram of DEAL, 'complicated'

'Brace' is sporting slang for a pair, so two F's report for duty; 'probing' pushes them into the shaken-up DEAL, with B in front.

21 down OVEREAT

Acquire too much fuel? (7)

Definition: Acquire too much fuel? · cryptic definition

  • fuel → not petrol or coal, which is where the surface points: the fuel a body runs on is food, and the question mark admits the stretch
  • Acquire too much → taking on board more of it than you need — the excess is the whole of the answer's meaning

A cryptic definition: food is the fuel the body runs on, so taking too much of it on board is exactly this.

22 down DEMONS

Model couple of Poles, people of great energy (6)

Definition: people of great energy · charade

  • Model → DEMO a demo = a demonstration model or version
  • couple of Poles → NS the magnetic poles, North and South

The capital P in 'Poles' points you at nationality, but these are the compass kind — N and S.

25 down BANAL

Embargo a line of a hackneyed nature (5)

Definition: of a hackneyed nature · charade

  • Embargo → BAN an embargo = a ban
  • a → A stands as itself
  • line → L l = line, as in 'l.10' of a poem

The definition is phrased as 'of a … nature' so an adjective can close the clue; L for line comes from printed editions of poetry and plays.
