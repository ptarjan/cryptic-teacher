Across

8 across ONE

I put the end of Endymion in the middle of poem (3)

Definition: I · container + last letter + middle letters

  • the middle of poem → O the first of the two central letters of POEM
  • the end of Endymion → N the last letter of Keats's long poem
  • the middle of poem → E the second central letter of POEM, closing round

Endymion is itself a poem, so the two nouns look like one idea; they are separate jobs, one supplying a single letter and one supplying a pair.

9 across INCESSANTLY

Constantly breaking Nancy's tiles (11)

Definition: Constantly · anagram

  • Nancy's tiles → NANCYSTILES eleven letters of fodder, apostrophe ignored as usual

The surface is a clumsy guest wrecking a bathroom. The definition is the very first word, which is the place solvers tend to look last in a long anagram.

11 across ACTUARIAL

Do you briefly join a western den of insurance workers? (9)

Definition: of insurance workers · charade + reversal

  • Do → ACT to do something is to act
  • you → U U is 'you' as a text message writes it
  • a → A the indefinite article taken as read
  • den → RIAL an animal's den is a LAIR, and here it runs backwards

The question mark flags a definition by relation: the answer describes what these workers do rather than naming them. Four chunks in a row makes it a long charade with only one turned piece.

12 across JUDEA

Law set by head of administration in province of 1D (5)

Definition: province of 1D · charade + first letter

  • Law → JUDE the actor Jude Law: the surname is printed and the first name is wanted
  • administration → A its opening letter

'Law' wants to be statute in a clue full of administration; it is a surname instead, and the cross-reference at the end makes the definition a Roman one.

13 across SAFE

Protected documents a felon guards (4)

Definition: Protected · hidden word

  • documents a felon → SAFE four consecutive letters straddle the gap between the first two of these

Hidden words hide best inside a phrase that sounds like a complete thought, and 'documents a felon guards' is one, so nothing draws the eye to the join.

14 across DIVERS

Endlessly varied loons in America (6)

Definition: loons in America · deletion

  • varied → DIVERSE varied in the sense of assorted or sundry
  • Endlessly → the E finishing that seven-letter word comes off

The birds are the whole point: what North America calls loons, Britain calls divers, so the definition is doing geography as well as ornithology.

15 across EOS

Right to leave archer for divine Greek lady (3)

Definition: divine Greek lady · deletion

  • archer → EROS the bow-carrying god of love, who shoots his arrows at people
  • Right → R R is the standard abbreviation for right

Archery points at Cupid under his Greek name, and losing one letter of him leaves the rosy-fingered goddess of the dawn — a neat piece of mythological sleight of hand.

17 across ANCHOVY

Wings quit fancy show with covers from Vanity and Fish (7)

Definition: Fish · charade + middle letters + outer letters

  • fancy → ANC that five-letter word without its outside pair
  • show → HO the same treatment again, leaving the two letters in the middle
  • Vanity → V the front cover of the magazine title
  • Vanity → Y and its back cover

Wings and covers are two ways of saying the outside of a word, and both are doing that job here. 'Fish' reads as more of the magazine surface, and that is exactly where the definition sits.

18 across PRESSES

They print letters on electoral system (7)

Definition: They print · charade

  • electoral system → PR PR is proportional representation, the voting system Britain keeps arguing about
  • letters → ESSES written out in full the letter S is an ess, and more than one of them are esses

Spelling a letter out as a word — ess for S, em for M, ex for X — is a device worth owning, and here the plural of one does all the heavy lifting.

20 across PUB

Essentially happy community cabin? (3)

Definition: Essentially happy community cabin? · &lit + middle letters

  • happy → P the middle letter of five
  • community → U the central letter of nine
  • cabin → B the middle letter of five again

Read once, the clue is a fair description of the thing itself; read again, the same words are three middle letters. The question mark is the setter flagging that double duty.

22 across OPIATE

How Eastender says "fingers crossed he consumed drug" (6)

Definition: drug · homophone

  • fingers crossed → HOPE crossing your fingers is hoping for luck
  • he consumed → HE ATE the plain words under the accent: the pronoun and the past tense of eat
  • fingers crossed he consumed → OPIATE a Cockney drops both aitches, and the three words then run together into one

The quotation marks do real work: everything inside them is the accented version, and only the last word of it is the definition rather than the sound.

24 across WORD

Promise the return of some imprisoned rowdies (4)

Definition: Promise · hidden word + reversal

  • imprisoned rowdies → DROW four consecutive letters straddle the gap between these two, and they are picked up backwards

'Imprisoned' is surface rather than instruction, which is what makes this hard — the concealment is signalled by the innocuous 'some', and the whole thing reads as a court report.

26 across INDRA

Asian god's atrocious nadir (5)

Definition: Asian god · anagram

  • nadir → NADIR five letters of fodder, the word for a lowest point

'Atrocious nadir' sounds like one gloomy phrase, which is how the instruction manages to stand right beside its own fodder without being noticed.

27 across PREVAILED

Quiet clergyman was sick, came through (9)

Definition: came through · charade

  • Quiet → P p is piano, the musical direction to play softly
  • clergyman → REV Rev. is the abbreviation for a Reverend
  • was sick → AILED to ail is to be unwell

Two abbreviations and then a plain synonym, with the comma the only sign of where the definition starts. P for piano is worth keeping: it is how music writes 'quiet'.

29 across OVERINDULGE

Go live under crooked gorge (11)

Definition: gorge · anagram

  • Go live under → GOLIVEUNDER eleven letters of fodder — the whole of the clue except its last two words

'Go live' is broadcasting jargon and 'under' looks like a positional instruction, so the fodder does not read as fodder at all. That disguise is the whole difficulty.

30 across SOL

Very large source of light for 1D (3)

Definition: source of light for 1D · charade

  • Very → SO so = very, as in 'so tired'
  • large → L L is the clothing-label abbreviation for large

The cross-reference is the whole trick: 1 down supplies the language, so the light source has to be named in Latin rather than English.

Down

1 down ROMANS

Country depicted in jacket of Rabelais book (6)

Definition: book · container + outer letters

  • jacket of Rabelais → R the front of RABELAIS, the outside letter it opens with
  • Country → OMAN the Arabian state on the Gulf, a favourite short country in wordplay
  • jacket of Rabelais → S and the back of the same name, closing the wrapper

Rabelais is there because a French novelist gives you a plausible book jacket, and the clue only ever wants the letters at his edges. The definition is biblical, not literary.

2 down BEATIFIC

Writer gulps high tea if blissful (8)

Definition: blissful · container + anagram

  • Writer → B the writer is a pen, and BIC is the everyday ballpoint brand; this is its first letter
  • tea if → EATIF the five letters of those two words, rearranged
  • Writer → IC the rest of the same brand name, closing round

'Writer' is a pen rather than an author, which is standard crossword shorthand. The surface has someone bolting their tea, so nothing in it reads as an instruction to swallow letters.

3 down PISA

Now and then, pair stay in city of 1 (4)

Definition: city of 1 · alternate letters

  • pair stay → PISA take the first, third, fifth and seventh of these eight letters

The surface is a couple pausing somewhere in Italy, and the instruction hides in the ordinary phrase that opens the clue. Cross-references like 'of 1' point at another entry's answer, not its number.

4 down ACTIVITY

Pastime of six, thoroughly discontented after start of play (8)

Definition: Pastime · charade + outer letters

  • start of play → ACTI a script labels its opening Act I, with the Roman numeral one
  • six → VI six in Roman numerals
  • thoroughly → TY the outer letters of that ten-letter adverb

Two Roman numerals arrive by different routes — one dressed up as the first scene of a play, one as the plain number. 'Of six' reads as part of the pastime, which is where the join hides.

5 down ISOLDE

Tragic heroine's flogged in Irish domain (6)

Definition: Tragic heroine · container

  • Irish domain → I .ie is Ireland's internet country domain; this is its first letter
  • flogged → SOLD to flog something is to sell it, and the past tense is wanted
  • Irish domain → E the second letter of that country domain closes it up

Web addresses ending .ie are Irish, and setters use national domains as ready-made two-letter chunks. 'Tragic' is doing definition work here rather than decorating the surface.

6 down STODGE

Say Dorothy's served up heavy food (6)

Definition: heavy food · charade + reversal

  • Say → EG e.g. is the abbreviation for 'say' in the sense of for example
  • Dorothy's → DOTS Dot is the pet form of Dorothy, and the possessive S rides along

The whole clue reads as dinner being brought out, and every word of it turns out to be an instruction. Dot for Dorothy is a nickname setters rely on constantly.

7 down SYNAPSES

Passes NY's bustling electrified junctions (8)

Definition: electrified junctions · anagram

  • Passes NY's → PASSESNY eight letters of fodder; the possessive S belongs to the surface only

'Passes NY' looks like commuter geography rather than raw material, and 'bustling' is exactly the word a New York surface wants — which is why it does not read as an instruction.

10 down ADJUSTED

Dead wrong about thin gravy - temperature is changed (8)

Definition: changed · anagram + container

  • Dead → DEAD four letters of fodder
  • temperature → T T is the standard abbreviation for temperature
  • thin gravy → JUS jus is the thin pan juice a chef serves in place of thick gravy

'Dead wrong' is one phrase on the surface and two separate instructions underneath, and the dash conceals where the fodder stops. Jus is worth learning as a three-letter gravy.

16 down COTOPAXI

Volcano's sulphur removed from bubbling toxic soap (8)

Definition: Volcano · anagram + deletion

  • sulphur → S S is the chemical symbol for sulphur, and this is the letter that goes
  • toxic soap → TOXICSOAP nine letters of fodder, one of which the line above takes away

A soap that bubbles is a fair surface for a volcano, and the removal is signposted before the shuffle rather than after. Cotopaxi is the snow-capped cone above Quito in Ecuador.

17 down AMPHIPOD

Australian politician greeting school's crustacean (8)

Definition: crustacean · charade

  • Australian → A A is the standard abbreviation for Australian
  • politician → MP an MP is a Member of Parliament
  • greeting → HI the everyday two-letter hello
  • school → POD whales travel in a school, also called a pod

Four short pieces laid end to end and nothing moved, which is what keeps a long charade fair. School and pod are interchangeable for whales, not for fish.

18 down POT-BELLY

Corporation's year under pressure from drug ring (3-5)

Definition: Corporation · charade

  • drug → POT pot is cannabis, the setter's standard three-letter drug
  • ring → BELL the thing that rings, swapping the noun in for the verb
  • year → Y Y is the standard abbreviation for year
  • pressure → surface only — it makes 'under pressure from a drug ring' read like a news headline and contributes no letters

'Corporation' for a paunch is Victorian British slang, still alive in crosswords and almost nowhere else. The surface is a company in trouble, which is the picture the definition hides behind.

19 down SPOTLESS

Notice habitual junkie Holmes is finally clean (8)

Definition: clean · charade + last letters

  • Notice → SPOT to spot something is to notice it
  • habitual junkie Holmes is → LESS the last letters of those four, read left to right

Sherlock really was a habitual cocaine user, so the surface is fair as well as funny — and the clue still only wants the final letter of his name. Four words clipped in a row is a lot of concealed work.

21 down BODIES

Corpses' nasty smell fades away (6)

Definition: Corpses · charade

  • nasty smell → BO BO is the familiar shorthand for the smell of stale sweat
  • fades away → DIES a sound or a light that fades away dies

The surface is a whiff in a changing room and the definition is a mortuary, which is a long way to travel in six letters. BO for body odour is an initialism setters take as read.

23 down IMPEDE

Block Mev's editor interrupting exercises (6)

Definition: Block · charade + container

  • Mev's → IM the setter speaking as themselves, so Mev's stands for I'm
  • exercises → P PE is physical education, the school's exercises; this is its first letter
  • editor → ED ed. is the standard abbreviation for an editor
  • exercises → E the final letter of that same school subject's initials, closing round

Setters put themselves into their own clues, so the byline at the top of the puzzle is available as a first-person pronoun. The surface reads as a row in a newsroom.

25 down DODDLE

European article following old comedian's simple task (6)

Definition: simple task · charade

  • old comedian → DODD Ken Dodd, the tickling-stick comic from Liverpool, a British household name for fifty years
  • European article → LE the French for 'the'

'European article' is the setter's standard way of asking for a foreign 'the', with French and German both fair game — the crossing letters settle which one is wanted.

28 down A FEW

Small number of sports administrators raised two points (1,3)

Definition: Small number · charade + reversal

  • sports administrators → AF the FA is the Football Association, which runs English football, turned round
  • two points → EW east and west, two of the four compass points

Compass points are the crossword's most reliable single letters, and 'two points' names none of them — the crossing entries decide which two you need.
