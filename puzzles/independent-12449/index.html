Across

1 across CRACKPOT

Foolish joke, better on reflection (8)

Definition: Foolish · charade + reversal

  • joke → CRACK a crack is a wisecrack or a gag
  • better → POT to top an offer is to better it, and TOP is turned round here

'Better' looks like a comparison and is a verb: to top somebody's bid is to better it. The comma is where the definition stops and the building starts.

6 across SMALL

Modest shaded avenue heading South? Quite the reverse (5)

Definition: Modest · charade

  • South → S S is the compass abbreviation for south
  • shaded avenue → MALL a mall was originally a tree-lined walk, as with the one running to Buckingham Palace

Read the wordplay as written and you build MALLS, which fits the enumeration and is wrong. 'Quite the reverse' is the setter telling you to swap the two pieces over.

10 across ARTISAN

Sinatra when performing, a master of his craft (7)

Definition: a master of his craft · anagram

  • Sinatra → SINATRA anagram fodder, seven letters
  • performing → anagram indicator

'A master of his craft' is exactly what people said about Sinatra, and that is the trap: the phrase is the definition, and the singer is only raw material.

11 across EARPLUG

European artist backing commercial noise-reduction system? (7)

Definition: noise-reduction system · charade + reversal

  • European → E E is the standard abbreviation for European
  • artist → AR RA is the Royal Academician, the crossword's stock artist, and it is reversed here
  • commercial → PLUG to plug a product is to give it a free advertisement

Calling a twist of foam a system is the joke the question mark flags. RA for artist is worth banking: setters reach for it constantly.

12 across KAISER

Imperial title periodically at issue between two kings (6)

Definition: Imperial title · charade + alternate letters

  • two kings → K K is the king of chess and of cards, the first of the pair
  • at issue → AISE the first, third, fifth and seventh letters of the phrase
  • two kings → R R is Rex, Latin for king, the other of the pair

Two kings, two different conventions: chess and cards supply K, while Latin supplies R for Rex, the same R that sits in ER on a British postbox.

13 across DRAUGHT

During dry spell, love going for a drink (7)

Definition: drink · substitution

  • dry spell → DROUGHT a drought is a long spell without rain
  • love → O love is nothing in tennis, written as O, and it is the letter that leaves
  • a → A the letter that moves into the vacated place

The surface has love going out for a drink during a dry spell, and it is all decoy: the drink is the definition and the dry spell is a word to be tampered with.

15 across MILD

Central Line stops bland (4)

Definition: bland · container

  • Central → MID mid means central, as in mid-morning
  • Line → L L is the standard abbreviation for line

The Central Line is a London Underground route, so the capital letter and the surface both push you towards stations. The printer supplies that red herring free.

16 across AWAKENING

Stirring tonic essentially into a weak gin cocktail (9)

Definition: Stirring · anagram + middle letter

  • tonic essentially → N the middle letter of tonic
  • a weak gin → AWEAKGIN anagram fodder, eight letters
  • cocktail → anagram indicator

'Stirring' is the definition and looks exactly like the instruction; the word that actually mixes is 'cocktail', sitting innocently at the end of a drinks order.

19 across GOTHAMITE

New Yorker bought cured meat item when reduced (9)

Definition: New Yorker · charade + deletion

  • bought → GOT got is what you did if you bought something
  • cured meat → HAM ham is the salted and cured leg of pork
  • item when reduced → ITE item cut short loses its closing M

Gotham has been a nickname for New York since Washington Irving used it in 1807, a long time before Batman borrowed the name for a darker city.

21 across MALT

Whisky makers: Ardbeg, Laphroaig and Talisker for starters (4)

Definition: Whisky · first letters

  • makers: Ardbeg, Laphroaig and Talisker → MALT the opening letter of the four names, with the linking 'and' passed over

Ardbeg, Laphroaig and Talisker are Scottish distilleries, two on Islay and one on Skye, so the list is genuine and the initials are the wordplay.

24 across GARDENS

Sierra Grande, ground which could produce vegetables? (7)

Definition: which could produce vegetables · anagram

  • Sierra → S Sierra is S in the radio alphabet
  • Grande → GRANDE anagram fodder, six letters
  • ground → anagram indicator

'Ground' wants to belong to the definition, as earth that grows vegetables, and it is the signal to crush the letters instead. Sierra Grande is a real Argentine town.

25 across BITTER

Resentful canine perhaps time will break (6)

Definition: Resentful · container

  • canine perhaps → BITER a canine is the tooth that does the biting, one example of the general word
  • time → T T is the standard abbreviation for time

The surface is a dog nursing a grudge. 'Canine perhaps' is the example marker doing its job: you want the general word for what a canine is.

28 across ANEMONE

Plant notice: "No men allowed around stores" (7)

Definition: Plant · hidden word + reversal

  • notice: "No men allowed → ANEMONE seven letters running backwards from the start of the last word to the end of the first, straight over the colon and the quotation mark

The colon and the quotation marks look as though they seal the phrase off from the rest. They are only punctuation, and a hidden run walks through them.

29 across UPGRADE

Character completing apprenticeship argued about raise (7)

Definition: raise · anagram + last letter

  • Character completing apprenticeship → P the last letter of apprenticeship
  • argued → ARGUED anagram fodder, six letters
  • about → anagram indicator

'Character' means a letter rather than a person, and 'about' shuffles rather than surrounds: two words pulling the solver the wrong way in one short clue.

30 across STOUT

Resolute hawk going after seconds (5)

Definition: Resolute · charade

  • seconds → S S is the standard abbreviation for seconds
  • hawk → TOUT to hawk goods is to tout them round the streets

'Hawk' is the verb, not the bird: to hawk is to peddle wares door to door. 'Seconds' is the clock's, not a second helping.

31 across REPORTER

Hack about, left and right, winning hole at last (8)

Definition: Hack · charade + last letter

  • about → RE re means about or concerning, as at the head of a memo
  • left → PORT port is the left-hand side of a ship
  • hole at last → E the final letter of hole
  • right → R R is the standard abbreviation for right

The surface is a round of golf and the wordplay is nautical. 'Hack' is Fleet Street's own word for a jobbing journalist, rude and worn with pride.

2 down RETRIAL

Hearing, once again, trailer being broadcast (7)

Definition: Hearing, once again · anagram

  • trailer → TRAILER anagram fodder, seven letters
  • broadcast → anagram indicator

The surface is television, a trailer going out on the channels, and the word doing the work is 'broadcast' in its older farming sense of scattering.

3 down CASKET

Chest cold carrier passing on bug originally (6)

Definition: Chest · charade + deletion

  • cold → C C is the standard abbreviation for cold, the letter on a tap
  • carrier → ASKET a basket is a carrier, shown here without the letter it hands over
  • bug originally → bug starts with B, and B is what the carrier passes on

The surface is an infection doing the rounds of an office. Underneath, the carrier is a container of wickerwork and the bug is only an initial.

4 down PONG

Pooh's half-forgotten no good Hum (4)

Definition: Hum · charade + deletion

  • Pooh's half-forgotten → PO POOH cut down the middle keeps its first two letters
  • no → N N is the usual short form of no, as on a tick-box form
  • good → G G is the standard abbreviation for good

Winnie-the-Pooh composes little songs he calls Hums, which is why the word is capitalised. The definition is the other hum, British slang for a stink.

5 down TREAD WATER

We trade art abroad to stay afloat (5,5)

Definition: stay afloat · anagram

  • We trade art → WETRADEART anagram fodder, ten letters
  • abroad → anagram indicator

The surface is a gallery keeping itself solvent, and the definition does carry that figurative sense too. The answer is the swimmer's literal version of the same idea.

6 down SCREAMER

Progress at last, a substitute for milk! (8)

Definition: ! · charade + last letter

  • Progress at last → S the final letter of Progress
  • a substitute for milk → CREAMER a creamer is the powdered whitener people stir into coffee instead of milk

The clue looks as though it has no definition at all, and the punctuation is it: a screamer is what printers and sub-editors call an exclamation mark.

7 down ALLEGRI

Italian football manager during final leg, riled (7)

Definition: Italian football manager · hidden word

  • final leg, riled → ALLEGRI seven consecutive letters, starting on the fourth letter of the first word and running through the comma into the last

Massimiliano Allegri has managed Juventus and Milan, so the definition points at a particular man rather than at a job. A comma is no barrier to a hidden run.

8 down LIGHT

Fine volley following shot (5)

Definition: Fine · deletion

  • volley → FLIGHT a flight of arrows is a volley loosed all at once
  • following → f. is the standard abbreviation for following, as in a page reference, and that F is the letter shot away

The surface is a footballer's fine volley and the parse is much drier: 'following' is a scholar's abbreviation, and 'shot' does the killing rather than the scoring.

9 down DARK

Grim day to be on lifeboat? (4)

Definition: Grim · charade

  • day → D D is the standard abbreviation for day
  • lifeboat → ARK Noah's ark carried its passengers safely through the flood

The question mark is the setter's apology for 'lifeboat': an ark rescued everyone aboard, but no lifeboat station would recognise the description.

14 down LADIESWEAR

In which salaried English women might be dressed? (10)

Definition: In which salaried English women might be dressed · anagram + &lit

  • salaried → SALARIED anagram fodder, eight letters
  • English → E E is the standard abbreviation for English
  • women → W W is the abbreviation for women, as on a changing-room door
  • dressed → anagram indicator

Every word does two jobs at once: read straight the sentence describes the clothes, and read again it hands over the letters. That is what an all-in-one clue is.

15 down MUG

Mush something to chew over (3)

Definition: Mush · reversal

  • something to chew → GUM gum is the stuff people chew for hours and never swallow

'Mush' is British slang for the face, as in 'shut your mush'. That is the sense wanted, not anything sloppy in a bowl.

17 down GUT

Taking regular breaks, group stop to plunder (3)

Definition: plunder · regular letters

  • group stop → GUT GROUPSTOP falls into GRO, UPS and TOP, and the letter kept is the one each trio starts with

Nine letters, three kept. The phrase is chosen because it divides exactly into three threes, which is the tell that a counting instruction is in play.

18 down TAKE ROOT

Lead batsman to become established (4,4)

Definition: become established · charade

  • Lead → TAKE to take somebody somewhere is to lead them there
  • batsman → ROOT Joe Root, the England batsman and former Test captain

Joe Root is an England cricketer, which lets 'Lead batsman' read as a job description on the surface while it is really two separate pieces of wordplay.

20 down TORPEDO

Scotch barely open, flipping rubbish party folds (7)

Definition: Scotch · charade + reversal + middle letters + container

  • rubbish → TOR rot is rubbish in the sense of nonsense, and it is turned round here
  • open → PE stripped of its outside letters, OPEN keeps only the two in the middle
  • party → DO a do is a party in British usage, as in the office Christmas do

'Scotch' reads as the whisky the rest of the clue seems to be drinking, and it is the verb: to scotch a plan is to put an end to it.

22 down ACTUATE

Switch on air-conditioning, worried about temperature you said? (7)

Definition: Switch on · container + homophone

  • air-conditioning → AC AC is the everyday short form of air-conditioning
  • temperature → T T is the standard abbreviation for temperature
  • you said → U the pronoun spoken aloud is a single letter of the alphabet
  • worried → ATE what's eating you means what is worrying you, so the past tense of that verb fits

The surface is somebody fiddling with the air-conditioning on a hot day. The step most solvers miss is 'worried' as the verb from 'what's eating you'.

23 down GINGER

Red spirit, say, turning up rarely at first (6)

Definition: Red · charade + reversal + first letter

  • spirit → GIN gin is the distilled spirit, not the ghostly kind
  • say → GE 'say' is eg, meaning for example, written upwards here
  • rarely at first → R the first letter of rarely

Watch 'say': here it is the two-letter eg rather than an instruction to the solver, and it has to be read up the column before it fits.

24 down GLASS

Under gravity, form striking carbon crystal (5)

Definition: crystal · charade + deletion

  • gravity → G G is the physicist's symbol for gravity
  • carbon → carbon is C, and that is the letter struck out
  • form → LASS a form is a class in a British school, here with its first letter struck away

'Form' is the school word, as in the fourth form, and 'striking' is the setter deleting a letter rather than praising the sparkle of the material.

26 down RYES

Some grasses become hostile if caught (4)

Definition: Some grasses · homophone

  • become hostile → RYES a people who rise turn on those set over them, which is becoming hostile

'Some' is the usual signal for a word buried inside the clue, and here it belongs to the definition instead; the mechanism is a sound.

27 down PUMP

Question dancer's choice of footwear (4)

Definition: Question · double definition

  • Question → the verb for working information out of somebody by asking and asking
  • dancer's choice of footwear → the flat, thin-soled shoe worn for dancing and for school gym

Two senses of one short word stand side by side with nothing joining them, which is why the clue reads as a single odd remark about a dancer.
