Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,006

Across

7 across STEAM IRON

Smoother artist after ammo initially loading type of gun (5,4)

Definition: Smoother · charade + container + first letter

A STEAM IRON is a smoother in the plainest sense: you push it over cloth to take the creases out.

  • type of gun → STEN the Sten was the British army's cheap wartime submachine gun
  • ammo initially → A the first letter of Ammo
  • artist → MIRO Joan Miró, the Catalan painter of floating shapes
  • after — 'After' fixes the order: what follows the word goes behind the piece already named.
  • initially — 'Initially' means at the very start, so only the opening letter of the word beside it is taken.
  • loading — A gun is loaded by pushing something down inside it, which is where these letters end up.

An artist packing ammunition into a gun.

'Smoother' is a noun — the thing that does the smoothing — rather than the comparative adjective it looks like, and the Sten is standard crossword hardware.

8 across WHEEL

Turner on The Hay Wain? (5)

Definition: Turner · cryptic definition

A WHEEL is a turner in the literal sense — an object whose whole purpose is to rotate — with no metaphor involved.

  • Turner → the surface offers J.M.W. Turner, one more painter in a grid stuffed with them
  • on The Hay Wain? → Constable's painting shows a farm wagon standing in a ford — look at what it rolls along on

One famous English painter apparently turning up in another's masterpiece.

Nothing here separates into wordplay: the capital T sells you a painter's name where the clue means a thing that goes round, and a hay wain rides on four of them.

9 across RETRIEVAL

Rescuing relative at sea, Romeo saved (9)

Definition: Rescuing · anagram

RETRIEVAL is the act of getting something back, which is what rescuing amounts to when the thing recovered is a person.

  • relative → RELATIVE anagram fodder, eight letters
  • Romeo → R R = Romeo in the NATO alphabet
  • at sea → anagram indicator
  • at sea — Anything at sea is adrift with nothing holding its position, which is what happens to these letters.
  • saved — 'Saved' here means kept rather than pulled from the water, so the extra letter is retained in the mix.

A family member adrift in the water and a man called Romeo brought safely home.

Romeo is the NATO alphabet's R, not the lover, and 'at sea' works both ways at once — the shipwreck in the picture, and the instruction that the letters have come loose.

10 across SLOOP

Sailing boat in lottery capsized (5)

Definition: Sailing boat · reversal

A SLOOP is a single-masted sailing boat, so the definition names it by its category rather than any special feature.

  • lottery → POOLS the pools are Britain's weekly bet on football results, a lottery in all but name; 'capsized' turns the word round
  • capsized — A capsized boat has gone right over, so the letters are read from the far end back.

A boat going over while the week's lottery is drawn.

The trap is reading 'lottery' as the national draw with the numbered balls; the pools came first, and it is that older word the clue turns over.

12 across MANTRA

Artist not entirely impressed by intro of traditional chant that’s sacred (6)

Definition: chant that’s sacred · deletion + container + first letter

A MANTRA is a sacred formula chanted in Hindu and Buddhist practice, so the definition reads straight with nothing bent.

  • Artist not entirely → MANRA Man Ray, the American surrealist, docked of his last letter
  • intro of traditional → T the first letter of Traditional
  • not entirely — 'Not entirely' stops you short of the finish, so the name arrives one letter shy of complete.
  • impressed by — To be impressed by something is to have it stamped into you, which is how the single letter gets inside.
  • intro of — An intro is the opening, so nothing but the first letter of the word after it is used.

A painter half-persuaded by the opening of an old sacred chant.

The last two letters look like the Royal Academician this grid leans on again and again; they are the tail of a photographer's surname instead.

13 across TOOL SHED

Told hoes (or hose), or those old screws – here? (4,4)

Definition: here · anagram

Hoes, hose and old screws are exactly what a TOOL SHED holds, so 'here' points at the building they are all kept in.

  • Told hoes → TOLDHOES anagram fodder, eight letters
  • (or hose) → swap the last two letters and the same eight are offered a second time
  • or those old → a third arrangement of the identical eight letters, with the setter enjoying himself
  • screws — To screw something up is to twist it out of shape, and twisted letters no longer sit in the order they were handed to you.

Somebody reeling off a list of old garden gear and rusty fixings.

The same eight letters are handed over three times in one sentence, and 'screws' finishes the shopping list while quietly issuing the instruction.

14 across KLEENEX

Tissue cut then by artist (7)

Definition: Tissue · charade + deletion

KLEENEX is a trademark that turned into the ordinary word for a paper tissue, so the definition names the thing by its best-known brand.

  • artist → KLEE Paul Klee, the Swiss-German painter of the Twittering Machine
  • cut then → NEX 'then' in the sense of next, with the last letter taken off
  • cut — Something cut is shortened, so the word arrives with its final letter trimmed off.

A tissue snipped up beside a working painter.

'Cut' looks as though it belongs to the tissue and belongs to 'then' instead, which is standing in for the word NEXT. The definition works because a brand name has become the everyday noun.

17 across GORMLEY

Wood nailed back by bloody artist (7)

Definition: artist · reversal + container

GORMLEY is a sculptor, and 'artist' covers sculptors as readily as painters — a category name for a maker of art of any kind.

  • bloody → GORY bloody = gory
  • Wood → ELM the elm is a timber tree; 'back' turns its three letters round
  • nailed — Nailing a plank drives it into whatever it is fixed to, so those letters end up buried inside the other word.
  • back — In an across entry, back is right to left, so the timber's letters are read the other way.

A short-tempered painter hammering a plank back into place.

'Bloody' is the swearword in the surface and plain gore in the wordplay. Antony Gormley made the Angel of the North, the giant steel figure beside the road at Gateshead.

20 across ANGELICO

Artist in cage with lion torn to pieces (8)

Definition: Artist · anagram

Fra ANGELICO painted altarpieces and frescoes in fifteenth-century Florence, so 'Artist' is the class he is named by.

  • cage with lion → CAGELION anagram fodder, eight letters
  • torn to pieces → anagram indicator
  • torn to pieces — Something torn to pieces is in bits, and bits can be put back together in any order at all.

A painter shut in a cage with a lion and torn apart.

'In cage' reads as an insertion — someone tucked inside — when the cage is supplying letters rather than holding any. Fra Angelico was a Dominican friar as well as a painter.

22 across FERMAT

Whistler recalled mother having little time for theorist (6)

Definition: theorist · reversal + charade

FERMAT proved and conjectured his way through number theory, so 'theorist' names him by the work rather than by any title he held.

  • Whistler → REF the official who blows the whistle at a match; 'recalled' turns the three letters round
  • mother → MA ma is the familiar word for mother
  • little time → T t is the short form of time, as on a stopwatch
  • recalled — To recall is to call back, and in an across entry back means the letters run right to left.

Whistler thinking back to his mother, who had no patience with some theorist.

Whistler's Mother is the painting the surface is trading on, while the whistler in the wordplay blows for offside. Pierre de Fermat did his number theory as a spare-time lawyer.

24 across ASIDE

Whisper first song on record? (5)

Definition: Whisper · charade

An ASIDE is a remark dropped quietly so that only some hear it, which is a whisper in the theatrical sense.

  • first → A the first of anything is its A — the A-list, the A-team, the A-road
  • song on record → SIDE a record has two of these and the songs sit on them

The opening track on somebody's record.

'Whisper' is a noun here — the remark, not the act of murmuring — and the lead track of a single goes on the A-side.

25 across DIETITIAN

Artist, it’s time to check out food expert! (9)

Definition: food expert · charade

A DIETITIAN is trained to advise on what people should eat, so 'food expert' describes the profession rather than naming it.

  • it’s time to check out → DIE to check out is to leave for good
  • Artist → TITIAN Titian, the Venetian master whose name is also a shade of red hair

A painter being told to go and take a look at some food expert.

The wordplay is an order shouted at a painter, which is why the pieces arrive in the clue back to front. 'Check out' means take a look on the surface and something far more final underneath.

26 across DEGAS

Artist burst bubble, in a manner of speaking? (5)

Definition: Artist · charade

Edgar DEGAS painted the ballet dancers and racecourses of Paris, so 'Artist' is the category rather than a description.

  • burst → DE DE- is the prefix that undoes a thing, as in defrost or derail
  • bubble → GAS a bubble is a pocket of it, and bursting lets that out
  • in a manner of speaking — The phrase owns up to the looseness: the name is being read as an invented verb rather than used strictly.

A painter poking at a bubble until it pops.

To de-gas something is to take the gas out of it, which is what popping a bubble does. The painter's name is said nothing like the verb hiding inside it — day-GAH, not the two flat syllables.

27 across CONSTABLE

Artifice still for artist (9)

Definition: artist · charade

John CONSTABLE painted the Suffolk countryside he grew up in, so 'artist' is the general class he is named by.

  • Artifice → CON a con is a trick — artifice in its dishonest sense
  • still → STABLE something stable does not move, which is what still means here

Trickery still going on in the art world.

'Still' is the adjective meaning motionless, not the adverb meaning yet, and that swap hides six of the nine letters. John Constable painted The Hay Wain, which 8 across is standing on.

Down

1 down ATHENA

Goddess at one time packing case in America (6)

Definition: Goddess · container + outer letters

ATHENA is the Greek goddess of wisdom and war, so 'Goddess' is the category she belongs to rather than a synonym.

  • America → AA the country begins and ends with the same letter, which makes a tidy shell
  • at one time → THEN 'at one time' = then, back in the day
  • packing — Packing something means carrying it around inside your luggage, so one word travels within another.
  • case — A case is the shell a thing sits in, so what is kept is the shell of the word — the letter at each end.

A goddess who once travelled across America with her case packed.

'Case' is the suitcase the surface is packing and a word's outer shell in the wordplay — packaging words are how setters ask for first-and-last letters, and America is a generous one.

2 down MAGRITTE

Bottle consumed by familiar artist (8)

Definition: artist · container

MAGRITTE painted the bowler hats and the pipe labelled 'this is not a pipe', so 'artist' is the class he is being placed in.

  • familiar → MATE a familiar is a friend, and a mate is what you call one
  • Bottle → GRIT in British slang bottle is nerve, and so is grit
  • consumed by — To be consumed is to be swallowed whole, so the shorter word disappears inside the longer one.

A painter's bottle being drunk by an old friend.

'Bottle' is British slang for nerve, with no drink in it, and 'familiar' is a noun here — the mate the surface is sitting with.

3 down RIVERA

Artist, artist after split (6)

Definition: Artist · charade

Diego RIVERA covered Mexico's public buildings in murals, so 'Artist' names the category he sits in.

  • split → RIVE to rive is to split or tear apart
  • artist → RA RA = Royal Academician, the crossword's stock painter
  • after — 'After' means later in the line, so the piece it introduces is written second, behind what is already down.

One painter trailing another after a break-up.

The word 'artist' appears twice and means something different each time: the first is what you are hunting for, the second is the Royal Academician setters use for any painter at all.

4 down HOGARTH

Bacon paintings hard for artist (7)

Definition: artist · charade

William HOGARTH engraved A Rake's Progress and Gin Lane, so the definition places him in the general class of artists.

  • Bacon → HOG bacon comes off a hog
  • paintings → ART paintings are art
  • hard → H H is the grade stamped on a hard pencil

Francis Bacon's canvases proving hard going for a fellow painter.

Capitalised, Bacon is Francis Bacon the painter on the surface and a pig underneath — the sort of proper-noun disguise this grid uses again and again.

5 down WHALES

Wife and artist keeping pea, essentially, in pod (6)

Definition: pod · charade + container + middle letter

A pod is the collective noun for WHALES travelling together, so they are defined by the group they swim in.

  • Wife → W W = wife, a standard abbreviation
  • artist → HALS Frans Hals, the Dutch portraitist who painted the Laughing Cavalier
  • pea, essentially → E the middle letter of pEa
  • keeping — To keep something is to hold on to it, so the small piece stays held inside the word that keeps it.
  • essentially — The essence of a word is what sits at its heart, so this takes the middle letter and nothing else.

A wife and a painter minding one pea in its pod.

The definition is a collective noun rather than a synonym — the group standing in for the creatures that make it up — which is why 'in pod' reads as part of the vegetable picture.

6 down VERONESE

Artist in clover, one senses (8)

Definition: Artist · hidden word

Paolo VERONESE filled Venetian walls with enormous banquet scenes, so 'Artist' is the category the clue drops him into.

  • clover, one senses → VERONESE eight letters in a row, starting four letters into the first word, crossing a comma and two gaps
  • in — 'In' is being literal: the letters are sitting inside the words that follow, already in the right order.

A painter living in luxury, or so somebody suspects.

'In clover' is an idiom for living comfortably, which is exactly why it never reads as an instruction, and 'one senses' rounds off the picture instead of hinting at concealment.

11 down POLO

Sport in which artist has lost tie (4)

Definition: Sport · deletion

POLO is the stick-and-ball game played on horseback, so 'Sport' is the category name standing in for it.

  • artist → TIEPOLO Giovanni Battista Tiepolo, the Venetian who frescoed ceilings across eighteenth-century Europe
  • tie → the three letters T, I and E are what goes, and they happen to sit at the very front
  • has lost — What is lost is gone from what remains, so those three letters are simply missing from the name.

A painter knocked out of a cup tie in some sport.

'Sport in which' looks like the start of a container — something tucked inside the game — and it is only joinery holding the sentence up. 'Tie' is a cup match on the surface and three letters underneath.

15 down LANDSEER

Artist excited ship’s lookout? (8)

Definition: Artist · charade

Edwin LANDSEER was the Victorians' favourite animal painter, so 'Artist' names the class he belongs to.

  • excited → LAND after weeks at sea the one sight that gets a crew shouting is the shore
  • ship’s lookout → SEER a seer is one who sees, which is the whole of a lookout's job

A man at the masthead yelling about what he can see.

A lookout who has just sighted the coast is a land-seer, and that is the whole of the wordplay — no letters move. Edwin Landseer painted the Victorians' stags and modelled the Trafalgar Square lions.

16 down EMIN

Artist, sign unframed? (4)

Definition: Artist · deletion

Tracey EMIN is one of the Young British Artists, so 'Artist' is the category rather than any description of what she makes.

  • sign → GEMINI the zodiac sign of the twins
  • unframed → the frame here is the G at one end and the I at the other
  • unframed — A picture out of its frame has lost the border round the edge, so the first and last letters go.

An artist's signature hung on the wall without a frame.

'Sign' is the zodiac rather than a signature, and 'unframed' is a picture-gallery word doing a letter's job. Tracey Emin's unmade bed and neon texts are the work behind the name.

18 down MORE THAN

Manet or Hockney originally turned over (4,4)

Definition: over · anagram + first letter

'Over' in the counting sense — over fifty, over budget — is exactly MORE THAN, and the two swap in freely.

  • Manet or → MANETOR anagram fodder — the painter's name plus the little word joining him to the next
  • Hockney originally → H the first letter of Hockney
  • turned → anagram indicator
  • turned — Something turned has been spun into a new arrangement, so the letters do not stay as they were given.
  • originally — 'Originally' means at the outset, so only the opening letter of the name beside it is taken.

Two painters' names being flipped over on a gallery label.

'Or' looks like a choice between two painters and supplies two of the eight letters, and the definition is the single last word, which reads as half of 'turned over'.

19 down MORISOT

Questionable claims come up briefly for artist (7)

Definition: artist · container + deletion

Berthe MORISOT showed at nearly every Impressionist exhibition, so 'artist' is the category the clue puts her in.

  • Questionable → MOOT a moot point is a questionable one, still open to argument
  • come up briefly → RIS to come up is to rise, and the E drops off the end
  • claims — To claim something is to take it in as your own, so the shorter piece is held inside the longer word.
  • briefly — 'Briefly' means cut short, so the word gives up its final letter before it goes anywhere.

Dubious allegations surfacing briefly about a painter.

'Come up' is not a reversal instruction, even in a down clue — it is the word RISE, trimmed. Berthe Morisot was the leading woman among the Impressionists.

21 down ENDEAR

Brow wiped of female, say, stealing a charm (6)

Definition: charm · deletion + container

To ENDEAR someone is to make them liked, which is 'charm' as a verb — the winning-over sense, not the amulet the surface dangles.

  • Brow wiped of female, say → ENDER female is one example of a gender, and 'say' turns the example into the category; then the leading G is wiped away
  • a → A used literally
  • Brow wiped — The brow is the top of a face, and the top of a down entry is its first letter, so wiping it takes the head off the word.
  • stealing — To steal something is to take it in and keep it, so the single letter is tucked away inside.

A woman's forehead being mopped dry while a lucky charm goes missing.

'Female, say' works upwards from an example to the class it belongs to, which is the reverse of the usual definition-by-example. 'Brow' is positional, and a down clue's brow is where it starts.

22 down FETISH

Foxtrot is the dancing obsession (6)

Definition: obsession · charade + anagram

A FETISH is a fixation somebody cannot leave alone, which is 'obsession' in its everyday sense.

  • Foxtrot → F F is Foxtrot in the NATO alphabet
  • is the → ISTHE anagram fodder, five letters
  • dancing → anagram indicator
  • dancing — Letters that are dancing are moving about the floor and will not be standing where they started.

The foxtrot being the one dance everybody is mad about.

Foxtrot is the NATO alphabet's F, so the dance hands over a single letter, and the two words a solver would skip straight past are the ones on the move.

23 down ANALLY

In a particular way, what a lone ranger might need? (6)

Definition: In a particular way · charade

Someone ANAL is obsessively particular about detail, so doing a thing ANALLY is doing it in exactly that fussy manner.

  • a lone ranger → AN he is one man riding by himself, and the article for one is spelt this way in front of a vowel
  • might need → ALLY what somebody out on their own is short of is a friend on their side

A ranger riding alone who could do with some company.

Answer the question aloud and the two words run together into a single adverb: AN + ALLY. 'Particular' is the fussy, detail-obsessed sense, not 'specific'.
