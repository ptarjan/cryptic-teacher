Across

1 across RAGGED

Teased, wearing tatty clothes (6)

Definition: Teased · double definition

  • Teased → RAGGED to rag someone is to tease them, so the past tense fits
  • wearing tatty clothes → RAGGED dressed in rags — frayed and torn

The comma is the split. 'Rag' as a verb — the same rag as a student rag week — means to tease or torment, and the tatty clothing is there to bury that sense.

4 across STUMPS

End of play is baffling (6)

Definition: End of play · double definition

  • End of play → STUMPS the umpire's call when a day's cricket finishes
  • is baffling → STUMPS a question that defeats you stumps you

Cricket supplies the noun and everyday speech the verb; the join is 'is', which quietly turns the second half into a definition of what the answer does.

9 across OMIT

Love university, but fail (4)

Definition: fail · charade

  • Love → O love is nothing — the tennis zero
  • university → MIT the Massachusetts Institute of Technology

Love for zero and MIT for university are two of the most reused pieces of crossword furniture; knowing them turns this into a two-second clue.

10 across CREEPINESS

Virginia’s eerie quality? (10)

Definition: Virginia’s eerie quality? · cryptic definition

  • Virginia’s → not the American state and not Woolf: the setter means the vine on the wall, and leaves you to supply its second word
  • eerie quality → read straight — the abstract noun for whatever makes your skin crawl

The whole clue defines, with a pun holding it up: the plant is the Virginia creeper, and the question mark is the setter owning the joke.

11 across BONSAI

A pretentious type rejected one little-grown tree (6)

Definition: little-grown tree · reversal + charade

  • A pretentious type → BONSA A SNOB is a pretentious type, and 'rejected' turns the whole phrase round
  • one → I the Roman numeral

The 'A' at the head of the clue is fodder, not filler: it travels with the snob when the reversal happens, and that is what makes six letters land.

12 across ESURIENT

Bubbling tureen is making us very hungry (8)

Definition: very hungry · anagram

  • tureen is → ESURIENT the eight letters of TUREEN IS, stirred

'Bubbling' does double duty: a pot on the stove for the surface, an instruction to stir eight letters for the solver. The 'is' is fodder, not grammar.

13 across SEGREGATE

Isolate small wader scratching back at barrier (9)

Definition: Isolate · charade + deletion

  • small → S the clothes-label abbreviation
  • wader → EGRE the wader is an EGRET, scratched at the back
  • barrier → GATE a gate bars the way

The wader has to be the egret — a heron leaves the wrong letters — and 'scratching back' docks its tail. 'At' is holding the two halves together, nothing more.

15 across KNEE

One submissively bent is taken to offer respect (4)

Definition: One submissively bent · double definition

  • One submissively bent → KNEE to bend the knee is to submit
  • taken to offer respect → KNEE to take the knee is the modern gesture

English has two idioms about the same joint and they point opposite ways — one is a subject's surrender, the other a deliberate public statement. The clue splits at 'is'.

16 across SOUK

Very British marketplace (4)

Definition: marketplace · charade

  • Very → SO 'so tired' is 'very tired'
  • British → UK the country's own abbreviation

No indicator at all — the shortest kind of charade, where refusing to read 'Very British' as one phrase is the whole battle.

17 across SHOW TRIAL

Rehearsal for musical that has prearranged result? (4,5)

Definition: that has prearranged result? · charade

  • musical → SHOW a musical is a show
  • Rehearsal → TRIAL a trial run is a rehearsal

The pieces arrive in the wrong order on purpose: the word that ends the answer is clued first. Both halves are innocent theatre words until they meet.

21 across PASHMINA

Note a mishap, tumbling shawl (8)

Definition: shawl · anagram

  • Note → N n. is the standard abbreviation for note
  • a mishap → AMISHAP seven letters that join the N in the shuffle

'Note' is not a musical note here but the footnote abbreviation, and it is fodder rather than a piece to be placed. 'Tumbling' does the rest.

22 across REDACT

Edit colourful performance (6)

Definition: Edit · charade

  • colourful → RED one colour standing in for 'colourful'
  • performance → ACT an act is a performance

Definition first, then two everyday words. The only real work is deciding which colour 'colourful' wants, and the crossing letters settle that.

24 across ATTACHMENT

Fondness for use of paperclip (10)

Definition: Fondness · double definition

  • Fondness → ATTACHMENT an attachment to somebody is a fondness for them
  • use of paperclip → ATTACHMENT what a paperclip is for — and the icon email borrowed for the same job

The link word 'for' does the hiding: it reads as part of the first definition, 'fondness for', when it is really the join between the two halves.

25 across WHET

Stimulate husband, outwardly feeble (4)

Definition: Stimulate · container

  • feeble → WET a wet is a feeble sort — old public-school slang
  • husband → H H is the standard abbreviation for husband

'Outwardly' says the feeble word goes on the outside, not that anything is removed — an easy one to misread as a deletion.

26 across NOTARY

Charitable society has new head, a public official (6)

Definition: a public official · substitution

  • Charitable society → ROTARY the Rotary club, the charitable society of the high street
  • new → N N for new, taking over the front and ousting the R

A letter is swapped, not added: the club keeps its body and loses its head. The comma before the definition reads as an ordinary pause in a sentence.

27 across IDLEST

Most useless papers in case (6)

Definition: Most useless · charade

  • papers → ID your ID is the papers you carry
  • in case → LEST lest means in case — 'lest we forget'

'In case' is not a container instruction but a straight synonym, lest. Spotting that small archaic word is the whole clue.

Down

1 down RAM HOME

Emphasise lost sheep is found? (3,4)

Definition: Emphasise · charade

  • lost sheep → RAM the sheep of the parable, taken as a ram
  • is found? → HOME where the strayed animal is once it is found — safely home

The wordplay is one picture rather than two synonyms, and the question mark is the setter admitting it: the parable's sheep, back where it belongs.

2 down GATES

Bill for garden features (5)

Definition: Bill · double definition

  • Bill → GATES the Microsoft founder's other name
  • garden features → GATES what you open to walk into a garden

Five letters and a capital B, and the clue never says a person is involved. A bare first name is the setter's standing notice that a surname is wanted.

3 down ETCHING

Eating out as an artistic activity (7)

Definition: Eating out · double definition

  • Eating out → ETCHING acid biting into a metal plate eats the surface away
  • an artistic activity → ETCHING the printmaker's craft, and the print it produces

The surface offers a restaurant meal; the setter means chemistry. Both halves are -ing nouns, which is what lets one replace the other.

5 down TAP OUT

A penny accepted by ticket seller to leave the Tube (3,3)

Definition: leave the Tube · container + charade

  • ticket seller → TOUT a tout is who sells you a ticket outside the ground; he does the accepting
  • A penny → AP A, then P for penny — the pair go inside

P for penny is standing crossword furniture and 'accepted by' is the container signal. Tapping out is what an Oyster card does at the barrier on the way out.

6 down MONSIGNOR

Some Mormons ignore Catholic dignitary (9)

Definition: Catholic dignitary · hidden word

  • Mormons ignore → MONSIGNOR it straddles the gap between the two words: MorMONSIGNORe

'Some' is the quietest hidden-word marker there is, and a religious surface makes the religious answer feel like a coincidence rather than a signpost.

7 down SUSANNE

Girl from South America banned, leaving the borders (7)

Definition: Girl · charade + deletion

  • South → S the compass abbreviation
  • America → US the States, abbreviated
  • banned → ANNE bANNEd without its outer B and D

'From South America' looks like one place and is really two abbreviations laid end to end. 'Borders' means the first and last letters — a deletion worth banking.

8 down SEVENTH HEAVEN

To keep smooth, shave, then change: utter bliss (7,6)

Definition: utter bliss · anagram

  • To keep smooth → EVEN to even something is to keep it smooth; those four letters join the fodder
  • shave, then → SHAVETHEN nine more letters, taken exactly as they stand

One chunk of the fodder has to be translated before anything can be shuffled — 'keep smooth' becomes EVEN — and the colon fences the definition off at the end.

14 down ROUGHCAST

Feeling unwell, throw off outdoor coat (9)

Definition: outdoor coat · charade

  • Feeling unwell → ROUGH feeling rough is feeling off-colour
  • throw off → CAST to cast is to throw — you cast off, cast a line

'Coat' is the trap: no garment is involved. Roughcast is the pebbly render put on an outside wall, and the traditional way to apply it really is to throw it.

16 down SPARTAN

Simple skill in bridge (7)

Definition: Simple · container

  • bridge → SPAN a bridge spans a gap — a span
  • skill → ART art in the sense of craft, tucked inside

'In' is the smallest container indicator in the language and the easiest to read as ordinary English. Sparta's soldiers gave their name to going without.

18 down WORSTED

Fabric of lowest quality last to be used (7)

Definition: Fabric · charade + last letters

  • of lowest quality → WORST the superlative of bad
  • to be used → ED the closing letter of each word that has one: bE, useD

'Last' is an instruction, not part of the surface's sense of finishing a job — it picks off final letters. Worsted wool is named after Worstead in Norfolk.

19 down ANCIENT

Very old person’s nice tan misguided (7)

Definition: Very old person · anagram

  • nice tan → ANCIENT the seven letters of NICE TAN, re-sorted

The fodder hides in plain sight as a compliment about somebody's holiday. Read the definition as a noun: the answer here names a human being, not a property.

20 down ZITHER

Spot that woman’s musical instrument (6)

Definition: musical instrument · charade

  • Spot → ZIT a zit is a spot on the skin
  • that woman’s → HER the possessive of 'that woman'

A pimple and a pronoun build a concert instrument, and the join is invisible because neither half looks like crossword material.

23 down DOWSE

A quantity of drug wife’s swallowed: look for water (5)

Definition: look for water · container

  • A quantity of drug → DOSE a dose is the measured quantity you take
  • wife → W W is the standard abbreviation for wife

W for wife is one of the small abbreviations every setter keeps in the drawer, and 'swallowed' is the container signal. The colon marks the definition off.
