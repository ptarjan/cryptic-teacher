Guardian Quiptic Crossword No 1,396

Across

1 across LONG-STANDING

Well-established desire to hug bear (4-8)

Definition: Well-established · container

  • desire → LONGING a longing is a desire, and it parts after its fourth letter
  • bear → STAND if you cannot bear a person you cannot stand them

The surface asks you to picture hugging a bear. 'Bear' is really the verb meaning put up with, and the hug is one word opening to let another in.

9 across OSCAR

Love Mark getting award (5)

Definition: award · charade

  • Love → O love is nothing in tennis, and nothing is written as a nought
  • Mark → SCAR a scar is the mark an old wound leaves behind

The capital M makes 'Mark' look like somebody's name when it is an ordinary noun — a trick setters use whenever a word doubles as a first name.

10 across AUTOMATON

Fruit held by gold, new clockwork device (9)

Definition: clockwork device · container + charade

  • gold → AU AU is gold's symbol on the periodic table
  • Fruit → TOMATO botanically a fruit, whatever the salad suggests
  • new → N N is the standard abbreviation for new

The comma is the disguise: 'gold, new' looks like a description of the device and is really two abbreviations with the long word wedged between them.

11 across SCHNAPS

Sleeps following second cold then hot drink (7)

Definition: drink · charade

  • second → S S is the standard abbreviation for a second of time
  • cold → C C is the letter on the cold tap
  • hot → H H is the letter on the hot tap
  • Sleeps → NAPS someone who sleeps briefly in the afternoon naps

The German spelling takes a single P, so check it against the crossing letters. C and H are the pair of letters on a set of taps, and both turn up constantly.

12 across MEANDER

Snake wrongly renamed (7)

Definition: Snake · anagram

  • renamed → RENAMED seven letters that resettle into the answer

'Snake' is a verb here — a river snakes through a valley — and the noun at the front of a clue is always the cheapest misdirection a setter has.

13 across PERPETUATE

Preserve and tea peer put out (10)

Definition: Preserve · anagram

  • tea peer put → TEAPEERPUT ten letters, exactly the length the enumeration asks for

Counting is the way in here. The surface reads as an affronted aristocrat — 'put out' also means offended — while the three words in the middle are simply material.

15 across LESS

Leaders of legal establishment start speechifying to a smaller degree (4)

Definition: to a smaller degree · first letters

  • legal establishment start speechifying → LESS the first letter of each of the four words

'Legal establishment' reads as one idea, which is how the setter hides two of the four initials inside a single phrase. Four words in a row is the tell.

18 across BASK

Bishop to question lie (4)

Definition: lie · charade

  • Bishop → B B is the bishop in chess notation
  • question → ASK to question somebody is to ask them

Nothing in the clue tells you to add the pieces up: it simply sets them side by side, which is how the plainest charades work. B for bishop comes from chess.

19 across HANDICRAFT

Workmanship shown by worker in charge a lot (10)

Definition: Workmanship · charade

  • worker → HAND a farm hand or a deck hand is a worker
  • in charge → IC IC is how the officer in charge is written on a duty roster
  • a lot → RAFT a raft of measures is a great many of them

'Worker' is the crossword's favourite ambiguity — ANT, BEE or HAND, decided by the letter count. 'A raft of' for a large number is worth banking as well.

22 across DIABOLO

Old boa I reshaped into circus prop (7)

Definition: circus prop · anagram

  • Old boa I → OLDBOAI seven letters, the exact length of the answer

The snake and the feather stole are both decoys — 'boa' is here for its three letters. A stray 'I' swept into the material is a standard way of making the count work.

24 across PASTURE

Meadow beyond river (7)

Definition: Meadow · charade

  • beyond → PAST beyond the church means past it
  • river → URE the Ure runs through Wensleydale, one of the short rivers crosswords rely on

Rivers are the crossword's favourite three-letter filler — Ure, Exe, Cam, Dee, Po — and the bare word 'river' is the usual signal to reach for one.

25 across EMOTIONAL

Upset menial too sensitive (9)

Definition: sensitive · anagram

  • menial too → MENIALTOO nine letters that resettle as the answer

'Upset' does double duty: it describes the state of the person the answer fits and instructs you to disturb the letters. 'Too' is material, not an intensifier.

26 across OCTET

College note in books for group (5)

Definition: group · container + charade

  • books → OT the Old Testament, the books crosswords mean when they say books
  • College → C C is the abbreviation for college, as written in an address
  • note → TE te is the seventh note of the sol-fa scale

'Books' is nearly always one Testament or the other, with the crossing letters deciding which. Sol-fa supplies the notes: do, re, mi, fa, so, la, te.

27 across UNDERDRESSED

Renders dudes drunk and overly casual (12)

Definition: overly casual · anagram

  • Renders dudes → RENDERSDUDES twelve letters, exactly the length of the answer

Drunk is the commonest anagram signal there is, and here it also carries the surface: dudes at a party, turned out for a different one entirely.

Down

1 down LOCK HORNS

Engage in combat using bolt and car parts (4,5)

Definition: Engage in combat · charade

  • bolt → LOCK a bolt slid across a door is one kind of lock
  • car parts → HORNS the horn is the part of a car you sound, and the plural fills the second word

Two stags jamming their antlers together is where the phrase comes from, so 'car parts' has to work hard to keep you thinking about a garage instead.

2 down NARRATED

Managed to rise and judge first of defendants chronicled (8)

Definition: chronicled · charade + reversal + first letter

  • Managed → NAR managed a shop = RAN it, and this down entry reads those letters up the column
  • judge → RATE to judge a performance is to rate it
  • defendants → D the opening letter of Defendants

The surface puts a courtroom on its feet while the clue quietly stacks three pieces. RATE for judge is worth banking — it appears whenever a clue mentions marking or scoring.

3 down SCAMS

Son caught in the morning with first of scarpering cons (5)

Definition: cons · charade + first letter

  • Son → S S is the standard abbreviation for son
  • caught → C C is how a catch is recorded on a cricket scorecard
  • the morning → AM ante meridiem, the morning half of the clock
  • scarpering → S the leading letter of Scarpering

'Caught in the morning' sounds like a single idea and is really two abbreviations laid side by side. Four separate pieces for five letters is the Quiptic being generous.

4 down ATTEMPTED

Tried allure during unusual date (9)

Definition: Tried · container + anagram

  • date → ATED the four letters of DATE resettled, then parted to make room
  • allure → TEMPT to allure somebody is to tempt them

A date that reads as a romantic evening is only four letters of material, and the longest word in the clue is the one that ends up buried in the middle.

5 down DEMEAN

Humble setter embraced by churchman (6)

Definition: Humble · container

  • churchman → DEAN a dean runs a cathedral chapter
  • setter → ME the person setting the puzzle calls themselves ME, one of the oldest crossword conventions

'Humble' is the verb here rather than the adjective, which is the whole difficulty. The setter turning up inside a word as ME is worth banking for good.

6 down NOTED

Celebrated journalist after negative time (5)

Definition: Celebrated · charade

  • negative → NO the plainest negative answer in the language
  • time → T T is the standard abbreviation for time, as in a physics formula
  • journalist → ED an editor is the journalist crosswords always reach for

The clue has to be read back to front: its last two words are the first letters of the answer. ED for a journalist is standing crossword vocabulary.

7 down GOSSIP

Try and go up for idle chat (6)

Definition: idle chat · charade + reversal

  • Try → GO a try at something is a go at it
  • go → SSIP 'go' is the household euphemism for PISS, and a down entry reads those four letters upwards

The same little word does two different jobs a few letters apart: once as an ordinary synonym, once as the euphemism small children are taught.

8 down SNARES

Various reasons love left traps (6)

Definition: traps · anagram + deletion

  • reasons → REASONS the seven letters the wordplay starts from
  • love → O love is nothing in tennis, written as a nought, and it is the letter that departs

Three instructions in five words: take a word, drop a letter, rearrange the rest. Love for O comes from the tennis score and is one of the most useful conventions there is.

14 down UNADORNED

Simple doctor adored nun (9)

Definition: Simple · anagram

  • adored nun → ADOREDNUN nine letters that rearrange into the answer

'Doctor' looks like a second character standing beside the nun and is really the instruction. Verbs meaning tamper — doctor, cook, fix — are the anagram signals to learn first.

16 down EVALUATED

Left Charlie out for line to be judged (9)

Definition: to be judged · substitution

  • Left → EVACUATED a building left in a hurry has been evacuated
  • Charlie → C Charlie is C in the radio alphabet, and it is the letter that goes
  • line → L L is the standard abbreviation for line, and it moves in

The answer is a single letter away from a word meaning left. Radio-alphabet names — Charlie, Oscar, Romeo, Victor — always stand for one letter each.

17 down SCISSORS

Small cross is reformed as cutting instrument (8)

Definition: cutting instrument · anagram

  • Small → S S is the label on the smallest size of shirt
  • cross is → CROSSIS seven letters more, which with the S make the eight wanted

The trap is reading 'is' as grammar. It is material, and once you count it in, the letter count matches the enumeration exactly.

18 down BUDGET

Mate to obtain spending allowance (6)

Definition: spending allowance · charade

  • Mate → BUD bud is the American way of addressing a mate
  • obtain → GET to obtain a thing is to get it

Both halves are everyday words, so all the difficulty lies in seeing where to cut. Deciding that 'Mate' means friend, not spouse or chess move, is the first step.

20 down TWENTY

Note clue number (6)

Definition: Note · double definition

  • Note → the everyday word for a banknote of that value, as heard in casual requests to borrow one
  • clue number → this entry is number 20 in the grid, so its own label is the answer

The second half looks as though it defines nothing, because it points at the clue itself rather than at the world. Self-reference like this is rare and always worth checking for.

21 down MOBILE

Crowd lie about phone (6)

Definition: phone · charade + anagram

  • Crowd → MOB a mob is a disorderly crowd
  • lie → ILE the three letters of LIE strewn into a new order

A British mobile is what an American calls a cell, so 'phone' alone is the definition. Three letters barely disturbed are the hardest kind of rearrangement to notice.

23 down ACORN

A seed fruit (5)

Definition: fruit · charade

  • A → A the clue's own opening word, taken at face value
  • seed → CORN corn is seed grain, the kernel sown in a field

Three words with nothing to spare: a letter, a synonym and a definition. Short clues hide their join because 'A seed' reads so naturally as one phrase.

24 down PULSE

Beat bean? (5)

Definition: Beat · double definition

  • Beat → the throb you feel at the wrist is the beat of the blood
  • bean → beans, peas and lentils belong to a crop family that carries this same name

Two words and a question mark. The mark is the setter admitting the second definition is only an example, since a bean is one kind of the answer rather than the whole class.
