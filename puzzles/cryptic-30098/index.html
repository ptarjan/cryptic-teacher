Across

9 across TEENSIEST

One of perhaps 16 having short nap – couldn’t be shorter (9)

Definition: couldn’t be shorter · charade + deletion

  • One of perhaps 16 → TEEN a sixteen-year-old is one of the teens, the years from thirteen to nineteen
  • short nap → SIEST a siesta is the afternoon nap, docked of its last letter

The bare 16 looks like a cross-reference to another entry in the grid. It is only the number, and the age it names is the whole point.

10 across MAINE

Here’s Augusta: important, second in Georgia (5)

Definition: Here’s Augusta · charade + second letter

  • important → MAIN the main issue is the important one
  • second in Georgia → E the second letter of that state's name, and nothing else of it

There is an Augusta in Georgia too, the home of the Masters, so the clue's own last word is the red herring and lends nothing but a single letter.

11 across LATERAN

Behind sailors touring a palace (7)

Definition: palace · charade + container

  • Behind → LATE a train running behind is running late
  • sailors → RN RN is the Royal Navy, the crossword's standard sailors
  • a → A the clue's own a, taken as a single letter

'Behind' opens the clue looking like the definition, a rear end, and turns out to be the first piece of the build instead.

13 across NERVY

Anxious, not at first very worried (5)

Definition: Anxious · first letter + anagram

  • not at first → N the opening letter of 'not' and no more of it
  • very → ERVY four letters shaken out of the order they arrived in

In British use nervy means jumpy and on edge; an American reader hears the opposite, since over there a nervy move is a bold one.

14 across MEALTIMES

Imogen has key to get back home for these regular occasions (9)

Definition: regular occasions · charade + reversal

  • Imogen → ME Imogen is this puzzle's setter, and a setter naming herself in her own clue means ME
  • key → ALT Alt is the key beside the space bar on every keyboard
  • home → IMES a semi is a semi-detached house, the standard British suburban home, written the other way round

Setters sign their own clues more often than you would expect, and 'Imogen has' reads as a sentence about a person rather than an instruction to write two letters.

16 across RUSSIAN ROULETTE

Bad service aboard Trans-Siberian railway, say, a perilous enterprise (7,8)

Definition: a perilous enterprise · container

  • Trans-Siberian railway, say → RUSSIANROUTE the line from Moscow to Vladivostok is one such way across that country, and it opens up near the end
  • Bad service → LET in tennis a let is a serve that clips the net and has to be taken again

Every word of the surface is about a shabby train journey, so the definition at the end reads like more of the same description rather than the answer.

19 across DECALITRE

Large quantity of drink: declare it bubbly (9)

Definition: Large quantity of drink · anagram

  • declare it → DECLAREIT nine letters of raw material, taken from the two words together

'Bubbly' is a noun in the surface — champagne, exactly the thing you might have to declare at customs — and an instruction in the parse.

21 across HEROD

Murderous monarch, one idolised by daughter (5)

Definition: Murderous monarch · charade

  • one idolised → HERO the person a fan idolises is their hero
  • daughter → D D is the family-tree abbreviation for daughter

The surface paints a king adored by his own child, which is close to the opposite of the story the definition is pointing at.

22 across NEONATE

Sort of light worried baby (7)

Definition: baby · charade

  • Sort of light → NEON neon is the gas in the tube, one sort of light among many
  • worried → ATE what's eating you? — to eat at somebody is to worry them

Both halves are ordinary words used in their second sense, and neither the light nor the worry has anything to do with a nursery.

23 across WOLFISH

Want to catch falcon regularly returning ravenous (7)

Definition: ravenous · container + alternate letters + reversal

  • Want → WISH a want is a wish, and the word opens after its first letter
  • falcon regularly → OLF the first, third and fifth letters of the bird, then turned round

The falconry surface is a decoy: the bird's name is there only to be raided for letters, and the definition is about appetite.

24 across CORFU

Island shivering in 4C? (5)

Definition: Island · anagram

  • 4C → FOURC the numeral written out in letters, with the C of the temperature after it

A bare number in a Guardian clue usually points at another entry, and four degrees really would make you shiver; both readings are there to keep you off the letters.

25 across SOPHOCLES

Dramatist’s concession, filling gaps with a bit of comedy (9)

Definition: Dramatist · charade + container + first letter

  • concession → SOP a sop is a small concession thrown to somebody to keep them quiet
  • gaps → HOLES gaps in a road or an argument are holes, and this word is prised open in the middle
  • a bit of comedy → C the opening letter of comedy and nothing more of it

The surface is a playwright patching up a script, and the joke is buried in 'a bit of comedy': the man wrote tragedies.

Down

1 down STALINGRAD

Last mad daring involved in horrendous battle (10)

Definition: horrendous battle · anagram

  • Last → LAST four letters of raw material, taken exactly as they stand
  • daring → DARING six more letters, to be jumbled together with the first four

The surface reads as a report of one last reckless act, and the two words that sound most like description, mad and involved, are both instructions.

2 down FEATURES

Exploit you reserve for films (8)

Definition: films · charade

  • Exploit → FEAT a feat is an exploit, a deed worth telling people about
  • you → U the letter U does duty for 'you', the way a text message does
  • reserve → RES res. is how reserve is written on a team sheet or a booking

Nothing here looks like an instruction, because all three pieces are ordinary words taken at face value and the surface stays a sentence about a film collection.

3 down ASTRAY

A second attempt to fix a wrong (6)

Definition: wrong · charade + container

  • A → A the clue's opening A, taken as a single letter
  • second → S S is the second on a stopwatch
  • attempt → TRY an attempt at something is a try at it
  • a → A the clue's other a, again a letter at face value

'A wrong' at the end wants to be a noun, an injustice somebody has to put right; the definition is the last word on its own, and it is an adverb.

4 down FERN

Plant conifer, not houses (4)

Definition: Plant · hidden word

  • conifer, not → FERN four consecutive letters run straight across the comma, beginning inside the tree

'Plant conifer' reads as an instruction from a gardening column, which is what stops you noticing that a second, smaller plant is sitting inside the first.

5 down STANDALONE

Time to wear first shoe? Capable of operating independently (10)

Definition: Capable of operating independently · charade + container

  • shoe → SANDAL a sandal is a shoe, and this one opens up after its first letter
  • first → ONE first is number one, written as a label the way 'take one' is
  • Time → T T is the standard abbreviation for time

'First shoe' is written the way a label is, shoe number one, and the question mark is the setter owning that liberty; the surface is just somebody getting dressed.

6 down IMMORTAL

Troy protected by unscrupulous Olympian (8)

Definition: Olympian · container

  • unscrupulous → IMMORAL someone unscrupulous has no morals, and the word parts near its end
  • Troy → T t is the abbreviation for troy, the system of weights gold and silver are sold by

Troy is capitalised to look like the besieged city, and the classical surface helps: an Olympian here is a god of the mountain, not an athlete at the games.

7 down WISDOM

Good advice in most of cricket book, offering much for openers (6)

Definition: Good advice · charade + deletion + first letters

  • most of cricket book → WISD Wisden is the fat yellow almanack that has recorded every first-class cricket match since 1864, and it hands over the greater part of itself
  • offering much → OM the opening letters of the two words, in the order they come

The whole surface is a review praising a cricket annual, and 'for openers' does double duty: batting slang in the picture, an instruction in the parse.

8 down SEXY

Turning up, agreed to accept kiss: so exciting (4)

Definition: exciting · reversal + container

  • agreed → SEY agreed is yes, set climbing up the column
  • kiss → X X is the kiss at the foot of a letter

Kiss meaning X is one of the first conventions worth learning, and the colon does the joining: everything before it builds, everything after it defines.

14 down MINSTRELSY

Old art of singing in church, energy dropping, intermittently lusty (10)

Definition: Old art of singing · charade + anagram + alternate letters

  • church, energy dropping → MINSTRE a minster is a great church, as at York or Beverley, with its E fallen one place down the column
  • lusty → LSY the first, third and fifth letters of the word, taken in turn

'In church' looks like where the singing happens and is really the building's other name; the E is not deleted, it only shifts down a place.

15 down SPEED CHESS

Talk about date followed by letter, quick way to seek a mate (5,5)

Definition: quick way to seek a mate · container + charade

  • Talk → SPEECH a talk given to an audience is a speech, and it splits after four letters
  • date → D d is the standard abbreviation for date
  • letter → ESS ess is how the letter S is spelt out in full

The surface is all online dating — talk, date, letter, seeking a mate — and the mate actually wanted is the one that ends a game at the board.

17 down IN LEAGUE

Working together, fit to join lineout (2,6)

Definition: Working together · anagram

  • fit → AGUE an ague is a shivering fit of fever, so 'fit' is the seizure and not the adjective for healthy
  • lineout → LINE the first four letters of the rugby set piece are the material to work on; the rest of the word is doing the other job

A lineout is the restart a player might be fit to join, and printing it as one word is what hides the seam: four letters of material, then the instruction.

18 down TERRIBLE

Barrel carried by dog finally dropped? Shocking (8)

Definition: Shocking · container + deletion

  • dog finally dropped → TERRIE a terrier is a dog, docked here of its final letter
  • Barrel → BL bl is the trade abbreviation for a barrel, the unit oil and beer are measured in

The surface is a shaggy picture of a dog with a barrel at its collar, and 'dropped' sits inside it doing the work of an instruction.

20 down CHOIRS

Rich, so getting replacement for several groups of singers (6)

Definition: several groups of singers · anagram

  • Rich, so → RICHSO six letters of raw material, taken from both words

'Rich, so' does nothing in the surface beyond sounding like a wealthy man's excuse, and that is exactly what makes it hard to see as raw material.

21 down HALLOW

Greeting wife, giving reverence (6)

Definition: reverence · charade

  • Greeting → HALLO hallo is the greeting, in the older spelling without an E
  • wife → W W is the standard abbreviation for wife

'Reverence' is a verb here rather than a noun, which is easy to miss: the definition is an action, and so is the answer.

22 down NECK

Kiss cheek (4)

Definition: Kiss · double definition

  • Kiss → the teenage sense of the verb: to kiss and cuddle at length
  • cheek → British slang for brass-faced impudence, the nerve to ask for something outrageous

Two words, two senses, and nothing at all between them — the shorter a double definition is, the harder it is to spot that it is one.

23 down WEPT

Showed sorrow as all of us part (4)

Definition: Showed sorrow · charade

  • all of us → WE all of us, speaking of ourselves, is we
  • part → PT pt is how part is abbreviated on a title page

'As all of us part' reads as a leave-taking, and the definition belongs to the same picture, so the surface never once breaks character.
