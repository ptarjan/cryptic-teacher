Across

1 across SPOT-ON

Spy working precisely (4-2)

Definition: precisely · charade

  • Spy → SPOT to spy something is to spot it — catch sight of it across a room
  • working → ON a machine that is working is on, the way a switch is

Two ordinary words sit side by side with nothing between them, and the surface makes them one job description. Neither is a noun about espionage: one is a verb, one is a setting.

4 across DESSERTS

Puddings - anxious - sent back (8)

Definition: Puddings · reversal

  • anxious → DESSERTS somebody anxious is stressed, and that word is turned end for end

In Britain 'pudding' names the sweet course whatever is on the plate, so the plural is fair. The pleasure of the clue is that the complaint and the cure are the same eight letters.

10 across HEEDFULLY

Henry necessarily not beginning in attentive manner (9)

Definition: in attentive manner · charade + deletion

  • Henry → H H is the symbol for the henry, the unit of electrical inductance
  • necessarily not beginning → EEDFULLY NEEDFULLY means necessarily, with its opening N gone

Nothing in the surface hints at physics, which is the trick: a man's first name shrunk to a single letter is one of the cheapest disguises a setter has.

11 across MISER

Flipping Everyman's ultimately parsimonious: code for 'skinflint' (5)

Definition: 'skinflint' · charade + reversal + last letters

  • Everyman's → MI the setter is Everyman, so 'Everyman's' is his own I'm — two letters, turned over
  • parsimonious: code for → SER the closing letter of each of those three words in turn

'Code for' reads as joinery announcing a synonym, and that is the trap — it is three of the five letters. The quotation marks are the tell: the quoted word is the definition.

12 across ADVISOR

I recommend Bill gets protection from sun (7)

Definition: I recommend · charade

  • Bill → AD a bill pasted on a wall is an advertisement, an ad
  • protection from sun → VISOR the stiff peak on a cap, or the flap you pull down in a car

'Bill' reads as a man being told to wear a hat, and the capital letter sells it. It is the poster sense, which is why the shortest word in the clue carries two letters.

14 across TOPKNOT

King wearing t-shirt, perhaps, not 'man-bun'-style hairdo (7)

Definition: 'man-bun'-style hairdo · charade + container

  • t-shirt, perhaps → TOP a t-shirt is one kind of top, the general word for anything worn above the waist
  • King → K K is king on a chessboard and on a playing card
  • not → NOT taken exactly as printed, three letters straight off the page

The king is wearing more than the t-shirt: his letter goes inside the pair of words, not inside either one. Hyphens and quote marks dress the rest up as fashion talk, so the plainest word on the page reads as grammar rather than letters.

15 across PERCUSSIONIST

Musician wanted for buffets? (13)

Definition: Musician wanted for buffets? · cryptic definition

  • Musician → the honest half of the clue: whoever this is, they play in the band and are being hired for it
  • wanted for buffets? → a buffet is also a blow — the wind buffets a ship — so what is being advertised for is somebody to hit things

Read the last word as the cold table and the clue books a player for a party. The other buffet is a battering, and delivering those all evening is the job.

18 across HARBOURMASTER

Damaged sub with mortar: hear who's overseeing the boats? (13)

Definition: who's overseeing the boats? · anagram

  • sub with mortar: hear → HARBOURMASTER thirteen letters spread across those three words, rearranged

'Hear' is the trap. Sitting after a colon it reads as an instruction to listen for a sound-alike, and it is raw material — four of the thirteen letters.

20 across CHEDDAR

Pop diva's entertaining, making a comeback, total cheese (7)

Definition: cheese · container + reversal

  • Pop diva → CHER the American singer of Believe, a one-name star since the sixties
  • total → ADD to total a column of figures is to add them up, and it goes in backwards

'Total cheese' is one insult on the surface — utter rubbish — and the setter has split it down the middle: the first word is an instruction, the second is the definition.

22 across CLEMENT

Kind of article mentioned (7)

Definition: Kind · hidden word

  • article mentioned → CLEMENT seven letters in a row, running across the join between the two words

'Mentioned' looks like an instruction to listen, and 'Kind of' reads as one phrase meaning 'a sort of'. Both readings send you straight past the letters lying in plain sight.

23 across ORGAN

The Observer's heart? (5)

Definition: The Observer · double definition

  • The Observer → a newspaper is the mouthpiece of a body or a cause, the word the trade uses for a house journal
  • heart → the pump in your chest, one of the body's working parts

Both halves are examples rather than synonyms, which is what the question mark is apologising for. A Sunday paper and a heart have nothing else whatever in common.

24 across CURVEBALL

Recreational vehicle inspired by piece of snooker equipment? That's unexpected (9)

Definition: That's unexpected · container

  • Recreational vehicle → RV RV is the American short form: the camper van you drive across a continent
  • piece of snooker equipment? → CUE BALL the white one, the only ball a player is allowed to strike

Snooker is the wrong sport entirely — the answer comes off a baseball mound. The question mark is there because a table holds twenty-two balls and only one of them is meant.

26 across LITTERED

Behaved antisocially and gave birth (8)

Definition: Behaved antisocially · double definition

  • Behaved antisocially → dropping rubbish in the street is the offence, and the verb for doing it comes straight off the noun
  • gave birth → what a sow or a bitch does: the whole brood arrives together

One verb in two worlds — the rubbish on the pavement and the puppies in the basket — with nothing but the shared noun holding them together.

27 across CACTUS

In Cuba and America, pretend to be one that can last a while without drink (6)

Definition: one that can last a while without drink · charade + container

  • Cuba → C C is Cuba's letter on international vehicle plates
  • pretend → ACT to act is to pretend, on a stage or off it
  • America → US the two-letter short form of the United States

A country either side and a bit of theatre in the middle make a plausible sentence about travelling under a false name, which is all the surface is for.

Down

1 down SCHNAPPS

Strong liquor: drunkard's called for pics (8)

Definition: Strong liquor · homophone

  • pics → SCHNAPPS pics are snaps, and thickened in the mouth of somebody who has had a few, that one syllable comes out with a German spelling

The colon works as an equals sign: everything after it is one word said badly. The joke is that the drink and the photographs are the same request once the speaker has been at the bottle.

2 down ONE OVER THE EIGHT

Nine or three sheets to the wind? (3,4,3,5)

Definition: Nine · double definition

  • Nine → read as arithmetic: add a single unit to eight and this is the count you reach
  • three sheets to the wind? → a sailor's phrase for very drunk — the sheets are ropes, and three of them loose leave the boat lurching

The same fifteen letters are handed over twice, once as a sum and once as an idiom, with 'or' doing nothing but joining them. British pubs supply the second half.

3 down OAF

Primarily obtuse, asinine fathead? (3)

Definition: Primarily obtuse, asinine fathead? · &lit + first letters

  • obtuse → O its opening letter; obtuse means slow on the uptake
  • asinine → A its opening letter; asinine means like an ass
  • fathead? → F its opening letter; a fathead is a blockhead

Read once for letters and once for sense: the three insults describe the very person their initials spell, which is what makes the whole clue do both jobs at once.

5 down EGYPT

Type up welcoming German country (5)

Definition: country · anagram

  • Type → TYPE used for its four letters, not for anything it means
  • German → G G is the dictionary's label for the German language

In a down clue 'up' usually lifts a word backwards up the grid, and that reading spells nothing here. It is the looser sense — left unsettled — that the setter wants.

6 down SEMI-PRIVATE

Spelled out imperatives, not entirely in public (4-7)

Definition: not entirely in public · anagram

  • imperatives → SEMI-PRIVATE one word supplies all eleven letters wanted, and no others

'Not entirely' looks as though it is trimming a letter off the fodder. It belongs to the definition instead — the four words after the comma are one phrase meaning halfway public.

7 down RISING TO THE BAIT

Hesitator, biting unwisely - fish's last action? (6,2,3,4)

Definition: fish's last action? · anagram

  • Hesitator, biting → RISING TO THE BAIT fifteen letters between those two words, exactly the fifteen the grid wants

'Biting unwisely' describes the phrase so neatly that it reads as the definition, which is the trap: it is the raw material, and the definition is the angler's view from the bank.

8 down STRATA

American pastries rejected, more than one rank (6)

Definition: more than one rank · charade + reversal

  • American → A A is the dictionary's short form for American
  • pastries → TARTS tarts are pastries, open cases with jam or fruit in them

'More than one' is a nudge that the answer is already a plural, of the Latin kind that does not end in S. 'Rank' is the layer sense — a level of society, not a military grade.

9 down ALFRESCO

Cake-burner not finishing half of scones: like a picnic? (8)

Definition: like a picnic? · charade + deletion

  • Cake-burner not finishing → ALFRE King Alfred, who in the old English story was left to watch a peasant woman's cakes and let them char, minus his last letter
  • half of scones → SCO the first three of its six letters

Cakes and scones keep one baking scene running from end to end, and only the first of them is about a person at all — the second is in the clue for three letters.

13 down SQUARE DANCE

Social event that's hardly a ball! (6,5)

Definition: Social event that's hardly a ball! · cryptic definition

  • Social event → the straight half: this really is a party with music, held in a hall with a caller shouting the moves to sets of four couples
  • that's hardly a ball! → two senses in four words — a formal dance IS a ball, and yet the shape this one is named after is the least round thing there is

The exclamation mark sells disappointment: hardly a ball, meaning hardly any fun. The setter means it geometrically, and the event he is describing is a ball after all.

16 down ON RECORD

Noted repeatedly concerning electrical component (2,6)

Definition: Noted · charade

  • concerning → ON a book on birds is a book concerning them
  • concerning → RE RE heads a memo to mean 'concerning', straight out of the Latin
  • electrical component → CORD the flex running from a lamp to the wall

One word is asked for two answers, and the surface hides that by making 'repeatedly' sound like part of the noting rather than an instruction about the next word.

17 down PRATTLES

Babbles, splatter splashing (8)

Definition: Babbles · anagram

  • splatter → PRATTLES its eight letters, redistributed

The two long words sit together so naturally that the second reads as description rather than instruction, and the definition is left doing all its work in one word at the front.

19 down SCROLL

Use your phone to find self-catering room with bill 50% reduced (6)

Definition: Use your phone · charade + deletion

  • self-catering → SC s/c is how a British holiday listing abbreviates self-catering, where you cook for yourself
  • room → RO the front half of its four letters
  • bill → LL the back half of its four letters

The surface is a small ad for a cheap holiday let, priced down and everything, while the definition is about a thumb. Half-price is the only genuine instruction in it.

21 down RECCE

Intelligence-gathering mission giving description of sunken ships, did you say? (5)

Definition: Intelligence-gathering mission · homophone

  • description of sunken ships → RECCE sunken ships are wrecks, so a stretch of coast full of them is wrecky — and the W at the front of that word is silent

British forces shorten reconnaissance to five letters and say it 'recky', which is the whole clue: the wreck word loses nothing but its silent W on the way.

25 down ERA

Age of beermat regularly noted (3)

Definition: Age · alternate letters

  • beermat → ERA the second, fourth and sixth of its seven letters

A beermat is the cardboard coaster a pub glass stands on, and 'noted' slips by as part of that picture instead of the instruction it really is.
