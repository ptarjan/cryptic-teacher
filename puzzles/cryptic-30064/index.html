Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,064

Across

8 across HALF-INCH

Nick and Henry Bird (4-4)

Definition: Nick · charade

Half-inch is Cockney rhyming slang for pinch, and to pinch a thing is to nick it, so the definition works through the rhyme.

  • Henry → HAL Hal is Shakespeare's Prince Hal, the future Henry V
  • Bird → FINCH a finch is a bird

The (4-4) enumeration says two hyphenated halves, and the join falls inside the bird rather than between the two clue words.

9 across CORAL

Pink cattle pen? Not right! (5)

Definition: Pink · deletion

Coral names the pinkish-orange colour of the sea creature's skeleton, so 'Pink' asks for a shade rather than the reef itself.

  • cattle pen → CORRAL a corral is the pen cowboys drive cattle into
  • right → R r is the standard abbreviation for right, and it is the letter dropped
  • Not — A blunt cancellation: whatever it points at is struck out of the letters already assembled.

Two exclamatory scraps that read as a rancher's grumble are really an instruction: keep the pen, lose one of its letters.

10 across UGLY

Articulated fruit is not fair (4)

Definition: not fair · homophone

Fair in its old sense means lovely to look at, so its negative is unlovely: the plain visual meaning, not unjust.

  • fruit → UGLY the ugli is a Jamaican citrus hybrid, and its name spoken aloud is what goes in the grid
  • Articulated — To articulate is to say something out clearly, so the word after it is wanted as a noise, not a spelling.

'Fair' is the old word for beautiful, so the definition has nothing to do with justice, which is exactly what the surface hopes you assume.

11 across BANKRUPTCY

Left and right wings of party turn back, collapsing into ruin (10)

Definition: ruin · anagram + outer letters

Ruin in the financial sense: the declared state of being unable to pay, which a court pronounces, rather than a general downfall.

  • party → PY the outer letters of PARTY, the wings at its left and right
  • turn back → TURNBACK anagram fodder, eight letters
  • Left and right wings of — The wings of a word are the letters at its two edges, and a party is the one thing that has a left and a right one.
  • collapsing — A collapsing thing loses its shape, so the letters no longer hold the order they were handed to you in.

The political surface does all the work: 'turn back' reads as an instruction to reverse something and is simply eight letters waiting to be jumbled.

12 across STRESS

Hair loss ultimately comes before anxiety (6)

Definition: anxiety · charade + last letter

Stress is the mental strain that 'anxiety' names in ordinary speech, not the engineer's load on a beam or the emphasis put on a syllable.

  • loss → S the last letter of loss
  • Hair → TRESS a tress is a lock of hair
  • ultimately — It points at the very end of the word beside it, so only that final letter is taken.
  • comes before — It fixes the order of the two pieces: the letter just taken goes in front, not behind.

Hair loss reads as one idea and is two pieces of machinery — and the answer is also a cause of it, which is the joke underneath.

14 across RAREFIED

Are fired (sacked), being thin (8)

Definition: thin · anagram

Rarefied air is thin air: the word describes a gas thinned out, and by extension a social circle too exclusive to breathe in.

  • Are fired → AREFIRED anagram fodder, eight letters
  • sacked — Beyond the dismissal, to sack a town is to ransack it, and what has been ransacked is left in complete disorder.

The bracketed word looks like a helpful gloss on 'fired' and is really the instruction, which is why it hides so well.

16 across PRECEDE

Herald’s power decline (7)

Definition: Herald · charade

To herald a thing is to go in front of it and announce it: the answer is the plain verb of going first, without the fanfare.

  • power → P P is the physicist's symbol for power
  • decline → RECEDE to recede is to decline, as a tide or a hairline goes back

The apostrophe-s is joinery, not possession: read it as 'herald is power plus decline' and the clue falls open.

18 across INQUEST

Hearing of pub expedition, neighbours start off (7)

Definition: Hearing · charade + first letter + deletion

An inquest is the coroner's formal hearing into a sudden death, so 'Hearing' names it by the kind of legal proceeding it is.

  • pub → INN a pub is an inn
  • neighbours → N the first letter of neighbours, and the one the inn gives up
  • expedition → QUEST an expedition in search of something is a quest
  • start — It asks for the head of the word beside it, the single letter that word begins with.
  • off — Off means away rather than departing: the letter just named comes out of what has been built.

The surface is village gossip about a pub outing, and the shortest word in it is the one carrying the instruction.

21 across TRIPTYCH

Panels damaged – try pitch (8)

Definition: Panels · anagram

A triptych is an altarpiece or picture painted on three hinged panels, so the definition points at it through the parts it is made of.

  • try pitch → TRYPITCH anagram fodder, eight letters
  • damaged — Something damaged has been knocked out of shape, and letters knocked about no longer sit where they started.

Both fodder words belong to rugby, which is what keeps 'damaged' reading as an injury report rather than an instruction.

23 across PATOIS

Dialect reveals smooth love lives (6)

Definition: Dialect · charade

A patois is the local vernacular of a region, often a creole one, so 'Dialect' is the plain word for the same kind of speech.

  • smooth → PAT a pat answer is a smooth one, delivered too readily
  • love → O love is nothing in tennis, written as O
  • lives → IS he lives = he is, the verb in its third person

'Love lives' reads as a headline about romance and is two separate scraps of crosswordese stacked on top of each other.

24 across ANKLE-BITER

Kid’s rental bike fixed (5-5)

Definition: Kid · anagram

Ankle-biter is Australian and British slang for a small child, low enough to bite you there, so 'Kid' is the everyday word for one.

  • rental bike → RENTALBIKE anagram fodder, ten letters
  • fixed — A fixed race is one arranged to somebody's liking, so these letters end up in an order somebody chose.

The (5-5) enumeration promises two hyphenated halves, and the surface, a child's hired bike being mended, could hardly look more innocent.

26 across GUTS

Bottle essential parts (4)

Definition: Bottle · double definition

Courage in slang, the nerve you either have or lose, and also the innards of a thing, the working parts without which it is a shell.

  • Bottle → GUTS bottle is British slang for nerve: losing your bottle is losing your courage
  • essential parts → GUTS the essential inner workings of a machine, the parts that make it function, are described this way

Two everyday senses side by side with nothing joining them, which is what makes a four-word clue this hard to see.

27 across EIGHT

Crew strive to get leader replaced (5)

Definition: Crew · substitution

A rowing eight is the crew of eight oarsmen who row it, so the number stands for the whole boatload, a sense crosswords use constantly.

  • strive → FIGHT to strive for a cause is to fight for it
  • leader → F the F at the front of FIGHT is the letter turned out
  • replaced → E the setter never names the incoming letter, so the definition is what fixes it as E
  • leader replaced — A word's leader is the letter at its front, and replacing it means a different letter takes that seat rather than the word simply losing one.

The surface is a leadership challenge and the cryptic reading wants one at the head of a word. Only the definition says which letter moves in.

28 across ARCHIVES

Shrewd US composer making records (8)

Definition: records · charade

Archives are the documents an institution keeps, so 'records' is the plain plural synonym, the stored papers rather than the act of recording.

  • Shrewd → ARCH an arch look is a knowing, shrewd one
  • US composer → IVES Charles Ives, the American composer of The Unanswered Question

'Making records' reads as a session in a studio; the composer is here for his surname alone, which is how setters use famous people.

Down

1 down GANGSTER

Tough comedian without name (8)

Definition: Tough · container

A tough is a noun for a violent thug, and a gangster is the organised professional version — the definition names the type, the answer the trade.

  • comedian → GAGSTER a gagster is a comedian, a trader in gags
  • name → N n. is the standard abbreviation for name
  • without — The archaic sense, as in 'without the city walls' — outside rather than lacking, so the comedian goes round what follows.

Everything turns on hearing 'without' as outside rather than lacking: the comedian keeps all his letters and merely opens up to admit one more.

2 down IFFY

Just a second to remove top? Doubtful (4)

Definition: Doubtful · deletion

Something iffy is unreliable and not to be trusted, so 'Doubtful' is the dubious-quality sense rather than a person harbouring doubts.

  • Just a second → JIFFY 'just a second' is a jiffy, the moment you ask someone to wait
  • top → J the first letter of JIFFY sits at the top of a down entry, and it is the one that goes
  • to remove top — In a down entry the top is where the word begins, so this asks for the opening letter to go.

The question mark earns its keep: it warns that the words before it are an instruction to the solver, not more of the plea to wait a moment.

3 down ANUBIS

A core island sun god (6)

Definition: god · charade

Definition by class: the answer is one named deity and 'god' is the whole category, leaving the three small pieces to say which one.

  • A → A taken at face value, straight off the page
  • core → NUB the nub of a matter is its core
  • island → IS Is. is the map-maker's abbreviation for island
  • sun → surface only: it paints the island scene and hands over no letters

Anubis is the jackal-headed Egyptian god of embalming and the dead, so 'god' alone is the definition and the sun belongs to the picture the surface paints.

4 down BHANGRA

Depend on getting in support for a music genre (7)

Definition: a music genre · container

Bhangra is the Punjabi dance music that crossed into British pop, so the definition places it by category rather than describing its sound.

  • support → BRA a bra is a support, the crossword's favourite undergarment
  • Depend → HANG to depend on something is to hang on it
  • getting in — It is an instruction to climb inside: the piece it follows ends up lodged within the piece it precedes.

'Depend' comes from the Latin for hanging down, which is why the swap is so clean; the surface reads as leaning on somebody for a favour.

5 down ACER

Wizard at foot of elder tree (4)

Definition: tree · charade + last letter

Acer is the botanical genus of maples and sycamores, so 'tree' defines the answer by the class its Latin name belongs to.

  • Wizard → ACE a wizard at something is an ace at it
  • elder → R the last letter of elder
  • at foot of — The foot of a word is its end, so only the closing letter of the word beside it is wanted.

Elder is itself a tree, which is the trap: it looks like the definition and is really just a word being asked for its final letter.

6 down GRAPEFRUIT

Fig, pear almost turn out to be the possible basis of fad diet (10)

Definition: possible basis of fad diet · anagram + deletion

The grapefruit diet was a real 1970s craze, so the definition reaches the answer through the fad rather than through botany.

  • Fig, pear → FIGPEAR both fruits go into the mix whole, seven letters
  • turn → TUR TURN with its last letter clipped off by 'almost'
  • almost — Almost a word is that word one letter short, so the final letter never makes it into the mix.
  • out — Out of order is the sense here: what stands before it is not in the arrangement you need.

Three fruits in one clue and only two of them are letters; the third is what you are looking for, which is the whole misdirection.

7 down CLOCHE

Chestnut one replaced with ring for crop protection (6)

Definition: crop protection · substitution

A cloche is the bell-shaped glass or plastic cover a gardener sets over young plants, so 'crop protection' describes it by the job it does.

  • Chestnut → CLICHE an old chestnut is a cliche, a joke told once too often
  • one → I one is I in Roman numerals, and it is the letter that leaves
  • ring → O a ring is drawn as the letter O, and it moves in
  • replaced with — It names a swap rather than a loss: one letter steps out and whatever comes after takes its seat.

Chestnut looks horticultural in a clue about crops, and is not: the tired old joke is the sense the setter wants.

13 down ESCAPOLOGY

Drugs defence involving college that’s associated with Houdini (10)

Definition: that’s associated with Houdini · charade

Escapology is the stage art of getting out of chains and locked trunks, and Houdini is its most famous practitioner, so the art is named by its exemplar.

  • Drugs → ES an E is an ecstasy tablet, so drugs in the plural are Es
  • college → C c. is the standard abbreviation for college
  • defence → APOLOGY an apology is a reasoned defence of a position, the sense in Newman's Apologia
  • involving — To involve something is to draw it in, so the letter it names joins the assembly rather than sitting outside it.

The step that unlocks it is reading 'defence' as the older sense of apology, a justification rather than a regret.

15 down RUN DRY

Stand for election (ironic) and get through all the booze (3,3)

Definition: get through all the booze · charade

To run dry is for a supply to give out, so drinking a bar's stock to the last drop is the phrase happening in front of you.

  • Stand for election → RUN in American usage a candidate runs for office where a Briton stands
  • (ironic) → DRY dry humour is the ironic, deadpan sort

The answer is split between two lights, 15 and 17, which the (3,3) enumeration announces; 'stand' and 'run' are the British and American halves of one idea.

19 down SKITTLES

Game of kiss and tell, perhaps, with time for Latin (8)

Definition: Game · anagram + substitution

Skittles is the pub game of bowling a ball at nine wooden pins, the ancestor of ten-pin bowling, so 'Game' defines it by category.

  • kiss and tell → KISSTELL both words hand over their letters, eight in all
  • Latin → L L is the standard abbreviation for Latin, and it is the letter that leaves
  • time → T t is the scientist's symbol for time, and it takes the L's place
  • perhaps — It concedes that the words before it are only an approximate version of what you want, so their letters must be rearranged.
  • for — 'X for Y' is the swap formula: what stands in front of it takes over the place of what stands behind.

A tabloid story and a Latin lesson make a plausible sentence, and neither survives the solve: the swap happens first, then the shuffle.

20 down WHITMAN

Poet’s wife and enforcer (7)

Definition: Poet · charade

Walt Whitman wrote Leaves of Grass, so 'Poet' defines by example: the class of person, with the surname alone standing for the man.

  • wife → W w. is the standard abbreviation for wife in a genealogy
  • enforcer → HITMAN a gang's enforcer is its hitman

The surface hands the poet a wife and a heavy; the apostrophe-s is joinery again, and the answer is a surname, which is how setters clue writers.

22 down RINGER

Twin drying aid, lidless (6)

Definition: Twin · deletion

A ringer is a person or thing indistinguishable from another, the 'dead ringer' sense, so 'Twin' means look-alike rather than sibling.

  • drying aid → WRINGER a wringer squeezes the water out of washing before it dries
  • lidless → W the W at the top of WRINGER is the lid that comes off
  • lidless — A lid is what sits on top, and in a down entry the top is where the word starts, so its first letter has gone.

A dead ringer is an exact double, a sense that survives almost nowhere else; the surface offers a twin tub instead.

23 down PIRACY

Devout, bawdy and buccaneering (6)

Definition: buccaneering · charade

Buccaneering is robbery on the high seas and piracy is the noun for that trade, so the definition names the crime, not the criminal.

  • Devout → PI pi is short for pious, the crossword's word for devout
  • bawdy → RACY racy means risque, close to the bone

The two halves are opposites, piety and smut, which is the joke; 'and' does nothing but hold them together.

25 down BUTT

Ram’s a laughing-stock (4)

Definition: Ram · double definition

One verb for the head-first shove a ram gives, one noun for the target of mockery: the same word for the blow and the person taking it.

  • Ram → BUTT this is the verb for the head-first shove a ram delivers
  • laughing-stock → BUTT the target of everyone's mockery, the one a joke is aimed at, is described this way

Four words and no wordplay at all: the apostrophe-s poses as a possessive so the clue reads as a sentence about a sheep.

26 down GLIB

Easy: massive return with little initially invested (4)

Definition: Easy · container + reversal + first letter

A glib answer is an easy one, fluent and unthinking, so 'Easy' catches the sense of too-ready smoothness rather than lack of difficulty.

  • massive → BIG massive = big, and 'return' turns those three letters round
  • little → L the initial letter of little
  • return — To return is to go back the way you came, so the word before it is read in the opposite direction.
  • initially — It asks only for the letter a word starts with, the initial standing in for the whole.
  • invested — Money invested is money put inside something, and the letter goes the same way.

'Easy' is the trap: the answer means facile rather than effortless, the smoothness of a reply that came too readily to be thought about.
