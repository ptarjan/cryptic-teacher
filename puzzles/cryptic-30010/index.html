1 across POSITIVE SIGNS

Sure indications, two of which are apparent in this puzzle (8,5)

Definition: Sure indications, two of which are apparent in this puzzle · cryptic definition

A plus sign is a positive sign, and 'sure indications' is the everyday phrase for omens you can rely on — both senses live in the same two words.

  • Sure indications → read straight, this is the doctor's or the forecaster's phrase for evidence that things are going the right way
  • two of which are apparent in this puzzle → the setter means it literally: the black squares draw two plus shapes, and the word PLUS is written inside two of the answers as well

Someone pointing out that the encouraging omens are there for anyone who looks.

The black squares of this grid make two plus shapes, and PLUS is spelled out inside two answers, so the clue is counting the setter's own handiwork.

10 across OPPOSED

Against being briefly affirmative in written opinion (7)

Definition: Against · container

To be OPPOSED to something is to be against it — the same relation, put as a participle where the clue offers a preposition.

  • written opinion → OPED an op-ed is the newspaper page opposite the editorial, where signed opinion pieces run
  • briefly affirmative → POS affirmative is POSITIVE, and 'briefly' docks it to its first three letters
  • in — 'In' is the instruction to bury one piece inside another instead of setting it alongside.

Somebody coming out against a cautiously upbeat newspaper column.

'Against being briefly affirmative' reads as one continuous grumble about half-hearted praise, and the definition is the first word by itself.

11 across CAPSULE

Plus ça change with European medicine (7)

Definition: medicine · anagram

A CAPSULE is the small soluble case a dose is packed into, so 'medicine' defines it by what it carries rather than what it is.

  • Plus ça → PLUSCA anagram fodder, six letters; the cedilla is not a letter and drops away
  • European → E E is the standing abbreviation for European
  • change → anagram indicator
  • change — 'Change' belongs to the French tag in the surface, and taken as an instruction it says these letters will not stay as they are.

The French shrug that the more things change, the more they stay the same.

Plus ça change is the opening of the French tag about nothing really changing, and the setter has used a quotation as fodder with its own verb as the instruction.

12 across GLEAM

Shine with half-century in fluctuating game (5)

Definition: Shine · anagram

A GLEAM is a brief shine of light, and as a verb to gleam is simply to shine.

  • half-century → L a century is C, a hundred in Roman numerals, and half of it is fifty — L; in cricket a half-century is a batter's fifty runs
  • game → GAME anagram fodder, four letters
  • fluctuating → anagram indicator
  • fluctuating — Something fluctuating will not hold still, and letters that will not hold still do not keep their order.

A batter's fifty lighting up a match that keeps swinging one way then the other.

'Shine with half-century' reads as one cricketing phrase, a fifty catching the light, and the definition is the first word standing on its own.

13 across SURPLUSES

Positive about asset’s small excesses (9)

Definition: excesses · container + charade

A SURPLUS is what is left when there is more than was needed, so 'excesses' names the plural of exactly that.

  • Positive → SURE positive in the sense of certain — 'I'm positive' — gives SURE
  • asset’s → PLUS an asset is a plus, something on the credit side; the possessive belongs to the surface
  • small → S S is the standing abbreviation for small, as on a clothing label
  • about — 'About' puts one word round the outside of another, the way arms go about a waist.

An accountant reporting that a modest asset has left money over.

'Positive' is the theme word doing double duty: in the surface it sits with the assets, in the wordplay it is nothing but a synonym for sure.

14 across EASEL

Soften line, something that artist can work on (5)

Definition: something that artist can work on · charade

An EASEL is the frame a painter stands a canvas on, so it is literally the thing an artist works at.

  • Soften → EASE to ease a rule or a pain is to soften it
  • line → L L is the standing abbreviation for line

An instruction to a painter to soften that line.

'Soften line' reads as advice to a painter, which is the misdirection: they are two separate pieces, and the artist belongs only to the definition.

16 across SAINTLIKE

Setter’s beginning – isn’t fancy, or as good as Paul (or Brendan)? (9)

Definition: as good as Paul (or Brendan)? · charade + first letter

SAINTLIKE means having the goodness of a saint, and St Paul and St Brendan are named as examples rather than as the quality itself.

  • Setter’s → S the opening letter of the word Setter
  • isn’t → AINT ain't is the colloquial form of isn't, and its apostrophe gets no square in the grid
  • fancy → LIKE to fancy something is to like it
  • beginning — 'Beginning' asks for the opening letter alone of the word standing in front of it.

One Guardian setter wondering aloud whether he is as virtuous as another.

Paul and Brendan are both Guardian setters and both saints, which is the joke, and 'fancy' looks like an anagram indicator when it is a straight synonym.

18 across TURNTABLE

Revolutionary device for playing truant out of order – nearly ran (9)

Definition: Revolutionary device · anagram + deletion

A TURNTABLE is the platter that spins a record, so 'Revolutionary device' describes it by the single thing it does: turn.

  • truant → TRUANT anagram fodder, six letters
  • ran → BLE a colour that ran BLED, and stopping short of the end leaves three of those letters
  • out of order → anagram indicator
  • out of order — Something out of order is not in the sequence it should be, and that is what becomes of the letters beside it.
  • nearly — 'Nearly' means you stop just short, so the last letter of the word it governs never arrives.

A rebel's gadget for bunking off school, broken and only just going.

'Revolutionary' wants to be a rebel and is literal instead — a device that revolves — while 'ran' is dye running in the wash, which is bleeding.

19 across CROSS

Go over something obvious left (5)

Definition: Go over · double definition

CROSS is the verb for going over something and the noun for the mark left by a person who cannot sign their name — visible at a glance.

  • Go over → to pass from one side of something to the other
  • something obvious left → the mark somebody puts on a page in place of a signature — plain to see, and still there on the paper afterwards

An instruction to check the obvious thing somebody has left behind.

The theme is what makes the second sense fair: this shape is a plus sign, and two of them are drawn in the black squares of the grid.

20 across MINUSCULE

Indication to remove awful clue, not easy to see (9)

Definition: not easy to see · charade + anagram

MINUSCULE means very small indeed, and something that small is exactly what is not easy to see.

  • Indication to remove → MINUS the sign written in front of a number to say take it away
  • clue → CLUE anagram fodder, four letters
  • awful → anagram indicator
  • awful — Calling the clue awful says its letters are in a state they should not be in, and they get re-laid.

An editor demanding that a dreadful, murky clue be cut.

The setter is describing his own trade — an awful clue nobody can see the point of — and the opening three words name the minus sign this puzzle keeps returning to.

23 across PEACE

Calm tips from positive pro (5)

Definition: Calm · charade + outer letters

PEACE is calm in the sense of quiet and freedom from disturbance — the state itself, not a manner of behaving.

  • positive → PE the first and last letters of the word positive
  • pro → ACE a pro is an expert, and an expert is an ace
  • tips from — Tips are the two extreme ends of a thing, so the outermost letters of the word are the ones taken.

Advice on staying calm handed down by an upbeat professional.

'Positive pro' reads as one description of a cheerful expert, and the two words take entirely different treatments: one is trimmed to its ends, the other swapped for a synonym.

24 across TERMINI

Final points, one of which is negative in its conclusion (7)

Definition: Final points · double definition

TERMINI are end points: of a railway line, or of an electrical device, where the pair are marked positive and negative.

  • Final points → the ends of a line, where a railway or a bus route stops
  • one of which is negative in its conclusion → the two connections on a battery, one marked + and one −, and the singular form of the word ends letter for letter in MINUS

A closing summary whose last item turns out to be a negative one.

A battery's two ends are its terminals, one of them the minus, and the singular of this word finishes on those very letters — Brendan pointing at his theme inside his own answer.

25 across TOURIST

Visitor is interrupting perturbed tutor (7)

Definition: Visitor · anagram + container

A TOURIST is somebody visiting a place for pleasure, so 'Visitor' is the plain synonym with the holiday sense left implied.

  • tutor → TUTOR anagram fodder, five letters
  • is → IS two letters that break into the middle of the shuffled word
  • perturbed → anagram indicator
  • interrupting — To interrupt is to break into something already under way, so this piece goes inside the other rather than beside it.
  • perturbed — Somebody perturbed has been unsettled, and letters that have been unsettled do not stay in their old order.

A flustered tutor whose lesson keeps being broken into.

'Is' looks like a link word joining a definition to its wordplay, and that is the whole disguise: here it is two letters of the answer.

26 across PHOTOGRAPHING

Shooting with negative, then positive, results (13)

Definition: Shooting with negative, then positive, results · cryptic definition

Shooting is the photographer's own word for taking pictures, and film yields a negative that is then printed as a positive — the whole clue describes the process.

  • Shooting → the surface hears gunfire, or at best a film crew on location
  • with negative, then positive, results → on film the exposure develops into a negative first, and the print made from that is called a positive — the order the setter is describing

A shoot that goes badly at first and then comes good.

Shooting is what a photographer calls it too, and the negative-then-positive sequence is the darkroom's rather than a run of luck.

2 down OPPRESSOR

Work on academic cancelling of tyrant (9)

Definition: tyrant · charade + deletion

An OPPRESSOR is one who holds a people down by force, which is what a tyrant does to those he rules.

  • Work → OP OP is the short form of opus, a work — the abbreviation printed before a composer's numbers
  • academic cancelling of → PRESSOR the academic is a PROFESSOR, and the O and the F that 'of' names are struck out of it
  • cancelling — To cancel is to strike out, so whatever is named next gets struck from the word standing before it.

An academic writing up the way a tyrant was got rid of.

'Work on academic' reads as one phrase, pressure applied to a scholar, when 'work' is standing alone as OP — the opus number printed on a piece of music.

3 down IN SUM

By way of review, minus changes (2,3)

Definition: By way of review · anagram

IN SUM introduces a summing-up — 'in sum, we were wrong' — which is the phrase a reviewer uses to gather everything into one verdict.

  • minus → MINUS anagram fodder, five letters
  • changes → anagram indicator
  • changes — A thing that changes does not stay as it was, so the five letters beside it come out in a different order.

An accountant summing up now that the deductions have shifted.

The theme word is doing the hiding: 'minus' looks like an accountant's term in a sentence about figures, and it is five letters waiting to be shuffled.

4 down INDUS

River trade subtracting tax at the end (5)

Definition: River · deletion

The INDUS is the great river of Pakistan that gave India its name, so 'River' defines it by the class it belongs to.

  • trade → INDUSTRY trade in the wide sense of commerce and manufacture, the long word to be trimmed
  • tax → TRY to tax someone is to try them, the way a tiresome child tries your patience
  • subtracting — Subtracting is taking away, so what it names comes off the word rather than being added to it.
  • at the end — 'At the end' says where the doomed letters were sitting: the last three, not the first.

Riverside commerce with the duty taken off at the end of the year.

'River trade' reads as one thing, cargo moving on the water, and only 'River' is the definition. The rest turns on tax meaning try — trying behaviour taxes your patience.

5 down EXCURSIVE

In kinky sex and vice you are audibly digressing (9)

Definition: digressing · anagram + homophone

EXCURSIVE describes talk that keeps making excursions away from its subject, which is what a digressing speaker does.

  • sex and vice → SEXVICE anagram fodder, seven letters; the 'and' only joins them
  • you are → UR said aloud, those two words are simply the names of two letters, and the pair joins the shuffle
  • kinky → anagram indicator
  • kinky — A kink is a twist, and letters described as twisted are letters bent out of the order they arrived in.
  • audibly — 'Audibly' asks you to hear the words rather than read them, so what goes in the grid is what they sound like.

Telling somebody that their talk of kinky sex and vice is wandering off the subject.

U and R come from hearing 'you are' rather than reading it, which is worth keeping: those two letters are otherwise almost impossible to clue.

6 down IMPOLITIC

Mostly rude, mostly unfriendly, totally foolish (9)

Definition: foolish · charade + deletion

IMPOLITIC means ill-judged — the foolishness of a move that will cost you, not stupidity of mind.

  • rude → IMPOLIT rude is IMPOLITE, and most of that is everything but the E
  • unfriendly → IC unfriendly is ICY, and most of ICY is its first two letters
  • totally → surface only, and the setter's joke as well: two mostlys laid end to end come to a totally
  • Mostly — 'Mostly' means all but a little, and the little left behind is the final letter of the word it governs.
  • mostly — The second one does the same job on the next word, docking its last letter before the two pieces are laid end to end.

A verdict on somebody who is rude, cold and stupid with it.

'Foolish' is doing all the defining on its own, and the word in front of it belongs to the insult in the surface. IMPOLITIC is the political sort of foolish: ill-judged rather than dim.

7 down NOUNS

Parts of speech using negative pieces from Sun (5)

Definition: Parts of speech · charade + anagram

NOUNS belong to the set of word classes grammarians call the parts of speech, so the definition names them by their category, in the plural.

  • negative → NO the plainest negative in the language, and the one crosswords reach for first
  • from Sun → SUN anagram fodder, three letters
  • pieces — Something in pieces has been broken apart, and the bits never go back in the order they came out.

Grammar assembled out of the gloomy fragments printed in a tabloid.

'Negative pieces' looks like one phrase, dispiriting articles in a newspaper, and it is two separate instructions — while the Sun here is only three letters long.

8 down ROUGH ESTIMATE

More or less inaccurate guess upset this team in red (5,8)

Definition: More or less inaccurate guess · anagram

A ROUGH ESTIMATE is a figure arrived at approximately, so 'more or less inaccurate' is fair — near enough, and knowingly not exact.

  • this team → THISTEAM anagram fodder, eight letters
  • in red → ROUGE red is rouge — the French word, familiar in English as the name of the red make-up
  • upset → anagram indicator
  • upset — To upset something is to tip it over and spill it, and letters spilled do not land in the order they were stacked.

A wild guess at how badly the team in red were beaten.

'This team in red' looks like a football side you are meant to identify, Liverpool or Arsenal, and nothing needs naming: red is simply rouge.

9 down REASSESSMENTS

Remarks that may require revisions, up or down (13)

Definition: Remarks · double definition

Marks are assessments, so re-marking a script is doing them over; and a figure revised up or down has been judged afresh.

  • Remarks → read it as re-marks: an examiner going through the same pile of papers a second time
  • revisions, up or down → a forecast or a valuation revised in either direction

Comments on a report that might have to be written again.

The hyphen you cannot see is the joke: 'Remarks' is re-marks, and marks are assessments, so marking again is doing them again.

15 down LATE SHIFT

Some overnight for this and others turning up quickly go (4,5)

Definition: Some overnight · charade + reversal

A LATE SHIFT is the working stretch that runs on into the night, so 'Some overnight' picks out the part of the night that gets worked.

  • and others → LATE 'and others' after a name in a list is et al, and those four letters are then read the other way
  • quickly go → SHIFT to shift is to move fast — 'shift it!' shouted at somebody dawdling
  • turning up — In a down entry 'up' points back towards the start, so the letters just named are written in from the bottom.

Staff who come in overnight for this job and others, then leave in a hurry.

Et al is the Latin tag that follows a name in a reference list, standing for 'and others', and here it is four letters to be read upwards.

16 down SUBSUMING

Adding fix minus bugs (9)

Definition: Adding · anagram

SUBSUMING is taking something in under a wider heading — adding it to a category rather than merely piling it on top.

  • minus bugs → MINUSBUGS anagram fodder, nine letters
  • fix → anagram indicator
  • fix — To fix something is to put it right by rearranging its parts, and that is what happens to the nine letters after it.

A software patch that gets rid of the bugs.

'Fix minus bugs' reads as a patch with the faults taken out, so 'minus' looks like an instruction to delete something when both words are plain fodder.

17 down ISOLATION

One sequence of notes being performed in which person’s unaccompanied (9)

Definition: in which person’s unaccompanied · charade

ISOLATION is the state of being cut off from other people, which is precisely where a person with nobody alongside them is.

  • One → I one is I, the Roman numeral
  • sequence of notes → SOLATI so, la and ti are three consecutive notes of the sol-fa scale, taken in that order
  • being performed → ON a show being performed is on

A single passage of music played by somebody with no accompanist.

'One sequence of notes' reads as a single tune, which is the misdirection: 'One' stands alone, and the notes are sol-fa syllables rather than music.

21 down NORTH

Somewhat unorthodox position of negative pole (5)

Definition: position of negative pole · hidden word

NORTH is a compass position, and the earth's pole up there attracts a magnet's north end, so it behaves as a south — the negative one.

  • unorthodox → NORTH five letters in a row, starting one letter in and stopping four short of the end
  • Somewhat — 'Somewhat' means to some extent, so what is wanted is part of the word beside it — a run of letters inside, not the whole thing.

An unconventional view of where a magnet's minus end sits.

'Unorthodox' looks like an anagram indicator and is the hiding place instead, with the short word in front of it doing all the instructing.

22 down EXTRA

Positive contribution, taken out of context randomly (5)

Definition: Positive contribution · hidden word

An EXTRA is something added beyond what was owed, a bonus — the plus side of a contribution rather than the part that was due.

  • context randomly → EXTRA five letters in a row, running from the middle of the first word over the gap into the second
  • taken out of — 'Taken out of' says the letters are lifted straight from what follows rather than built up, so you read across the words for them.

A helpful remark quoted out of context and at random.

'Randomly' looks like an anagram indicator and is carrier text holding two of the letters, while 'taken out of context' is the setter describing his own extraction.

23 down PLUSH

Luxurious and hot (5)

Definition: Luxurious · charade

PLUSH means richly comfortable — a plush hotel — from the deep-piled velvet fabric of that name.

  • and → PLUS 'and' and 'plus' do the same job: joining one thing to another
  • hot → H H is the standing abbreviation for hot, the letter on the tap

A hotel room described as luxurious and warm.

A three-word clue with no indicator at all is working entirely by substitution, and a whole four-letter chunk coming from 'and' is what makes it hard to see.
