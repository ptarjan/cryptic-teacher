Across

1 across BEAR PIT

Scene of confusion, hearing organ accompanied by piano in piece (4,3)

Definition: Scene of confusion · charade + container

  • hearing organ → EAR the ear is the organ of hearing
  • piano → P p = piano, the musical marking for soft
  • piece → BIT EAR and P sit inside it

Organ, piano, piece — the surface is all music, and every one of those words is doing something else underneath. The organ here is the one you hear with, not the one in the church loft.

5 across TURN THE OTHER CHEEK

He repeatedly breaks into truck to rent out – don’t retaliate (4,3,5,5)

Definition: don’t retaliate · anagram

  • He repeatedly → HEHEHE HE three times over
  • truck to rent → TRUCKTORENT the rest of the fodder, 11 letters
  • breaks into → anagram indicator — the HEs break into the rest
  • out → second anagram indicator

A burglary report on the surface, the Sermon on the Mount underneath. 'He repeatedly' is the device worth remembering: a word plus 'repeatedly' means write it out more than once.

9 across CHINO

Fabric, only some of which I notice (5)

Definition: Fabric · hidden word

  • which I notice → CHINO hidden: whi-CH I NO-tice

Hidden words are easiest to miss when the surface is this flat. Nothing in 'only some of which I notice' asks for attention, which is exactly how the fabric slips past you.

10 across ELEMENTAL

Basic conclusions of article reveal state of mind (9)

Definition: Basic · charade + last letters

  • article reveal state → ELE their 'conclusions' (last letters): articlE / reveaL / statE
  • of mind → MENTAL mental = of the mind

'Conclusions of' reaches across three words, not one. Stopping at the first word after the indicator is the commonest reason a last-letters clue refuses to come out.

12 across TRAP

Speaker’s role reversal? (4)

Definition: Speaker’s · reversal

  • role → TRAP a role is a PART; reversed it becomes TRAP
  • reversal → reversal indicator

'Role reversal' is a phrase in its own right, which is what hides the instruction inside it. And the definition is a single word: your trap is your mouth, as in shut your trap.

14 across CHIPPENDALE

Damage finish installed by inferior furniture designer (11)

Definition: furniture designer · charade + container

  • Damage → CHIP to damage the edge of something is to chip it
  • finish → END goes inside PALE
  • inferior → PALE as in a pale imitation

The surface is a botched bit of home improvement, and it holds together because damage, finish and installed all belong to that world before they belong to the wordplay.

18 across RE-EMERGENCE

Second Coming? It’s concerning, church embracing person writing in odd genre (2-9)

Definition: Second Coming? · charade + container + anagram

  • concerning → RE re = concerning
  • church → CE Church of England
  • person writing → ME the setter himself
  • genre → GENRE made 'odd' — anagrammed — around ME and CE

'Second Coming?' is the wry part, question mark and all — a religious phrase stretched over an everyday word. The build is unusually layered: an anagram that has to be assembled around two inserted pieces rather than on its own.

21 across SKIN

Coverage for everyone in small family (4)

Definition: Coverage for everyone · charade

  • small → S s = small, as on clothing labels
  • family → KIN your kin are your family

'Coverage for everyone' is fair in a pleasing way — everybody comes wrapped in it — while the surface sounds like a line from an insurance advertisement.

22 across IN-YOUR-FACE

A fury I once fomented, impossible to ignore (2-4-4)

Definition: impossible to ignore · anagram

  • A fury I once → AFURYIONCE anagram fodder, 10 letters
  • fomented → anagram indicator

The fury the surface describes is precisely the mood of the answer, so the fodder never looks like fodder. It looks like a sentence that means what it says.

25 across ON THE NOSE

Option for better location for spectacles (2,3,4)

Definition: Option for better · double definition

  • Option for better → ONTHENOSE a better (bettor) can put money on the nose — a win-only bet
  • location for spectacles → ONTHENOSE where your glasses perch

Read 'better' as the gambler rather than the comparative. That one re-reading is the whole clue, and once it flips, the spectacles perching on your nose do the rest.

26 across BROWN

Toast former PM (5)

Definition: Toast · double definition

  • Toast → BROWN to toast bread is to brown it
  • former PM → BROWN Gordon Brown, prime minister 2007–2010

Two words, two definitions, no wordplay at all — and the surface pretends to be a toast raised at a dinner for the former prime minister.

27 across TEMPLES

Flat parts below domes in places of worship (7)

Definition: Flat parts below domes · double definition

  • Flat parts below domes → TEMPLES the flat sides of the head, below the 'dome' — the crown
  • places of worship → TEMPLES the buildings

The surface keeps both halves architectural, domes and places of worship together, so it takes a moment to notice that the first dome is the top of a head.

28 across EYELETS

Holes in European agreement about permit (7)

Definition: Holes · charade + container

  • European → E standard abbreviation
  • agreement → YES goes 'about' LET
  • permit → LET to let is to permit

The surface is a paragraph of Brussels news, which is why 'European' looks like the subject of a sentence rather than a single letter waiting at the front of the answer.

Down

1 down BECKON

Consider taking bishop instead of rook in attempt to draw (6)

Definition: attempt to draw · charade + deletion

  • Consider → ECKON RECKON = consider, minus its R
  • bishop → B chess notation — it takes the R's place
  • rook → R in chess notation, the letter removed

'Attempt to draw' looks like part of the chess game the rest of the clue is describing, and it is nothing of the sort. The definition hiding inside the surface's own subject is the clue's best moment.

2 down ALIGHT

Stop training or being coached, say, for a match? (6)

Definition: Stop training or being coached, say · charade

  • a → A
  • match? → LIGHT a match is what you offer when asked for a light

The definition is the whole joke: to alight is to step down from a train or a coach, so 'Stop training or being coached' is about transport, not exercise, with 'say' flagging both as examples.

3 down PROTRACTED

Long for pamphlet on education cut (10)

Definition: Long · charade + deletion

  • for → PRO pro = for, as in pros and cons
  • pamphlet → TRACT a tract is a short pamphlet
  • education → ED EDUCATION 'cut' down to its familiar short form

'Long for' reads as yearning, and the split falls right between the two words — 'Long' is the definition and 'for' has already started the wordplay. Seeing that seam is the hard part.

4 down TEETH

Effective power used by moderate ethically (5)

Definition: Effective power · hidden word

  • moderate ethically → TEETH hidden across the two words: modera-TE ETH-ically

'Effective power' is fair because of the idiom: a law with teeth is one that can actually bite. The surface, a moderate behaving ethically, reads like a leader column.

5 down THEREUPON

Article about state of India occurring immediately after that (9)

Definition: immediately after that · charade

  • Article → THE the definite article
  • about → RE re = about, concerning
  • state of India → UP UP = Uttar Pradesh, India's most populous state
  • occurring → ON what's occurring is on

Four small pieces laid end to end with no instruction word anywhere. The difficulty is all in 'state of India' — two letters naming Uttar Pradesh, not a remark about the country's condition.

6 down REEF

Part of sail that mariners steer clear of (4)

Definition: Part of sail · double definition

  • Part of sail → REEF the strip of sail that can be rolled in to shorten it
  • that mariners steer clear of → REEF the ridge of rock near the surface

Two nautical senses, and the surface keeps them in one world: same sailors, very different feelings about each.

7 down TUTORIAL

University closed in case this class has few students (8)

Definition: this class has few students · container

  • University → U standard abbreviation
  • closed → TO a door pulled to is closed
  • case → TRIAL a court case — it wraps around U and TO

The definition is the entire back half of the clue, which is unusual and easy to miss. And 'closed' for TO — a door pulled to — is the kind of two-letter trick worth memorising.

8 down ELLIPSES

Orbits … more than once (8)

Definition: Orbits · double definition

  • Orbits → ELLIPSES planetary orbits are ellipses — Kepler's discovery
  • … more than once → ELLIPSES the '…' mark is an ellipsis; several are ellipses

The clue's own punctuation is the wordplay: those three dots are not the setter trailing off, they are an ellipsis printed there for you to read as a thing.

13 down UNWEARABLE

Wrong to assume it’s hard to accept when chess player yields to opponent (10)

Definition: Wrong to assume · charade + deletion

  • it’s hard to accept → UNBEARABLE the starting word — its first B will give way
  • chess player → B = Black, the player who drops out
  • opponent → W W = White, who takes Black's place

The definition turns on an older sense of assume — to assume a garment is to put it on, so what cannot be worn cannot be assumed. Without that, 'Wrong to assume' looks like more chess talk.

15 down INGENIOUS

Present information, evidence of problems with money originally gifted (9)

Definition: originally gifted · charade

  • Present → IN in = present, as in 'is she in?'
  • information → GEN gen = information, British slang
  • evidence of problems with money → IOUS a pile of IOUs — notes acknowledging debts

The definition repays a moment's thought: 'originally gifted' means gifted from the origin — born with genius in you, which is what the word's Latin root actually says.

16 down CROSS OUT

Aggrieved, having downed tools for strike (5,3)

Definition: strike · charade

  • Aggrieved → CROSS cross = annoyed, aggrieved
  • having downed tools → OUT workers who have downed tools are out — on strike

The industrial-action surface hides a stationery-drawer answer: 'strike' here means striking a word from a list, not walking off the job.

17 down SERIATIM

A nameless minister demolished, point by point (8)

Definition: point by point · anagram + deletion

  • A → A joins the fodder
  • minister → MIISTER MINISTER made 'nameless' — without N for name
  • demolished → anagram indicator

Two operations stacked on each other: 'nameless' takes the N out before 'demolished' shuffles what remains. Setters do this often, and the fodder never counts until you do the deletion first.

19 down PAROLE

A character under pressure, temporarily free (6)

Definition: temporarily free · charade

  • pressure → P p = pressure in physics; A ROLE sits under it in this down entry
  • A character → AROLE a character = A ROLE

'A character under pressure' is a phrase from a hundred book reviews, which is why it takes a while to hear 'under' as a position: A ROLE sits beneath P, this being a down clue.

20 down TENNIS

Succeeded, caught up in court action? (6)

Definition: court action? · charade + reversal

  • Succeeded → S s = succeeded, the genealogy abbreviation
  • caught → INNET caught = IN NET

'Court action?' is the pun, and the question mark is the setter's warning that he does not mean the legal kind. In a down clue, 'up' is what tells you to read the letters backwards.

23 down OXEYE

Love letter specifying location I announced for plant (5)

Definition: plant · charade + homophone

  • Love → O love = zero in tennis, written as O
  • letter specifying location → X X marks the spot
  • I → EYE 'announced', EYE sounds like I

Three different tricks inside five letters, and the surface — a love letter naming a rendezvous — holds them together so smoothly that not one of them announces itself.

24 down VEIL

Screen features as live broadcast (4)

Definition: Screen features · anagram

  • live → LIVE anagram fodder
  • broadcast → anagram indicator

'Live broadcast' reads as a single television phrase, which is the trap: 'broadcast' belongs to the wordplay and 'live' is the fodder. 'Screen features' then means hiding a face, not TV.
