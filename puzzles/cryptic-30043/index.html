Across

1 across BASS CLARINET

Bans recitals – where this is played? (4,8)

Definition: where this is played? · anagram

  • Bans recitals → BANSRECITALS anagram fodder, 12 letters
  • played → anagram indicator — the letters are 'played' with

The definition points back into the clue: 'where this is played?' asks for the instrument you would hear at those very recitals, so the fodder is also the scene of the answer.

9 across POLKA

Old President leading America a dance (5)

Definition: a dance · charade

  • Old President → POLK James K Polk, 11th US President
  • America → A A = America(n)

The surface borrows the idiom 'to lead someone a dance', so 'leading' looks like part of that phrase rather than an instruction about where to put the president.

10 across ASTRONAUT

Australian working with Australian in support for Nasa employee (9)

Definition: Nasa employee · charade + container

  • Australian → A the first Australian: A, as in A$
  • working → ON working = on, like a machine
  • Australian → A the second Australian, another A
  • support → STRUT a strut is a support; ON A goes inside it

Two Australians for two As is the sly part: the same word clued twice, landing in two different places, so you go hunting for something more elaborate than a repeated letter.

11 across SKID LID

Went down the Cresta carrying child’s protective headgear? (4,3)

Definition: protective headgear? · container

  • Went down the Cresta → SLID you SLID down the Cresta Run toboggan track
  • child → KID child = kid

Capitalised, Cresta looks like a place name in a holiday snapshot. The answer is biker slang, and it comes from the same idea — sliding when you would rather not.

12 across CHILEAN

South American or Greek character offering little of profit (7)

Definition: South American · charade

  • Greek character → CHI chi is the Greek letter χ
  • offering little of profit → LEAN lean years offer little profit

'Offering little of profit' looks like an instruction to shorten a word, and it is nothing of the kind: it is a plain definition of LEAN. That false signal is the whole difficulty.

13 across SASH WINDOW

Source of light remains current in plant (4,6)

Definition: Source of light · charade + container

  • remains → ASH remains after a fire = ash
  • current → WIND a wind is a current of air
  • plant → SOW to plant seed is to sow; ASH WIND sits inside it

The surface reads like a report from a factory — remains current in plant — and 'Source of light' for a window is the kind of sideways definition worth getting used to.

15 across RIPE

Ready to complain after leader’s ousted (4)

Definition: Ready · deletion

  • to complain → GRIPE gripe = complain; its leader G is then ousted

The surface is a party grumbling after a coup, and 'leader' does the same job in both readings: the head of a party, and the head of a word.

18 across TRAY

Go to bring in a salver (4)

Definition: salver · container

  • Go → TRY a go = a try
  • a → A the A from the clue, brought inside

Read as an order to a servant it is one smooth sentence, and that hides the fact that 'Go' is a noun here — a go, a try — rather than the verb it looks like.

19 across COME-HITHER

Alluring? Happen to have success with leading man (not love) (4-6)

Definition: Alluring? · charade + deletion

  • Happen → COME to come to pass = to happen
  • success → HIT a success = a hit
  • leading man → HER HERO, the leading man, minus O — 'love' in tennis

The pieces divide as COME-HIT-HER, which is not how the answer reads at all. And 'love' for O comes from tennis, one of the handful of ways a setter can clue nothing.

22 across SILVERY

A lot of mud, intensely gleaming (7)

Definition: gleaming · charade + deletion

  • mud → SIL 'A lot of' SILT — all but its last letter
  • intensely → VERY intensely = very

'A lot of' is the indicator to learn here: most of a word, not all of it. The surface, a muddy field described with feeling, gives nothing away about gleaming.

24 across UNCORKS

No longer suppresses scorn – UK’s struggling (7)

Definition: No longer suppresses · anagram

  • scorn → SCORN anagram fodder
  • UK → UK more fodder, mixed in with the rest
  • struggling → anagram indicator

'No longer suppresses' looks too loose to be a definition until you think of uncorking your feelings rather than a bottle. Then it is exact.

25 across ATROPHIED

Moved fast after active harbour regressed, becoming degenerate (9)

Definition: becoming degenerate · charade + reversal

  • active → A a = active
  • harbour → TROP PORT ('harbour') 'regressed' — written backwards
  • Moved fast → HIED hied = hurried, placed 'after' the rest

The surface is a port town in decline, which is the same story the answer tells. 'Regressed' is the reversal word: in an across clue it means backwards, right to left.

26 across NATAL

Describing birth name in any way? Not entirely (5)

Definition: Describing birth · charade + deletion

  • name → N n = name
  • in any way → ATAL AT ALL = in any way; 'Not entirely' trims the final L

It reads like a question on a form, and 'Not entirely' is the giveaway — a phrase that merely qualifies the sentence on the surface, but underneath tells you to drop a final letter.

27 across OCKHAM'S RAZOR

Wild shamrock Portuguese islands twice cut down, reducing unnecessary quantities (7,5)

Definition: reducing unnecessary quantities · anagram + deletion

  • shamrock → SHAMROCK anagram fodder, 8 letters
  • Portuguese islands → AZOR the AZORES, 'twice cut down' — losing their last two letters
  • Wild → anagram indicator

The definition is the principle stated properly: entities are not to be multiplied beyond necessity. The clue then enacts it, cutting the Azores down before shaving the whole thing into shape.

Down

1 down BALTIC SEA

Basalt cracked with ice in chilly water (6,3)

Definition: chilly water · anagram

  • Basalt → BASALT anagram fodder
  • ice → ICE more fodder, mixed in with the rest
  • cracked → anagram indicator

The surface reads as a geology field note, and the trap is that it already sounds like chilly water — when a clue's plain meaning matches the answer's, you stop looking for letters to move.

2 down SHALLOWS

Mum permits paddling here? (8)

Definition: Mum permits paddling here? · charade + &lit

  • Mum → SH keeping mum = staying quiet: sh!
  • permits → ALLOWS permits = allows

Every word works twice over: read straight it is a mother at the beach, read as wordplay it is silence plus permission, and the shallows really are where paddling is allowed.

3 down CHARD

Vegetable, cold, resistant to the bite (5)

Definition: Vegetable · charade

  • cold → C c = cold, as on a tap
  • resistant to the bite → HARD something hard resists biting

'Vegetable, cold, resistant to the bite' reads like a gardener's note on a hardy crop, which is exactly what stops you seeing 'cold' as its one-letter abbreviation.

4 down ARTICHOKE

Vegetable skill – I fail at a crucial moment (9)

Definition: Vegetable · charade

  • skill → ART skill = art
  • I → I I stands for itself
  • fail at a crucial moment → CHOKE to choke = to fail under pressure

The happy accident is that an artichoke really does have a choke — the hairy heart you cut away — so the last piece of the charade is also part of the vegetable.

5 down ICONIC

Admired pair about to study carbon (6)

Definition: Admired · container + charade

  • pair → II a pair of ones — two Is
  • to study → CON con is an old word for to study
  • carbon → C C is the chemical symbol for carbon

The surface is two admirers signing up for chemistry together; the trap is 'pair', which is not people at all but simply the letter I written twice.

6 down ERASE

Delete Times article finally (5)

Definition: Delete · charade + last letter

  • Times → ERAS times = eras of history, not the newspaper
  • article finally → E the last letter of articlE

The capital T on 'Times' points hard at the newspaper, and the wordplay wants nothing of the sort — just plain eras, stretches of history.

7 down OPUSES

Applications supporting section of stage work (6)

Definition: work · charade

  • section of stage → OP OP ('opposite prompt') is the side of a theatre stage opposite the prompter
  • Applications → USES applications = uses

In a down clue 'supporting' means sitting underneath rather than coming after. Across and down entries read position words differently, and this one only works pointing down. OP is theatre jargon for the side of the stage opposite the prompter.

8 down AT ONCE

Immediately agreeing to bring in Conservative (2,4)

Definition: Immediately · container

  • agreeing → ATONE to be at one with someone is to agree
  • Conservative → C C = Conservative, the party label

The surface is coalition horse-trading, which is why 'agreeing' reads as politics rather than as the phrase AT ONE waiting to be prised open.

14 down NEODYMIUM

Metal? You mined rocks, gaining mass (9)

Definition: Metal · anagram

  • You mined → YOUMINED anagram fodder, 8 letters
  • mass → M m = mass in physics, 'gained' by the fodder
  • rocks → anagram indicator

'You mined rocks' is exactly how you would come by the metal, and that is what makes the fodder invisible: it describes the answer while spelling it.

16 down INHERITOR

I note that woman with sex-appeal and gold – recipient of legacy? (9)

Definition: recipient of legacy? · charade

  • I → I I stands for itself
  • note → N n = note
  • that woman → HER that woman = her
  • sex-appeal → IT it = sex appeal, as in 'she's got it'
  • gold → OR or = gold in heraldry

Five pieces in a row and not one instruction word — a pure charade. The leer at a woman in gold is there to keep you reading the clue as a sentence.

17 down PINCE-NEZ

They help one view fix over Church elevating Eastern philosophy (5-3)

Definition: They help one view · charade + reversal

  • fix → PIN to fix = to pin
  • Church → CE CE = Church of England
  • Eastern philosophy → ZEN ZEN, 'elevating' — reversed upwards in this down entry

The bonus sits in the answer's own French: nez is nose, and a pince-nez pinches it. Both 'over' and 'elevating' are positional words that only work because this is a down clue.

18 down TUSCAN

Parts swapped in Latin song from part of Italy (6)

Definition: from part of Italy · anagram

  • Latin song → CANTUS cantus is Latin for song, as in cantus firmus

Not the usual full jumble: 'Parts swapped' means two chunks changing places, CAN and TUS. Worth spotting when an anagram refuses to come out — sometimes only the halves move.

20 down RESULT

Concerned with Muslim leader rejecting an outcome (6)

Definition: outcome · charade + deletion

  • Concerned with → RE re = concerning, as in memo headers
  • Muslim leader → SULT a SULTAN who 'rejects' the AN

'Rejecting' sounds as though it should reverse something; here it simply throws letters away, and the news-bulletin surface keeps you thinking about the man rather than his title.

21 down TEMPEH

Beautiful spot to get hot Javanese food (6)

Definition: Javanese food · charade

  • Beautiful spot → TEMPE the Vale of Tempe in Greece, a classical byword for beauty
  • hot → H h = hot, as on a tap

The Vale of Tempe is worth filing away — setters reach for it whenever they want a beauty spot — and the holiday-brochure surface gives no hint that the answer is a soybean cake.

23 down LYRIC

Only rich seen around this theatre? (5)

Definition: this theatre? · hidden word

  • Only rich → LYRIC hidden inside onLY RICh

A hidden word whose cover story does the hiding: 'seen around' sounds like gossip about who frequents the place, and the place is the Lyric — Hammersmith, or the West End.

24 down UNDER

No place to steal from subordinate (5)

Definition: subordinate · deletion

  • to steal → PLUNDER to plunder = to steal
  • place → PL pl = place, as on street maps; 'No place' removes it

'No place' reads as moral disapproval — nowhere is fit to steal from — and that is what disguises it as an instruction to delete two letters.
