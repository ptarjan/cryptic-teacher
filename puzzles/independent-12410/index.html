Across

1 across BUSHRANGER

Former US President - R Reagan - mistakenly ignoring a backwoodsman (10)

Definition: backwoodsman · charade + deletion + anagram

  • Former US President → BUSH Two Presidents of that surname reached the White House, so the name is a straight lift.
  • a → A This is the letter that goes: R REAGAN is told to ignore an A.
  • R Reagan → RANGER What is left of R REAGAN is R, R, E, G, A, N, and those six letters resettle.

Two US Presidents in one clue and neither of them is the answer: one surname is a straight lift, the other is raw material. The dashes are punctuation for the surface only.

6 across BLOW

Beer initially on verge of running out? Calamity (4)

Definition: Calamity · charade + first letter

  • Beer → B Only the first letter of the word is wanted.
  • verge of running out → LOW Supplies on the verge of running out are running low.

The surface is a pub running dry and the definition is a misfortune, so the sense wanted is 'it came as a blow', not the punch.

9 across SCANTINESS

Lack of coverage from tin spikes on board ship (10)

Definition: Lack of coverage · charade + container

  • ship → SS SS is a steamship, and what goes aboard sits between the two letters.
  • tin → CAN A tin is a can.
  • spikes → TINES The tines of a fork are its spikes.

'Coverage' is clothing here rather than news or insurance, and that is what makes the definition so hard to see coming.

10 across BULL

Cow without tail? Nonsense (4)

Definition: Nonsense · deletion

  • Cow → BULLY To cow someone is to bully them — the verb, not the animal.
  • tail → Y A word's tail is its last letter, and that letter here is Y.

The farm animal is a decoy: 'cow' is a verb, so the clue is about intimidation and the question mark warns that the reading has shifted.

11 across SPECTACLE

Description of job, with story about cocaine - a remarkable thing (9)

Definition: a remarkable thing · charade + container

  • Description of job → SPEC A job spec is the description an employer writes.
  • story → TALE A story is a tale.
  • cocaine → C C is the standing abbreviation for cocaine.

'Spec' for a job description is modern office shorthand, and the lurid surface — a story about drugs — is exactly the sort of thing the definition covers.

16 across VARIATION

New take on music: bit of opera follows version, one in fashion (9)

Definition: New take on music · charade + container

  • version → V V is the abbreviation for version.
  • bit of opera → ARIA An aria is a self-contained number in an opera.
  • one → I I is the Roman numeral for one.
  • fashion → TON The ton, from the French, is fashionable society.

'The ton' for fashionable society is Regency vocabulary that now survives mostly in crosswords, and the colon makes the whole sentence read as a music review.

18 across RESIN

Green is erratic, partly reflecting amber? (5)

Definition: amber · hidden word + reversal

  • Green is erratic → NISER A run of five consecutive letters starts inside 'Green' and stops inside 'erratic'.

Green and amber set up a traffic light, which is the whole misdirection: the definition is the fossil sap and the letters are lying in the clue back to front.

19 across RIDER

Attacker offing one horseman (5)

Definition: horseman · deletion

  • Attacker → RAIDER An attacker who strikes and runs is a raider.
  • one → A 'One' here is the indefinite article A, and that is the letter removed.

Attacker and horseman both suit a battlefield surface, so the definition hides in plain sight and only the letter count separates them.

20 across TERABYTES

Failed battery starts to endanger substantial amounts of data (9)

Definition: amounts of data · anagram + first letters

  • battery → BATTERY The seven letters of the word are the raw material for the shuffle.
  • endanger substantial → ES The initial letters of the two words are E and S.

A dead battery and a unit of storage share nothing but their letters, and 'starts to endanger' reads as a warning rather than as an instruction.

21 across RUNABOUTS

Races including contest chasing American cars? (9)

Definition: cars · charade + container

  • Races → RUNS Someone who races runs, and the -s comes along with it.
  • American → A A is the standard abbreviation for American.
  • contest → BOUT A boxing contest is a bout.

The surface is motor sport and the definition is parked at the end, with the question mark conceding that not every car is one of these.

26 across WEIR

Dam that is put back in Western Region (4)

Definition: Dam · container + reversal

  • Western Region → WR WR was British Rail's Western Region, the network running west out of Paddington.
  • that is → IE 'That is' is i.e. written out, and it goes in the other way round.

The surface reads like a railway memo. British Rail's regions left crosswords a stock of two-letter abbreviations, and i.e. for 'that is' is another piece of standing kit.

27 across PINA COLADA

Fix a drink, getting US lawyer a cocktail (4,6)

Definition: a cocktail · charade

  • Fix → PIN To fix something in place is to pin it.
  • a → A The clue's own 'a' is taken as a single letter.
  • drink → COLA A cola is a drink.
  • US lawyer → DA The district attorney is the American prosecuting lawyer.

Four pieces laid end to end and the surface still reads as an order at the bar. DA comes straight from American courtroom drama and is a crossword regular.

28 across HILL

Elevated area cold? Skip the top (4)

Definition: Elevated area · deletion

  • cold → CHILL A cold in the air is a chill.
  • the top → C The top of that word is its first letter, C.

The question mark warns that 'cold' is a noun here, and the instruction is dressed as friendly advice about not bothering with the summit.

29 across ANIMADVERT

Complain train’s getting rackety? Prepared to limit volume internally (10)

Definition: Complain · anagram + container

  • train → TRAIN The five letters of the word go into the shuffle.
  • Prepared → MADE Something prepared has been made, and those four letters join the shuffle too.
  • volume → V V is the abbreviation for volume, as on a book spine.

Nine letters are shuffled and the tenth is dropped in afterwards. The answer is a formal, bookish verb: one animadverts ON something, publicly and critically.

1 down BUSH

Wild country? Public transport heading for home (4)

Definition: Wild country · charade + first letter

  • Public transport → BUS The public transport that runs to a route number is a bus.
  • home → H Only the first letter of the word is taken.

The question mark flags a definition that points rather than translates, and the surface is an ordinary commuter sentence about catching one home.

2 down SHAW

Playwright’s maxim about hearts (4)

Definition: Playwright · container

  • maxim → SAW A saw is an old proverb or maxim — 'many a true word', that sort of thing.
  • hearts → H H is the card-table abbreviation for hearts.

A maxim about hearts sounds like a line of dialogue, which is the point: the surface is theatrical and the wordplay is a card suit inside an old word for a proverb.

3 down ROTA

List eighteen letters in reverse order (4)

Definition: List · reversal

  • eighteen letters → ATOR A to R is a span of eighteen letters of the alphabet, so the clue writes it as A TO R.

Counting the alphabet is the whole trick, and 'letters' looks like part of the surface sentence rather than the instruction it is.

4 down NINES

Note Spanish girl’s standard of perfection? (5)

Definition: standard of perfection · charade

  • Note → N N is the crossword shorthand for a musical note.
  • Spanish girl → INES Inés is a common Spanish girl's name, wanted here in full.

An abbreviation plus a first name is one of the oldest charades going, and the question mark admits the definition is an idiom rather than a dictionary equivalent.

5 down EASTERNER

English more authoritarian about a person from the Orient (9)

Definition: person from the Orient · charade + container

  • English → E E is the standard abbreviation for English.
  • a → A The clue's own 'a' is taken at face value as one letter.
  • more authoritarian → STERNER Someone more authoritarian is sterner; the comparative form is what is wanted.

A sentence about strictness that is really about geography. 'More X' almost always asks for the -ER comparative spelled out, which is worth banking.

7 down LAUNCH SITE

A name involved with this clue? Cape Canaveral, perhaps (6,4)

Definition: Cape Canaveral, perhaps · anagram

  • A → A The opening word is one of the letters.
  • name → N N is the usual abbreviation for name.
  • this clue → THISCLUE The clue points at itself: its own two words supply the remaining eight letters.

Self-reference is the joke — the material really is the words of this clue — and the comma before 'perhaps' marks Cape Canaveral as one example rather than the thing itself.

8 down WILDERNESS

Playwright with point about river and wasteland (10)

Definition: wasteland · charade + container

  • Playwright → WILDE Oscar Wilde is the five-letter playwright setters reach for.
  • river → R R is the map abbreviation for river.
  • point → NESS A ness is a headland — the point of land running out into the sea.

Two playwrights turn up in this grid and this is the other one. NESS for a point is worth banking: setters use it whenever a clue goes near a coastline.

12 down THROBS

Beats first couple of thieves with sacks (6)

Definition: Beats · charade + first letters

  • thieves → TH Only the two letters at the front of the word are used.
  • sacks → ROBS To sack a city is to rob it, and the -s carries across.

Both halves of the surface are crime and neither is the definition: the sense wanted is the pulse, the thump of a heart or a headache.

13 down OVERGROWTH

Government dispute blocking blatant hospital extension beyond limits (10)

Definition: extension beyond limits · charade + container

  • blatant → OVERT Something blatant is overt — done in the open.
  • Government → G G stands for government, as in the American G-man.
  • dispute → ROW A row is a dispute.
  • hospital → H H is the signpost abbreviation for hospital.

'Blocking' reads as bureaucratic obstruction in the surface while working as an insertion instruction, which is the neatest thing in the clue.

14 down PRUDENTIAL

Puritan translation of Latin is circumspect (10)

Definition: circumspect · charade + anagram

  • Puritan → PRUDE A prude is the puritanical sort, easily shocked.
  • Latin → NTIAL The five letters of the language come out in a new order.

Only half the clue is shuffled — the opening five letters are a straight synonym — and 'is' is doing nothing but joining wordplay to definition.

15 down ZAGREB

South Africa’s composer turning up in Croatian city (6)

Definition: Croatian city · charade + reversal

  • South Africa → ZA ZA is South Africa's international vehicle and internet code.
  • composer → BERG Alban Berg, who wrote Wozzeck, supplies four letters that are then read upwards.

ZA is the code on South African number plates, and Berg belongs with Bax and Arne in the drawer of very short composers setters keep handy.

17 down INTRUSION

Interruption popular in tours? Wrong (9)

Definition: Interruption · charade + anagram

  • popular → IN Something popular is in — the fashion sense of the word.
  • in tours → TRUSION These seven letters, shuffled, supply everything after the opening pair.

The word 'in' does two jobs here, once as a synonym for popular and once as raw material, which is exactly where the letter count catches solvers out.

22 down SPASM

Succeeded in blocking annoying emails in abrupt movement (5)

Definition: abrupt movement · container

  • Succeeded → S S is the genealogist's abbreviation for succeeded.
  • annoying emails → SPAM Unwanted marketing email is spam.

Spam for junk mail is recent vocabulary, and the surface — a message stopped from getting through — hides the fact that a letter is being pushed inside.

23 down WOOD

Court decision offering only opening for club (4)

Definition: club · charade + first letter

  • Court → WOO To court someone is to woo them — the verb, not the tennis or the law.
  • decision → D Only the opening letter of the word is used.

A courtroom ruling is the picture, and neither key word means what it seems: the courting is romantic and the club belongs on a golf course.

24 down CAGE

Prison reporting not concluded (4)

Definition: Prison · deletion

  • reporting → COVERAGE News reporting is coverage.
  • concluded → OVER Something concluded is over, and those four letters are struck out of the longer word.

'Over' for concluded is the pivot the whole clue turns on, and the surface reads as a newsroom bulletin rather than as an instruction to delete.

25 down PART

Separate opportunity for actor (4)

Definition: Separate · double definition

  • Separate → the verb for two things drawing away from each other, as a crowd does to let someone through
  • opportunity for actor → the role in a play that an audition is held to hand out

Four words and no letters move. The trick is that the two halves are different parts of speech — a verb, then a noun — so the sentence reads as one continuous phrase.
