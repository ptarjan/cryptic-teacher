Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,150

Setter: Everyman · Published: Saturday 2 May 2026 · Difficulty: Tough (harder than 77% of the puzzles here) · Grid: 15×15

Across

1 across WOOD PIGEON

Club's language discussed in flyer (4,6)

Definition: flyer · charade + homophone

  • Club → WOOD a wood is the golf club with the big rounded head, once carved from persimmon
  • language → PIGEON a pidgin is the makeshift language two peoples invent when they share no tongue, and it is said exactly like the bird

'Club' wants to be a night out and is the golfer's kind. Two quite different spellings of one sound are what the second half turns on.

6 across ASIA

A lot of land in limits of atlases' Iberia (4)

Definition: A lot of land · outer letters

  • atlases' → AS the edges of the word, its first letter and its last
  • Iberia → IA the same treatment of the peninsula Spain and Portugal share

One instruction has to reach across two words here, and the geography in the surface — atlases, Iberia — is there to keep you looking for a country instead of at letters.

9 across GRECO-ROMAN

Moan, flailing with grocer, wrestling thus? (5-5)

Definition: wrestling thus · anagram

  • Moan → MOAN four letters lifted straight out of the clue, the comma after them the only hint they are fodder
  • grocer → GROCER six more, untouched, to be mixed in with the first four

A moaning grocer in a wrestling match is a good enough picture to stop you counting: ten letters in the clue, ten in the grid, and 'thus' pointing at a style rather than a sport.

10 across LIMP

In retreat, Starmer largely ailing and weak (4)

Definition: weak · charade + deletion + reversal

  • Starmer → PM Keir Starmer is the British prime minister, and PM is the abbreviation for that office
  • ailing → IL ailing is ill, with the last of its three letters left off

Politics is the misdirection: a prime minister in retreat over a policy is a headline, not wordplay. Build the two short pieces first, then read them the other way.

11 across DOUBLE-BOOKED

Incapable of satisfying demands - like the Bible, largely? (6-6)

Definition: Incapable of satisfying demands · double definition

  • Incapable of satisfying demands → the diary sense: two people promised the same hour, so one of them is certain to be let down
  • like the Bible, largely → the hyphenated phrase read literally — Old Testament and New, two collections bound as one volume

'Largely' is the setter being honest: each Testament is itself a shelf of books, so the count of two only holds at the level everybody actually thinks of them.

15 across ONE-TIME

Formerly a magazine (3-4)

Definition: Formerly · charade

  • a → ONE the indefinite article counts as one of a thing
  • magazine → TIME Time is the American weekly news magazine, founded in 1923

Three words and nothing to grip: the trap is taking 'a magazine' for the definition and hunting through periodicals for a synonym.

16 across THE FENS

Adolescent's getting hot - and later, loud - somewhere in E England (3,4)

Definition: somewhere in E England · container

  • Adolescent's → TEENS a teen is an adolescent, and the clue's apostrophe-s supplies the final letter
  • hot → H H is the letter on the hot tap
  • loud → F f is forte in music, the instruction to play loudly

This is the flat drained marshland of Cambridgeshire and Lincolnshire, much of it below sea level — a region British solvers know by name and nobody else has to.

17 across CADENZA

Exotic dance with last and first characters showing musical flourish (7)

Definition: musical flourish · anagram

  • dance → DANCE five letters lifted straight out of the clue
  • last and first characters → ZA the characters are letters of the alphabet, whose last and first are Z and A

'Characters' wants to mean people in a story and 'last and first' wants to mean a running order; both belong to the alphabet instead.

19 across MATADOR

Some beam at adorable fellow in jacket and tights (7)

Definition: fellow in jacket and tights · hidden word

  • beam at adorable → MATADOR seven consecutive letters, starting inside the first word and running on across both gaps

The definition is doing its own bit of hiding: the costume is the traje de luces, the sequinned suit of lights, and 'fellow' keeps you hunting for a man's name.

20 across SINGLE-HANDED

Unaccompanied, like a swimming-pool clock (6-6)

Definition: Unaccompanied · double definition

  • Unaccompanied → the everyday sense: doing the whole job on your own, with nobody else's help
  • like a swimming-pool clock → the pace clock on a poolside wall carries one big sweep hand for swimmers to time their lengths by

The comma is the seam. The second half is a picture rather than a dictionary sense — a clock described by how many hands it happens to have.

23 across IOTA

Everyman: old? Bless you! A little bit (4)

Definition: A little bit · charade

  • Everyman → I the setter signs these puzzles Everyman, and a setter referring to himself is I
  • old → O o is the standard abbreviation for old
  • Bless you! → TA ta is the everyday British thank-you, what you say when somebody does you a small kindness

The surface is a sneeze and a blessing. 'Everyman' is the setter's own name dropped into his clue, and he is entitled to use it as a letter standing for himself.

24 across TELEVISION

Challenged Soviet line that's set (10)

Definition: set · anagram

  • Soviet → SOVIET six letters straight from the clue, with the Cold War surface doing the hiding
  • line → LINE four more, and ten in total is exactly what the grid is asking for

'Challenged Soviet line' reads as a news headline, which is the trick: those words are fodder rather than politics, and the definition is the small word left looking like part of the sentence.

25 across GOSH

I ask you to leave quietly (4)

Definition: I ask you · charade

  • leave → GO to leave a place is to go
  • quietly → SH sh is the noise you make when asking for quiet

'I ask you' looks like the setter addressing the solver directly, and is really a mild explosion of disbelief, the sort said with the hands in the air.

26 across DECAF LATTE

At year's end, an unstimulating brew - mostly that? (5,5)

Definition: an unstimulating brew · charade + deletion

  • At year's end → DEC December is the month the year finishes in, and Dec is its abbreviation
  • an → A the indefinite article, which in wordplay is simply the letter
  • unstimulating → FLAT a flat drink has lost its fizz and its lift
  • brew → TE the brew is tea, with the last of its three letters left off

The words that spell the drink out also describe it, which is what the closing 'that?' points back at. 'A flat' is a musical key too, and that is the joke the surface leans on.

Down

1 down WAGE

Carry On Nurse's finale introduced by comedian (4)

Definition: Carry On · charade + last letter

  • comedian → WAG a wag is a joker, the man always ready with a quip
  • Nurse's → E the last letter of the word, and the clue's own apostrophe-s marks where it ends

Carry On Nurse is one of the British Carry On comedy films, which is why the title is italicised; splitting that title in two is the move the clue hides.

2 down OWED

Outstanding poem recited (4)

Definition: Outstanding · homophone

  • poem → OWED a poem, especially a lofty one addressed to somebody or something, is an ode

Three letters against four, sharing only two of them: in a clue of three words the ear is the only thing you can use.

3 down PROMOTIONAL

In favour of proposal initially addressing lewd, concerning advertising (11)

Definition: concerning advertising · charade + first letters

  • In favour of → PRO to be pro something is to be in favour of it
  • proposal → MOTION the proposal put before a meeting for debate is a motion
  • addressing lewd → AL the opening letters of the two words, taken in the order the clue gives them

The comma after 'lewd' does the hiding: it makes the surface a complaint about smut and disguises two ordinary words that are there only for their first letters.

4 down GOODBYE

Spin round, ultimately knackered: run's so long (7)

Definition: so long · charade + last letter

  • Spin → GO a spin at something is a go at it, one attempt
  • round → O the round letter, drawn as a circle
  • knackered → D its final letter; knackered is British slang for worn out
  • run → BYE in cricket a bye is a run scored when the batter never touches the ball

Four separate pieces inside seven letters, and the colon invites you to read 'knackered' as a description of a runner rather than as a single letter.

5 down ON ALERT

Ready and available beer, right? (2,5)

Definition: Ready · charade

  • available → ON a drink that is available is on, as in on tap or on the menu
  • beer → ALE ale is the crossword's standard beer, three letters and endlessly useful
  • right → RT Rt is the abbreviation for right, as in the Rt Hon before a minister's name

The question mark sells 'beer, right?' as pub conversation, and that is exactly what stops those two words being read as two separate pieces of a build.

7 down STICKY ENDS

Misadventures, as glue guns are wont to lead to? (6,4)

Definition: Misadventures · double definition

  • Misadventures → the idiom: to come to one is to finish badly, the fate the villain meets in the last chapter
  • as glue guns are wont to lead to → read literally instead — what comes out of the nozzle leaves every join tacky

The question mark is the setter admitting the second half is a joke rather than a dictionary sense, and both halves still land on the same two words.

8 down ASPIDISTRA

Apiarist's worried about dead evergreen (10)

Definition: evergreen · anagram

  • Apiarist's → APIARISTS an apiarist keeps bees, and the clue's apostrophe-s hands over a ninth letter for the mix
  • dead → D d is the standard abbreviation for dead, the one used on family trees

The surface has a beekeeper fretting over a lost hive, so the last thing it invites you to do is count the letters of his job title. Nine of them, plus one, is the whole clue.

12 down OVER-THE-HILL

Conspicuous chuckle: trouble getting on (4-3-4)

Definition: getting on · charade

  • Conspicuous → OVERT an overt act is done in the open where anyone can see it
  • chuckle → HEH the half-laugh as it is written down, three letters of quiet amusement
  • trouble → ILL the ills of the world are its troubles, the noun rather than the adjective

The enumeration promises three words and the build supplies three pieces, but the seams fall in completely different places, which is the whole difficulty.

13 down PODCASTING

Sticking around, old policeman chatting online (10)

Definition: chatting online · charade + container

  • Sticking → PASTING sticking paper down is pasting it, and this is the word that does the wrapping
  • old → O o is the standard abbreviation for old, as in the O of OAP
  • policeman → DC DC is a detective constable, a rank in a British police force

'Sticking around' reads as one phrase meaning hanging about, which is why it is so hard to see that the first word is a wrapper and the second the instruction to wrap.

14 down TENDONITIS

Inflammation in it doesn't hurt (10)

Definition: Inflammation · anagram

  • in it doesn't → TENDONITIS three tiny words, ten letters between them, and every one of the ten is used

'Doesn't hurt' is such a natural phrase that it hides a whole anagram, and the fodder is the three words nobody stops to read as words.

18 down ATHLETE

One having, primarily, a terrifically healthy lifestyle; example: track eventer! (7)

Definition: One having, primarily, a terrifically healthy lifestyle; example: track eventer! · &lit + first letters

  • a terrifically healthy lifestyle → ATHL the opening letters of those three words, in the order the clue sets them down
  • example: track eventer → ETE the same treatment carries on past the semicolon: three more words, three more openings

An &lit, so nothing is left over for a separate definition: read straight through, the sentence describes precisely the sort of person whose initials it has been spelling.

19 down MANDELA

Old President calling for some Imodium and Elastoplasts (7)

Definition: Old President · hidden word

  • Imodium and Elastoplasts → MANDELA the run starts at the end of the first brand name and stops early in the last; Elastoplast is the British name for a sticking plaster

A medicine cabinet makes a good hiding place: both brand names are long, and neither is the sort of word anyone scans for a spare statesman.

21 down RIOT

Going up to Reading, say, entertaining one that'll amuse you (4)

Definition: that'll amuse you · charade + reversal + container

  • to → TO two letters taken straight from the clue's own preposition
  • Reading, say → R reading is one of the three Rs, the schoolroom trio with writing and arithmetic, so it is an example of an R
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one

'Going up to Reading' looks like a train journey to the Berkshire town, and the town has nothing to do with it. Turn the two small pieces round first, then let the third in.

22 down ANTE

Verbally opposed requirement to play poker (4)

Definition: requirement to play poker · homophone

  • opposed → ANTE to be opposed to something is to be anti it, and that prefix said aloud lands on a different spelling

Two four-letter words a single vowel apart and belonging to different worlds: one is a stance and the other is money on the table.
