Across

1 across ARMAGNAC

Spirit embodied by secular Magna Carta (8)

Definition: Spirit · hidden word

  • secular Magna Carta → ARMAGNAC the tail of seculAR runs straight into MAGNA and picks up the C of Carta

The join between two words is where a hidden run is easiest to spot, and this one crosses three, which is why the constitutional surface holds together so well.

5 across ANUBIS

Uncover Blue River island figure revered along the Nile (6)

Definition: figure revered along the Nile · charade + middle letters

  • Blue River → ANUB the Blue River is the Danube of the waltz, and uncovered it loses its D and its E
  • island → IS Is. is the map-maker's abbreviation for island

Blue and River belong to one name rather than two hints, and spotting Strauss's waltz behind them is the step that opens the clue.

10 across CORKSCREW

Irish city's ship's company that gets stuck into the vino (9)

Definition: that gets stuck into the vino · charade

  • Irish city's → CORKS Cork is the city on the Lee, and the possessive S it owns comes along as a letter
  • ship's company → CREW a ship's company is its crew

Cork's own name is the joke: the Irish city and the thing in the bottleneck are the same word, and only one of them is doing the wordplay.

11 across MEDOC

Peninsula: sea freezing cold (5)

Definition: Peninsula · charade

  • sea → MED the Med is the sea, shortened the way everyone shortens it
  • freezing → O water freezes at nought degrees, and nought is written as the letter O
  • cold → C C is the abbreviation for cold, and for Celsius on the same thermometer

Freezing cold is 0C on a thermometer, which is the joke the last two letters are built on.

12 across FETA

Source of protein, iron? Thanks! (4)

Definition: Source of protein · charade

  • iron → FE Fe is iron's symbol on the periodic table
  • Thanks → TA ta is the everyday British thanks

Two of the most reliable abbreviations in the game sit side by side, and the exclamation mark makes them read as chat rather than chemistry.

13 across ANDROMEDA

Group of stars departs capital in open landau (9)

Definition: Group of stars · container + charade + middle letters

  • landau → ANDA opened up, LANDAU parts with the L and U at its ends
  • departs → D D is the timetable's abbreviation for departs
  • capital → ROME the capital wanted is Italy's

D for departs comes straight off a railway board, and 'capital' asks for a city rather than a capital letter: two habits worth keeping.

15 across CINDERELLA

Sweeper coached away from the ball? (10)

Definition: Sweeper coached away from the ball? · cryptic definition

  • Sweeper → not the defender playing behind the back four, which is how the football surface reads it: this sweeper works a broom round a hearth full of ashes
  • coached away from the ball → 'coached' is transport rather than training - a pumpkin turned into a carriage - and 'the ball' is the palace dance that has to be left by midnight

Every word carries a football reading and a pantomime one at the same time, and the question mark is the setter admitting the whole sentence is the definition.

17 across FAVE

Best-loved shanty town bulldozing large area (4)

Definition: Best-loved · deletion

  • shanty town → FAVELA a favela is a Brazilian shanty town
  • large area → L for large and A for area, the two letters flattened out of the favela

Fave is the teenage clipping of favourite, and the slum clearance in the surface is the setter's joke about which letters go.

19 across ROOD

Cross and abusive on the phone (4)

Definition: Cross · homophone

  • abusive → ROOD abusive means rude, and that word said aloud sounds identical to the differently-spelled word this clue wants

A rood is the great crucifix mounted above the chancel screen of an old church, so the definition is architectural rather than bad-tempered.

20 across USED-CAR LOT

Where one might find old Reg Ford? (4-3,3)

Definition: Where one might find old Reg Ford? · cryptic definition

  • old Reg Ford → reads as a bloke with a first name and a surname, which is the whole disguise: a reg is a registration plate and a Ford is the make wearing it
  • Where one might find → the question mark warns that a place is being described rather than named, and 'old' is what the motor trade politely prints as second-hand

Two ordinary trade words are wearing capital letters: Reg for the plate on the windscreen, Ford for the badge on the bonnet.

22 across NON-PERSON

Breaking news: poor Spooner being ostracised (3-6)

Definition: being ostracised · anagram

  • news → NN N is the abbreviation for new, and crossword shorthand doubles a letter to make it plural, so news is two of them
  • Spooner → SPOONER the reverend hands over the seven letters of his surname, and they go straight into the pile

Spooner is wanted for his letters, not his manner of speech: nothing here asks you to swap any initial sounds, which is the trap his name always sets.

24 across IGBO

Foreign language starts to influence opinion around part of UK (4)

Definition: Foreign language · container + first letters

  • influence opinion → IO the opening letters of influence and of opinion
  • part of UK → GB GB is Great Britain, the part of the UK that leaves Northern Ireland out

'Part of UK' is a precise description rather than a loose one: Great Britain really is a proper part of the United Kingdom.

26 across TITHE

What idiot rejected cash meant for the church? (5)

Definition: cash meant for the church · charade + reversal

  • What → EH eh? is what you say when you did not catch it - 'what' out loud
  • idiot → TIT a tit is a mild British word for an idiot

A tithe was the tenth of a year's produce the parish took by law, so this is a historical levy rather than a collection plate.

27 across DEHYDRATE

Heading back east late, he would dry out (9)

Definition: dry out · charade + reversal

  • east → E E is the compass abbreviation for east
  • late → TARDY late is tardy
  • he would → HED he would contracts to he'd

Nothing is shuffled here: three pieces line up in clue order and the whole line is then read from the other end.

28 across NEEDLE

Make cross stitch using this? (6)

Definition: Make cross · double definition

  • Make cross → NEEDLE this half asks for a verb meaning to keep picking at someone until they are cross
  • stitch using this → NEEDLE the implement the stitching is actually done with

The two halves are glued together by 'cross stitch', a real embroidery term that belongs to neither of them, and that join is the joke.

29 across AMETHYST

Hot, steamy, lacking oxygen, turning violet (8)

Definition: violet · anagram + deletion

  • Hot, steamy → HOTSTEAMY nine letters of sauna to work from
  • oxygen → oxygen is O on the periodic table, and that O is the letter dropped before the shuffle

Amethyst is the violet quartz, so 'turning violet' describes a colour change on the surface and a gemstone underneath.

1 down ARCH

Roguish Austen hero undressed Elizabeth finally (4)

Definition: Roguish · charade + middle letters + last letter

  • Austen hero → ARC the Austen hero is Darcy, and undressed he keeps only his middle
  • Elizabeth → H Darcy's match ends in H, and that is all she contributes

Pride and Prejudice supplies both halves, so the clue reads as gossip about the novel rather than as two instructions.

2 down MARIE ANTOINETTE

Trashing contents of Petit Trianon, mean European princess (5,10)

Definition: princess · anagram + middle letters

  • Petit Trianon → ETITTRIANO the queen's own little chateau at Versailles, giving up its outer P and N
  • mean → MEAN four more letters, taken as they stand
  • European → E E is the standard abbreviation for European

The Petit Trianon was her private retreat in the grounds of Versailles, so surface and letters are drawn from the same place.

3 down GOSSAMER

When spread, marge's so light (8)

Definition: light · anagram

  • marge's so → MARGESSO eight letters including the possessive S, and nothing is added or thrown away

Marge is margarine and spreading is what you do with it, so the instruction hides inside an entirely sensible sentence about a tub.

4 down AORTA

Car crash south of Aleppo cleared arterial route (5)

Definition: arterial route · charade + outer letters

  • Aleppo → AO clear the inside out of Aleppo and the two letters at the ends survive
  • Car crash → RTA RTA is a road traffic accident, the police shorthand for a car crash

RTA off a police report is the abbreviation worth banking here; the Syrian city keeps the sentence sounding like a news bulletin.

6 down NIMROD

Hunter set up sleeping accommodation at home (6)

Definition: Hunter · charade + reversal

  • sleeping accommodation → DORM a dorm is a room full of beds, sleeping accommodation in one word
  • at home → IN someone at home is in, the crossword's hardest-working two letters

Nimrod is the mighty hunter of Genesis, which is also why Elgar called a friend's variation by his name.

7 down BODLEIAN LIBRARY

Robin Day worked with braille books here (8,7)

Definition: books here · anagram

  • Robin Day → ROBINDAY Sir Robin Day, the BBC's great interrogator, hands over the eight letters of his name
  • braille → BRAILLE the raised-dot reading system joins the pile whole, seven letters more

'Day worked with braille' reads as a career and a cause rather than as fifteen letters, which is what keeps the anagram out of sight.

8 down SACRAMENTO

Seated, eating cold Japanese noodles over US city (10)

Definition: US city · container + charade

  • Seated → SAT seated is sat, and it opens up to take the meal
  • cold → C C is the letter on the cold tap
  • Japanese noodles → RAMEN ramen are the Japanese noodles
  • over → O O is the cricket scorer's abbreviation for an over

Cricket's O for an over is the convention to keep; the restaurant scene is doing all the misdirection on its own.

9 down SWADDLED

Well wrapped up, son walked with clumsy gait (8)

Definition: Well wrapped up · charade

  • son → S S is the family-tree abbreviation for son
  • walked with clumsy gait → WADDLED to waddle is to walk with the rolling, ungainly gait of a duck

'Well wrapped up' sounds like advice about cold weather, which is exactly what hides the baby in its swaddling clothes.

14 down ACCRINGTON

Where to see Stanley, Bill and Charlie call Tony endlessly? (10)

Definition: Where to see Stanley · charade + deletion

  • Bill → AC a bill is an account, written a/c
  • Charlie → C Charlie is C in the radio alphabet
  • call → RING to call someone is to ring them
  • Tony → TON Tony without his last letter stops at TON

Bill, Charlie and Tony read as three blokes in a pub and are really three separate conventions, which is the whole disguise.

16 down EAST SIDE

Each second flood covers small part of Manhattan (4,4)

Definition: part of Manhattan · charade + container

  • Each → EA ea. is the price-list shorthand for each
  • second → S S is the clock's abbreviation for a second
  • flood → TIDE the flood tide is the incoming one, so flood alone gives TIDE
  • small → S S is the size on a small label

Manhattan's East Side is named by compass point, and the rising water of the surface is what keeps three abbreviations from looking like abbreviations.

18 down SAMIZDAT

We're told Samuel's girlfriend abridged dissident literature (8)

Definition: dissident literature · charade + homophone + deletion

  • Samuel's → SAMIZ Samuel shortens to Sam, and Sam's spoken aloud ends on a Z sound, written out as you hear it
  • girlfriend → DAT a girlfriend is a date, and abridged she is DAT

Samizdat was writing the Soviet censor never saw: typed at home, copied by carbon paper and passed from hand to hand.

21 down SEWELL

Novelist comfortable after spine drained (6)

Definition: Novelist · charade + outer letters

  • spine → SE drain the middle out of SPINE and the two end letters are left
  • comfortable → WELL someone comfortable in themselves is well

Anna Sewell wrote Black Beauty and nothing else, so a bare 'Novelist' is doing a lot of work while the surface complains about a bad back.

23 down NAHUM

Note buzz surrounding Alqosh's top prophet (5)

Definition: prophet · charade + container + first letter

  • Note → N N is a note in music, one of the seven from A to G
  • buzz → HUM a buzz is a hum
  • Alqosh's → A the opening letter of the Iraqi town's name

Alqosh in northern Iraq keeps the tomb traditionally said to be this prophet's, which is why that town and no other is in the clue.

25 down KENT

County clerk gossiped (4)

Definition: County · homophone

  • clerk → KENT a clerk is said 'clark' on this side of the Atlantic, and Clark is the mild-mannered reporter of the Daily Planet whose surname goes in the grid

Half a famous name is a standing invitation to supply the other half: a setter can write Sherlock for Holmes, or a spoken Clark for this one, with no further signal.
