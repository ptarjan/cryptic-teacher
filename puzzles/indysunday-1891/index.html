Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,891

Across

1 across COGENT

Convincing suggestion for businessman? (6)

Definition: Convincing · charade

  • businessman → CO the business half of the word is a company, and CO is how a company signs itself
  • businessman → GENT the man half is a gent, the everyday clipping of gentleman

The clue never splits the long word for you, and spotting that one word is really two is the whole solve. Six letters, two pieces, no indicator anywhere.

4 across SHADOW

Tail Miss Piggy had is splitting (6)

Definition: Tail · container

  • Miss Piggy → SOW Miss Piggy is a pig, and the word for a female pig is a sow
  • had → HAD the clue's own word, dropped in whole at face value

The surface pretends the Muppet owns a tail. 'Tail' is really the verb here — to follow somebody about — and the pig is only there for her three letters.

9 across INORDINATE

Extravagant old princess wearing fancy clothes (10)

Definition: Extravagant · container + charade

  • wearing → IN somebody in a hat is wearing it, so this is the two letters of that little word
  • fancy → ORNATE fancy as an adjective: heavily decorated
  • old princess → DI the princess crosswords mean is Diana, and 'old' says the former holder of the title rather than the present one

Two garment words doing different jobs: one is a two-letter word meaning 'wearing', the other is the instruction to wrap. Only one of them contributes letters of its own.

10 across MUFF

Warmer boob (4)

Definition: Warmer · double definition

  • Warmer → a tube of fur you push both hands into on a cold day
  • boob → British slang for a silly blunder, and for making one

Four letters and two everyday senses with no join between them: one is a piece of old-fashioned outerwear, the other is what a fielder does to an easy catch.

11 across KINDLE

Reader loving literature superficially (6)

Definition: Reader · charade + outer letters

  • loving → KIND kind and loving name the same warmth of character
  • literature → LE the outside of the word, its opening and closing letters only

Amazon's e-reader is the definition, so a brand name is standing in for a common noun — and the ordinary verb, to set alight, has nothing to do with the clue.

12 across GLOSSARY

Specialised dictionary idiot brought over in triumph (8)

Definition: Specialised dictionary · container + reversal

  • triumph → GLORY a triumph is a glory, the noun rather than the verb the surface offers
  • idiot → SSA an idiot is an ass, and those three letters arrive back to front

The surface is a bad-tempered scholar. The wordplay is kinder: 'triumph' is a thing you have, not a thing you do, and the insult is only there to be turned round.

13 across MARCHESI

Italian peers east regularly on walk back from Aldi (8)

Definition: Italian peers · charade + alternate letters + last letter

  • walk → MARCH to march is to walk in step
  • east → ES the first and third letters, picked out at every other step
  • Aldi → I Aldi is a discount supermarket chain, and only the letter it ends on is wanted

The surface is a walk home from the shops, and every word of it is doing a separate job. 'Peers' means noblemen here, not people of your own age.

15 across NOODLE

Strip to plunge into boiling water? (6)

Definition: Strip to plunge into boiling water? · cryptic definition

  • Strip → the surface asks you to picture somebody undressing for a hot bath; read it as a noun and it is a long flat ribbon instead
  • to plunge into boiling water → which is what a cook does with that ribbon — a few minutes in a rolling pan and supper is ready

No letters are hidden here: the whole sentence is one description read two ways. The same word also means a head, or the fool attached to it.

17 across BECALM

Still covered with random lace, bride twirled (6)

Definition: Still · hidden word + reversal

  • random lace, bride → BECALM six letters run without a break from the end of the first word to the start of the last, and they arrive facing the wrong way

The wedding surface does all the work: 'random' looks like a shuffle instruction and 'lace' like its fodder, when in fact the answer is lying right across them.

19 across GADGETRY

BBC boss starts to examine tweets stored by Lineker's devices (8)

Definition: devices · container + first letters

  • Lineker → GARY Gary Lineker, the England striker turned Match of the Day presenter, is on first-name terms with British viewers
  • BBC boss → DG the BBC's boss is its Director-General, DG for short
  • examine tweets → ET one letter from the front of each word

A BBC boss and Lineker's tweets really did make headlines together, which is why the surface reads like news; the pieces of the clue have nothing to do with each other.

21 across MARMOSET

Planet invaded by second alien creature with tufty ears (8)

Definition: creature with tufty ears · container + charade

  • Planet → MARS the planet named after the Roman god of war
  • second → MO a mo is a moment — 'hang on half a mo' — so it does duty for a second
  • alien → ET ET is the crossword's standard alien, borrowed from the film

The surface is science fiction and every piece of it is ordinary: a planet, a moment and a film alien, with the tufted ears belonging to a monkey.

23 across VOLLEY

Fire's lovely after a ramble (6)

Definition: Fire · anagram

  • lovely → LOVELY six letters taken straight from the clue and rearranged

'Fire's lovely' is a sentence about a hearth, and that is the trick: the first word is the definition, the second is raw material, and neither is about warmth.

25 across GO ON

Last member of 1950s radio comedy (2,2)

Definition: Last · double definition

  • Last → the verb: to endure, to carry on, the way a supply lasts a week
  • member of 1950s radio comedy → one of the cast of the anarchic BBC radio show that ran through the fifties — Milligan, Sellers, Secombe and Bentine

The enumeration is the whole game: two words in the grid, one word on the wireless. The Goon Show's name gave English a word for a thug and a fool alike.

26 across ARCHDEACON

Vagrant had race against senior minister (10)

Definition: senior minister · anagram + charade

  • had race → HADRACE seven letters of fodder, taken from the two words as they stand
  • against → CON the con of 'pros and cons' is the case against

'Vagrant had race' sounds like a story about a tramp, and only the first word of it is an instruction. The rest is letters, plus the crossword's usual word for opposition.

27 across TIRADE

Harangue weary assistant on the phone (6)

Definition: Harangue · homophone

  • weary assistant → TIRADE a weary helper is a tired aide, and said at speed the two words run into one

Say the two words quickly and the join disappears. The surface, an exhausted office junior taking a call, exists only to hide where one word stops and the next starts.

28 across CRISES

Conservative is revolting in difficult moments (6)

Definition: difficult moments · charade

  • Conservative → C C is the ballot-paper abbreviation for a Conservative candidate
  • is revolting → RISES a population that is revolting rises against its rulers

'Revolting' is the political verb here rather than the adjective the surface offers, and 'Conservative' is a party letter rather than a temperament.

Down

1 down CANDIDATE

Examinee may go out taking papers (9)

Definition: Examinee · container + charade

  • may → CAN may and can grant the same permission, one word swapped for the other
  • go out → DATE to go out with somebody is to date them
  • papers → ID your papers are your ID, the documents that say who you are

The exam-hall surface is a decoy. 'Go out' is dating rather than leaving, and the papers are the ones in a wallet, not the ones on the desk.

2 down GORED

Flush stuck (5)

Definition: stuck · charade

  • Flush → GO a face that flushes goes a colour, and the verb comes first
  • Flush → RED and red is the colour it goes

'Flush' wants to be the definition — level, or well off — but it is the whole of the wordplay, and it has to be heard as two little words rather than one.

3 down NAIVETE

Negative jerks disregarding good quality of Forrest Gump? (7)

Definition: quality of Forrest Gump · anagram + deletion

  • Negative → NEGATIVE the first word is itself the raw material, and it arrives one letter too long for the light
  • good → G G is the crossword's letter for good, as on an old school report

'Negative jerks' reads as an insult aimed at people, and that is the misdirection: the first word is not describing the jerks, it is the letters themselves.

5 down HOEDOWN

Did weed have an energetic dance? (7)

Definition: an energetic dance · charade

  • Did weed → HOED to hoe a bed is to weed it, and 'did' asks for the past tense
  • have → OWN to have something is to own it

Read as English the clue asks whether a plant went dancing; read as wordplay both halves are plain verbs, laid end to end with nothing joining them.

6 down DIME STORE

Suburban property beginning to dry up, rent cheap place in NYC? (4,5)

Definition: cheap place in NYC · charade + first letter + reversal

  • Suburban property → SEMI a semi is a semi-detached house, the standard British suburban home
  • dry → D the opening letter of the word, and nothing more of it
  • rent → TORE rent is what a rip did: the past of rend, which matches the past of tear

'Beginning to dry up' begs to be read as one phrase about a drought, and the clue cuts straight through it. A dime is the American ten-cent coin the shop is priced by.

7 down WAFER

Slice iron through rolling green (5)

Definition: Slice · anagram + container

  • green → RAW green means raw or unripe, and those three letters are the ones tumbled about
  • iron → FE Fe is the chemical symbol for iron

'Rolling green' paints a golf course, which is also where a sliced iron shot goes. The whole surface is golf and not one word of the wordplay is.

8 down LAUGHING STOCK

Spooner's providing canal-side labour for ridiculous figure (8,5)

Definition: ridiculous figure · spoonerism

  • providing canal-side labour → STAFFING LOCK the labour on offer is staffing a lock, the gated chamber that lifts boats from one canal level to the next
  • Spooner's → LAUGHING STOCK trade the sounds at the front of those two words, ST for L, and say what comes out

The joke only works out loud, so say the canal job first and then trade its opening sounds. What comes back is a person nobody takes seriously.

14 down COALMINER

Caroline squirms letting Mike in, a smutty labourer (9)

Definition: smutty labourer · anagram

  • Caroline → CAROLINE the name's eight letters are the bulk of the fodder
  • Mike → M Mike is M in the NATO alphabet that pilots and police spell with

'Smutty' is literal here — smut is soot and coal dust — which is the opposite of what the surface wants you to hear. Caroline is fodder rather than a character.

16 down LARCENOUS

Thieving needs clear criminal intelligence (9)

Definition: Thieving · anagram + charade

  • clear → CLEAR five letters of fodder; the word contributes nothing but them
  • intelligence → NOUS nous is common sense or brains, a Greek word English helped itself to

'Criminal intelligence' is a phrase the surface hands you whole, and the clue cuts it in half: one word is the instruction and the other is a piece of the answer.

18 down MUSTARD

Earth going round sun which is yellow and hot (7)

Definition: yellow and hot · container

  • Earth → MUD earth in the gardener's sense is mud
  • sun → STAR the sun is a star, the one nearest us

The surface draws the solar system and the wordplay draws a flowerbed. 'Yellow and hot' belongs to the condiment shelf rather than to any planet's weather.

20 down DIVIDER

Upsetting bloody film about India's partition (7)

Definition: partition · container + reversal

  • bloody → RED blood is red, so the adjective hands over the colour
  • India → I India is I in the NATO alphabet
  • film → VID a vid is a video, the everyday clipping

India's partition is a real event and the whole surface leans on it. In the wordplay the country is only its signal letter, and a partition is a screen in a room.

22 down ABORT

Interrupt Kazakh journalist, giving answer up front (5)

Definition: Interrupt · deletion + charade

  • answer → A A is the standard short form of answer, as in a Q and A
  • Kazakh journalist → BORT the fictional Kazakh reporter of the Sacha Baron Cohen film, left without the A that has moved

Borat is the Kazakh television reporter Sacha Baron Cohen played, and the clue takes his name apart by moving one letter rather than by shuffling the lot.

24 down LOADS

See interruptions on YouTube much? (5)

Definition: much · charade

  • See → LO lo is the old imperative for look, as in 'lo and behold'
  • interruptions on YouTube → ADS what breaks into a video you are watching for free

Reading the clue as one exasperated question is the trap. It splits after the first word, and the rest is simply what free video keeps handing you.
