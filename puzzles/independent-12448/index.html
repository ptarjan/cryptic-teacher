9 across CHIPOLATA

Sausage French fry with oil, salt and bay occasionally (9)

Definition: Sausage · charade + alternate letters

A chipolata is a small thin sausage, the kind wrapped in bacon at Christmas, so the definition names the class it belongs to.

  • French fry → CHIP What Americans call a French fry is a chip in Britain.
  • oil, salt and bay → OLATA Read the three seasonings as one run and take every second letter, starting with the very first.
  • occasionally — What happens occasionally happens every so often, and here that means picking up every second letter as you read along.

The whole clue reads as a recipe, and that is the disguise: the seasonings are there to be spelled out, not cooked.

10 across ICIER

Cooler setter essentially renounced drink (5)

Definition: Cooler · charade + deletion

Both are comparatives for a lower temperature, and both carry the same second sense of a frostier manner towards someone.

  • setter → I The setter speaks as 'I' — the person who wrote the clue is in it.
  • drink → CIDER The pressed-apple drink, five letters long.
  • essentially → D A word's essential letter is the one at its very centre, and this sits at the centre of that drink.
  • renounced — To renounce is to give something up, so the letter singled out as the essential one is surrendered.

'Cooler' first reads as a noun — an icebox, or a prison cell — when it is the comparative adjective, which is what makes the ending of the answer fair.

11 across RIGHTED

Set straight communist about conflicts without borders (7)

Definition: Set straight · container + deletion

To right a capsized boat, or an old injustice, is to set it straight again, and the past tense matches the definition's.

  • communist → RED Red is the standing crossword word for a communist, after the colour of the flag.
  • conflicts → IGHT Conflicts are FIGHTS, and losing the letters at either edge leaves this.
  • about — To go about something is to go round it, so one piece wraps itself outside the other.
  • without borders — A word's borders are the letters at its two edges, and without them only the middle survives.

The politics is the misdirection: 'communist about conflicts' reads like a news headline while both words are quietly doing letter work.

12 across KIPPERS

Fish captain primarily shipped to East End (7)

Definition: Fish · cycling

Kippers are herrings that have been split, salted and smoked, so the definition names them by the creature they started as.

  • captain → KIPPER The captain of a boat or a team is a skipper; this is what remains once its leading letter sets sail.
  • primarily → S The letter at the head of that word for a captain, the one sent travelling.
  • primarily — Primarily means first of all, so it points at whichever letter stands at the front.
  • shipped to East End — East is the right-hand side of a grid, so the letter is carried from the front of the word round to the back.

'Fish captain' reads as a single job, and the definition really stops after the first word. No letter is shuffled — only the starting point moves.

13 across HIT AND RUN

Third nun wrongly charged with a cowardly crime (3,3,3)

Definition: cowardly crime · anagram

Knocking someone down and driving off without stopping is the offence the phrase names, and the cowardice is in the fleeing.

  • Third nun → THIRDNUN Eight letters of fodder, taken exactly as the clue prints them.
  • a → A A ninth letter joins the pile before anything is rearranged.
  • wrongly — Something done wrongly has not been done in the proper order, which is the licence to put these letters in another one.

The surface has a nun wrongly accused, so 'charged with' reads as an indictment; in the parse it is only the join between the letters and their definition.

14 across FIDDLER CRAB

One who wangles tip from passenger in taxi? Animal! (7,4)

Definition: Animal · charade + container + last letter

A fiddler crab is the small shore crab whose one huge claw looks like a fiddle being bowed, so the definition names its class.

  • One who wangles → FIDDLER To fiddle something is to wangle it by a little quiet cheating.
  • taxi → CAB A taxi is a cab.
  • passenger → R The letter this word finishes on.
  • tip — The tip of a word is the letter at its far end, the point it finishes on.
  • in — Here 'in' is a placing instruction rather than scene-setting: the letter goes inside the word that follows.

'Tip from passenger' reads as coins handed over at the end of a ride, which is the picture the setter is selling; in the parse a tip is one letter.

17 across DONNA

Italian lady returning an unspoken acknowledgement (5)

Definition: Italian lady · reversal

Donna is the Italian courtesy title placed before a woman's first name, the feminine counterpart of don.

  • an → AN The article is taken at face value, both letters of it.
  • unspoken acknowledgement → NOD A nod is how you agree without saying anything.
  • returning — Something returning comes back the way it went, so the letters are read from the far end towards the near one — right to left in an across entry.

'Returning' looks like part of the picture — a lady coming home — and it is the instruction instead. The acknowledgement is unspoken because it is a gesture.

19 across SINEW

Suture holds in tendon (5)

Definition: tendon · container

A sinew is the tough cord tying muscle to bone, which is what a tendon is — the same fibre under an everyday name.

  • Suture → SEW To suture a wound is to sew it up.
  • in → IN The little word is taken exactly as it stands and slotted into the gap.
  • holds — To hold something is to keep it within you, so the first word closes around what comes next.

The surface is entirely surgical, so no word sticks out as an instruction; the whole battle is hearing 'suture' as a verb.

21 across WHEELBARROW

Pub brawl after Welsh emptied fish cart (11)

Definition: cart · charade + outer letters

A wheelbarrow is the hand-pushed garden cart with a single wheel at the front, so the definition names the class it sits in.

  • Welsh → WH First and last letters of the word, all that is left once it is emptied.
  • fish → EEL The long snake-like fish, a crossword regular.
  • Pub → BAR A bar is a pub, or the counter you order at inside one.
  • brawl → ROW A row, rhyming with cow, is a noisy quarrel.
  • after — In an across entry whatever comes after something sits to its right, so these two pieces land at the end.
  • emptied — Empty a word and its middle is gone, leaving only the letters standing at the two ends.

'Welsh emptied fish cart' is nonsense as English, which is the tell that the clue runs its pieces out of order: 'after' sends the pub and the brawl to the back.

25 across CHATEAU

Tom consumes hot water in French stately home (7)

Definition: stately home · container + charade

A château is a French country house or castle, the grand seat of a landed family — their equivalent of an English stately home.

  • Tom → CAT A tom is a male cat; the capital letter is the disguise.
  • hot → H H is the letter on the hot tap, and the standard abbreviation.
  • water in French → EAU Eau is French for water, as in eau de cologne.
  • consumes — What is consumed is swallowed whole, so the letter after it disappears inside the animal named before.

The definition is only the last two words: 'French' has been borrowed by the wordplay for its water, which is what makes the clue feel a word short.

27 across PENDANT

Ornament from Norway acquired by purist (7)

Definition: Ornament · container

A pendant is the ornament that hangs from a chain round the neck; the name comes from the French for hanging.

  • purist → PEDANT A pedant is a purist about small rules.
  • Norway → N N is Norway's international vehicle code, the letter on the back of its cars.
  • acquired by — Whatever acquires something takes it in and keeps it, so the longer word closes around the single letter.

A pedant would be the first to tell you the two words are spelled differently, which is the joke the setter is leaning on.

28 across SHEER

Thin bedcover Romeo brought in for model (5)

Definition: Thin · substitution

Sheer fabric is so thin you can see through it — the sense used of stockings and curtains, not the one meaning utter.

  • bedcover → SHEET The plain cotton sheet on a bed.
  • Romeo → R R in the NATO alphabet, where every letter is given a name.
  • model → T Ford's Model T, the first mass-produced car — the letter that steps aside.
  • brought in for — Bringing one thing in for another is a swap: the second letter leaves and the first stands where it stood.

'Model' looks like a definition, or an anagram signal, and is neither: it names one letter, and Romeo takes that letter's place.

29 across GUNPOWDER

Grown-up desperate to secure extremely destructive explosive (9)

Definition: explosive · anagram + container

Gunpowder is the black mixture of saltpetre, charcoal and sulphur that fired cannon and muskets for six centuries.

  • Grown-up → GUNPOWR The seven letters of the hyphenated word, thrown into a different order.
  • extremely destructive → DE First and last letters of the second word.
  • desperate — A desperate person is past keeping order, and the letters lose theirs too.
  • to secure — To secure something is to have it held safely inside your grip, so the pair ends up enclosed.
  • extremely — Extremes are the far ends of a thing, so the letters taken are the ones at each edge of the word.

The hyphen makes 'Grown-up' look like scene-setting rather than fodder, and 'desperate' like the mood of the sentence rather than an instruction.

1 down SCORCH

Show contempt for church when snubbing cardinal's char (6)

Definition: char · charade + deletion

To char is to burn a surface brown or black, which is what a hot iron does to a shirt or the sun does to grass.

  • Show contempt for → SCORN To scorn something is to show contempt for it.
  • cardinal → N North is a cardinal point of the compass, and this is the letter that gets cut.
  • church → CH CH is the standard abbreviation for church, the one printed on maps.
  • snubbing — To snub is to cut someone dead, and here the cutting is literal: the letter named next is dropped out of the word in front.

The cardinal is a compass point, not a churchman in red — the surface stands him next to the church precisely so you read him the other way.

2 down KINGSTON

Family goes on vacation to northern capital (8)

Definition: capital · charade + outer letters

Kingston is the seat of government of Jamaica, so the plain noun names the answer by the category it belongs to.

  • Family → KIN Your kin are your family, your blood relations.
  • goes → GS First and last letters of the word, all that survives the emptying.
  • to → TO The little word is taken at face value and joined on as it stands.
  • northern → N N is the map abbreviation for northern.
  • on vacation — A word sent on vacation has its insides emptied and its house left standing, so only the letters at each end stay behind.

'Goes on vacation' reads as one phrase about a holiday, and that is the join: the verb is fodder and the holiday is the instruction to hollow it out.

3 down PORT OF CALL

Spooner's apostle's train makes scheduled stop (4,2,4)

Definition: scheduled stop · spoonerism

A ship's port of call is a harbour written into its itinerary, a place the voyage is timetabled to break at.

  • train → COURT A grandee's train is the retinue trailing after them — their court.
  • apostle's → PAUL The apostle who wrote most of the letters in the New Testament.
  • Spooner's → PORT OF CALL Say the apostle's retinue aloud, swap the opening C and P, and the possessive turns into an 'of' along the way.
  • Spooner's — The Reverend Spooner was famous for trading the opening sounds of two words, so naming him is the instruction to trade them back.

'Train' is the retinue that follows a great man, not the thing at a platform — and the surface keeps a railway in view right up to the last word.

4 down MAID

Abigail is prepared for eavesdroppers (4)

Definition: Abigail · homophone

An abigail is a lady's personal maid, named after a servant in a Jacobean play and now surviving mostly in crosswords.

  • prepared → MAID Something prepared has been made ready, and that word spoken is spelt a second way.
  • for eavesdroppers — An eavesdropper only ever hears a word, never sees it written, so the sound is what has to match.

Abigail arrives with a capital letter and reads as somebody's name; it is a job, and the whole clue turns on demoting it.

5 down BACKSLIDER

Recidivist regularly concealed gear under rugby player's top (10)

Definition: Recidivist · charade + alternate letters

A recidivist keeps relapsing into crime, and a backslider is anyone who slips back into old bad habits — the same relapse in plainer words.

  • rugby player's → BACKS A back is one of the players behind the scrum, and the possessive 's' comes along for the ride.
  • top → LID The top of a jar or a saucepan is its lid.
  • gear → ER Every other letter of the word, starting from the second.
  • regularly concealed — Letters tucked away at even intervals — every second one — inside the word that follows.
  • under — A down entry is read top to bottom, so whatever is under something else lands later in the answer.

'Rugby player's top' sounds like a shirt, which is exactly the picture wanted: it is two separate pieces, a position on the pitch and a cover for a pot.

6 down WIMP

Weed provided by fellow impresario (4)

Definition: Weed · hidden word

A weed, in the playground sense, is a feeble specimen of a person rather than a garden plant, and that insult is what the answer means.

  • fellow impresario → WIMP Four consecutive letters straddle the join, the end of one word running into the start of the next.
  • provided by — The two words after it supply the letters themselves, handing them over in order with nothing done to them.

Nothing is rearranged, removed or sounded here: the surface offers a showbiz backer and a chum, and the letters simply lie across the gap between them.

7 down LINEAR

Straight man discovered aboard ship (6)

Definition: Straight · container + middle letter

Something linear runs in a straight line rather than curving — the adjective a mathematician uses of a graph that never bends.

  • ship → LINER A liner is a large passenger ship.
  • man → A Take the cover off each end of the word and this middle letter is left standing.
  • discovered — Read it as dis-covered: the covering letters at either end come off, leaving what was underneath.
  • aboard — Whatever is aboard a vessel is inside it, so the uncovered letter is placed within the ship.

'Straight man' looks like one phrase, the comedian's partner, and prising the two words apart is the whole battle — only the second is fodder.

8 down CROSSBOW

Flapper admits boss shot shooter (8)

Definition: shooter · container + anagram

A crossbow shoots bolts down a grooved stock, so the loose noun names the weapon by the job it does.

  • Flapper → CROW A bird that flaps — the black, cawing sort.
  • boss → SSBO The letters of the word, thrown into a new order.
  • admits — To admit someone is to let them in, so the bird's letters open up and take the other piece inside.
  • shot — Shot silk has its colours thrown through one another, and the same scattering happens to these four letters.

'Flapper' wants to be a 1920s dancer in a fringed dress. Hear the bird instead and the rest of the clue opens up.

15 down DRAMATURGE

Doctor initially assists German dissecting aged script consultant (10)

Definition: script consultant · charade + container + first letter

A dramaturge is a theatre's in-house adviser on texts, working with writers on the shape and staging of a play.

  • Doctor → DR The everyday abbreviation, as on a brass plate.
  • assists → A The letter this word begins with.
  • aged → MATURE Aged cheese or aged wine is mature.
  • German → G G is the standard dictionary abbreviation for German.
  • initially — Initially means at the start, so only the opening letter of the word beside it is taken.
  • dissecting — To dissect is to cut something open down the middle, and whatever does the cutting lodges inside.

'Doctor' is a favourite anagram signal and here it is nothing of the kind — it stands for its own two letters, and the German is a letter too, not a person.

16 down ROSE WINDOW

One might provide over-optimistic worldview? (4,6)

Definition: One might provide over-optimistic worldview? · cryptic definition

The answer is the great circular panel of stained glass in a cathedral's west front, and tinted glass is the literal way to see life too cheerfully.

  • over-optimistic → The surface sense: someone looking through rose-tinted spectacles, which is an attitude and not an object.
  • worldview → The setter's sense: not a philosophy but a literal view out, through coloured glass set in a cathedral wall.

There is no separable wordplay here, just one joke told twice: a rose window is the wheel of stained glass above a cathedral door, and a rose tint is what makes any view too sunny.

17 down DOWNCAST

Subdued party with Nollywood's top actors (8)

Definition: Subdued · charade + first letter

Someone downcast has low spirits and lowered eyes, which is the quiet, flattened mood the definition asks for.

  • party → DO A do is a party — an office do, a birthday do.
  • with → W W is the standard shorthand for with, as in 'w/ milk'.
  • Nollywood's → N The opening letter of the name given here.
  • actors → CAST The cast of a film is its actors.
  • top — The top of a written word is where it begins, so only its opening letter is wanted.

Nollywood is Nigeria's film industry, the third of the -ollywoods after Hollywood and Bollywood; the clue wants nothing from it but the letter it starts with.

20 down NORMANDY

Random drunk entering city where many disembarked (8)

Definition: where many disembarked · anagram + container

This stretch of French coast is where the Allied armies came ashore on D-Day in 1944, the largest seaborne landing ever mounted.

  • city → NY New York, shortened the way it is on a baseball cap.
  • Random → ORMAND The six letters of the word, staggered into a new order.
  • drunk — A drunk cannot walk a straight line, and these letters lose their line too.
  • entering — To enter a place is to go inside it, so the staggered letters are planted between the two the city gives.

'Random drunk' is one person on the surface, and that is where the join hides: the first word is the letters, the second is the instruction.

22 down ENAMEL

Partially retired middleman enjoys dental cover (6)

Definition: dental cover · hidden word + reversal

Enamel is the hard glassy layer capping a tooth, so the two words name it by where it lies and the job it does.

  • middleman enjoys → LEMANE Six consecutive letters straddle the join between these two words, sitting there the wrong way round.
  • Partially — Only part of what follows is wanted — a run of letters sitting inside the words, not the words themselves.
  • retired — Someone retired has withdrawn and gone back, and the letters go back too, read from their end towards their start.

'Partially retired middleman' is a plausible little biography and every word of it is an instruction — part of it only, and read backwards.

24 down NATURE

Character in novella that you consider memorable, ultimately (6)

Definition: Character · last letters

A person's nature is their character, the settled disposition you mean when you call someone good-natured.

  • in novella that → NAT Three words in a row, each handing over the letter it ends on.
  • you consider memorable → URE Three more final letters, still read left to right.
  • ultimately — What is ultimate comes last, so from each word you take the letter it finishes on.

'Character in novella' is the trap: it reads as somebody in a book, and the instruction sits right at the far end of the sentence, after a comma.

26 down EARL

Nobleman almost loses case (4)

Definition: Nobleman · deletion

An earl is the British peer ranking between a viscount and a marquess, so the definition names his rank by its class.

  • almost → NEARLY Almost and nearly are the same word in different clothes.
  • case → NY A word's case is the shell at either end of it — here its first letter and its last.
  • loses — What is lost is gone for good, so the letters named next simply drop away.

The clue reads as a lord losing a court case, and that is the whole disguise: 'case' is a shell rather than a lawsuit, and 'almost' is fodder rather than a hedge.

27 down PINE

Long needle supplier (4)

Definition: Long · double definition

To pine is to long for somebody, and a pine is the evergreen whose leaves are needles — two senses sharing only a spelling.

  • Long → The verb, not the adjective: to long for someone is to ache for them.
  • needle supplier → The needles are the stiff evergreen leaves of a conifer, nothing to do with sewing.

'Long needle' reads as one object, a big darning needle, and the split falls straight through the middle of it — one word to each definition.
