9 across OVOID

Like 0, nothing (0) divides 51500 (5)

Definition: Like 0 · charade + container

Ovoid means egg-shaped, and a written 0 is drawn as that same closed oval, so 'Like 0' points at the shape.

  • nothing → O nothing is written as a nought, and a nought on the page is the letter o
  • 51500 → VID the digits break into Roman numerals: 5 is V, 1 is I, 500 is D
  • (0) → O the bracketed nought is a second o, and it is the one that goes in
  • divides — To divide a thing is to come between its parts, so the letter named goes into the middle of what follows and splits it.

A note about zero going into a five-figure number.

The '(0)' looks like a helpful gloss on the word in front of it and is a second nought in its own right, while the definition is the shape of the numeral itself.

10 across THORNIEST

It’s not her fault being most difficult (9)

Definition: most difficult · anagram

A thorny problem is a prickly, awkward one, so the superlative is the hardest of them all — superlative for superlative.

  • It’s not her → ITSNOTHER nine letters of fodder, and the apostrophe is not one of them
  • fault — A fault is a break, the crack that runs through rock, and letters broken like that come out in a new order.

Somebody insisting a woman is not the one to blame.

'It's not her fault' is a complete sentence of excuse, which is exactly why none of it looks like material waiting to be shuffled.

11 across SPIKE-NAIL

One secures Milligan no trouble (5-4)

Definition: One secures · charade

A spike-nail is the long heavy nail driven through timbers to fasten them, so 'One secures' names it by the job it does.

  • Milligan → SPIKE Spike Milligan, the Goon Show comedian, went by that first name

Somebody landing the comedian with no bother at all.

'no trouble' reads as how easily the job went, and each of its two words is a separate piece of the build.

12 across GASSY

Bubbly girl in Germany on vacation, not Luxembourg (5)

Definition: Bubbly · container + deletion + outer letters

A gassy drink is full of bubbles, so 'Bubbly' is the fizz sense of the word and not the personality one.

  • Germany → GY emptied out, the country's name keeps only its first and last letters
  • girl → LASS a lass is a girl, in the northern English word
  • Luxembourg → L l is Luxembourg's international plate letter, and it is the letter taken away
  • in — 'In' puts one thing inside another, so the letters named before it sit within the word that follows.
  • on vacation — A house on vacation stands empty, so the word keeps only the letters at its two ends and loses everything between.
  • not — 'Not' cancels what comes after it, so the letter standing for the country named there is taken away.

A cheerful girl holidaying in Germany, though not going on to Luxembourg.

'Bubbly girl' reads as one description of a lively person, and those two words end up on opposite sides of the clue's join.

Afternoon tea with butter and bread laid out.

'Tea' wants to be the drink on the tray, and the word needed is the Indian one — which is also where the bread on the plate comes from.

18 across GEO

On retreat, say love of the earth (3)

Definition: of the earth · reversal + charade

Geo- is the combining form meaning earth, from the Greek ge: geology and geography are both studies of it.

  • say → GE 'say' is eg, for example, and here it is turned round
  • love → O love is a score of nothing at tennis, written as a nought
  • On retreat — An army in retreat turns and goes back the way it came, so the letters beside it are written in the opposite order.

Somebody away on a retreat, professing love of the earth.

'On retreat, say' reads as a hedge, the way people say 'so to speak', and 'say' is the piece being turned rather than a softener.

20 across OMEGA

Definition: The final · hidden word

Omega ends the Greek alphabet, so 'The final' names it by position — the phrase 'the alpha and the omega' turns on that.

  • some game → OMEGA five letters in a row, starting partway through the first word and running over the gap
  • shown — What is shown is already on display, so these letters need no changing: they are sitting in view in the words beside it.

The last of some game being televised.

'The final of some game' reads as a cup tie, and the answer is a Greek letter rather than a match: the last one in that alphabet.

22 across MACHREE

Machree is the Irish-English endearment from mo chroi, 'my heart', used exactly where an English speaker says 'my dear'.

  • runs → R r is runs on a cricket scorecard
  • telecom company → EE EE is the British mobile network, once Everything Everywhere
  • completes — To complete a thing is to supply its last part, so the letters named beside it go on the end rather than the front.

A phone company finishing its high-speed rollout across Ireland.

The definition is the whole opening phrase, an Irish endearment, and 'completes' is quietly an instruction rather than a word about the rollout.

27 across BOARDROOM

Where executives are allegedly uninterested by discharge (9)

Definition: Where executives are · homophone

The boardroom is where a company's directors sit, so 'Where executives are' names it by its occupants.

  • uninterested → BOARD uninterested is bored, and said aloud that is also the company's body of directors
  • discharge → ROOM rheum is the watery discharge from the eyes and nose, and it sounds exactly like the word for a space
  • allegedly — 'Allegedly' says the words are only being reported, and a reported word is one you hear rather than see spelled.

Directors said to be unmoved by some medical discharge.

Both halves are heard rather than spelled, and the second turns on rheum, a word few people meet outside a dictionary.

  • Spain → E e is Spain's international plate letter, for Espana

A tipsy admiral on a tour of Italy and Spain.

'Sent flying' reads as a punch landing, and it is the postal sense instead: carried by plane rather than knocked across a room.

  • 50,500 → LLO read the digits in pairs: 50 is L in Roman numerals, then 50 again, then a nought written as o
