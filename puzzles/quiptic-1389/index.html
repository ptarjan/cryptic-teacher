Across

1 across AD AGENCY

E.g. Red Cross wanting information for marketing firm (2,6)

Definition: marketing firm · deletion

  • E.g. Red Cross → AIDAGENCY the Red Cross is one example of an aid agency — hence the 'e.g.'
  • information → I i = information, as on tourist signs — the letter AID AGENCY goes 'wanting'

The 'e.g.' is working, not decorating: the Red Cross stands for AID AGENCY, which then loses its I. Losing a letter turns one business into a very different one.

5 across SNIPES

Has a go at finding these birds (6)

Definition: Has a go at · double definition

  • Has a go at → SNIPES to snipe at someone is to have a go at them — potshots from cover
  • birds → SNIPES snipe are long-billed wading birds

Split before 'finding': a verb about criticism on one side, a wading bird on the other.

9 across FRAGRANCE

Silly farce about elderly relative getting bouquet (9)

Definition: bouquet · anagram + container

  • farce → FRACE 'silly' shuffles it, and it opens around GRAN
  • elderly relative → GRAN grandmother, tucked inside the shaken FARCE

The jumbled FARCE opens up to admit GRAN — 'about' says the farce goes around her. 'Bouquet' is the wine-taster's word for a scent.

11 across WIDER

More extensive work involved designing employment rules initially (5)

Definition: More extensive · first letters

  • work involved designing employment rules → WIDER the first letters of the five words

'Initially' governs the whole run of five words, and the office-jargon surface keeps them looking innocent.

12 across SECOND SEASON

Summer is what new TV shows look forward to! (6,6)

Definition: Summer · double definition

  • Summer → SECONDSEASON of the year's four seasons, spring is first and summer second
  • what new TV shows look forward to → SECONDSEASON a new show's biggest hope is getting renewed for another run after its debut year

The exclamation mark admits the whimsy: neither half is a dictionary definition, but both point squarely at the answer.

15 across TUTS

Complains about empty threats by workers group (4)

Definition: Complains · charade + deletion

  • threats → TS 'empty' strips out the middle, leaving the outer T and S
  • workers group → TU TU = trade union

'By' just sets the pieces side by side, with the union first even though the clue names it last. Tutting is that little click of the tongue in disapproval.

16 across STAR CHARTS

Establishes repository for daily sources of astronomical data (4,6)

Definition: sources of astronomical data · container

  • Establishes → STARTS establishes = starts (a business, say) — it opens around CHAR
  • daily → CHAR a char — charwoman — is 'a daily', the old word for a cleaner who comes each day

'Daily' for a charwoman is vintage British usage and a favourite way to smuggle in CHAR. 'Repository for' is the container signal, dressed up as library language.

18 across GRAND PIANO

Instrument on parading ground (5,5)

Definition: Instrument · anagram

  • on parading → ONPARADING anagram fodder, 10 letters
  • ground → anagram indicator — ground as in milled, the past tense of grind

'Ground' looks like the parade ground it sits next to, but it is the past tense of grind — the letters of 'on parading' are milled into the answer.

19 across TAXI

Test current means of transport (4)

Definition: means of transport · charade

  • Test → TAX an old sense of 'to test' is to strain or burden someone heavily
  • current → I I is the physics symbol for electric current

'Test' is a verb here — a heavy load taxes your strength — and physics lends the I.

21 across ANY MINUTE NOW

New mutiny on a reshuffle is imminent (3,6,3)

Definition: imminent · anagram

  • New mutiny on a → NEWMUTINYONA anagram fodder, 12 letters
  • reshuffle → anagram indicator

A perfectly political surface — mutiny brewing, a reshuffle coming — with the indicator posing as a noun at the end of the fodder.

24 across IMAGE

Icon that is featured on outside of journal (5)

Definition: Icon · container

  • that is → IE i.e. = that is — its two letters split to the two ends
  • journal → MAG a mag — magazine — is a journal

'That is' for i.e. is worth banking. So is the trick underneath it: a two-letter piece can split to opposite ends of the answer, which solvers miss because they expect pieces to stay whole.

25 across EVERY TIME

Reverse part of extremity revealed consistently (5,4)

Definition: consistently · hidden word + reversal

  • extremity revealed → EVERYTIME read backwards inside: extrEMITY REVEaled

A hidden answer running right to left across the join of two words — 'reverse' gives the direction, 'part of' says it is buried.

26 across NUDISM

Philosophy advocating a stripped-down life? (6)

Definition: Philosophy advocating a stripped-down life? · cryptic definition

  • stripped-down life → literally a life with the clothes stripped off
  • Philosophy advocating → the -ISM ending: a doctrine with advocates

'Stripped-down' usually means pared back to essentials; here it is literal — a life with no clothes on. The question mark signals the pun.

27 across AT A PINCH

If necessary move slowly after a knock (2,1,5)

Definition: If necessary · charade

  • a → A stands as itself
  • knock → TAP a tap is a light knock
  • move slowly → INCH to inch along is to move slowly

'After' inverts the order you read the clue in, which is the whole difficulty here. Note too that the definition is the entire phrase 'If necessary', not the single word 'If'.

1 down ALFA

How Nato spells out start of attack regularly falls flat (4)

Definition: How Nato spells out start of attack · alternate letters

  • falls flat → ALFA the even letters of fAlLs FlAt, taken 'regularly'

The definition is the whole opening clause: on the radio, NATO says 'Alfa' for the letter A. The wordplay then finds the same word sitting in 'falls flat'.

2 down ALAS

Unfortunately it’s essential to medical assessment (4)

Definition: Unfortunately · hidden word

  • medical assessment → ALAS hidden across the join: medicAL ASsessment

'Essential to' points at the core of the words — the answer runs straight across the gap between them.

3 down EARNER

Student lacks head for income-generating activity (6)

Definition: income-generating activity · deletion + first letter

  • Student → LEARNER a learner — the L-plate sense — about to lose its top letter

In a down entry the 'head' is the top letter, so LEARNER loses its L. 'A nice little earner' is the idiom the definition leans on.

4 down CAN-DO ATTITUDE

Excitedly tout candidate showing focus on achievement (3-2,8)

Definition: focus on achievement · anagram

  • tout candidate → TOUTCANDIDATE anagram fodder, 13 letters
  • Excitedly → anagram indicator

The electioneering surface — someone excitedly touting a candidate — supplies every letter, and 'excitedly' is the word that shakes them up.

6 down NEW DELHI

Capital hotel invested in fresh food store (3,5)

Definition: Capital · charade + container

  • hotel → H Hotel is the NATO codeword for H
  • fresh → NEW fresh = new
  • food store → DELI a delicatessen — it takes the H in

Hotel for H comes from the NATO alphabet, and there is a second codeword in this corner of the grid — a setter who uses one often uses another. 'Invested in' dresses the container up as finance.

7 down PEDESTRIAN

Kind of crossing that’s slow and boring (10)

Definition: Kind of crossing · double definition

  • Kind of crossing → PEDESTRIAN a crossing meant for people on foot, rather than vehicles
  • slow and boring → PEDESTRIAN writing that's plodding, dull and unimaginative is often given this same label

Split at 'that’s': the road-safety sense on one side, the sniffy adjective on the other.

8 down STRONG SUIT

Healthy and fit? It may be one’s best feature (6,4)

Definition: one’s best feature · charade

  • Healthy → STRONG healthy = strong, in rude health
  • and → surface glue joining the two pieces
  • fit → SUIT a verb: clothes that suit you fit you

'Fit' is the trap — paired with 'healthy' it looks like the same idea twice, but it is the tailoring verb SUIT. The question mark flags the playful definition.

10 down ENDURANCE TEST

Misplaced utterances end long difficult trial (9,4)

Definition: long difficult trial · anagram

  • utterances end → UTTERANCESEND anagram fodder, 13 letters
  • Misplaced → anagram indicator

'Misplaced' opens the clue looking like a comment on the utterances; it is the instruction to rearrange them.

13 down STAGNATION

Men-only race showing lack of progress (10)

Definition: lack of progress · charade

  • Men-only → STAG a stag do is a men-only party
  • race → NATION a race = a nation, a people

The surface conjures a strange sporting event, but 'race' is the peoples sense, and the men-only affair is a stag night.

14 down STEADY HAND

Stable worker, one unlikely to get flustered (6,4)

Definition: one unlikely to get flustered · charade

  • Stable → STEADY stable = steady, firm
  • worker → HAND a hand = a worker, as in farmhand

The joke is that a 'stable worker' could literally be a stable hand — the same two crossword staples read as one job.

17 down ADMIRERS

A doctor’s concealing soft spot for lovers (8)

Definition: lovers · charade + container

  • A → A stands as itself
  • doctor’s → DRS DR plus the ’s — it wraps round MIRE
  • soft spot → MIRE a mire is literally a soft spot — boggy ground

'Soft spot' is the trap: it means literal soft ground, MIRE, which the doctor conceals. The affection sense of 'soft spot' belongs to the answer instead.

20 down PONY UP

Pay for father to take on big city university (4,2)

Definition: Pay · container

  • father → POP pop = father — he opens up to take the letters in
  • big city → NY NY = New York
  • university → U U = university, as in degree listings

POP 'takes on' — swallows — New York and its U; read together the insides are NYU, the famous university. To pony up is American slang for settling what you owe.

22 down LION

Predator consumes only half of live ones (4)

Definition: Predator · first letters

  • live → LI the front half of LIve
  • ones → ON the front half of ONes

'Only half of' applies to each word in turn, keeping the front of both — and the predator doing the consuming in the surface is the answer itself.

23 down METH

Ran into hospital to get drug (4)

Definition: drug · charade

  • Ran into → MET met = ran into, encountered
  • hospital → H H = hospital, as on road signs

'Ran into' is the past tense MET, and the road-sign H completes the street name for methamphetamine.
