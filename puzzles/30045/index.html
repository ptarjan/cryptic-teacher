Across

1 across ADAM AND EVE

Dave made an alternative biblical origin story (4,3,3)

Definition: biblical origin story · anagram

  • Dave made an → DAVEMADEAN anagram fodder, 10 letters
  • alternative → anagram indicator

Dave contributes nothing but his letters — 'alternative' does the shuffling. Two biblical names make the answer hard to see in such a modern, chatty surface.

6 across STOP

Component of post-operative check (4)

Definition: check · hidden word

  • post-operative → STOP hidden inside poST-OPerative

'Component of' flags a hidden word, and the hyphen is no barrier — hidden answers run straight across punctuation. 'Check' as in halt something.

9 across PROVENANCE

History of article found in French region (10)

Definition: History · container

  • article → AN the indefinite article
  • French region → PROVENCE AN drops inside it

Provence supplies the shell and the article drops in. Provenance is the history of an object's ownership — an auction-house word, which is why 'History' alone defines it.

10 across MEME

Widespread idea setter repeated (4)

Definition: Widespread idea · charade

  • setter → ME the setter referring to himself

'Setter' pointing at the setter himself is classic crossword self-reference: ME, said twice. A meme is exactly an idea that spreads.

12 across IN RETROSPECT

Restrict open representation during review (2,10)

Definition: during review · anagram

  • Restrict open → RESTRICTOPEN anagram fodder, 12 letters
  • representation → anagram indicator: a re-presentation of the letters

'Representation' earns its length as the anagram signal — a re-presentation of the fodder. 'During review' captures looking back at events once they are over.

15 across SPAGHETTO

Recreation area beside slum provides very small portion of pasta (9)

Definition: very small portion of pasta · charade

  • Recreation area → SPA a spa is a recreation area
  • slum → GHETTO a ghetto is a slum

The definition is the joke: spaghetto really is the Italian singular of spaghetti — one solitary strand, the smallest possible portion of pasta.

17 across MUG UP

Do some research for facelift? (3,2)

Definition: Do some research · charade

  • facelift? → MUGUP read it in two halves: mug = face, lifted = up

To mug up is British slang for cramming a subject. The question mark licenses splitting 'facelift' in two: a mug is slang for a face, and something lifted goes up.

18 across RETCH

Almost vomit from contents of secret chamber (5)

Definition: Almost vomit · hidden word

  • secret chamber → RETCH hidden across secRET CHamber

'Contents of' says look inside what follows, and the answer runs across the word break. To retch is to heave without quite succeeding — hence 'almost' vomit.

19 across OBSTACLES

Blocks former pupil’s heartless challenges (9)

Definition: Blocks · charade + deletion

  • former pupil’s → OBS OB = old boy, the crossword's former pupil, keeping his ’s
  • challenges → TACLES TACKLES with its heart (the K) removed

OB for old boy is a standard former pupil, and his possessive S is part of the build. 'Heartless' then strips the middle letter from TACKLES.

20 across AN ALL-TIME LOW

Limbo dancer’s record performance? It couldn’t be worse for some (2,3-4,3)

Definition: Limbo dancer’s record performance? · double definition

  • Limbo dancer’s record performance? → a limbo record is literally the lowest anyone has ever gone
  • It couldn’t be worse for some → the everyday phrase for rock bottom

Both halves define the same phrase: a limbo champion's record really is the lowest ever achieved, and rock bottom, for some, couldn't be worse. The question mark flags the literal reading.

24 across UNIT

Incompetent fellow quits squad (4)

Definition: squad · deletion

  • Incompetent → UNFIT incompetent = unfit
  • fellow → F F = fellow, as in academic titles like FRS

F for fellow comes from academic honours — FRS and friends. Send him away from the middle of UNFIT and the squad remains.

25 across ISAAC HAYES

I dismiss unclear vision articulated by soul pioneer (5,5)

Definition: soul pioneer · homophone

  • I dismiss → I SACK said aloud: ISAAC
  • unclear vision → HAZE said aloud: HAYES

'Articulated' asks you to say both halves aloud: 'I sack' and 'haze'. Isaac Hayes — the voice of Shaft — is the soul pioneer being spelled out.

26 across LEND

Advance! It’s part of command Nelson rejected (4)

Definition: Advance · hidden word + reversal

  • command Nelson → DNEL hidden across commanD NELson

The fragment hides across 'command Nelson' and 'rejected' sends it back — a backwards reading, which suits an across entry. 'Advance' means lend, as a bank advances money.

27 across BETTER HALF

Spouse felt breath getting uneven (6,4)

Definition: Spouse · anagram

  • felt breath → FELTBREATH anagram fodder, 10 letters
  • getting uneven → anagram indicator

'Getting uneven' shakes up the fodder. 'Better half' is the affectionate term for a spouse — a warm answer hiding in a worrying surface about laboured breathing.

1 down AMPS

Current measures while politician is inside (4)

Definition: Current measures · container

  • while → AS while = AS, the two-letter shell
  • politician → MP the usual Member of Parliament

AS meaning 'while' is a workhorse container shell — two letters that swallow almost anything — and MP is one of the first abbreviations every solver learns. 'Current' here is electrical, not up to date.

2 down ATOP

Queue said to follow these characters on the summit (4)

Definition: on the summit · homophone + cryptic definition

  • Queue said → 'queue' said aloud is the letter Q
  • to follow these characters → ATOP the characters Q follows in the alphabet are A to P — which spells the answer

Say 'queue' aloud and you have the letter Q, which comes straight after the characters A to P. Read the answer as 'A to P' and the alphabet joke lands.

3 down ACE IN THE HOLE

Champion, before successful putt, reveals secret edge (3,2,3,4)

Definition: secret edge · charade

  • Champion → ACE an ace is a champion
  • successful putt → INTHEHOLE where a successful putt leaves the ball

'Before' just sets the order: ACE, then where a holed putt ends up. The phrase itself comes from poker, not golf — the face-down card that gives you a hidden advantage.

4 down DRAPE

Cover beginnings of debate regarding act prohibiting euthanasia (5)

Definition: Cover · first letters

  • debate regarding act prohibiting euthanasia → DRAPE the beginnings — first letters — of the five words

'Beginnings of' points at the initial letters of the five words that follow. The sombre parliamentary surface is a decoy; the clue is a pure acrostic.

5 down VICARIOUS

Indirect service provider promises to pay (9)

Definition: Indirect · charade

  • service provider → VICAR the service is a church service, so its provider is a vicar
  • promises to pay → IOUS IOUs are written promises to pay

'Service provider' wants you thinking broadband; the service is in a church. Vicarious pleasure is felt indirectly, through someone else — hence the definition.

7 down THE SEAGULL

Drama created by one stealing chips (3,7)

Definition: Drama · double definition

  • Drama → Chekhov's 1896 play The Seagull
  • one stealing chips → the British seaside menace that swoops on your lunch

Chekhov's play — and the definite article works hard, because at the seaside the seagull really is the one stealing your chips.

8 down PRETTY PASS

Difficult spot with picturesque route through mountains (6,4)

Definition: Difficult spot · double definition

  • Difficult spot → 'come to a pretty pass' = reach a sorry state
  • picturesque route through mountains → a mountain pass that happens to be pretty

'Come to a pretty pass' is an old idiom for landing in a sorry state; a mountain pass can certainly be pretty. One phrase, two readings, no letters moved.

11 down ASK ME ANOTHER

One has market in chaos? I can’t answer that (3,2,7)

Definition: I can’t answer that · anagram

  • One has market → ONEHASMARKET anagram fodder, 12 letters
  • in chaos → anagram indicator

The definition is the whole exasperated phrase at the end — 'ask me another' is what you say when you can't answer. The question mark excuses the odd economics surface.

13 down USER MANUAL

Lama unsure about guidance it provides (4,6)

Definition: guidance it provides · anagram

  • Lama unsure → LAMAUNSURE anagram fodder, 10 letters
  • about → anagram indicator

'About' is the anagram signal, easy to dismiss as filler. The 'it' in the definition points at the answer itself: a user manual is exactly guidance it provides.

14 down CASTRATION

Actors’ share that ensures kids are excluded in future (10)

Definition: that ensures kids are excluded in future · charade

  • Actors’ → CAST the actors in a production
  • share → RATION a share = a ration

CAST plus RATION, with a pitch-black definition: 'kids' are offspring — including baby goats, where the operation is routine — so it guarantees none in future.

16 down TOOK ISSUE

Kidnapped but had disagreement (4,5)

Definition: Kidnapped · double definition

  • Kidnapped → issue = children in legal English, so kidnapping is taking someone's issue
  • had disagreement → the everyday sense: took issue with something

'Issue' meaning children survives mainly in legal English and in crosswords — worth memorising. The everyday sense of objecting shares exactly the same two words.

21 down EXALT

Praise plasticity of latex? (5)

Definition: Praise · anagram

  • latex? → LATEX anagram fodder, 5 letters
  • plasticity of → anagram indicator

'Plasticity of' is the anagram signal — latex is mouldable, which is why the surface reads so naturally. Five letters reshape into a word of praise.

22 down MYNA

Bird impersonator sounds lightweight to some (4)

Definition: Bird impersonator · homophone

  • lightweight → MINOR a lightweight concern is a minor one; said aloud it gives the bird

The myna is the great avian mimic, hence 'bird impersonator'. 'To some' is the setter being honest: only in non-rhotic accents does 'minor' sound quite like MYNA.

23 down USAF

It protects Americans caught in calamitous aftermath (4)

Definition: It protects Americans · hidden word

  • calamitous aftermath → USAF hidden across calamitoUS AFtermath

'Caught in' flags the hidden word, running across the break in calamitoUS AFtermath — and a surface about protection after calamity suits the United States Air Force nicely.
