Across

1 across SHAFT

Quiet at the back, Roger! (5)

Definition: Roger · charade

  • Quiet → SH SH is the noise you make to hush someone
  • at the back → AFT aft is the nautical word for towards the stern

The surface is a teacher hushing the back row, and 'Roger!' arrives looking like radio confirmation rather than a verb.

4 across CLEOPATRA

Queen with palace close to Ebbsfleet or Barking (9)

Definition: Queen · anagram + last letter

  • palace → PALACE anagram fodder
  • close to Ebbsfleet → T the last letter of EbbsfleeT
  • or → OR the word 'or' joins the fodder whole
  • Barking → anagram indicator: barking = barking mad

Ebbsfleet and Barking are both places on the London map, so the shuffling instruction sits camouflaged in the geography; barking = mad is the sense wanted.

9 across ODD COUPLE

Unlikely double act formed when poodle dances with short duck (3,6)

Definition: Unlikely double act · anagram + deletion

  • poodle → POODLE anagram fodder
  • short duck → DUC DUCK docked of its last letter
  • dances → anagram indicator

The Odd Couple is Neil Simon's play about two mismatched flatmates. A poodle dancing with a duck is itself an improbable pairing, which is the setter's joke.

10 across CARRY

Bear in horror film released from trap (5)

Definition: Bear · homophone

  • horror film → CARRY Carrie, the Stephen King horror film

Your trap is your mouth, so what is released from it is speech — the setter's roundabout way of saying 'spoken'. The film supplies the sound, not the spelling.

11 across TANGLED

Confused old German director's on set at last (7)

Definition: Confused · charade + last letter

  • set at last → T the last letter of seT
  • old German → ANGLE the Angles were a Germanic tribe, from Angeln
  • director → D d is the standard abbreviation for director

The Angles were a Germanic people before they were English, which is what 'old German' points at; and in an across clue 'on' puts a piece after the one it names.

12 across TOURISM

Travel to ancient city with one manuscript from the East (7)

Definition: Travel · charade + reversal

  • to → TO the preposition, taken as it stands
  • ancient city → UR Ur of the Chaldees, the crossword's default ancient city
  • one → I one is the Roman numeral I
  • manuscript → SM MS, the shortening of manuscript, coming the other way

UR and MS are both worth banking: the standard ancient city and the standard manuscript. An across entry running from the east is one read right to left.

13 across AMITY

A film screened by Bluebird's generating goodwill (5)

Definition: goodwill · charade + container

  • A → A the clue's own 'A' leads off
  • Bluebird's → MY Bluebird set this puzzle, so the setter's = my
  • film → IT It is a film title, and a two-letter one

Bluebird is this puzzle's setter, so 'Bluebird's' is simply MY — a setter pointing at themselves is standard practice. 'Screened' covers both showing a film and hiding something.

14 across ATONEMENT

One month camping following a repayment (9)

Definition: repayment · charade + container

  • a → A the clue's 'a', which everything else follows
  • camping → TENT camping means under canvas, so a TENT, prised open as T...ENT
  • One → ONE the word 'one' goes in whole
  • month → M m is the standard abbreviation for month

Camping puts you in a tent, and this tent is opened up to hold what goes inside it. The answer's own history is worth knowing: at-one-ment, being made one again.

16 across BOOKPLATE

Surprising announcement by King worried about revolutionary record label (9)

Definition: label · charade + container + reversal

  • Surprising announcement → BOO BOO is what you shout to startle someone
  • King → K K is the chess and card abbreviation for king
  • revolutionary record → PL an LP spun round
  • worried → ATE worried in the sense of 'what's eating you'

'Worried' = ATE is the sense in 'what's eating you', and a record is an LP that 'revolutionary' spins round. The surface promises a royal scandal instead.

18 across GEEKY

E key fixed by guy primarily interested in computers? (5)

Definition: interested in computers? · anagram + first letter

  • E key → EKEY anagram fodder
  • guy primarily → G the first letter of Guy
  • fixed → anagram indicator

'Fixed' means mended on the surface, where somebody is repairing a keyboard, and rearranged in the wordplay. The question mark admits the definition is a caricature.

19 across RUSH OUT

Hastily release Lulu single after group experiences regular losses (4,3)

Definition: Hastily release · charade + alternate letters

  • group → RU gRoUp keeping only its even letters
  • Lulu single → SHOUT Shout was Lulu's breakthrough hit

'Regular losses' is worth learning as an instruction to drop every other letter. Lulu's 'Shout' supplies the rest, and 'release' pretends to belong to the record too.

21 across ANNOYED

Anyone getting aggravated and dead angry (7)

Definition: angry · anagram

  • Anyone → ANYONE anagram fodder
  • dead → D d. is the standard abbreviation for died
  • aggravated → anagram indicator

'Aggravated' is the shuffling instruction and very nearly the definition as well, which is the setter having it both ways in one word.

23 across TETRA

Lawrence emptied trawler - one fish! (5)

Definition: fish · charade + outer letters

  • Lawrence → TE T. E. Lawrence, of Arabia, is the crossword's Lawrence
  • trawler → TR TrawleR emptied of everything between its ends
  • one → A one = a, as in one moment or a moment

Lawrence of Arabia's initials do duty for the whole man all over cryptic crosswords, and 'emptied' hollows a word out to its first and last letters.

24 across EIDERDOWN

On the radio, setter had a clue type getting blanket coverage (9)

Definition: blanket coverage · homophone + charade

  • setter had a → EIDER the setter is I, so this is 'I'd a'
  • clue type → DOWN clues come in two types, across and down

The setter is always 'I', which is the convention this depends on; and down is both a direction in the grid and the feathers the quilt is stuffed with.

25 across MANHATTAN

Isle of Man Tourist Trophy area overrun by Chinese people (9)

Definition: Isle · charade + container

  • Man → MAN the Isle of Man is MAN
  • Chinese people → HAN the Han are China's majority people
  • Tourist Trophy → TT the TT motorcycle races
  • area → A a is the standard abbreviation for area

The Isle of Man really does host the Tourist Trophy, so the opening reads as one continuous place name — and only its first word belongs to the definition.

26 across LAYER

Film actor quietly leaving (5)

Definition: Film · deletion

  • actor → PLAYER an actor is a player
  • quietly → P p is piano, quietly, in music — and it is the letter that goes

P for 'quietly' is the piano marking off a sheet of music, one of the first abbreviations to learn. Here 'film' is the thin coating, nothing cinematic.

Down

1 down SHOOT

Film part of Black Narcissus? (5)

Definition: Film · double definition

  • Film → the verb a director uses: to film a scene is to shoot it
  • part of Black Narcissus → the title hands you a flower, and a narcissus puts out shoots

Black Narcissus is a 1947 film, so the italic title looks like the whole point of the clue; the plant hiding inside it is what the second definition wants.

2 down ANDANTINO

An experienced martial artist can flay you slowly (9)

Definition: slowly · charade + middle letter

  • An → AN the clue's opening word, taken as it stands
  • experienced martial artist → DAN a dan is a black-belt grade, so its holder is a dan
  • can → TIN a can is a tin
  • you → O yOu skinned of its outside letters

A dan is the black-belt grade, so an experienced martial artist is a DAN; and 'flay', to take the skin off, is a neat way of saying strip a word's outer letters.

3 down TROLLEY

One's filled with food yet goes around snaffling bread (7)

Definition: One's filled with food · container + reversal

  • yet → TEY YET written the other way round
  • bread → ROLL a roll is a piece of bread

'Goes around' is a turn, not a shuffle — YET simply runs backwards. The surface is a supermarket sweep, with 'snaffling' passing itself off as theft.

4 down CUPID

People love getting shot by him (5)

Definition: People love getting shot by him · cryptic definition

  • People love getting shot → the surface reading is gunfire, which nobody could love — that jar is the joke
  • by him → the archer meant here fires arrows that cause love, so his victims welcome the wound

Cupid is the Roman god of love, drawn as a winged boy with a bow, and everything his arrow hits falls in love. There is no wordplay to find.

5 down ELECTRODE

Vote for Hull ensemble's lead conductor (9)

Definition: conductor · charade + first letter

  • Vote for → ELECT to vote for someone is to elect them
  • Hull → ROD Rod Hull, the comedian with the emu, supplies the missing first name
  • ensemble's lead → E the leading letter of Ensemble

A surname asking for its owner's first name is a standard Independent trick. Meanwhile the surface offers a Yorkshire choir and 'conductor' of the musical sort.

6 down PICTURE

Film old northerner with uniform on (7)

Definition: Film · charade

  • old northerner → PICT the Picts were the ancient people of northern Britain
  • uniform → U U is uniform in the NATO alphabet
  • on → RE re means on, as in a memo on expenses

Two abbreviations worth banking for good: U is 'uniform' in the NATO alphabet, and RE is the crossword's usual 'on'.

7 down TORSI

Sort out one's trunks (5)

Definition: trunks · anagram

  • Sort → SORT anagram fodder
  • one → I one is the Roman numeral I

The surface is a packing instruction for a beach holiday. 'Trunks' means bodies here, and TORSI is the Italian plural galleries prefer to torsos.

8 down ASYMMETRY

Lack of balance in upcoming TV awards defended by a judge (9)

Definition: Lack of balance · container + reversal

  • a → A the clue's own 'a', defending from the front
  • TV awards → SYMME the EMMYS, climbing the down entry
  • judge → TRY to try a case is to judge it

The Emmys are the television awards, and 'upcoming' is the right word for a reversal in a down entry. 'Judge' wants the courtroom verb, not the person.

13 down ARBORETUM

Wild boar turned quieter in garden (9)

Definition: garden · anagram + reversal

  • boar → ARBO BOAR with its letters loose
  • quieter → RETUM MUTER, meaning quieter, written backwards

The step that hides this one is hearing 'quieter' as the comparative MUTER, a word nobody says out loud. Boar and garden keep the surface rural.

14 down APARTMENT

Divorced blokes finally get Penthouse? (9)

Definition: Penthouse · charade + last letter

  • Divorced → APART a divorced couple live apart
  • blokes → MEN blokes are men
  • get → T the last letter of geT

'Divorced' looks like an instruction to split something up and is really just a synonym, which is the main misdirection here. The question mark flags a definition by example.

15 down EVERYBODY

Dotty, Brody and Nye discovered going after Eve, Tom, Dick and Harry (9)

Definition: Tom, Dick and Harry · charade + anagram + middle letter

  • Eve → EVE the name Eve, taken whole
  • Brody → BRODY anagram fodder
  • Nye discovered → Y nYe with its covering letters taken off

'Discovered' is the trick: not found out but dis-covered, its covering letters removed. Chief Brody and Nye Bevan keep the surface looking like a guest list.

17 down PRO RATA

Just over half of paratroopers punished proportionately (3,4)

Definition: proportionately · anagram + first letters

  • paratroopers → PARATRO the first seven letters of a twelve-letter word
  • punished → anagram indicator

'Just over half' is arithmetic rather than vagueness: paratroopers runs to twelve letters, so seven of them are the fodder. Count first, shuffle second.

18 down GENERAL

Universal Soldier (7)

Definition: Universal · double definition

  • Universal → general = applying to everyone, without exception
  • Soldier → the most senior soldier in an army is a general

Two words, two definitions and no wordplay whatever. The italics make the pair read as a film title, which is exactly what hides the split between them.

20 down SET ON

Determined boy conceals alien (3,2)

Definition: Determined · container

  • boy → SON a boy is somebody's son
  • alien → ET ET is the crossword's standard alien

ET for 'alien' is one of the most-used shorthands in cryptics, and 'boy' nearly always wants SON or a short first name.

21 down AUDEN

Poet with a bare head is depressed (5)

Definition: Poet · charade + cycling

  • a → A the clue's own 'a'
  • bare → UDEN NUDE means bare, and its leading N drops to the foot

In a down entry 'head is depressed' means the top letter is pushed to the bottom of the word — a rotation rather than a reversal, and a device worth recognising.

22 down DINER

One who eats at home, embarrassed to go about outside (5)

Definition: One who eats · container + reversal

  • embarrassed → DER RED, meaning embarrassed, turned round
  • at home → IN somebody at home is in

'At home' = IN and 'embarrassed' = RED are two of the most reliable short synonyms in cryptics; the surface makes the answer sound too shy to leave the house.
