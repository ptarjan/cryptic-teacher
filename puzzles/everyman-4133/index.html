Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,133

Setter: Everyman · Published: Sunday 4 January 2026 · Difficulty: Moderate (harder than 17% of the puzzles here) · Grid: 15×15

Across

1 across WALKING STICKS

Type of surgery, German, with jabs: they aid mobility (7,6)

Definition: they aid mobility · charade

  • Type of surgery → WALKIN in Britain a surgery is a doctor's practice, and a walk-in one takes patients who turn up without an appointment
  • German → G G is the standard one-letter abbreviation for German
  • jabs → STICKS to stick something into a person is to jab them

Everything before the colon is a scene at a health centre, jabs and all, which is why the German sits there so quietly as a single letter.

8 across FURLONG

Roll up! Not one old nag in bit of horse-racing (7)

Definition: bit of horse-racing · charade + deletion

  • Roll up! → FURL to furl a sail or a flag is to roll it up
  • old → O O is the usual abbreviation for old
  • nag → NAG a nag is a worn-out horse
  • one → A the letter that leaves: 'one' here is the article a, taken out of the horse

'Roll up!' is a fairground barker's shout, which is what makes the sentence hang together; it is an instruction to a sail instead.

9 across PARSNIP

Score and trim root vegetable (7)

Definition: root vegetable · charade

  • Score → PAR par is the score a golfer is expected to take at a hole
  • trim → SNIP to snip is to trim with a quick cut of the scissors

'Score and trim' reads as two things a cook does to a vegetable, which is precisely what keeps the two separate words from showing.

11 across LOSING IT

Snapping: 'Down with that thing!' (6,2)

Definition: Snapping · charade

  • Down → LOSING a side that is down in a match is behind on the scoreboard
  • that thing → IT the pronoun that stands in for a thing already pointed at

The quotation marks sell it as a protester's shout — a demand that something be got rid of — and a shout is read as one phrase, never as two pieces laid end to end.

12 across SOLAR

Eco option - wanting money, morals abandoned (5)

Definition: Eco option · deletion + anagram

  • money → M the letter that leaves: economists write the money supply as M, and crosswords take M for money
  • morals → ORALS six letters less that M leaves five, and those five are what gets shuffled

The surface is a character sketch of someone broke and unscrupulous, so the two instructions look like judgements. Both of them land on the single word between them.

14 across OILS

Art gets lubricated (4)

Definition: Art · double definition

  • Art → pigment ground in linseed, and by extension the pictures painted with it
  • gets lubricated → what you do to a hinge or a bicycle chain to stop it squeaking

Three words, and the likeliest reading of them is a bloke named Art getting drunk. Nothing in the clue announces that it is two definitions in a row.

15 across LIGHTENS UP

Huge splint, after combustion, cools down? (8,2)

Definition: cools down? · anagram

  • Huge splint → LIGHTENSUP ten letters between the two words, and ten in the answer

A splint here is not the surgical kind but the thin strip of wood used to carry a flame — which is what lets the middle of the clue sound like a description rather than an order.

17 across BEDFELLOWS

FBI agent yells, as Spooner tells associates (10)

Definition: associates · spoonerism

  • FBI agent → BED a fed is an agent of the US federal government; it gives up its F and takes the B coming the other way
  • yells → FELLOWS to bellow is to yell, and this word hands over its B in exchange for the F

Say the plain version out loud first — an agent bellowing — and the swap does the rest. The surface keeps both words in the same newsroom so neither gives itself away.

19 across PACT

Treaty very popular in the auditorium (4)

Definition: Treaty · homophone

  • very popular → PACT a show that is very popular plays to a packed house, and that word said aloud is written quite differently

'In the auditorium' looks like more of the picture of a full theatre. It is the instruction, and the picture is what keeps it invisible.

21 across AGING

Getting on with a drink: good (5)

Definition: Getting on · charade

  • a → A the article, taken as the single letter it is
  • drink → GIN gin is the crossword's default spirit, three letters and always to hand
  • good → G G is the standard abbreviation for good

British solvers may want the spelling with an E in the middle; the grid takes the shorter form, and both are current.

22 across IN EFFECT

15C - shivering, actually (2,6)

Definition: actually · anagram

  • 15C → INEFFECT spell the number out in letters and keep the C beside it: seven and one make the eight needed

'15C' looks like a cross-reference to another entry, and with 'shivering' next to it, like a temperature. It is neither: it is a number waiting to be written out in words.

25 across SWEAR BY

Trust son with organ, Times (5,2)

Definition: Trust · charade

  • son → S S is the family-tree abbreviation for son
  • with → W w with a stroke through it is the everyday shorthand for with
  • organ → EAR the ear is an organ of the body, not of a church or a newspaper
  • Times → BY in multiplication you say three by four, so 'times' is the word BY

The italics dress the last word up as the newspaper, and 'organ' is happy to be one too. Both belong to arithmetic and anatomy instead.

26 across OZEMPIC

Omnipresent, zeitgeisty, expensive medicine putatively inhibiting cuddliness, primarily? (7)

Definition: Omnipresent, zeitgeisty, expensive medicine putatively inhibiting cuddliness, primarily? · &lit + first letters

  • Omnipresent, zeitgeisty, expensive medicine putatively inhibiting cuddliness → OZEMPIC seven words, seven initials, taken in the order they are written

An &lit: the sentence describes the thing and spells it at the same time, and the run of adjectives that would be padding anywhere else is the whole mechanism.

27 across KNOCKED FOR SIX

Crazy kids reckon fox blown away (7,3,3)

Definition: blown away · anagram

  • kids reckon fox → KNOCKEDFORSIX thirteen letters across three words, and thirteen in the grid to fill

'Blown away' sits at the end looking like the fox's fate in a windy field, which is what stops it looking like a definition.

Down

2 down AEROSOL

Diffused a looser spray (7)

Definition: spray · anagram

  • a looser → AEROSOL seven letters in and seven out, the two Os keeping each other company

The trap is reading 'Diffused' as what the answer does rather than as an order given to the two words after it — a can does diffuse things, which is exactly why it sits first.

3 down KNOWN

Understood: some work now non-essential (5)

Definition: Understood · hidden word

  • work now non-essential → KNOWN five letters run without a break from the end of the first word, through the whole of the second and into the third

The colon makes the first word look like a curt reply, and 'non-essential' looks like an instruction to throw something away. Neither is doing any work beyond the surface.

4 down NEGLIGIBLE

Barely-there short nightgown covering one - bold bit of licentiousness (10)

Definition: Barely-there · container + deletion + first letter

  • nightgown → NEGLIGE a negligee is a filmy nightgown; docked of its final E it hands over seven letters
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one
  • bold → B B is the printer's mark for bold type
  • licentiousness → L the opening letter of the word, and nothing more of it

The joke is how close the two words are on the page: the nightgown and the answer share their first six letters, so the surface can describe the garment while the wordplay quietly builds something else.

5 down SAPS

Drains visible in rest areas in retreat (4)

Definition: Drains · reversal

  • visible in rest areas → SPAS what is on show at a place built for resting: a spa is a resort of baths and treatments

'Retreat' is doing two jobs at once — a health retreat is precisely the sort of place the first half of the clue describes, and the same word is the instruction.

6 down INRUSHES

Floods among the reeds (8)

Definition: Floods · charade

  • among → IN to be among a crowd is to be in it
  • the reeds → RUSHES rushes are the tall stiff marsh plants that grow where reeds do

Three words make one picture of a riverbank, and there is no indicator anywhere to break the spell. The whole trick is hearing the same phrase as two pieces instead of one.

7 down KINDLES

German child with the French books in here (7)

Definition: books in here · charade

  • German child → KIND Kind is the German for child
  • the French → LES les is French for 'the' when what follows is plural

Two languages in six words, and both are asking to be translated rather than pictured — the surface's small child on a French holiday never existed.

8 down FULL OF BEANS

Dashing, having knocked back some runners? (4,2,5)

Definition: Dashing · double definition

  • Dashing → lively and brimming with energy, the way you describe someone who bounds into a room
  • having knocked back some runners? → runner beans, the climbing green pods of every British allotment, are runners — and to knock a plateful back is to get it down you

'Knocked back' looks like an instruction to turn something round and 'some' like the start of a hidden word. Both belong to the pub-and-plate picture, and neither is a mechanism here.

10 down PERIPATETIC

Itinerant rashly precipitate (11)

Definition: Itinerant · anagram

  • precipitate → PERIPATETIC eleven letters out of eleven, two Is and two Ts among them to place

Both of the last two words mean hasty, so they read as one idea and neither looks like raw material. A near-synonym standing next to its own fodder is a favourite piece of cover.

13 down SHOWING OFF

Gosh! Now I will jump around, very loudly - doing this? (7,3)

Definition: doing this? · anagram

  • Gosh! Now I → GOSHNOWI eight letters, the exclamation included, handed straight over
  • very loudly → FF ff is the musician's fortissimo, the marking for very loud

A semi-&lit: the sentence being taken apart for its letters is also the behaviour being defined, so the speaker demonstrates the answer while spelling it.

16 down BERGERAC

Merrily deadheaded herbgrace in town of French literature? (8)

Definition: town of French literature? · deletion + anagram

  • herbgrace → HERBGRACE herb of grace is the old name for rue, run into one word here so that its letters can be used as raw material
  • deadheaded → H the letter that goes: deadheading a plant is cutting its head off, and the head here is the first letter of the word that follows

The whole first half reads as gardening, and 'herbgrace' looks like a plant to be translated into a shorter name. It is a bag of letters instead.

18 down DRIVE-IN

Almost in pieces, overcome by noise in cinema (5-2)

Definition: cinema · container + deletion

  • noise → DIN a din is a continuous racket, and this one opens up to take a passenger
  • in pieces → RIVE something in pieces is riven, and stopping a letter short of that leaves RIVE

'Noise in cinema' reads as a single complaint about other people's popcorn, which is what hides the fact that the last word alone is the definition.

20 down ADELPHI

Plays seen here in part of Philadelphia (7)

Definition: Plays seen here · hidden word

  • Philadelphia → ADELPHI the city's name carries seven consecutive letters that spell something else, though not from its start

The surface offers a neighbourhood in an American city, and American cities do have theatre districts — which is why the geography feels like the answer rather than the container.

23 down FREER

Jacques's one with energy rising increasingly, Frank (5)

Definition: increasingly, Frank · reversal

  • Jacques's one → FRERE the French nursery rhyme is Frere Jacques, so the one that belongs with the name is frere, French for brother

A French first name usually asks for the French word for whatever comes next. Here the name is half a nursery rhyme, and what the clue wants is the word that always precedes it.

24 down HYDE

On vacation - holiday - starts to detest Everyman's hideous alter ego (4)

Definition: hideous alter ego · charade + outer letters + first letters

  • holiday → HY hollowed out, the word leaves only its first and last letters
  • detest Everyman's → DE two words, two opening letters, in the order they are written

Two dashes chop the clue into three, so 'On vacation - holiday' reads as one idea about being away rather than as an instruction and the word it acts on. Everyman naming himself mid-clue is this setter's signature.
