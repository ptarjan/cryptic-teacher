7 across GINS

Spirits chant with wings the wrong way round (4)

Definition: Spirits · reversal + outer letters

  • chant → SING to chant is to sing, and it is this word whose two end letters change places

'Spirits' offers ghosts and the surface offers a choir; the drinks cabinet is where the answer actually lives.

8 across INDISCREET

Strangely iridescent rash (10)

Definition: rash · anagram

  • iridescent → IRIDESCENT ten letters, and the only ten on offer — the giveaway that they are the raw material

'rash' sits beside a word about shimmering skin, so it reads as the medical noun; it is the adjective meaning hasty.

9 across SNUG

Arms on the counter somewhere in pub (4)

Definition: somewhere in pub · reversal

  • Arms → GUNS arms in the military sense are guns, and it is these four letters that get turned

'Arms' wants to be a pub name — the Queen's Arms — which is the trap; here it is weaponry, and 'on the counter' does bar-top duty in the surface only.

10 across INTERMEZZO

Rooney's latest title with foreign team - one Zinedine Zidane oversaw originally (10)

Definition: Rooney's latest title · charade + first letters

  • foreign team → INTER Internazionale of Milan, the Italian club everyone shortens this way
  • one → ME 'one' can stand for the speaker, the sense behind 'looking after number one'
  • Zinedine Zidane → ZZ the footballer's two initials, which is also where the nickname Zizou comes from
  • oversaw → O the letter this verb begins with

Two famous number tens hold the surface together, but the Rooney who matters is Sally, and the title is her 2024 novel.

12 across ILLUSIONS

Tricks Methuselah will use shortly ... is one briefly taken in? (9)

Definition: Tricks · charade + deletion + container

  • Methuselah will → ILL the compiler of this puzzle signs himself Methuselah, so he is 'I', and 'I will' contracts
  • use → US the word docked of its final letter
  • is → IS two letters at face value, and they are about to be prised apart
  • one → ON the word with its tail cut off

Setters call themselves 'I' or 'me', so a pen name inside a clue is usually just a pronoun; the ellipsis is punctuation, not an instruction.

14 across SIREN

One of The Wailers on the road with The Police (5)

Definition: One of The Wailers on the road with The Police · cryptic definition

  • One of The Wailers → the capital letters push you towards Bob Marley's band; read small, a wailer is simply a thing that wails
  • on the road with The Police → the second band name does the same double duty — what travels on the roof of a patrol car is the thing making the noise

Two band names in one line make the surface read like a tour poster, and the capital letters are the whole of the misdirection.

16 across HET UP

Anxious sheep following that bloke (3,2)

Definition: Anxious · charade

  • that bloke → HE bloke is British for man, and the pronoun for one is two letters
  • sheep → TUP a tup is a ram, a male sheep — a farming word British setters reach for often

The surface is a field and a farmer; the answer is a mood, and the join between its two words falls in the middle of the animal.

18 across EBB

Back in saddle, a pair of bachelors go out (3)

Definition: go out · charade + last letter

  • saddle → E the letter this word finishes on
  • a pair of bachelors → BB B is the abbreviation for bachelor, as in BA and BSc, and a pair means two of them

'Back in the saddle' is a phrase the surface wants you to hear whole; the setter has split it, and 'Back in' is an instruction rather than idiom.

20 across BESET

Attack dog's half-cut after two drops of beer (5)

Definition: Attack · charade + first letters + deletion

  • two drops of beer → BE two drops taken off the top of the word beer
  • dog's → SET a setter is a breed of gundog, and half of six letters is three

'dog' in a crossword is very often a breed and very often a setter, which is also the word a compiler uses for themselves.

21 across SWEDE

Root vegetable, one of a group found among cabbages? (5)

Definition: Root vegetable · double definition

  • Root vegetable → SWEDE the big yellow-fleshed root boiled and mashed with carrot, called a neep in Scotland and a rutabaga in America
  • one of a group → SWEDE a person from the Scandinavian country whose capital is Stockholm — the same five letters, but with a capital at the front
  • found among cabbages → surface support for the first reading: the vegetable is a brassica, so it grows in the same beds as its cabbage cousins

The comma looks like it joins one description to a second description of the same thing; it is really the seam between a vegetable and a nationality.

23 across CHARACTER

Someone who's made-up about rare catch (9)

Definition: Someone who's made-up · anagram

  • rare catch → RARECATCH nine letters across two words, which is exactly the count the grid needs

'made-up' looks like the shuffling instruction, and that is the trap — it is doing definition work, and the small word after it tips the letters over.

25 across CASABLANCA

Spaniard's "house white" is port (10)

Definition: port · charade

  • house → CASA the Spanish word for a house
  • white → BLANCA the Spanish for white, in the feminine form the noun before it requires

The quotation marks make 'house white' a wine list, which is the misdirection; the two words are meant one at a time and in translation.

27 across MUSO

Player obsessed with technique knocked back whole band (4)

Definition: Player obsessed with technique · charade + reversal

  • whole → MUS the whole of something is its sum, and that word arrives here having been knocked over
  • band → O a band is a ring, and a ring is written as the letter shaped like one

'whole band' looks like one phrase about a group turning up complete; it is two separate words to translate, and only the first is turned.

28 across SPELLBOUND

Transported upon sled, somehow fitting every other bit of globe in (10)

Definition: Transported · anagram + alternate letters

  • upon sled → UPONSLED eight of the ten letters, in two small words the surface needs anyway
  • globe → LB skip the first letter and take the next, all the way along, and two of the five survive

'Transported' offers a ride on the sledge, which is why it sits next to the sled; it is the figurative sense of being carried away.

29 across INNS

Travel stops Nordic people missing their capital (4)

Definition: Travel stops · deletion

  • Nordic people → FINNS the people of Finland, whose five letters are one too many
  • their capital → F a capital letter is the big one a proper noun begins with, and the surface offers Helsinki to distract you

A country's capital is a city; a people's capital, printed, is the letter at the front. The clue leans on both senses at once.

  • cancel → ANNUL to cancel an order is to annul it, and this word opens up to take a letter
  • article → A the indefinite article is a single letter

'dated' pulls towards old-fashioned, which is the trap; here it is literal, a book that carries a year on its cover.

3 down BIDETS

Bathroom facilities spoiled bedsit (6)

Definition: Bathroom facilities · anagram

  • bedsit → BEDSIT a bedsit is a British single rented room with the bed and the living in it, and its six letters are the raw material

The surface sells a wrecked flat, which is why the six letters read as somewhere to live rather than as plumbing.

4 down SCAMPS

Swindles bringing in very little money for troublemakers (6)

Definition: troublemakers · container

  • Swindles → SCAMS a swindle is a scam, and the plural opens up between its last two letters
  • very little money → P p is a penny, the smallest British coin, written after the figure as in 50p

'bringing in' reads as income in the surface, which is the misdirection: it is an instruction about position, not about earnings.

5 down SEIZURES

Unknown uniform seen in upsetting programme's medical episodes (8)

Definition: medical episodes · reversal + container

  • programme → SEIRES a television programme is a series, and here it arrives tipped over
  • Unknown → Z algebra calls an unknown quantity x, y or z
  • uniform → U U is uniform in the NATO alphabet that radio operators spell with

Three conventions stack up in six words: the algebraic unknown, the radio alphabet, and a programme meaning a run of episodes.

6 down OTTO

Old German chancellor perhaps unaffected by revolution (4)

Definition: Old German chancellor perhaps · palindrome

  • Old German chancellor perhaps → 'perhaps' marks a definition by example: one particular holder of the office, given by his first name rather than the surname everyone quotes
  • unaffected by revolution → this is the whole of the wordplay — the letters must read the same in both directions, which over four of them means the outer pair and the inner pair each match

Bismarck, first chancellor of a united Germany, is the man being pointed at, and it is his forename the grid wants.

7 down GASLIGHTS

Woman from 25A doing a turn during gig that is occasionally lacking tricks (9)

Definition: tricks · container + reversal + alternate letters

  • gig that is → GGHTS run the three words together and keep the first letter and every other one after it
  • Woman from 25A → ASLI the woman at the centre of the entry at 25 across is Ilsa, and she is turned round here

A clue pointing at another entry has to wait for that entry: 25 across supplies a name, and the rest of the letters come from words you can see.

11 down RSVP

Request from host exhausted religious viewership (1,1,1,1)

Definition: Request from host · charade + outer letters

  • religious → RS the two letters left at the ends once the middle is gone
  • viewership → VP the same treatment on the longer word: outside letters kept, everything between them dropped

The enumeration is the tell: four separate single letters means initials, and an invitation is the only set of four a host sends out.

13 down ODE

Poem's outstanding when read aloud (3)

Definition: Poem · homophone

  • outstanding → ODE a bill still outstanding is one still owed, and saying that word gives a different spelling of the same sound

'when' does the joining and 'outstanding' is the only word doing any wordplay, which is why a three-letter answer needs so little of the clue.

15 down NOTORIOUS

No 10 tears into doughnut with bad reputation (9)

Definition: with bad reputation · charade + container

  • No → NO the two letters as printed, standing at the front
  • doughnut → TORUS a doughnut is a torus, the ring shape a circle makes when spun about an outside axis
  • 10 → IO written in figures, ten is a one and a nought, and those two shapes read as letters

'No 10' reads as Downing Street, the Prime Minister's address, which is the trap; the two characters are being taken at face value instead.

17 down THE ASHES

Some seethe as he strokes trophy (3,5)

Definition: trophy · hidden word

  • seethe as he strokes → THE ASHES run the four words together and eight consecutive letters span them, starting inside the first word

The Ashes is the little urn England and Australia play their cricket series for, named after a mock obituary for English cricket printed in 1882.

19 down BEA

A certain lady smiles when leaving M&S (3)

Definition: A certain lady · deletion

  • smiles → BEAMS someone who smiles beams, which is the word to work on
  • M&S → MS the initials of Marks & Spencer, the British high-street chain, and the two letters that depart

A three-letter woman is nearly always a name, and this one is short for Beatrice; the shop is real, which is what holds the surface together.

20 down BRAG

Stuck-up old Stockholm-born actress failing to finish show off (4)

Definition: show off · deletion + reversal

  • old Stockholm-born actress → GARB Greta Garbo, the Swedish star of silent and early sound films, born in Stockholm — her surname arrives a letter short

'Stuck-up' does two jobs at once: haughty in the surface, and an instruction about which way a down entry is read.

22 down EMBALM

Make mummy different meal when doctor intervenes (6)

Definition: Make mummy · anagram + container

  • meal → EALM the four letters of the word, reordered
  • doctor → MB MB is Bachelor of Medicine, the degree behind the title, and a standard crossword shorthand

'Make mummy' reads as a family joke and is meant literally: the process named is the one that turns a body into one.

23 down CRAYON

Wax cylinder working after century, some hope? (6)

Definition: Wax cylinder · charade

  • century → C C is a hundred, both the Roman numeral and the cricketer's hundred runs
  • some hope? → RAY a ray of hope is the smallest amount of it anyone can claim
  • working → ON a machine that is working has been switched on

'working after century' invites you to put the switched-on word straight behind the hundred; it goes behind both other pieces, which the comma is quietly saying.

24 down TRUANT

Someone badly missing school days (6)

Definition: Someone badly missing school days · cryptic definition

  • badly missing → read as longing: to miss something badly is to pine for it, which is the nostalgic grown-up the surface offers
  • school days → read as absence: the same verb simply means not being there, and the days are what is being skipped

The whole clue turns on one verb: 'missing' is either pining for something or plainly not being present, and only the second reading names a person.

25 down CASH IN ON

After husband's in a better place, new squeeze? (4,2,2)

Definition: squeeze · container + charade

  • a better place → CASINO a better is someone who bets, and this is the place they go to do it
  • husband → H H is the standard abbreviation for husband, the one used on family trees
  • new → N N is the usual abbreviation for new

'a better place' reads as heaven, which is the trap: 'better' here is one who bets, and the widow the surface conjures is doing nothing else.

26 down CENT

Penny caught bouquet (4)

Definition: Penny · homophone

  • bouquet → CENT a wine's bouquet is its smell, and the word for that smell said aloud loses nothing but a letter on the page

'bouquet' is a wine's nose here rather than a bunch of flowers, and 'Penny' is a woman's name in the surface and a coin in the answer.
