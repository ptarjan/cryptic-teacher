Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,860

Across

1 across SOB STORY

Help Conservative to hide billions, moving account (3,5)

Definition: moving account · container + charade

  • Help → SOS SOS is the distress call, the signal that means send help
  • billions → B b is the everyday shorthand for a billion, as in a company valued at £5b
  • Conservative → TORY a Tory is a member of the Conservative party in Britain

The surface has a politician quietly salting money away. 'Moving' is the trap: it looks like a shuffling instruction and belongs to the definition, where it means affecting.

5 across EGRESS

Departure for one on ship (6)

Definition: Departure · charade

  • for one → EG e.g. is short for the Latin exempli gratia, which is what 'for one' announces
  • on → RE a memo headed 're pay' is about pay, and a row on pay is the same row
  • ship → SS SS is the prefix on a steamship's name

Someone leaving on a boat is the picture, and there is nothing in it: all three words are abbreviations laid end to end, none of them meaning what the sentence says.

10 across HERMITAGE

Remote location for one affected their game (9)

Definition: Remote location for one · anagram

  • their game → THEIRGAME these two words hand over their nine letters to be rearranged

The surface sounds like a shooting estate someone's hobby has ruined. 'For one' is not counting anything — it is pointing at the single occupant the answer implies.

11 across OUTRE

Oddball dismissed seconds from predicted retirement (5)

Definition: Oddball · charade + second letters

  • dismissed → OUT a batsman given out has been dismissed, and a sacked employee is out too
  • predicted retirement → RE the second letter of each word in turn, in the order the clue gives them

Cricket runs under the whole surface — a player dismissed, a retirement forecast — and neither word survives it. One is a synonym, the other a pair of letters.

12 across NOEL

Carol never felt unlimited (4)

Definition: Carol · charade + middle letters

  • never → NO 'never!' in speech is an emphatic refusal, the same answer said harder
  • felt → EL the two letters left standing once the outer pair go

'Carol' looks like a woman's name in a sentence about someone who never felt free. It is the definition, and the answer is the French word every carol sheet shouts.

13 across VITAL SPARK

Trace of life left in Sackville-West's grand garden? (5,5)

Definition: Trace of life · container + charade

  • Sackville-West's → VITAS Vita Sackville-West, the writer who made the garden at Sissinghurst, with the clue's possessive s attached
  • left → L L is the map and stage-direction abbreviation for left
  • grand garden? → PARK a park is a garden on the grand scale, laid out around a country house

Sissinghurst, the Kent garden Vita Sackville-West made famous, is the picture the surface paints. The clue wants only her first name — and the possessive s that comes with it.

15 across DALEY THOMPSON

Running, shot and pole? My hero (5,8)

Definition: hero · anagram

  • shot and pole? My → SHOTANDPOLEMY the italicised 'and' is one of the words to be used rather than a joiner, so all four hand over their letters

Read straight, running, shot and pole are three decathlon events, which is why the sentence holds together. The first of them is really the instruction.

18 across PORTLAND STONE

Builder's facing police officer, 100 mph in left lane (8,5)

Definition: Builder's facing · charade + container

  • left → PORT port is the left-hand side of a ship as you face the bow
  • lane → LANE the word is taken whole and then broken open near its end to admit what follows
  • police officer → DS DS is a detective sergeant in the British police
  • 100 mph → TON doing a ton is British slang for a hundred miles an hour

The surface is a speeding driver watching a police car in the mirror. 'Facing' is the builders' word for the outer skin of a wall, not the way anyone is pointing.

22 across CHATTERBOX

Prepare to deep-fry Mars bars for Spooner, whose utterances keep coming (10)

Definition: whose utterances keep coming · spoonerism

  • Prepare to deep-fry → CHATTER to deep-fry anything in a chip shop you first coat it in batter, and the B at the front is then traded away
  • Mars bars → BOX Mars bars are chocs, and the CH that opens that word moves across to take the B's place

The deep-fried Mars bar is a Scottish chip-shop legend, which is the only reason battering chocs is a sentence anyone would write.

24 across IDEA

Bride arranged the setting for fantasy (4)

Definition: fantasy · hidden word

  • Bride arranged → IDEA four consecutive letters run across the join between these two words

'Arranged' is exactly the word that makes a solver start rearranging, and nothing here is to be rearranged: the letters are wanted just as they already stand.

26 across LIMBO

Book to be collected by car, where you're put on hold? (5)

Definition: where you're put on hold? · container

  • Book → B B is the standard abbreviation for book
  • car → LIMO a limo is the long hired car of airport pickups and weddings

The question mark admits the definition is a picture rather than a dictionary entry: suspended, waiting, neither one thing nor the other.

27 across ASTRONAUT

Since fish nets unavailable, one loses weight on ship (9)

Definition: one loses weight on ship · charade + container

  • Since → AS since and as both mean because
  • fish → TROUT the trout is the freshwater fish of every angling picture
  • unavailable → NA n/a is what a form prints where something is not available

'Ship' is a spacecraft and 'loses weight' is free fall rather than dieting, so the definition is a small joke that only lands once the answer is in.

28 across DANISH

Return wrong filling delivered for pastry (6)

Definition: pastry · container + reversal

  • delivered → HAD a woman who had a baby delivered it, and the two verbs are interchangeable there
  • wrong → SIN a sin is a wrong, the moral sort

'Filling' looks like part of the pastry and is really an instruction, and once the letters are assembled the whole lot has to be read the other way round.

29 across INFANTRY

Foot part of insect eaten by cook (8)

Definition: Foot · charade + container

  • part of → IN to be part of something is to be in it, and the little word is taken at face value
  • cook → FRY to fry is to cook in hot fat
  • insect → ANT the ant is the crossword's insect of choice, and a hard-working one

'Part of' is doing letter work rather than joining the words either side of it, which is easy to read straight past on the way to the insect.

Down

1 down SPHINX

Species, cross including head of human and every other bit of lion (6)

Definition: Species, cross including head of human and every other bit of lion · &lit + charade + container + first letter + alternate letters

  • Species → SP sp. is the biologist's abbreviation, printed after a genus when the species is not named
  • human → H the head of the word is its first letter, and that is all the clue takes
  • lion → IN counting every other letter from the second leaves two of the four
  • cross → X X is a cross — the mark in a ballot box or at the foot of a letter

An all-in-one. Read as instructions the words hand over four pieces; read straight they describe the creature guarding the Giza pyramids, human above and lion below.

2 down BARTENDER

Local worker saving money (9)

Definition: Local worker · charade

  • saving → BAR saving and barring both mean leaving something out — barring accidents, saving your blushes
  • money → TENDER legal tender is the money a creditor is obliged to accept

Neither word is doing what the surface suggests: one is an old preposition meaning except, the other the legal term for cash. Nobody in this clue is economising.

3 down TWIG

Stick the rug down in Ilkley? (4)

Definition: Stick · charade

  • the → T Yorkshire drops everything but the opening letter, writing t' where the rest of the country writes the word out
  • rug → WIG a rug is slang for a hairpiece

The surface is somebody laying a rug flat on the floor. Ilkley Moor is the Yorkshire landmark of the county's best-known song, which is what makes the accent fair.

4 down REALITY

Try a lie to distort truth (7)

Definition: truth · anagram

  • Try a lie → TRYALIE these three words hand over their seven letters as raw material

The surface is advice to a witness in the box. 'Truth' sits at the end where a solver expects more wordplay, and it is the definition instead.

6 down GROUSE MOOR

Complain again, hearing that firing range after August 12th? (6,4)

Definition: firing range after August 12th? · charade + homophone

  • Complain → GROUSE to grouse is to grumble on and on about something
  • again → MOOR as much again is as much more, and that little word said aloud is spelt quite differently on the page

'Firing range' pulls towards a rifle butt at an army camp. The date is what redirects it to a hillside: shooting, not soldiering.

7 down EXTRA

Abandoned lover with skill climbing on top (5)

Definition: on top · charade + reversal

  • Abandoned lover → EX your ex is the partner you are no longer with
  • skill → TRA skill is art, and the climb sends those three letters back the way they came

An abandoned lover and a skill make a romantic picture, and 'on top' looks like part of it. It is the definition, sitting where the wordplay seems to be still running.

8 down SPEAKING

Quietly interrupting naval chief, talk (8)

Definition: talk · container

  • naval chief → SEAKING a sea king was a Norse chief who commanded ships, the raiding captain of the sagas
  • Quietly → P p is the musician's mark for playing softly, from the Italian piano

'Naval chief' looks as though it wants a rank — an admiral, a first sea lord. It wants two ordinary words run together instead, and no navy is involved.

9 down REHASH

Work over, what remains after term for professor? (6)

Definition: Work over · charade + last letter

  • professor? → R the word stops on this letter, and the clue wants nothing else from it
  • what → EH 'eh?' and 'what?' are the same request to have something said again
  • remains → ASH what a fire leaves behind is ash

The surface is an academic joke about end-of-term leftovers, and 'term' is not the university's. It is the older sense: the limit, the point at which a thing stops.

14 down ULULATIONS

Eurovision winner, upset country's losing lead, wails (10)

Definition: wails · reversal + charade + deletion

  • Eurovision winner → ULUL the Scottish singer who tied for the 1969 Eurovision win with Boom Bang-a-Bang has a four-letter stage name, here tipped over
  • country's → ATIONS a country is a nation, the possessive s comes along too, and then the front letter goes

Lulu won Eurovision for Britain in 1969, which is the fact the clue leans on. 'Upset' is not a shuffling instruction here but an order to turn a piece over.

16 down STEADFAST

Constant force cracks nuts as stated (9)

Definition: Constant · anagram

  • force → F F is the physics symbol for force, as in F = ma
  • as stated → ASSTATED these two words give up their eight letters to join the F in the mix

'As stated' reads as an instruction to write down what you hear, and that is the trap: the two words are the raw letters, not a sound to be copied.

17 down SPECKLED

Spotty son managed to bag kiss (8)

Definition: Spotty · charade + container

  • son → S S is the abbreviation for son in family records and genealogies
  • kiss → PECK a peck is a quick kiss on the cheek
  • managed → LED whoever managed the team led it

'Managed to bag' reads as one phrase meaning succeeded in getting. It is two separate instructions, and pulling them apart is the whole job.

19 down NORWAY

Land with many inlets, as if cut by river (6)

Definition: Land with many inlets · container

  • as if → NOWAY 'as if!' is the same sarcastic refusal to believe a thing as the two words it stands in for
  • river → R R is the map-maker's abbreviation for river

'Cut by river' looks as if it wants a river's name — the Exe and the Po are crossword regulars. It wants the single letter that maps use.

20 down SHORTEN

Dock's our standard base behind seafront, mostly (7)

Definition: Dock · charade + deletion

  • seafront → SHOR the seafront is the shore, with its last letter dropped
  • our standard base → TEN we count in base ten, which is what decimal means

'Dock' is the verb — you dock a tail or a wage — rather than a harbour, and 'base' is arithmetic rather than military. Both misreadings are the setter's doing.

21 down PALTRY

Mean person you'd be pleased to see go (6)

Definition: Mean · charade

  • person you'd be pleased to see → PAL a pal is a friend, and a friend is who you are pleased to see
  • go → TRY have a go at it and have a try at it are the same invitation

The clue reads as one sentence about somebody you would rather see the back of. It splits after 'see', and the last word is a noun, not the verb it looks like.

23 down ADMIN

African dictator smuggling diamonds, mundane business (5)

Definition: mundane business · container

  • African dictator → AMIN Idi Amin seized power in Uganda in 1971 and ruled it for eight years
  • diamonds → D D is the card player's abbreviation for the diamonds suit

'Business' is not commerce here but the day's chores, and the smuggled gems of the surface leave nothing behind but a single letter.

25 down SOFA

Large item from IKEA perhaps unfinished until now (4)

Definition: Large item from IKEA perhaps · deletion

  • until now → SOFA the two-word phrase meaning up to this point, with its last letter left off

'Until now' is SO FAR, and the clue wants it a letter short. IKEA is the Swedish flat-pack chain, so 'perhaps' marks a definition by example.
