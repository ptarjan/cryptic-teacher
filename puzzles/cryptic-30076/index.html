Across

1 across BESPOKE

Customized bicycle’s hollow wheel feature (7)

Definition: Customized · charade + outer letters

  • bicycle’s → BE BICYCLE emptied out keeps only its first and last letters
  • wheel feature → SPOKE a spoke is the wire running from a wheel's hub out to the rim

Bicycles and spokes paint one picture; the answer belongs to a different trade altogether, and that gap between tailoring and cycling is the whole of the misdirection.

5 across DISABLE

Located outside building’s entrance, Ladies sadly put out of action (7)

Definition: put out of action · anagram + container + first letter

  • Ladies → LADIES six letters to rearrange, and they close round the single letter
  • building’s → B the way into BUILDING is its opening letter

Three instructions are packed into one smooth sentence about people waiting outside a door, which is why nothing in it sounds like an instruction at all.

9 across BURNT OFFERINGS

Fetches round container with sweets (not drugs) as sacrifices (5,9)

Definition: sacrifices · container + charade + deletion

  • Fetches → BRINGS to fetch is to bring; BRINGS splits after its B to take the rest inside
  • container → URN an urn is a container
  • sweets → TOFFE the sweet is a TOFFEE, with an E taken out of it

The answer runs on into 10 across. In the kitchen sense the phrase means a ruined dinner, which is the picture the surface is quietly offering you.

11 across SEMANTICS

A meaningful area of study (9)

Definition: A meaningful area of study · cryptic definition

  • meaningful → the reading the surface pushes: a subject worth your time, one that matters
  • A meaningful area of study → the reading the setter means: a field whose entire subject matter is meaning itself

There is no separable wordplay to find here — one sentence, read two ways, and the pivot is the word 'meaningful'.

12 across PALMA

Use sleight of hand with ace? Capital! (5)

Definition: Capital · charade

  • Use sleight of hand → PALM the verb for concealing a small object in your hand, as a magician does with a card
  • ace → A A is the ace on a playing card

The exclamation mark sells 'Capital!' as a cry of approval, which is the misdirection: it is a place, and the ace is a card, not praise.

13 across TULIP

Pull it out to eject large bulb (5)

Definition: bulb · anagram + deletion

  • Pull it → PULIT PULL IT once an L has gone — five letters to rearrange
  • large → L L is the clothing-size abbreviation for large, and it is the letter thrown out

The whole clue reads as advice about lifting bulbs in autumn, which is why the anagram hides so well: nothing in it sounds addressed to a solver.

15 across TITLE ROLE

Championship register reportedly taken by lead player? (5,4)

Definition: taken by lead player? · charade + homophone

  • Championship → TITLE the championship a boxer holds is the title
  • register → ROLE a register of names is a ROLL — as in roll-call — and said aloud that is the part an actor plays

Two very different registers collide: the boxing kind and the roll-call kind. Only one of them is written the way you hear it.

18 across STREETCAR

Vehicle gives way, getting around a river (9)

Definition: Vehicle · charade

  • way → STREET a street is a way through a town
  • around → C c. is circa, the historian's 'around' in front of a date
  • a → A the clue's own article, taken straight into the answer
  • river → R R is the map abbreviation for river

C for circa is small change every solver needs — 'around' before a date — and here it hides behind a car swerving round a bend.

19 across SATYR

Was an MP your heartless lecher? (5)

Definition: lecher · charade + deletion

  • Was an MP → SAT an MP sits in the House, so one who was an MP sat
  • your → YR YOUR with its middle OU taken out — and YR is the standard abbreviation anyway

'Sitting' for holding a seat in Parliament is a convention that turns up constantly, and here the question mark lets the whole thing pose as gossip.

21 across OXFAM

Organisation is the essence of paradox, famously (5)

Definition: Organisation · hidden word

  • paradox, famously → OXFAM the letters run consecutively across the join in 'paradox, famously', spanning the end of one word and the start of the next

'Famously' is perfect cover — it makes the sentence sound like a remark about a well-known paradox, when its only job is to finish the buried letters.

23 across BOOKSHELF

Reserve: quiet little creature’s novel resting place (9)

Definition: novel resting place · charade

  • Reserve → BOOK to reserve a table is to book it
  • quiet → SH SH is the noise you make when you want quiet
  • little creature → ELF an elf is the storybook little creature

Every word of the surface pulls towards a fairy tale, and 'novel' is the hinge: an adjective meaning new as you read it, a noun by the time you solve it.

25 across SPECTATOR SPORT

Carpets, top sort, woven for Rugby? (9,5)

Definition: Rugby? · anagram

  • Carpets, top sort → CARPETSTOPSORT anagram fodder, fourteen letters
  • woven → anagram indicator

Carpets really are woven, so the instruction sounds like part of the sales patter — nothing in the surface asks the solver to do anything.

27 across REDUCER

Casanova gets a new top for chemical solution (7)

Definition: chemical solution · substitution

  • Casanova → SEDUCER a Casanova is a seducer — the word the swap is made in
  • a new top → R R moves in as the opening letter; the definition and the crossing lights are what pin down which letter is wanted

A photographer's reducer is a bath that thins a negative down, so the definition is chemistry rather than arithmetic, and the ladies' man is only one letter away.

28 across PAINFUL

Disagreeable one having fun touring in China (7)

Definition: Disagreeable · container + charade + cycling

  • China → PAL china plate is Cockney rhyming slang for mate, so a china is a pal; it splits after PA
  • one → I I is the Roman numeral for one
  • fun → NFU FUN sent round a circuit: the N leaves the end and rejoins at the front

The surface sounds like a bad holiday, and the trick is that China here is a person rather than a country.

1 down BABY-SIT

Tabby is sickly – mind the mites (4-3)

Definition: mind the mites · anagram

  • Tabby is → TABBYIS anagram fodder, seven letters
  • sickly → anagram indicator

The surface is a poorly cat and a flea problem, which is why the definition reads as pest control rather than childcare. The enumeration 4-3 tells you it is hyphenated.

2 down SCRAMBLER

Communication made meaningless by this climber (9)

Definition: Communication made meaningless by this · double definition

  • Communication made meaningless by this → SCRAMBLER the signals-room device that turns speech into noise for anyone listening in
  • climber → SCRAMBLER the same word for someone working up rough ground on hands and feet

Neither half is a metaphor — both are plain, ordinary uses of one word — and the clue holds together because a signal and a hillside sit so oddly in one sentence.

3 down OFTEN

A lot of figure (5)

Definition: A lot · charade

  • of → OF the word is lifted straight out of the clue, letters and all
  • figure → TEN a figure is a numeral, and this one is spelt out

'A lot of' is a single phrase in the surface, and the entire trick is cutting it after 'lot' so that 'of' stops being a preposition and starts being two letters.

4 down EGOTISTIC

Foreign cities limiting energy and temperature in vain (9)

Definition: vain · anagram + container

  • cities → CITIES six letters to rearrange; they come out as E...ISTIC, opening after the E
  • energy → GO go is energy — the get-up-and-go sense
  • temperature → T T is the standard abbreviation for temperature
  • Foreign → anagram indicator

Two conventions worth banking here: 'go' for energy and T for temperature. The surface promises a holiday abroad and delivers a character flaw.

5 down DAFFS

Fancy backing with fibrous edges for cut flowers (5)

Definition: cut flowers · reversal + charade + outer letters

  • Fancy → DAF a fancy is a FAD, turned end for end
  • fibrous → FS the outermost letters of FIBROUS, in the order they stand

The definition is quietly doing two jobs: daffodils really are sold as cut flowers, and the word itself has been cut down to its first syllable.

6 down STRAPLESS

How disciplinarians show restraint describing a dress? (9)

Definition: describing a dress · charade

  • How → surface only — the question word that turns the wordplay into a riddle
  • disciplinarians → STRAP the strap is the disciplinarian's instrument
  • show restraint → LESS to show restraint is to do the thing less

The joke turns on 'restraint': a strap is one, and using it less is showing some. Two senses of the same idea, a sentence apart.

7 down BANAL

Stock bar with drink – 1/3 off (5)

Definition: Stock · charade + deletion

  • bar → BAN to bar something is to ban it
  • drink → AL the drink is ALE, and a third of it — the final E — comes away

A bar and a drink make one picture and neither word means what it looks like: the bar is a prohibition and the drink is only two-thirds present.

8 down ENSNARE

Net Tennyson carried regularly (7)

Definition: Net · alternate letters

  • Tennyson carried → ENSNARE every second letter of tEnNySoNcArRiEd

Alfred, Lord Tennyson has nothing to do with the answer; his name is simply a convenient run of letters, and 'carried' is there to lengthen it.

14 down PNEUMATIC

Blown up – impact spread over northern Brussels (9)

Definition: Blown up · anagram

  • impact → IMPACT six of the nine letters
  • northern → N N is the map abbreviation for northern
  • Brussels → EU Brussels is the seat of the EU, so the city stands for those two letters
  • spread over → anagram indicator

Brussels for EU is worth banking: a city standing in for the institution it houses is a habit setters use constantly, and the surface here sells it as news.

16 down TURBOPROP

Mixed up rotor with BP engine (9)

Definition: engine · anagram

  • up → UP two of the letters to be rearranged, not part of the instruction
  • rotor → ROTOR five more letters for the mix
  • BP → BP the oil company's initials, taken at face value
  • Mixed → anagram indicator

The trap is 'Mixed up', which reads as one phrase. Only the first word gives the instruction; the second is material, and a word being shuffled cannot also do the shuffling.

17 down ON THE HOOF

Moving position of horseshoe? (2,3,4)

Definition: Moving · double definition

  • Moving → ON THE HOOF doing something this way means doing it while on the move, improvising as you go
  • position of horseshoe? → ON THE HOOF read literally: a farrier nails the shoe to the horse's foot, and that is where it sits

Both halves look equine until you notice the first one isn't: the phrase left the stable centuries ago and now means improvising as you go along.

18 down SCOUSER

Northwesterner has to rummage around Kent etc (7)

Definition: Northwesterner · container

  • rummage → SCOUR to scour a drawer is to rummage through it
  • Kent etc → SE Kent and its neighbours are the South East, and SE is how a map writes it

The surface has somebody ransacking the Home Counties while the answer lives two hundred miles away, and the pivot is Kent standing for a compass point.

20 down REFUTAL

Denial of tearful drunk (7)

Definition: Denial · anagram

  • tearful → TEARFUL anagram fodder, seven letters
  • drunk → anagram indicator

The hard part is trusting the word exists: it is the rarer sibling of 'refutation', and once you accept it the letters were never in doubt.

22 down FREUD

Dispute about queen’s dream interpreter (5)

Definition: dream interpreter · container

  • Dispute → FEUD a dispute is a feud, and it opens after its F
  • queen → R R is Regina, the Latin queen who signs royal documents

R for Regina is small change in cryptics — queen, king or monarch each buys you one letter — and the surface spends it on a royal quarrel.

23 down BITER

‘Beer time’s off!’ one snaps (5)

Definition: one snaps · deletion

  • Beer → BITTER bitter is the beer, and it is the word the letter comes out of
  • time → T T is the standard abbreviation for time, and one of the two Ts goes

The quotation marks turn it into a shout across a bar at closing time, which is precisely what stops you noticing that a bitter is a drink.

24 down SUSHI

Shut your mouth after ejecting hot Italian starter snack? (5)

Definition: snack · deletion + charade + first letter

  • Shut your mouth → SUSH SHUSH means shut your mouth, here with an H taken out of it
  • hot → H H is the letter on a hot tap, and it is the one ejected
  • Italian → I the opening letter of ITALIAN

The Italian starter is the trap: you reach for antipasto, and all the setter wants is the letter the word begins with.
