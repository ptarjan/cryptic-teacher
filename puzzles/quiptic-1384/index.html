Across

1 across MISCREANT

Various crimes associated with soldier, a criminal (9)

Definition: a criminal · anagram

a criminal → MISCREANT: a plain synonym for a wrongdoer, carrying a faintly old-fashioned, disapproving edge that 'criminal' on its own lacks.

  • crimes → CRIMES six letters handed straight over from the clue for reordering
  • soldier → ANT ant colonies keep a soldier caste, so setters use soldier for the insect
  • Various — Calling the crimes various says they come in no settled form, and letters with no settled form are letters waiting to be reordered.

Assorted offences being pinned on a serviceman.

A soldier here is an ant: colonies really do have a soldier caste, and setters lean on that sense whenever an army turns up in the wordplay.

6 across DEGAS

Recalled very old Southern French artist (5)

Definition: French artist · charade + reversal

French artist → DEGAS: Edgar Degas, the Impressionist of the ballet dancers — a definition by example, one painter standing for the category.

  • very old → AGED somebody very old is aged
  • Southern → S S is the compass abbreviation for southern
  • Recalled — To recall is to call something back, and in an across clue calling letters back reads them from right to left.

A painter from the south being remembered in his old age.

'Recalled' reads as remembered in a sentence about an elderly painter, and it is an instruction instead. Impressionists are the setter's favourite artists to clue.

9 across MANDARIN CHINESE

Small orange feature, see, represented in an Asian language (8,7)

Definition: an Asian language · charade + anagram

an Asian language → MANDARIN CHINESE: the standard form of Chinese spoken across most of China, so it names one particular Asian language.

  • Small orange → MANDARIN a mandarin is the small loose-skinned orange
  • feature → CHIN the chin is a feature of the face
  • see → ESE the three letters of SEE set down in a new order
  • represented — Read as re-presented, the word means shown again in a fresh arrangement, which is what happens to the three letters before it.

A small citrus fruit, apparently, drawn with its parts labelled in a foreign tongue.

The trap is reading 'Small orange' as two adjectives describing the feature; it is a noun standing alone, the fruit itself.

10 across CREW

Team boasted (4)

Definition: Team · double definition

Team → CREW: the people who work a boat or a shift together; and boasted → CREW, the archaic past tense of crow, and to crow is to boast.

  • Team → the group who work a boat, a shift or a film set together
  • boasted → the past tense of crow, which is what a cockerel and a winner both do

A sports side bragging about the result.

The past tense of crow survives now almost only in 'the cock crew', which is why the second half of this clue reads as a different word altogether.

11 across RECYCLED

Type of transport in Post Office colour given a second chance? (8)

Definition: given a second chance? · container

given a second chance? → RECYCLED: material that has been used, reclaimed and put back into service, which is a second chance in the physical sense.

  • Post Office colour → RED British post boxes and Royal Mail vans are red, the Post Office's own livery
  • Type of transport → CYCLE a cycle is a way of getting about
  • in — The little word puts one thing inside another, so the transport named before it goes within the colour named after it.

Some form of transport turning up in postal red.

Pillar-box red is the colour of British post boxes and Royal Mail vans — a shade a whole country knows by the Post Office's name.

14 across HIERARCHY

People in power greeting revival of archery (9)

Definition: People in power · charade + anagram

People in power → HIERARCHY: the ranked order of authority in an organisation, used loosely for the set of people occupying its top ranks.

  • greeting → HI hi is the everyday greeting
  • archery → ERARCHY the seven letters of the sport rearranged
  • revival of — A revival brings something back in a new form, and letters brought back in a new form are letters reordered.

A warm welcome given to the comeback of the bow and arrow.

'Revival of archery' reads as a news story about a sport coming back into fashion, and the word 'revival' is the setter's instruction hiding inside it.

15 across HANOI

Starts to herald a new operation in Vietnamese city (5)

Definition: Vietnamese city · first letters

Vietnamese city → HANOI: the capital of Vietnam, up in the north of the country; a definition by example, since the country has other cities.

  • herald → H the first letter of the word
  • a → A a one-letter word, so its opening letter is all of it
  • new → N the initial of the word
  • operation → O the first letter of the word
  • in → I the initial of the last word in the run
  • Starts to — The start of a word is the letter it begins with, and the plural says to take one from each of the words that follow.

The announcement of a new military campaign.

Five words in a row give up their opening letters, and 'in' is one of them — the small linking words are exactly where a solver stops looking.

16 across USAGE

A long time in pursuit of American custom (5)

Definition: custom · charade

custom → USAGE: usage is established practice, the way a thing has come to be done, which is what a custom is once it settles.

  • American → US US is the standard short form of the country, so it covers American
  • A long time → AGE an age is a long stretch of time
  • in pursuit of — Whatever is in pursuit of something follows along behind it, so the letters named first are written after the ones named next.

Somebody chasing an American habit for years on end.

'A long time' opens the clue where a definition usually sits and is wordplay instead; the definition is the single word left at the end.

18 across EMOTIONAL

Energy proposal for conference almost entirely affected by feelings (9)

Definition: affected by feelings · charade + deletion

affected by feelings → EMOTIONAL: the adjective for a person governed by their feelings rather than their judgement, or a scene charged with them.

  • Energy → E E is the physicist's symbol for energy
  • proposal for conference → MOTION a motion is the proposal put before a conference for debate
  • entirely → AL ALL means entirely, and it turns up here one letter short
  • almost — Almost means falling just short, so the word it governs arrives with its final letter missing.

An energy proposal going to conference, very nearly complete.

Deletion clues hang on a word you have to supply first: 'entirely' is not describing anything here, it is the word ALL waiting to be trimmed.

20 across GRADUATE

BA or MA? DA, GA, true, would be wrong (8)

Definition: BA or MA? · anagram

BA or MA? → GRADUATE: both are university degrees, and whoever has been awarded one has by definition graduated.

  • DA, GA, true → DAGATRUE eight letters offered as two pairs and a word, to be reordered
  • would be wrong — Something written wrong has its letters out of place, so the pieces listed before this are not yet in the order the grid wants.

Somebody reeling off degree abbreviations and getting them muddled.

The clue is a run of two-letter pairs, so the eye takes the whole line as one list and never notices that only the first two belong to the definition.

21 across EMMA

In the morning the writer returned a classic novel (4)

Definition: a classic novel · charade + reversal

a classic novel → EMMA: Jane Austen's 1815 novel of a matchmaker who gets everything wrong — one named classic standing in for the category.

  • In the morning → AM am marks the hours before noon on a clock
  • the writer → ME the writer of the clue is the setter, referring to himself
  • returned — To return something is to send it back the way it came, and in an across clue that means reading the letters right to left.

An early call from the author, bringing a borrowed book back.

'The writer' is the setter talking about himself, a convention worth owning: when a clue mentions the author, the writer or yours truly, it usually wants ME or I.

25 across ANYONE FOR TENNIS

Theatrical encouragement to play in afternoon? Yes, possibly involving any number (6,3,6)

Definition: Theatrical encouragement to play · anagram

Theatrical encouragement to play → ANYONE FOR TENNIS: the catchphrase entrance line of 1920s drawing-room drama, an invitation to come out and play a game.

  • in afternoon? Yes → INAFTERNOONYES fourteen letters offered up to be rearranged
  • any number → N n is the mathematician's letter for an unspecified number
  • possibly — Saying a thing is possibly so admits it is not so as printed, and letters not as printed are letters to be reordered.
  • involving — What is involved is drawn into the business at hand, so the letter named after this word joins the pool being rearranged.

An afternoon game being agreed to, with the numbers still to be settled.

'Anyone for tennis?' is the stock line of drawing-room comedy — the cheerful young man arriving through the French windows with a racquet — which is what makes it theatrical.

26 across SITAR

Top performer adopting one Indian instrument (5)

Definition: Indian instrument · container

Indian instrument → SITAR: the long-necked, many-stringed lute of Indian classical music — a definition by example, one instrument standing for the class.

  • Top performer → STAR the top performer on the bill is the star
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one
  • adopting — A family adopting a child takes them in as their own, so the letter named next is taken inside the word before it.

A leading act taking up an unfamiliar instrument.

The SITAR is the long-necked Indian lute Ravi Shankar played, and the 'one' inside it arrives as a Roman numeral rather than as a word.

27 across CAMEMBERT

Pet going round leg, perhaps, to get cheese (9)

Definition: cheese · container

cheese → CAMEMBERT: the soft, bloomy-rinded cheese from Normandy — a definition by example, one named cheese standing for the whole class.

  • Pet → CAT the household pet a setter reaches for first
  • leg, perhaps → MEMBER a member is a limb, and a leg is one instance of one — hence the 'perhaps'
  • going round — Something going round another thing wraps itself about it, so the word before is split to sit either side of what follows.

A cat winding itself round somebody's leg by the fridge.

'Member' for a limb is an old sense kept alive largely by crosswords, and 'perhaps' is the flag that a leg is only one example of it.

Down

1 down MIMIC

I’m twice upset about impersonator (5)

Definition: impersonator · charade + reversal

impersonator → MIMIC: a mimic copies another person's voice and manner, which is what an impersonator does for a living.

  • I’m twice → IMIM I'M as its two letters, then the same two letters again
  • about → C c is short for circa, about, as used before an uncertain date
  • twice — Doing something twice means writing it out a second time, so the two letters before this word are set down again.
  • upset — In a down clue upset means tipped over end to end, so the letters just built are read from the bottom upwards.

The speaker is rattled at being quoted back at themselves twice over.

'Upset about' reads as one phrase describing a mood, and the two words are separate jobs; c for circa is the small piece hiding at the end.

2 down SINCERE

Trustworthy from the period of religious education (7)

Definition: Trustworthy · charade

Trustworthy → SINCERE: sincere means genuine in what you say and feel, and somebody who means what they say is somebody you can trust.

  • from the period → SINCE since means from the time that, covering a stretch running up to now
  • religious education → RE RE is what British school timetables call religious education

Dependability dating back to somebody's schooldays in religion.

'Period' is doing double duty: a lesson on the school timetable in the surface, and a stretch of time in the wordplay.

3 down ROAM

Travel widely – Italy’s capital, we hear (4)

Definition: Travel widely · homophone

Travel widely → ROAM: to roam is to wander with no fixed route, covering ground for its own sake rather than heading anywhere.

  • Italy’s capital → ROAM the capital city of Italy is Rome, and said aloud it comes out on the page with different vowels
  • we hear — What you hear is the noise a word makes, so the grid takes the sound of the place named rather than its spelling.

Travel advice, with the Italian capital held up as the place to go.

'Italy's capital' wants to be the letter I, the capital letter the country's name starts with, and it is the city instead — said out loud, not spelled.

4 down ARID

Desert-like? Help to bring in river (4)

Definition: Desert-like? · container

Desert-like? → ARID: arid means so dry that nothing will grow, which is exactly the quality that makes a desert a desert.

  • Help → AID aid is help, as noun and as verb
  • river → R r is the map-maker's abbreviation for river
  • to bring in — Bringing something in carries it indoors, so the letter named next is placed inside the word before it.

Relief supplies being brought in along a waterway.

The question mark flags a definition by comparison rather than a synonym: nothing here is a desert, only like one.

5 down TICKETY-BOO

Excellent access to concert? O, boy, excited (7-3)

Definition: Excellent · charade + anagram

Excellent → TICKETY-BOO: British slang for a situation that is in perfect order, said of how things are going rather than of a thing.

  • access to concert? → TICKET a ticket is what gets you in at the door of a concert
  • O, boy → YBOO the four letters of O and BOY stirred into a fresh order
  • excited — Something excited will not sit still, and letters that will not sit still come out in an order other than the printed one.

Sheer delight at landing a seat for the concert.

TICKETY-BOO is dated British slang for everything running smoothly — the sort of thing a 1940s film pilot says over the radio.

6 down DAISY CHAIN

Floral arrangement is presented within 24 hours – church gets one in (5,5)

Definition: Floral arrangement · container + charade

Floral arrangement → DAISY CHAIN: the string of daisies children thread together, an arrangement of flowers in the most literal and least florist sense.

  • 24 hours → DAY a day is the 24 hours the clock takes to come round
  • is → IS the word itself, dropped in unchanged
  • church → CH CH is the standard short form of church, as printed on maps
  • one → A a stands in for one, as in a hundred pounds
  • in → IN the word again taken at face value, two letters as they stand
  • presented within — To be presented within something is to be handed over inside it, so those two letters land in the middle of the word that follows.

Flowers delivered inside a day, with the church getting a bunch as well.

Both halves hand you words to use exactly as printed — the tiny 'is' and 'in' contribute their own letters, which is easy to miss while hunting for a mechanism.

7 down GREMLIN

Source of mischief? Grand rock band getting nothing back (7)

Definition: Source of mischief? · charade + reversal

Source of mischief? → GREMLIN: the imaginary imp blamed when machinery misbehaves, so it names the cause of the trouble rather than the trouble.

  • Grand → G g is the standard short form of grand
  • rock band → REM REM were the American rock band of Losing My Religion
  • nothing → NIL nil is nothing, the word used for a score of none
  • back — Sending a word back turns it round, so the three letters just named are written out in the opposite order.

A big-name band who get nothing in return for their trouble.

GREMLINs were invented by RAF pilots in the 1920s: the imps that sabotage an aircraft, which is why a machine failing for no reason still has one.

8 down SPEED-DIAL

Quickly call major player and face receiving bit of pushback (5-4)

Definition: Quickly call · container + charade + first letter

Quickly call → SPEED-DIAL: to speed-dial somebody is to ring them off a single stored key, calling faster than dialling the number out.

  • major player → SEED a seed is a ranked player kept apart in the draw at a major tournament
  • pushback → P the first letter of the word
  • face → DIAL the dial is the face of a clock or a gauge
  • receiving — A word that receives something takes it in, so the letter named next lands inside the piece before it.
  • bit of — A bit of a word is not the whole of it, and the bit a setter means by default is the letter it opens with.

Ringing a star player who is meeting a little resistance.

'Face' is the trap: it looks like the verb, one player facing another, and it is the noun — the face a pair of hands go round.

12 down PAGE-TURNER

Thrilling tale from assistant to pianist (4-6)

Definition: Thrilling tale · double definition

Thrilling tale → PAGE-TURNER: a book so gripping you keep turning pages; and assistant to pianist → the person who literally turns the pages of the score.

  • Thrilling tale → a story so gripping that you keep going to the next page
  • assistant to pianist → the person who sits at the keyboard's left and turns the score at the right bar

A gripping story coming from the person sitting beside the piano.

The second sense is a real job: at a recital somebody sits beside the pianist and turns the sheet music at exactly the right bar.

13 down SCIENTIFIC

Breakdown of insect, if I start to consider like an entomologist? (10)

Definition: like an entomologist? · anagram + first letter

like an entomologist? → SCIENTIFIC: an entomologist is a scientist, so proceeding like one is proceeding scientifically; the question mark flags the definition by example.

  • insect, if I → INSECTIFI nine letters handed over to be rearranged
  • consider → C the opening letter of the word
  • Breakdown of — A breakdown takes a thing apart into its components, and components can be put back together in any order.
  • start to — The start of a word is the letter it begins with, so only that opening letter joins the mixture.

A bug specialist beginning to take a specimen apart.

An entomologist is an insect scientist, so the letters being shuffled and the definition describe the same person's working day and the seam between them barely shows.

14 down HOURGLASS

A measure of time for us tucking into hot drink container (9)

Definition: A measure of time · container + charade

A measure of time → HOURGLASS: the twin-bulbed glass whose sand runs through in a fixed span, so the object itself measures out a period.

  • hot → H H is the letter on the hot tap
  • for us → OUR what belongs to us is our, the possessive of the same pronoun
  • drink container → GLASS a glass is what a drink is served in
  • tucking into — To tuck into something is to get right inside it, so the letters named before end up in among what comes after.

Something poured out for us into a hot-drink flask.

'Hot drink container' wants to be one object, a mug or a thermos, and it splits: the hot is a single letter, and the container is what a cold drink comes in.

17 down ANALYST

Researcher etching a line in any stone (7)

Definition: Researcher · container + charade

Researcher → ANALYST: an analyst is paid to take data apart and report what it shows, which is research in the commercial and intelligence sense.

  • any → ANY the word as it stands, prised open to take the insertion
  • a → A the single letter, used literally
  • line → L l is the abbreviation for line, as in a page-and-line reference
  • stone → ST st is the British abbreviation for the stone, the fourteen-pound unit of body weight
  • etching — To etch is to cut a mark into a surface, so the letters named next are set down inside the word that follows.

Somebody scratching a line into a piece of rock.

'Any' looks like a vague adjective attached to the stone, and it is a whole word to be prised apart — the plainest words in the clue are the fodder.

19 down NOMINEE

No source of coal linked to European candidate (7)

Definition: candidate · charade

candidate → NOMINEE: a nominee is the person formally put forward for a post or a prize, which is what standing as a candidate amounts to.

  • No → NO the word taken as it stands
  • source of coal → MINE coal is dug out of a mine
  • European → E E is the standard abbreviation for European

A European put forward despite having no coal behind them.

'Source of' almost always asks for a first letter, and here it means what it says: the place the coal comes out of.

22 down ASSET

Something useful when fixed (5)

Definition: Something useful · charade

Something useful → ASSET: anything of value working in your favour, from an item on a balance sheet to a helpful person on the team.

  • when → AS as can replace when: as I arrived, when I arrived
  • fixed → SET something set is fixed, like a set time or a jelly that has set

Something that comes in handy once it has been mended.

'When fixed' reads as a condition — useful only after repair — and both of its words are separate synonyms to be laid end to end.

23 down GRIM

Crag’s lowest edge is dark and gloomy (4)

Definition: dark and gloomy · charade + last letter

dark and gloomy → GRIM: grim describes a scene that is bleak and forbidding, the mood of a dark, gloomy place rather than its light level.

  • Crag’s → G the last letter of the word, sitting at its foot in a down clue
  • edge → RIM the rim is the edge of a cup or a wheel
  • lowest — In a down clue the lowest letter of a word is the one at its end, so the final letter of the word before is all that is taken.

The bottom lip of a rock face looking bleak.

Down clues can point at a letter by its height on the page, so 'lowest' is not describing the crag but picking the letter at the bottom of the word.

24 down TEAM

A cuppa with the first of my co-workers? (4)

Definition: co-workers? · charade + first letter

co-workers? → TEAM: a team is the group of people who work together on something, so it names the colleagues collectively rather than one by one.

  • A cuppa → TEA a cuppa is British for a cup of tea, and almost never means coffee
  • my → M the opening letter of the word
  • the first of — The first of a word is the letter it opens with, so a single letter comes across from what follows.

Sharing a brew with the people you work alongside.

The question mark is carrying the weight: the clue names the individuals and the grid wants the single word for the body they add up to.
