Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,128

Across

1 across PARLIAMENTS

Those passing laws showing regrettable paternalism (11)

Definition: Those passing laws · anagram

  • paternalism → PATERNALISM eleven letters, exactly the count the enumeration asks for

'Regrettable paternalism' reads as a complaint about a nannying government, which is why the second noun looks like an opinion rather than eleven letters waiting to be moved around.

9 across OUT OF IT

No longer working in computing in La-La Land (3,2,2)

Definition: in La-La Land · charade

  • No longer working in → OUTOF someone who has left a trade is out of that trade, two short words
  • computing → IT IT is information technology, the usual initials for the computing business

Two 'in's run together and the join between the halves is hard to spot: the first belongs to the wordplay, the second opens the definition.

10 across DENSEST

Doofus on backside in comfy room with TV, as thick as can be (7)

Definition: as thick as can be · charade + container + last letter

  • comfy room → DEN the name for the small snug room in a house where people go to slump
  • TV → SET a television is a set, the old word still used for the box in the corner
  • Doofus → S only one letter of this word is wanted, the one standing at the very end

The surface paints an idiot slouched in front of the telly. 'Comfy room with TV' is not one thing to look up but two separate words laid side by side.

11 across LOIRE

French banker investing nothing in Turkish capital (5)

Definition: French banker · container

  • Turkish capital → LIRE capital here is money, and the Turkish unit is the lira — this is its plural
  • nothing → O nothing is written as a zero, and a zero on the page is a letter O

'Turkish capital' wants to be Ankara and 'French banker' wants to be a man in a suit. Both are the wrong half of the pun: one is money, the other geography.

12 across EGG TIMER

Alarm triggered by poacher? (3,5)

Definition: Alarm triggered by poacher? · cryptic definition

  • poacher? → the surface offers a man creeping across someone else's land after game, and the question mark warns that this is not the sense wanted
  • Alarm triggered by → the setter means a poacher at the stove, who needs telling when the three minutes are up, and something small beside the pan does the ringing

There is no wordplay to separate out here. Everything turns on which kind of poacher you picture: the one with a gun in the woods, or the one at the hob.

14 across FIRST-CLASS

Terrific group taught after the register? (5-5)

Definition: Terrific · double definition

  • Terrific → FIRSTCLASS the everyday praise word for something of the very highest quality
  • group taught after the register? → FIRSTCLASS the register is the attendance roll a British teacher calls at the start of the day, so the lesson following it is the earliest one

The question mark is the setter admitting the second reading is a joke rather than a dictionary sense: the two halves of the phrase are being taken apart and read literally.

15 across BRAG

Boast of fancy clothing you've sent back (4)

Definition: Boast · reversal

  • fancy clothing → GARB clothing of any kind, the word a costume drama would reach for

'Fancy' looks like it is telling you to jumble the letters. It is only an adjective on the clothing, and the real instruction waits until the end of the clue.

17 across HYMN

That man's recited a song of praise (4)

Definition: a song of praise · homophone

  • That man → HYMN the pronoun you would use for a male person already mentioned, said out loud

Said aloud the two are identical; on the page they share only one letter, with a Y doing the work of an I and a silent letter on the end.

19 across ENDANGERED

Finish up less serene and under threat (10)

Definition: under threat · charade

  • Finish up → END to finish a thing up is to bring it to a close, and there is a three-letter word for that close
  • less serene → ANGERED someone made less serene has been made cross, in the past tense

'Finish up' is a whole phrasal verb on the surface and only its plainest sense is wanted, while 'less serene' is a comparative standing in for a past participle.

21 across DISCRETE

Unattached: that's prudent, you say? (8)

Definition: Unattached · homophone

  • prudent → DISCRETE someone prudent keeps things to themselves, and the word for that is spelt with two Es at the end

These two spellings are the classic confusion in English and the clue is built on it: the sound belongs to one word and the meaning wanted belongs to the other.

23 across NEGEV

Renege - viciously, somewhat - and desert (5)

Definition: desert · hidden word

  • Renege - viciously → NEGEV five consecutive letters sit across these two words, running straight through the dash between them

The dashes make 'viciously, somewhat' look like an aside about the reneging. They are the setter's way of parking the instruction where nobody will look at it.

25 across CHAPTER

Part of society that moves the narrative along (7)

Definition: Part of society · double definition

  • Part of society → CHAPTER a society or club divides into local branches, and each branch carries this name
  • that moves the narrative along → CHAPTER a novel advances one numbered division at a time

It reads as one smooth phrase, which is the whole trick: the words after 'society' look like they are describing the branch when they are a second definition.

26 across ARSENIC

Races in to treat poison (7)

Definition: poison · anagram

  • Races in → RACESIN seven letters, exactly the length of the entry

'Races in' looks like an instruction to put one thing inside another. It is not an instruction at all but the seven letters themselves, dressed up as a hospital scene.

27 across ASSIDUOUSLY

After training, son, you'd sail around America in diligent manner (11)

Definition: in diligent manner · anagram

  • son, you'd sail → SYOUDSAIL son shortens to its usual single letter and the next two words are taken as they stand
  • America → US the two initials that stand for the United States

The comma makes it look as though the setter is addressing you as son, when the word is there for its abbreviation. Sailing around America is a picture and nothing more.

Down

1 down PITHIER

Shorter jetty to harbour: this short (7)

Definition: Shorter · container + deletion

  • jetty → PIER a jetty running out into the water is a pier, and it splits to make room for a guest
  • this → THI the italics mark the word itself rather than anything it points at, and it is handed over docked of its final letter

The italics are the whole instruction: that word is not pointing at anything in the clue, it is the four-letter word itself, handed over with its tail docked.

2 down REFLECTS

Considers what a still lake does (8)

Definition: Considers · double definition

  • Considers → REFLECTS to consider a matter is to turn it over in the mind, and there is one verb for that
  • what a still lake does → REFLECTS water with no ripple on it throws back the image of everything above it

There is nothing to take apart here: two straight definitions sit end to end, and the second is a description standing in for a single verb.

3 down IOTA

Greek character reading Riot Act (but not in full) (4)

Definition: Greek character · hidden word

  • Riot Act → IOTA four consecutive letters run across these two words, straight through the space between them

'Reading the Riot Act' is a set phrase for a stern telling-off, and the capital letters sell it as history. The bracket at the end is where the real instruction hides.

4 down MADAGASCAR

18 curtailed shipping commercial silver somewhere in Africa (10)

Definition: somewhere in Africa · container + charade + deletion

  • 18 → MASCAR the answer at 18 down, less its final letter, and it opens up to take cargo
  • commercial → AD a commercial on television is an ad, short for advertisement
  • silver → AG Ag is silver's symbol in the periodic table, from the Latin argentum

A cross-reference clue, so another entry has to be solved first. 'Commercial silver' reads as a phrase off the metals market and is two separate abbreviations.

5 down NONET

The bravest trapeze artists perform with this group (5)

Definition: group · charade

  • The bravest → NO the ones with the most nerve use none of the safety gear, and 'none of it' shrinks to two letters
  • trapeze artists perform with this → NET the mesh slung under a high wire to catch anyone who falls

Trapeze artists work above a safety net and the bravest go on without one, which is the picture the clue is painting. 'Group' at the end is the only straight word in the sentence.

6 down STEAMER

Boat's second crew with sign of uncertainty (7)

Definition: Boat · charade

  • second → S a second of time is written s, the standard scientific abbreviation
  • crew → TEAM a crew is a set of people working together, which is what a team is
  • sign of uncertainty → ER the noise someone makes while hesitating, written down as two letters

'Boat's second' invites you to count into the word and take its second letter. 'Second' is the unit of time here, and the boat is the definition standing at the front.

7 down ROLL OF THE DICE

Risky attempt, forced 'hello'; it wavers (4,2,3,4)

Definition: Risky attempt · anagram

  • forced 'hello'; it → FORCEDHELLOIT thirteen letters, exactly the count the enumeration asks for

'Forced' looks like the word doing the shuffling and is not: it describes a strained greeting and is part of the raw material. The instruction to move things comes last.

8 down STORAGE DEVICE

Expressed anger after abrupt halt: electric vehicle to freeze; it's the computer's memory (7,6)

Definition: the computer's memory · charade + deletion

  • halt → STO a four-letter word for a halt, broken off before its last letter
  • Expressed anger → RAGED someone who expressed anger raged, in the past tense the clue is using
  • electric vehicle → EV the two initials now standard for an electric vehicle on any forecourt
  • freeze → ICE to ice something is to freeze it, and ice is what frozen water becomes

The punctuation does the hiding: a colon and a semicolon chop the sentence into four unrelated-looking scraps, so it reads as a list of complaints rather than a build.

13 down ILL-NATURED

Sullen Everyman's going to drive across the countryside (3-7)

Definition: Sullen · charade + container

  • Everyman's going to → ILL the setter calls himself Everyman, so 'Everyman is going to' becomes I'll, and the grid drops the apostrophe
  • the countryside → NATURE open country and its wildlife, as against the town
  • drive → D D is the single letter for drive, as on the selector of an automatic gearbox

Everyman signs his clues in the first person, so his name is a pronoun wherever it appears. The country outing on the surface is doing nothing but hiding that.

16 down AGONISES

Feels the strain, so easing off (8)

Definition: Feels the strain · anagram

  • so easing → SOEASING eight letters, matching the length of the entry exactly

'So easing off' reads as pressure letting up, the opposite of what the definition wants. The middle two words are raw material and the last one is the instruction.

18 down MASCARA

Cosmetic with which, at first, my Aunt Agatha will conceal blemish (7)

Definition: Cosmetic · container + first letters

  • my Aunt Agatha → MAA three words, and only the letter each one starts with is wanted
  • blemish → SCAR the mark left on skin once a wound has healed

Aunt Agatha is a Wodehouse terror and pure surface decoration; she is here for her initials. Covering a blemish is what the product genuinely does, which makes the sentence sit still.

20 down REGENCY

Period genre recreated with hints of cravats and yestermorns? (7)

Definition: Period · anagram + first letters

  • genre → GENRE five letters of raw material, taken exactly as they stand
  • cravats and yestermorns → CY the opening letter of each of these two words joins the pile

'Yestermorns' is not a word anybody uses, and that is the tell: it is there to donate a letter and to let the question mark's costume-drama joke land.

22 down ROTAS

After some resistance, Oscar sat up: there's work to be done (5)

Definition: there's work to be done · charade + reversal

  • some resistance → R R is the physicist's symbol for resistance, and only a bit of the word is being asked for
  • Oscar → O Oscar is O in the radio alphabet, alongside Papa and Quebec
  • sat → TAS a three-letter word for having taken a seat, written the other way round

Everyman is being generous with the definition here, pointing at the work rather than at the paperwork that lists it, and a colon is all that separates the two halves.

24 down IAGO

Primarily insidious antagonist goading Othello? (4)

Definition: Primarily insidious antagonist goading Othello? · &lit + first letters

  • insidious antagonist goading Othello → IAGO the first letter of each of these four words, taken in the order they come

An all-in-one: read the sentence once as a description of Shakespeare's villain, then read the very same words again as the instruction that spells his name.
