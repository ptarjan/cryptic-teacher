Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,094

Across

1 across PEACEFUL

Still pace fuel supply (8)

Definition: Still · anagram

  • pace fuel → PEACEFUL the eight letters of PACE FUEL rebuilt

The surface sells you a filling station, where 'supply' looks like a noun about fuel. It is the adverb of 'supple' instead.

6 across GOTHIC

Understood this Roman style of architecture (6)

Definition: style of architecture · charade

  • Understood → GOT 'got it' means understood
  • this Roman → HIC hic is 'this' in Latin, the language a Roman speaks

'Roman' points at the Latin word, not at architecture - which is the trap, since the answer names the manner that Rome's classical one is not.

9 across GROUSE

Bird in Greek river (6)

Definition: Bird · charade

  • Greek → GR Gr. is the standard abbreviation for Greek
  • river → OUSE the Ouse, the setter's favourite English river

Nothing Greek about the water: the Ouse runs through Yorkshire, and 'in' is only holding the sentence upright.

10 across COMPOSED

Calm company man initially sat for artist (8)

Definition: Calm · charade + first letter

  • company → CO Co. is the everyday abbreviation for company
  • man → M the initial letter of man
  • sat for artist → POSED to sit for a painter is to pose

The surface is a portrait sitting, and the definition is what a sitter has to stay for an hour - hidden in plain sight at the front.

11 across VICTORIES

Leader in Cambridge blues after six wins (9)

Definition: wins · charade + first letter

  • six → VI VI is six in Roman numerals
  • Cambridge → C the letter leading Cambridge
  • blues → TORIES the Conservatives are the blue party

A Cambridge blue is a sportsperson who has played against Oxford, which is the picture on offer. The party colour is the sense that pays.

13 across CHECK

Pick up European vet (5)

Definition: vet · homophone

  • European → CHECK a Czech is a European, and the nationality said aloud is spelt this way in the grid

'Vet' sits beside a European looking like an animal doctor; it is the verb, and the two spellings share one sound exactly.

15 across TICKET

Mark it right in report card (6)

Definition: card · homophone

  • Mark it right → TICKET marking something as correct is having ticked it, and TICKED spoken lands on the answer

The surface is a school report, and the mechanism is the D at the end of a spoken 'ticked', which the ear hears as a T.

17 across TOECAP

One returning in jumper, say – it’s for protection (6)

Definition: it’s for protection · container + reversal

  • jumper, say → TOP a jumper is one kind of top, which is what 'say' signals
  • One → ECA one is an ace, and ACE comes back as ECA

'Jumper' teases a kangaroo or a horse before it settles into the wardrobe, and the definition names the answer only by the job it does.

18 across RELAID

Section of sundial erroneously rotated and put down again (6)

Definition: put down again · hidden word + reversal

  • sundial erroneously → RELAID DIALER runs across sundiAL ERroneously, and turned about it spells the answer

'Erroneously' stands there looking like an anagram signal and is really half of the material - the letters are taken in order, only from the wrong end.

19 across ADVERB

Perhaps surprisingly, notice very short book (6)

Definition: Perhaps surprisingly · charade + deletion

  • notice → AD an ad in the paper is a notice
  • very → VER VERY docked of its last letter
  • book → B b. is the standard abbreviation for book

The opening two words read as the setter's aside about the sentence to come, which is exactly what stops you noticing they are the definition.

21 across BRAND

Stamp grain product with date (5)

Definition: Stamp · charade

  • grain product → BRAN bran is the husk milling leaves behind
  • date → D d. is a standard abbreviation for date

'With' joins the two halves and does nothing else, while the breakfast-cereal surface keeps the branding iron well out of sight.

22 across UNDERLIES

Is the cause of stories behind two foreign articles (9)

Definition: Is the cause of · charade

  • two foreign articles → UNDER French UN and German DER, one article apiece
  • stories → LIES a story can be an untruth

Two languages for the price of one phrase, and 'stories' means untruths rather than tales - the sense a newspaper never admits to.

25 across STEAMING

Chef’s team in grey trousers getting very hot (8)

Definition: getting very hot · hidden word

  • Chef’s team in grey → STEAMING the answer runs unbroken across four words: Chef’S TEAM IN Grey

Nothing in the kitchen scene is fodder to be worked on - the words simply carry the answer across the gaps, and one verb says so.

26 across SONATA

Boy attending a musical performance (6)

Definition: musical performance · charade

  • Boy → SON a boy of the family is a son
  • attending → AT someone attending an event is at it
  • a → A the clue's own A, taken as it stands

'Attending' for AT is the piece worth banking. The grammar makes the clue read as going to a show rather than as three parts laid end to end.

28 across CRESTS

Charlie takes a break in mountain-tops (6)

Definition: mountain-tops · charade

  • Charlie → C Charlie is C in the NATO alphabet
  • takes a break → RESTS to take a break is to rest

Charlie is the radio alphabet's C rather than a person - a convention worth owning, since setters use the whole of that alphabet the same way.

29 across ANYWHERE

A New Year with present in whichever location (8)

Definition: in whichever location · charade

  • A → A the A that opens the clue, taken as itself
  • New → N N is the standard abbreviation for new
  • Year → Y Y is the standard abbreviation for year
  • with → W w/ is the everyday shorthand for with
  • present → HERE answering present at a roll-call means here

The register-call sense of 'present' is the step to find: not a gift under the tree, but what you say when your name is read out.

2 down EAR

Finally tune dulciana for organ (3)

Definition: organ · last letters

  • tune → E the last letter of tune
  • dulciana → A the last letter of dulciana
  • for → R the last letter of for - even the small joining word is fodder here

A dulciana is a real organ stop, so the surface never leaves the organ loft while the definition means the one on the side of your head.

3 down COURT

Sovereign’s palace captured, we hear? (5)

Definition: Sovereign’s palace · homophone

  • captured → COURT whatever has been captured has been caught, and CAUGHT spoken is what goes in the grid

In non-rhotic British speech the two spellings are one noise, which is what the question mark is apologising for.

4 down FLEUR-DE-LIS

Engineer used filler for heraldic design (5-2-3)

Definition: heraldic design · anagram

  • used filler → FLEUR-DE-LIS the ten letters of USED FILLER rebuilt

'Engineer' reads as a trade on the surface, standing next to a builder's filler. The 5-2-3 enumeration is the tell that a French phrase is coming.

5 down LICHEN

Crusty growth in part of metallic henneries (6)

Definition: Crusty growth · hidden word

  • metallic henneries → LICHEN the answer straddles the join: metalLIC HENneries

Henneries are hen-houses, and the seam between two words is exactly where the eye refuses to look.

6 down GAME

Up for tennis? (4)

Definition: Up for · double definition

  • Up for → GAME being up for something is being willing to try it
  • tennis? → GAME tennis is one, and one is also the scoring unit inside a set

Four words and no wordplay at all: the question mark warns that tennis is only an instance, and the clue reads as a single invitation.

7 down TOOTHACHE

Part of cog starts to aggravate cyclist’s healthy exercise – such a pain (9)

Definition: such a pain · charade + first letters

  • Part of cog → TOOTH the parts around the rim of a cog are its teeth
  • aggravate cyclist’s healthy exercise → ACHE the opening letters of Aggravate, Cyclist's, Healthy and Exercise

The surface grumbles about a cyclist, and the definition works twice over: a figure of speech in the sentence, a literal complaint in the grid.

8 down INESCAPABLE

Cook special bean? Sure! (11)

Definition: Sure! · anagram

  • special bean → INESCAPABLE the eleven letters of SPECIAL BEAN reordered

It opens like an order barked at a chef and closes like agreement, and neither exclamation is what it seems: one is the instruction, one the definition.

12 down INTERPRETER

Translator to bury Peter Rabbit’s introduction inside (11)

Definition: Translator · charade + container + first letter

  • to bury → INTER to inter a body is to bury it
  • Peter → PETER the name is taken whole, then opened up after its first letter
  • Rabbit’s → R the introduction to Rabbit is its R

'To bury' is the one piece of ordinary vocabulary doing crossword work here - the verb inter, not the funeral. Beatrix Potter supplies the rest of the picture.

14 down WORDLESSLY

Recklessly sells dowry without a sound (10)

Definition: without a sound · anagram

  • sells dowry → WORDLESSLY the ten letters of SELLS DOWRY reordered

A dowry sold off in a hurry is a whole small story, and every word of it is either the instruction or the raw material.

16 down CALENDARS

Doctor: send Clara for lists of appointments (9)

Definition: lists of appointments · anagram

  • send Clara → CALENDARS the nine letters of SEND CLARA rearranged

The colon makes it read as an instruction shouted at a receptionist, which is precisely the reading that keeps the verb 'doctor' out of sight.

20 down ENIGMA

Missing first letter, imagine resolved riddle (6)

Definition: riddle · deletion + anagram

  • imagine → MAGINE IMAGINE with its opening I taken away

Two instructions stacked on one word: strip the front of 'imagine', then rebuild what is left. 'Resolved' is also what happens to a riddle.

23 down LUNCH

Send off without a meal (5)

Definition: meal · deletion

  • Send off → LAUNCH to send off a rocket is to launch it
  • a → A the A that comes out of LAUNCH

'Send off' pretends to be a red card and turns out to be the rocket; the letter it loses is spelt out for you in the clue.

24 down MISS

Overshoot M1 service station’s entrances (4)

Definition: Overshoot · charade + first letters

  • M1 → MI the motorway as written: M, then 1 read as the Roman numeral I
  • service station’s → SS the first letters - the entrances - of service and station

Motorway signage is the whole surface, and the trick worth banking is that a 1 beside a letter is usually a Roman numeral.

27 down TOR

Heads off to outlying rock-strewn mountain (3)

Definition: mountain · first letters

  • to outlying rock-strewn → TOR the heads of To, Outlying and Rock-strewn

'Heads off' sounds like a walker setting out for the hills, which is the picture wanted while the phrase quietly does the letter-picking.
