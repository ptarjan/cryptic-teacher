Across

1 across ONE AT A TIME

Nuts in AA to meet separately (3,2,1,4)

Definition: separately · anagram

Doing things one at a time is doing them separately rather than all at once, so the phrase works as an adverb of manner.

  • in AA to meet → INAATOMEET every letter of these four words goes into the mix exactly as it stands
  • Nuts — someone nuts has lost all order in their head, and letters described that way will not stay in the sequence they are given

AA is a support group on the surface and just two letters in the wordplay, and 'Nuts' has to be read as the state the letters are in rather than as the people in the room.

6 across ZOLA

Snake-haired monster leaving cheese for writer (4)

Definition: writer · deletion

Émile Zola was the French novelist of Germinal and the open letter 'J'accuse', so 'writer' names him by his trade.

  • cheese → GORGONZOLA gorgonzola is the blue Italian cheese
  • Snake-haired monster → GORGON the Gorgons of myth, Medusa among them, had snakes for hair
  • leaving — once someone has left a room they are no longer in it, so those letters walk out of the word that held them

The cheese is named after the Italian town of Gorgonzola, and the coincidence the setter has spotted is that a Greek monster sits in the front of it.

10 across ANIMAL HUSBANDRY

Andy Burnham is cross about a Liberal raising stock (6,9)

Definition: raising stock · anagram

Animal husbandry is the farming business of breeding and rearing livestock, and 'stock' is the farmer's own word for a herd.

  • Andy Burnham is → ANDYBURNHAMIS the mayor's name and the verb after it all go into the mix
  • a → A the article counts as one more letter
  • Liberal → L L is the standard abbreviation for Liberal
  • cross — to cross two breeds is to mix them together into something new, and these letters are interbred the same way
  • about — 'about' scatters the extra letters in among the rest instead of leaving them in a block at one end

Andy Burnham is the mayor of Greater Manchester, and reading 'cross' as angry rather than as interbreeding is what keeps the sentence sounding like politics.

11 across LAKE ERIE

50 with a king by unearthly American feature (4,4)

Definition: American feature · charade

Lake Erie is one of the five Great Lakes on the border between the United States and Canada, so 'feature' names it as geography.

  • 50 → L L is fifty in Roman numerals
  • a → A the article is taken as read
  • king → K K is the king on a chessboard and on a playing card
  • unearthly → EERIE eerie is the everyday word for unearthly

Roman fifty and the chess king are two single letters worth banking, and the surface's hint of a ghost story is there only to make 'unearthly' look like scene-setting.

12 across AEGIS

Protection from serviceman in ebbing tide (5)

Definition: Protection · container + reversal

The aegis was the shield Zeus lent Athena, and to be under someone's aegis is still to be under their protection.

  • tide → SEA the tide is the sea, in the poets' sense of the word
  • serviceman → GI a GI is an American soldier
  • in — 'in' puts the soldier inside the other word rather than alongside it
  • ebbing — an ebbing tide is running back out the way it came in, so its letters run the other way too

The whole picture — a serviceman, a beach, the water going out — is built to keep you thinking about a landing rather than about Greek myth.

14 across ICECAPS

Pole features in Room 101 making comeback (7)

Definition: Pole features · charade + reversal

The polar ice caps are the permanent sheets of ice covering the Arctic and the Antarctic, so they are the features of both poles.

  • Room → SPACE room in the sense of space, as in room to move
  • 101 → CI CI is 101 in Roman numerals
  • making comeback — a comeback brings someone back the way they went, so the assembled letters return in reverse

Room 101 belongs to Orwell on the surface, where nobody reads the number as arithmetic — which is exactly what the wordplay needs you to do.

16 across SKIDPAN

Slippery surface sends child into bridge (7)

Definition: Slippery surface · container

A skid pan is the deliberately slippery yard where drivers are taught to control a skid, so it is a surface built to lose grip.

  • bridge → SPAN a span is the reach of a bridge, and the bridge itself
  • child → KID a kid is a child
  • sends — to send a thing somewhere is to put it there, and the clue names the destination immediately afterwards
  • into — 'into' means ending up inside something rather than merely beside it, so one word finishes in the middle of the other

Nothing here looks like an instruction, which is the trick: 'sends... into' is an ordinary piece of English doing a container's job.

17 across EPHESUS

Wild sheep accompanies us in ancient city (7)

Definition: ancient city · anagram + charade

Ephesus was the great Greek and Roman city on the coast of what is now Turkey, famous for its temple of Artemis.

  • Wild sheep → EPHES the five letters of SHEEP resettled into a new order
  • us → US the pronoun is written in as it stands
  • Wild — wild means untamed and unordered, so the letters will not stay in the tidy line the clue lends them
  • accompanies — one thing that accompanies another walks along beside it, so these two letters sit next to the rest and not inside them

A wild sheep is a real animal — a mouflon — which is why the phrase reads as description rather than as an instruction to shuffle.

19 across BONANZA

Granny seen in Dickens a godsend (7)

Definition: godsend · container + charade

A bonanza is a sudden run of riches or good luck, which is what a godsend is when it turns up unasked for.

  • Dickens → BOZ Boz is the name Dickens signed his early sketches with
  • a → A the article joins on as it stands
  • Granny → NAN nan is what a great many children call their grandmother
  • seen in — what is seen in something is inside it, so these letters show through from within the words around them

The affectionate-sounding 'Granny seen in Dickens' is a plain instruction once you know Boz, and 'a godsend' has to give up its article to the wordplay.

20 across HEADS

Captains perhaps in privy at sea (5)

Definition: Captains perhaps · double definition

The head of an organisation is its chief, so captains are heads; and a ship's lavatory has been called the heads since the age of sail.

  • Captains perhaps → HEADS the head of a body or a business is its chief, and a captain is one kind of chief
  • privy at sea → HEADS the traditional name for a ship's lavatory, found forward near the bow in the age of sail

The seam is hidden by 'in', which joins the two halves so smoothly that the sentence reads as one picture of officers in a ship's toilet.

21 across AGNOSTIC

Sceptical as a good number remain we hear (8)

Definition: Sceptical · charade + homophone

An agnostic holds that nothing can be known either way about God, which is exactly the doubting position 'sceptical' describes.

  • a → A the article is taken as read
  • good → G G is the standard abbreviation for good
  • number → NO no. is the everyday abbreviation for number
  • remain we hear → STIC to stick is to remain, and STICK said aloud is what these four letters spell out
  • we hear — 'we hear' asks for the sound of a word and not its spelling, so the letters follow the ear

Three of the four pieces are abbreviations you can bank, and the last one only works if you say it: STIC is no word at all, but it sounds like one.

24 across WILLIAM OF ORANGE

Ruler's order for backer briefly touring two states (7,2,6)

Definition: Ruler · charade + deletion + container

William of Orange took the English throne as William III in 1689, so 'ruler' names him by the office rather than the man.

  • order → WILL a will is the order somebody leaves for what must happen
  • two states → IAMO IA is Iowa and MO Missouri, two postal abbreviations
  • for → FOR the little word is written straight in, letter for letter
  • backer briefly → ANGE an angel is the backer who puts money into a show, docked of its last letter
  • briefly — said briefly, a word is cut short, so the last letter of this one never gets written
  • touring — a tour goes all the way round a place rather than past it, so these letters end up wrapped around what they visit

The apostrophe-s is the join, and everything after it is machinery: an angel here is theatre money rather than a cherub, and the states are pure postcode.

25 across RARE

Exquisite morsel missed in savoury dish (4)

Definition: Exquisite · deletion

A rare talent is an exquisite one — the sense of rare that means outstanding, not the one that means seldom found.

  • savoury dish → RAREBIT a rarebit is the cheese-on-toast savoury
  • morsel → BIT a bit is a small morsel of something
  • missed — what is missed is not there when you look for it, so those three letters are absent from the dish

A Welsh rarebit is cheese on toast and nothing to do with rabbit, and the clue's 'morsel' is the very piece of it that has to go.

26 across GRETA GARBO

Star to rage when drunk grabs dress (5,5)

Definition: Star · anagram + container

Greta Garbo was the Swedish actress of Grand Hotel and Ninotchka, so 'star' names one of the biggest names in Hollywood.

  • to rage → GRETAO the six letters of TO RAGE resettled into a new order
  • dress → GARB garb is what you dress in — clothing
  • drunk — a drunk cannot walk a straight line, and these letters will not keep to theirs either
  • grabs — to grab something is to close your hand round it, so the letters shut around what they take hold of

'Star' is the definition and the joke at once: the wordplay has a screen legend raging drunk and grabbing at a frock.

Down

1 down OMAN

Female no wife for Saudi neighbour (4)

Definition: Saudi neighbour · deletion

Oman shares a long desert border with Saudi Arabia, so it is literally one of the kingdom's neighbours on the Arabian peninsula.

  • Female → WOMAN a female is a woman
  • wife → W W is the crossword's standard abbreviation for wife
  • no — 'no wife' announces that the wife is absent, so the letter standing for her is not there to be written

Crossword shorthand does all the work here, and W for wife is worth memorising: the surface reads as a description of one person, never as an instruction.

2 down ELIZABETH TAYLOR

American visiting The Ritz all obey — volatile player? (9,6)

Definition: player? · anagram

A player is anyone who acts a part, and Elizabeth Taylor was one of Hollywood's great screen actresses — an example of the class, not a synonym.

  • American → A A is the standard abbreviation for American
  • The Ritz all obey → THERITZALLOBEY all four words are raw material, letter for letter
  • visiting — a visitor arrives among the people already there, so this letter joins the others rather than standing apart from them
  • volatile — a volatile substance will not sit still in the state you found it, and neither will this handful of letters

The Ritz and a well-behaved crowd make a nice picture and are doing nothing but supplying letters. The question mark admits that one actress is only an example of the definition.

3 down TRACE

Hint from artist forsaking daily grind (5)

Definition: Hint · deletion

A trace of something is the faintest sign that it was ever there, which is what a hint amounts to when nobody says anything outright.

  • daily grind → RATRACE the rat race is the daily grind of commuting and the office
  • artist → RA RA is a Royal Academician, the crossword's standard artist
  • forsaking — to forsake something is to walk out on it for good, so those two letters quit the phrase and do not come back

'Daily grind' has to be heard as the idiom rather than as a charwoman's work, and RA is worth banking: it is the artist in a great many clues.

4 down T-SHIRTS

Garments: those in which to sleep almost removed? (1-6)

Definition: Garments · deletion

T-shirts are the plain short-sleeved cotton tops named for their outline, so 'garments' is the general word for one particular kind.

  • those in which to sleep → NIGHTSHIRTS nightshirts are what you sleep in
  • almost → NIGH nigh means almost, as in 'the end is nigh'
  • removed — something removed is taken out and carried off, so that block of letters is lifted clean out of the longer word

The colon does the misdirecting: it makes 'those in which to sleep' look like a second description of the answer when it is really the word being cut down.

5 down MASTERS

Overcome small albatross sometimes seen here? (7)

Definition: albatross sometimes seen here? · charade

The Masters is the Augusta major, so it is one of the few places a golfing albatross is ever likely to be witnessed.

  • Overcome → MASTER to master a fear is to overcome it
  • small → S S is the size printed on a clothing label

Two golf words do the misdirecting: an albatross is a seabird until you remember it is also three under par on a single hole.

7 down OLD-AGE PENSIONER

One possibly retired hurt alongside opener? (3-3,9)

Definition: One possibly retired · anagram

An old-age pensioner has reached the age of drawing a state pension, and 'possibly' allows for the retired people who are nothing of the kind.

  • alongside opener → ALONGSIDEOPENER the two words supply every letter, exactly as they are written
  • hurt — a hurt limb has been knocked out of its proper shape, and these letters are knocked out of theirs

The cricket is a decoy from end to end — retired hurt, an opener, a fielder alongside — and none of it does anything but hand over letters.

8 down ABYSSINIAN

Significant hole in one by an old African? (10)

Definition: old African? · charade

Abyssinia was the name Ethiopia went by until the twentieth century, so an Abyssinian is what an Ethiopian used to be called.

  • Significant hole → ABYSS an abyss is a chasm too deep to see the bottom of
  • in → IN the little word is written in as it stands
  • one → I I is the Roman numeral for one
  • an → AN the indefinite article comes across unchanged

'Hole in one' is a golfer's phrase on the surface and three separate pieces underneath, which is where this clue hides its seams.

9 down BAHAMIAN

Caribbean sort to sight whaler up north (8)

Definition: Caribbean sort · charade + reversal

A Bahamian is a native of the Bahamas, an island nation in the Caribbean, so 'sort' is asking for a person and not a place.

  • to sight → AIM to sight a target is to aim at it
  • whaler → AHAB Captain Ahab is the whaler hunting Moby-Dick
  • north → N N is the compass abbreviation for north
  • up — this is a down entry, so 'up' asks you to read those letters from the foot of the column back to the top

Ahab is the crossword's whaler and worth remembering. The compass point stays the right way round because it is added after the turn rather than before it.

13 down EISENHOWER

Ground where one welcomes island leader (10)

Definition: leader · anagram

Dwight D. Eisenhower commanded the Allied armies in Europe and then served two terms as US president, so 'leader' names him by his job.

  • where one → WHEREONE both words go into the mix exactly as they stand
  • island → IS Is. is the map-maker's abbreviation for island
  • Ground — grain that has been ground comes out as loose meal with no shape left to it, and so do these letters
  • welcomes — to welcome somebody is to take them in, so the short abbreviation joins the letters already gathered

'Ground' looks like a noun — a piece of land — right up to the moment you need it as the past participle of grind.

15 down ASSASSIN

One kills when elite troops stray from path (8)

Definition: One kills · charade

An assassin is one who kills by stealth, for hire or for politics, so the clue's bare 'one kills' is the whole trade.

  • when → AS 'as' and 'when' swap freely: as I arrived, when I arrived
  • elite troops → SAS the SAS is the British army's special forces regiment
  • stray from path → SIN to sin is to stray from the straight and narrow

The military surface does the hiding: 'stray from path' looks like a manoeuvre going wrong when it is really a churchman's word.

18 down STARMER

Is it Ringo installing Ringoe as PM? ()

Definition: PM · container

PM is the everyday shorthand for prime minister, the office Sir Keir Starmer took for Labour in 2024.

  • Ringo → STARR Ringo Starr, the Beatles' drummer
  • Ringoe → ME Ringoe sets this puzzle, and a setter referring to themselves is ME
  • installing — to install a part is to fit it inside the housing, so the shorter piece goes into the middle of the longer

A setter writing 'me' about themselves is a standing convention, and here the name that does it is one letter away from a Beatle's.

19 down BENEFIT

Assist insect outside close to sunken well (7)

Definition: Assist · container + charade + last letter

To benefit someone is to do them good, the same helping hand 'assist' names, though benefit can be a noun as well.

  • insect → BEE the bee is the crossword's default insect
  • close to sunken → N the letter that closes the word sunken
  • well → FIT someone fit and well is in good health
  • outside — standing outside something means going round it, so the insect wraps around the single letter
  • close to — the close of a word is where it stops, so only its final letter is wanted

'Well' is the pivot: it reads as part of 'sunken well' in the surface and as a word for healthy in the wordplay.

22 down SPROG

Seconds for good child (5)

Definition: child · charade

Sprog is British services slang for a small child, the half-affectionate word a parent might use about a new baby.

  • Seconds → S s is the abbreviation for seconds on any stopwatch
  • for → PRO pro means in favour of, which is to say for
  • good → G G is the standard abbreviation for good

Every word before the definition stands for something shorter, and 'for' is the surprise: it is not joining the pieces, it is one of them.

23 down NEMO

Revolutionary augury one found in movie? (4)

Definition: one found in movie? · reversal

Nemo is the lost clownfish whose recovery gives the Pixar film its title, so the definition names one example rather than a class.

  • augury → OMEN an augury is an omen, a sign of what is coming
  • Revolutionary — a revolution turns a thing right round, so the letters come out facing the other way

The definition is a joke as well as a definition, since the whole plot of the film is the search that the title promises will succeed.
