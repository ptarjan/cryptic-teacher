Across

1 across PASSED PAWN

Future queen perhaps declined uncle's security (6,4)

Definition: Future queen perhaps · charade

A passed pawn has no enemy pawn left to stop it, so it is the one likely to reach the eighth rank and be promoted.

  • declined → PASSED to decline an offer is to pass on it
  • uncle's security → PAWN 'uncle' is old British slang for a pawnbroker, and the thing you leave with him as security is the pawn

A royal heir turning down the protection her uncle had arranged for her.

A pawn that reaches the far end of the board is promoted, almost always to a queen, so a monarch in waiting is a fair description of one. 'Uncle' for the pawnbroker is Victorian street slang.

6 across NOOB

Return favour for rookie (4)

Definition: rookie · reversal

A rookie is a first-timer who has not learned the ropes, which is precisely the scorn the online word carries.

  • favour → BOON a boon is a favour or blessing
  • Return — to return something is to send it back the way it came, so the letters run the other way along the row

Paying a kindness back to the newest recruit.

Noob is internet slang for a raw beginner, squashed out of 'newbie', and it is far newer vocabulary than most grids allow themselves.

10 across SHARP

Small instrument with strings out of tune (5)

Definition: out of tune · charade

A note played sharp sits above the pitch it should have, which is one particular way of being out of tune.

  • Small → S s = small, as on a clothing label
  • instrument with strings → HARP the harp is the stringed instrument being described the long way round

A little stringed instrument that has drifted off pitch.

'Small instrument' wants to read as one phrase, a modest little thing; the S breaks off on its own and the instrument is everything after it.

11 across PRESIDENT

Chief executive parking at home (9)

Definition: Chief executive · charade

A president heads the executive branch of a state, or a company's board, so 'chief executive' is the job title rather than a loose synonym.

  • parking → P p = parking, the letter on the road sign
  • at home → RESIDENT someone at home in a place is resident there

A driver leaving the car outside the house.

The surface is a car and a house with nothing corporate in sight, so 'Chief executive' at the front reads as scene-setting when it is doing all the defining.

12 across MEGAVERTEBRATE

English sailor bet priest a stone knocked over large spined creature (14)

Definition: large spined creature · charade + reversal

A vertebrate is a creature with a backbone and mega- makes it a large one, so the definition translates the coinage piece by piece.

  • English → E e = English, the standard abbreviation
  • sailor → TAR a tar is a sailor, from the tarpaulin clothing of the old navy
  • bet → BET the clue's own word, used as it stands
  • priest → REV the Reverend, shortened the way it is written on an envelope
  • a → A used literally
  • stone → GEM a gem is a precious stone
  • knocked over — a thing knocked over ends up the wrong way round, so the whole chain is read from its far end back

An English sailor wagering a jewel with a clergyman, and the stake getting knocked over.

TAR for a sailor and REV for a priest are the two bits of crossword vocabulary worth banking here. The answer is a real if chunky term from conservation biology, used of big backboned animals.

14 across APRONED

Bill keeps lying down in a pinny (7)

Definition: in a pinny · container

Someone in a pinny is wearing one, and aproned is the adjective for exactly that state, kept alive mostly in descriptions of cooks.

  • Bill → AD a bill pasted on a wall is an ad, and it opens between its two letters
  • lying down → PRONE prone means lying face down
  • keeps — to keep something is to hold on to it, so the word before this one closes around what comes next

Bill lying about instead of doing the cooking he is dressed for.

A pinny is British household shorthand for a pinafore apron, the thing you tie on to cook. 'Bill' is the poster on a wall, not the man the surface offers.

16 across INSIPID

Filling little drink I had without much taste (7)

Definition: without much taste · charade

Insipid food lacks savour, so 'without much taste' is the plain sense, ahead of the word's other use for a dull person.

  • Filling → IN a filling is what sits in a thing, whether a sandwich or a tooth
  • little drink → SIP a sip is the smallest drink you can take
  • I had → ID I had contracts to I'd, and the apostrophe is dropped on the way into the grid

A modest snack and a small drink that turn out to be bland.

'Filling' looks like an instruction to put one thing inside another and is only a word for 'in'. Dropping the apostrophe from I'd is a liberty every setter takes.

18 across AT WORST

Couple bitten by mad rats given dreadful prognosis (2,5)

Definition: dreadful prognosis · anagram + container

You say 'at worst' when naming the grimmest outcome available, which is what a dreadful prognosis states.

  • mad rats → ARST RATS with its four letters deranged, opening up after the A
  • Couple → TWO a couple is two
  • bitten by — a bite closes around whatever it takes hold of, so what is bitten ends up inside the biter
  • mad — a mad thing is deranged, and deranged letters do not keep the arrangement they were given

Someone bitten by rabid rats being handed the grimmest possible diagnosis.

'Mad rats' does double duty on the surface, where rabies is exactly what produces a dreadful prognosis. The definition is the phrase you reach for when naming a worst case.

20 across GO-CARTS

Castro hectored on grand soapboxes (2-5)

Definition: soapboxes · anagram + charade

A soapbox is the British name for a home-made downhill racer, so the definition is the same vehicles under their nickname.

  • grand → G g = grand, the standard abbreviation
  • Castro → OCARTS the six letters of CASTRO rearranged
  • hectored — to hector is to bully and harangue, and a name knocked about like that comes out in a different order
  • on — in an across clue, one thing on another sits after it, so the shuffled letters follow the single letter

Fidel Castro being harangued from a grand platform.

A soapbox in Britain is the home-made cart children race downhill as well as the box an orator stands on, and the surface is leaning on the orator.

22 across CROSS ONE'S HEART

Actress on horse ordered "Show you really mean it" (5,4,5)

Definition: Show you really mean it · anagram

Crossing your heart is the gesture that swears you are telling the truth, so the definition says what the action is for.

  • Actress on horse → ACTRESSONHORSE fourteen letters to rearrange — check them against the enumeration before you start
  • ordered — this is the sense of being told to form up anew, not of being put into sequence, so the letters fall in differently

A film star on horseback barking an instruction at somebody.

The phrase is the children's promise — cross my heart and hope to die — which is why the definition is a whole instruction rather than a noun.

26 across REALISTIC

Sensible English celebrity divides half of wealth (9)

Definition: Sensible · container + deletion

Being realistic is taking a sober view of what can actually happen, which is what we mean by calling a person sensible.

  • half of wealth → RIC the front half of RICHES, split again to take the insert
  • English → E e = English, the standard abbreviation
  • celebrity → ALIST an A-lister is a celebrity of the top rank
  • divides — to divide a thing is to come between its parts, so what precedes this word splits the word that follows
  • half of — keeping half of a six-letter word leaves its first three letters and throws the rest away

A well-known Englishman splitting a fortune down the middle.

'Divides' is the surface's word for splitting money and the wordplay's word for splitting a word. A-list for celebrity is modern crossword shorthand worth having ready.

27 across SADDO

One's not groovy, not even when twisting (5)

Definition: One's not groovy · charade + reversal

A saddo is British slang for a person nobody could call cool, and 'not groovy' is the sixties way of saying it of them.

  • not even → ODD a number that is not even is odd
  • when → AS as = when, in the sense of 'he arrived as I left'
  • twisting — a thing that twists turns right about, so the pieces just assembled come out the other way round

A person with no groove in them, not even out on the dance floor.

The Twist is the dance the surface has in mind, and 'groovy' comes from the same decade. The answer is British slang for someone pitiably uncool.

28 across MITE

Couple cycled a little bit (4)

Definition: a little bit · cycling

A mite is a small quantity — 'wait a mite', 'a mite tired' — so this is the everyday sense rather than the insect.

  • Couple → ITEM a couple going out together are an item
  • cycled — a cycle brings the end round to the front, the letters keeping their order but starting from a different point

A pair who went out on their bikes for a short ride.

The surface has two people on bicycles, and the instruction is the wheel rather than the ride. A mite is a small amount as well as a very small creature.

29 across PRESS AGENT

PR man, second wise guy for 11's team (5,5)

Definition: PR man · substitution

A press agent handles publicity for a client, which is the job 'PR man' names — public relations, by its initials.

  • 11's → PRESIDENT the answer to 11 across, which is the word being operated on
  • team → SIDE a side is a team, and one is sitting in the middle of that word: pre-SIDE-nt
  • second → S s = second, as written on a stopwatch
  • wise guy → SAGE a sage is a wise man
  • for — 'for' is the word of exchange — one thing standing in place of another, as a deputy stands for a boss

A publicist and a smart alec picked out for the boss's line-up.

11 across has to be solved first, because its answer is the raw material here. 'Wise guy' is a smart alec on the surface and a sage underneath.

Down

1 down PUSH

Offensive American in pub (4)

Definition: Offensive · container

An army's push is its offensive — a concerted advance, so this is the military noun rather than the everyday shove.

  • pub → PH PH is a public house, the abbreviation printed on British maps; it opens up to take the insert
  • American → US US = American, the standard abbreviation
  • in — what happens in a thing happens inside it, so the letters before this word are planted in the middle of the word after it

An American making trouble in a bar.

'Offensive' looks like the adjective, and the clue wants the noun: an army's big attack. PH for pub is map shorthand, not a word anyone says.

2 down SCARECROW

Mind Charles feeding pig? It'll help the farmer (9)

Definition: It'll help the farmer · container

A scarecrow works for the farmer by keeping birds off the crop, so the definition says what the thing does rather than naming it.

  • pig → SOW a sow is a female pig, and it splits after its first letter
  • Mind → CARE to mind about something is to care about it
  • Charles → CR Carolus Rex, the king's cypher, with R for rex
  • feeding — to feed something into a machine is to put it in, so what comes before this word goes inside what comes after

A warning to keep an eye on Charles while he feeds the pig.

CR is the royal cypher — Carolus Rex, Charles the King — and it is the piece a solver has to be told about once. The farmhand the definition promises turns out to be stuffed with straw.

3 down EXPLAIN

Partner no longer straight subject to analysis (7)

Definition: subject to analysis · charade

To explain something is to take it apart and account for it, which is what putting it under analysis amounts to.

  • Partner no longer → EX an ex is a former partner
  • straight → PLAIN plain speaking is straight speaking, direct and unadorned

A former partner, no longer honest, being sent off to the analyst.

The surface glues 'no longer straight' into one idea about the ex, and the cryptic reading breaks it in half, with 'straight' beginning the second piece.

4 down PUPAE

New app starts to unravel early, developing bugs (5)

Definition: developing bugs · anagram + first letters

Pupae are insects in the middle stage of metamorphosis, still turning into adults, so the definition names a stage rather than a species.

  • app → APP the clue's own three letters, used as they stand
  • unravel early → UE the opening letter of each of the two words
  • New — something remade comes out new, and letters that have been remade do not stay in the order they were handed over in
  • starts to — a start is the front of a word, so only the opening letter of each word that follows is taken

A new phone app coming apart early on and proving full of bugs.

'Developing' sits right beside 'bugs' and looks like the shuffling word; the instruction is 'New' at the very front, and those last two words are the definition.

5 down WRESTLING

Girls went off occasionally muddy sport (9)

Definition: occasionally muddy sport · anagram

Wrestling is the sport named, and 'occasionally muddy' points at the mud-wrestling version rather than at any part of the mechanism.

  • Girls went → GIRLSWENT nine letters to rearrange — count them against the enumeration before you start
  • off — food that has gone off has turned, and turned letters do not stay in the order they arrived in

Girls storming off from a sport that sometimes ends up played in mud.

The definition runs to three words, because 'occasionally muddy' is the setter's nod to the novelty version of the sport; 'sport' on its own would leave two words with nothing to do.

7 down OPERA

An answer given after 'term for Rigoletto?' (5)

Definition: An answer given after 'term for Rigoletto?' · &lit + charade + last letter

Rigoletto is one of Verdi's, so naming the form it belongs to is exactly what answers the question the clue asks.

  • term for Rigoletto? → O the termination — the last letter — of Rigoletto
  • An → PER fifty miles an hour is fifty miles per hour, so 'an' = per
  • answer → A a = answer, as in a Q and A
  • term — 'term' is standing in for termination, the end of a thing, so it asks for the final letter of the word beside it
  • after — what comes after a thing follows it, so the letters just built are parked behind the letter already found

A quiz question about Verdi's hunchbacked jester, and the answer somebody gives to it.

Read straight, this is a question with its own answer attached; read as wordplay, 'term' is short for termination and hands over Rigoletto's last letter. Both readings use every word.

8 down BUTTER DISH

Fat keeper bowled by absolute beauty (6,4)

Definition: Fat keeper · charade

Butter is a fat, and the dish is the lidded thing that keeps it, so the definition names contents plus function.

  • bowled → B b for bowled, as it is written on a cricket scorecard against a batter dismissed that way
  • absolute → UTTER utter nonsense is absolute nonsense
  • beauty → DISH a dish is a good-looking person, in the dated slang sense

A cricketer knocked over by the sight of a beautiful woman.

The scorecard's b for 'bowled' is the piece to spot first, and 'keeper' plays two parts: the wicketkeeper of the surface, and a thing that keeps something on the kitchen table.

9 down NIMBUS

Cloud lifted in less than 60 seconds? (6)

Definition: Cloud · charade + reversal

Nimbus is a specific type of cloud, the rain-bearing one, so the definition names the class the answer is an instance of.

  • less than → SUB sub- means under or below, as in subzero
  • 60 seconds → MIN min is the everyday shortening of minute, which is sixty seconds
  • lifted — in a down clue, lifting a thing carries it up the grid, so those letters are read from the bottom upwards

A weather front clearing away in under a minute.

A nimbus is the dark rain-bearing cloud, and elsewhere the halo painted round a saint's head. The surface's promise of a time limit is the misdirection.

13 down JAMAICA RUM

Jam sandwiches mother and son taking flight cut - WI produce? (7,3)

Definition: WI produce? · container + deletion

WI is the West Indies, and rum from Jamaica is the islands' best-known export — a product named for where it is made.

  • Jam → JAM the clue's own word, splitting after two letters to take the filling
  • mother → MA ma is mother
  • son taking flight → ICARUS Icarus, the son who flew on wax wings until the sun got at them
  • sandwiches — a sandwich keeps its filling between two slices, so the word before this one splits and takes the rest inside
  • cut — something cut is left shorter than it was, and here the trimming takes the final letter

A mother and son making a run for it, with jam sandwiches packed for the journey.

'Jam' and 'WI' together summon the Women's Institute and its jam stall, and the WI wanted here is the West Indies. Icarus arrives one letter short.

15 down DETONATOR

Setter off upcoming schedule (denied - Ed) (9)

Definition: Setter off · charade + reversal

A detonator sets off a charge, so the definition is an agent noun built from the verb rather than a dictionary synonym.

  • schedule → ROTA a rota is a duty schedule — whose turn it is, week by week
  • denied → NOT to deny a thing is to say it is not so
  • Ed → ED the name as it stands
  • upcoming — in a down clue, something upcoming is travelling up the grid, so those letters are read from the bottom of the entry

A setter taking time off, and the timetable the editor has refused.

'Setter off' reads as a setter having a day off, and the definition wants a setter-off: a thing that sets something else going. Ed is the editor of the surface and just a name in the wordplay.

17 down PARTRIDGE

First thing true love offered - some Crest (9)

Definition: First thing true love offered · charade

The song's first day brings the bird in the pear tree, so the definition identifies it by its place in the list of gifts.

  • some → PART some of a thing is a part of it
  • Crest → RIDGE the crest of a hill is its ridge

A sweetheart's opening present, with a bit of a hilltop thrown in.

In 'The Twelve Days of Christmas' the true love's first gift is a partridge in a pear tree, which is why the definition runs to a whole clause. The capital C on 'Crest' is surface dressing and carries no instruction.

19 down RUSTIC

Coarse orange coat nice on the inside (6)

Definition: Coarse · charade + middle letters

Rustic describes the countryside and then, by transfer, the manners attributed to it, and coarse is that transferred sense.

  • orange coat → RUST rust is the orange coating iron grows when it is left out in the wet
  • nice → IC the two letters at the centre of the word
  • on the inside — what is on the inside of a word is its core, so the outer letters are dropped and the middle kept

A rough orange jacket that is pleasant enough against the skin.

'Coat' is the layer that forms on neglected iron rather than a garment. Rustic means countrified and, by an old snobbery, unpolished — the definition takes that second step.

21 down CHELSEA

Football team break the rules briefly getting golfer involved (7)

Definition: Football team · container + deletion

Chelsea is a west London club in the Premier League, so the definition is by example: one team standing in for the category.

  • break the rules → CHEAT to break the rules is to cheat
  • golfer → ELS Ernie Els, the South African major winner, whose three letters make him a crossword regular
  • briefly — said briefly, a word is cut short, and the letter that goes is the last one
  • involved — getting someone involved is bringing them in, so the golfer's letters land inside the other word

A team bending the rules and dragging a golfer into the business.

'Football team' looks like the sort of phrase that would be the wordplay's subject and is the definition. Els turns up constantly in grids because his surname is three useful letters.

23 down OP ART

Role given Oscar before moving pictures, apparently (2,3)

Definition: moving pictures, apparently · charade

Op art paintings appear to shimmer and shift while hanging perfectly still, so 'apparently' is what makes the definition exact.

  • Oscar → O Oscar is O in the phonetic alphabet
  • Role → PART an actor's role is a part
  • before — what goes before a thing is placed in front of it, so the single letter leads and the rest follows

An actor handed a role and an Academy Award before the film even opens.

'Moving pictures' wants to be the cinema, and 'apparently' quietly turns it into paintings that only seem to move — the 1960s optical-illusion style.

24 down SOCKS

Tootsie covers male hidden in blouses (5)

Definition: Tootsie covers · deletion

Tootsies are feet in nursery slang, and socks are what cover them, so the definition names the body part instead of the garment.

  • blouses → SMOCKS smocks are the loose blouse-like tops
  • male → M m = male, the standard abbreviation, and it is the letter being concealed
  • hidden in — something hidden in a word is tucked inside it out of sight, so the letter named is the one to take back out

The film Tootsie, in which a man conceals himself in women's clothes.

The instruction runs the opposite way to usual: conceal the M inside the answer and the blouses appear. Tootsies are feet in nursery slang, which the italicised film title is there to disguise.

25 down COPT

Christian's quiet in bed (4)

Definition: Christian · container

A Copt belongs to the Egyptian church founded by tradition by St Mark, so the definition names the faith and the answer one of its members.

  • bed → COT a cot is a bed, and it opens after its first two letters
  • quiet → P p is piano in music, the mark telling you to play quietly
  • in — a thing in another thing sits inside it, so the letter is planted in the middle of the word that follows

A man called Christian lying quiet in bed.

'Christian' reads as a first name on the surface, which is what makes the possessive apostrophe-s look like part of it; in the cryptic reading it only means 'is'.
