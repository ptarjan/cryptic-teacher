Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,164

Setter: Everyman · Published: Saturday 8 August 2026 · Difficulty: Moderate (harder than 27% of the puzzles here) · Grid: 15×15

Across

1 across BUMS

Freely obtains seats (4)

Definition: Freely obtains · double definition

  • Freely obtains → to bum a cigarette or a lift is to cadge it, getting it for nothing
  • seats → the part of a person that meets the chair, and the part of the trousers that covers it

Four letters and no machinery: two meanings of one blunt word laid side by side. Everyman traditionally opens gently, which is worth knowing when the rest of the grid looks forbidding.

3 across ON ALL FOURS

Starting to crawl - and fall; our son's wobbly (2,3,5)

Definition: Starting to crawl · anagram

  • fall; our son → FALL OUR SON ten letters taken exactly as they stand, the semicolon ignored

A toddler learning to move is the picture and the meaning at once, which makes the anagram hard to see: every word of the surface belongs to the same scene.

9 across ACRE

After starters of awfully creamy rissoles, Everyman's beginning to see a bit of a spread? (4)

Definition: a bit of a spread · charade + first letters

  • awfully creamy rissoles → ACR the first letter of each of the three words, in order
  • Everyman → E the setter signs his puzzles Everyman, and here he is reduced to his initial

A setter writing himself into his own clue is a standing convention, and it nearly always means letters rather than a person. A spread here is an estate, not a buffet.

10 across GUEST ROOMS

Having made alterations to some rugs where visitors sleep (5,5)

Definition: where visitors sleep · anagram

  • to some rugs → TO SOME RUGS the three words hand over all ten letters just as they stand

The surface pictures a house being redecorated, which is why 'rugs' reads as furnishing rather than as raw material. Watch for 'to' and 'some': small words are fodder as often as they are filler.

12 across STAGE FRIGHT

Reluctance to having your part in something noticed? (5,6)

Definition: Reluctance to having your part in something noticed? · cryptic definition

  • your part in something → the surface offers your share in some piece of business; the setter means an actor's role in a play
  • noticed → under lights, being noticed is the entire job, and dreading it is what locks a performer up

There is no wordplay to find — the clue is one picture read two ways. 'A part in something' sounds like blame until you hear it as a role, and then the whole sentence changes profession.

15 across DRAGOON

Coerce fierce type to accept love (7)

Definition: Coerce · container

  • fierce type → DRAGON a dragon is the standing example of a fierce creature
  • love → O love is nothing on a tennis scoreboard, and nothing is written O

Love for O is one of the first conventions to learn: it turns up wherever a clue needs a single round letter. Read 'fierce type' as a person and you will never find it.

16 across EPISODE

Poised, aquiver, before House's final instalment (7)

Definition: instalment · anagram + last letter

  • Poised → POISED six letters into the mixture, taken as printed
  • House → E the last letter of the italicised title

'Poised' and 'aquiver' contradict each other, and that jar is the signal: the first word is material and the second is the instruction. Italics mark the programme title.

17 across FREEDOM

Loudly repulsed: tick on woodland creature in range (7)

Definition: range · charade + reversal

  • Loudly → F f is the musician's mark for forte, meaning played loudly
  • tick → MO a tick and a mo are both a moment — 'hang on half a tick'
  • woodland creature → DEER deer are the standing inhabitants of a wood

The single letter is the hard part: f for forte is a musical direction, not an abbreviation of anything English, and it turns up whenever a clue says loudly or softly.

19 across TAKE TEN

Filming evidently proving problematic? Have a break (4,3)

Definition: Filming evidently proving problematic? · double definition

  • Filming evidently proving problematic? → a director calling for the tenth attempt at one shot is having a bad morning
  • Have a break → the same words as an instruction to the crew: stop for ten minutes

Both readings live in the same film studio, which is what makes the split hard to see: one counts attempts and the other counts minutes.

20 across INFINITIVES

To have and to hold? (11)

Definition: To have · double definition

  • To have → the opening words of the marriage vow, and grammatically a verb in its bare to-form
  • to hold → the phrase that completes the vow, and a second verb in exactly the same form

The vow is so familiar that you read it as a promise rather than as grammar, which is the whole trick. Ask what kind of word each half is and the answer arrives at once.

23 across ROUND ROBIN

Singer following multi-voice song in competition (5,5)

Definition: competition · charade

  • multi-voice song → ROUND a round is a song sung in overlapping parts, like Frere Jacques
  • Singer → ROBIN the robin is the garden bird that sings through the winter

'Singer' with a capital could be a name, a sewing machine or a bird, and only the crossing letters decide. Music holds the surface together while the meaning is a sports fixture list.

24 across IDEA

Plan in E End: hole up in this location (4)

Definition: Plan · charade + deletion + homophone

  • hole up → HIDE to hole up somewhere is to hide there
  • in this location → HERE this location is simply here
  • hole up in this location → IDEA stripped of their aitches the two words run together into 'ide 'ere, which is what you say and not what you write

A dropped aitch is worth remembering: Cockney, E End or Bow Bells in a clue nearly always means an H has gone missing from the front of a word.

25 across ABOVE BOARD

Legitimate position of chess player's hand (5,5)

Definition: Legitimate · double definition

  • Legitimate → describes an arrangement that is open and honest, with nothing going on out of sight
  • position of chess player's hand → taken literally, the hand hovering over the pieces is exactly where the phrase puts it

The phrase comes from card play, where hands kept in sight cannot swap a card underneath the table; chess borrows the same picture without meaning anything by it.

26 across POOL

Kitty to circle back (4)

Definition: Kitty · reversal

  • circle → LOOP a loop is a circle drawn in a length of line or rope

Read 'circle' as a verb and there is nothing to find; read it as a shape and four letters arrive ready to turn. Short reversals are the ones most often missed.

Down

1 down BRASSED OFF

Piqued, as edges of four sofa beds bashed up (7,3)

Definition: Piqued · anagram + outer letters

  • edges of four → FR the outside letters of FOUR, front and back
  • sofa beds → SOFA BEDS eight letters taken whole, straight off the page

The surface is a house move gone wrong, which is why 'edges' reads as furniture corners rather than as an instruction. Ten letters is a long anagram: pencil them out before guessing.

2 down MARMALADES

Spreads out arms and a medal (10)

Definition: Spreads · anagram

  • arms → ARMS four letters straight into the mixture
  • a medal → A MEDAL the article counts too, so its A joins the six letters of the award

The surface shows a soldier laying out kit, and 'arms' is the word doing the misleading — weapons in the picture, limbs nowhere, letters all along.

4 down NEUTRON

Found in northern EU (Trondheim), something small and free of charge (7)

Definition: something small and free of charge · hidden word

  • northern EU (Trondheim) → NEUTRON the run crosses three words in order: norther-N, then EU, then TRON-dheim

A city in brackets looks like a helpful aside and is really where half the letters live. Norway is not in the EU, so the geography is doing nothing but hiding.

5 down LASAGNE

Chopped sage and piece of lardo: an Italian dish (7)

Definition: Italian dish · anagram + first letter

  • sage → SAGE the herb hands over all four of its letters
  • lardo → L the opening letter of the italicised Italian word
  • an → AN the article looks as though it belongs to the definition, and is really two more letters of fodder

The colon invites you to read everything after it as the meaning, which is exactly where the last two letters of the mixture are hiding. Italics in a clue usually flag a foreign word.

6 down FIRST-AID KITS

Help following mishaps if it's dark and it's stormy (5-3,4)

Definition: Help following mishaps · anagram

  • if it's dark → IF IT'S DARK nine letters, the apostrophe ignored as punctuation always is
  • and it's → IT'S the 'and' only joins the two phrases; the three letters beside it go into the mixture

'It was a dark and stormy night' is the joke, and it is also the whole of the fodder — a quotation is a good hiding place because you read it as one lump.

7 down UFOS

Represented initially: Uranian fleet overhead, supposedly? (4)

Definition: Uranian fleet overhead, supposedly? · first letters + &lit

  • Uranian fleet overhead, supposedly → UFOS the first letter of each of the four words, in the order written

Uranian means of the planet Uranus, not of any alien, so the surface plants astronomy where the initials plant a sighting. A sceptical adverb is often the setter winking at you.

8 down SASH

Blonde perhaps going after small band (4)

Definition: band · charade

  • small → S S is the size on a clothing label
  • Blonde perhaps → ASH ash blonde is one shade of fair hair, and 'perhaps' is what flags a single example

'Perhaps' almost always means an example is standing in for its category, and here it works backwards: the category is in the clue and the example is in the grid.

11 down SECOND FIDDLE

Subordinate to assist with con (6,6)

Definition: Subordinate · charade

  • assist → SECOND to second a motion is to back it, so seconding and assisting are the same act
  • con → FIDDLE a fiddle is a swindle, which is what a con is

Neither half of the wordplay is a musical word, yet both halves land in an orchestra — that collision is the whole joke, and it is why the surface reads as prison slang.

13 down MONTEVIDEO

Three-card trick seen on film in S American capital (10)

Definition: S American capital · charade

  • Three-card trick → MONTE three-card monte is the street swindle with two losing cards and one winner
  • film → VIDEO a video is a film in its recorded form

Both halves are worth banking: monte is the crossword word for a card sharp's game, and 'film' points at the medium far more often than at the cinema.

14 down TENNIS BALL

Spooner tells us: Franklin's elevated item for service (6,4)

Definition: item for service · spoonerism

  • Franklin's → TENNIS Benjamin Franklin is Ben, and Spooner takes his B away and hands him a T
  • elevated → BALL elevated means tall, and it collects the B that Ben's gave up

Say 'Ben's tall' and then trade the two opening sounds; the result runs together into the answer. A surname alone is often a first name in disguise.

18 down MAFIOSO

Member of a family who may have performed many hits (7)

Definition: Member of a family who may have performed many hits · cryptic definition

  • Member of a family → the surface offers an ordinary relative; the family the setter means is a crime syndicate
  • performed many hits → not chart-toppers but contract killings, which is that family's line of work

No letters to assemble — the clue turns entirely on two senses of 'hit'. When a surface mentions music and family together, crosswords are nearly always pointing at organised crime.

19 down TINNIER

Smaller around North, and higher pitched (7)

Definition: higher pitched · container

  • Smaller → TINIER smaller in the comparative is tinier
  • North → N N is the map abbreviation for north

The two words differ by a single letter, so the insertion is nearly invisible; comparative definitions like this one are a good check, because both clue and answer must end -er.

21 down AREA

Region better ventilated, did you say? (4)

Definition: Region · homophone

  • better ventilated → AREA a better ventilated room is airier, and airier spoken aloud is what you write in the grid

Comparatives make good soundalikes because the -er ending is swallowed in speech. Four squares and a question mark at the end of the clue is usually a sound clue.

22 down OUZO

Drink bouzoukist's drunk? (4)

Definition: Drink · hidden word

  • bouzoukist → OUZO the drink's name is buried whole inside the word for the player of the instrument

A bouzouki is the Greek long-necked lute, so the surface and the answer share a country — which is exactly why you read the word as scenery instead of searching it.
