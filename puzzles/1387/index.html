Across

1 across FIRE ALARM

Company receiving genuine anonymous warning (4,5)

Definition: warning · container + charade

  • Company → FIRM a company is a firm; it opens up to take the rest inside
  • genuine → REAL genuine = real
  • anonymous → A A = anonymous, the abbreviation used for an unnamed author

A for 'anonymous' is the abbreviation used for unsigned writing, worth banking. The surface suggests a whistleblower's tip; the answer is a much more literal warning.

6 across HASH

Possesses heroin and cannabis (4)

Definition: cannabis · charade

  • Possesses → HAS possesses = has
  • heroin → H H is street shorthand for heroin, a crossword staple

Every word of the surface stays inside the drugs world — possession charges and all — which is exactly why the simple HAS + H build is easy to miss.

8 across DRUMBEAT

Mad brute altering rhythm? (8)

Definition: rhythm · anagram

  • Mad brute → MADBRUTE anagram fodder, eight letters

'Mad' looks like the anagram signal but is actually fodder — 'altering' does the shuffling.

9 across REAGAN

Anger a volatile US President (6)

Definition: US President · anagram

  • Anger a → ANGERA anagram fodder, six letters

'Volatile' is an instability word, a classic anagram signal — and anger makes apt fodder for a head of state.

10 across TYRANT

Despot hurried into tryst regularly (6)

Definition: Despot · container + alternate letters

  • hurried → RAN hurried = ran
  • tryst → TYT the odd letters of TrYsT, taken 'regularly'

'Regularly' is the instruction to take every other letter — one of the quieter signals, easy to mistake for surface. The picture is a runner interrupting a secret meeting; the answer is nothing so romantic.

11 across ABU DHABI

A friend with addiction, briefly, in emirate (3,5)

Definition: emirate · charade + deletion

  • A → A the clue's own A
  • friend → BUD friend = bud, informal American
  • addiction → HABI HABIT (addiction) cut short by 'briefly'

The pieces respell themselves across the answer's two words: A BUD HABI(T) becomes ABU DHABI, capital of the UAE.

12 across LADDER

Large snake that gets one climbing? (6)

Definition: that gets one climbing · charade

  • Large → L L = large, as on clothing labels
  • snake → ADDER the adder is Britain's native venomous snake

A snake inside the answer to a climbing clue is a wink at Snakes and Ladders, where snakes send you down and ladders send you up.

15 across TRIANGLE

Integral changes in shape (8)

Definition: shape · anagram

  • Integral → INTEGRAL anagram fodder, eight letters

INTEGRAL and TRIANGLE are a classic anagram pair — the mathematical surface is a bonus.

16 across STEP ON IT

Wasted set point in hurry (4,2,2)

Definition: hurry · anagram

  • set point → SETPOINT anagram fodder, eight letters

A tennis surface hides a driving phrase: waste the SET POINT and you are left with an instruction to accelerate.

19 across GREECE

Announcement of oil in country (6)

Definition: country · homophone

  • oil → GREECE sounds like GREASE, an oily lubricant

GREASE said aloud gives the country — the same pun the musical Grease has traded on for decades.

21 across LIKEABLE

Tell story about Blake Lively being nice (8)

Definition: nice · container + anagram

  • Tell story → LIE to tell a story is to lie
  • Blake → KEABL BLAKE shuffled by 'Lively'

LIE goes 'about' (around) the scrambled BLAKE. The actor Blake Lively is pure misdirection — her surname is the anagram signal.

22 across REHEAR

Try again: what is stuck in bottom? (6)

Definition: Try again · container

  • what → EH 'Eh?' — the puzzled way of saying 'what?'
  • bottom → REAR bottom = rear

The definition is courtroom language: a case tried again is reheard. 'Eh?' for 'what?' is a little interjection worth banking.

24 across TUVALU

Where you might need to go, some might say, in Pacific islands (6)

Definition: Pacific islands · homophone

  • Where you might need to go → TUVALU said loosely, 'to a loo' — the place a person needing 'to go' is headed

Needing 'to go' is needing the loo; say 'to a loo' quickly and the island nation appears. 'Some might say' flags the deliberately loose pronunciation.

25 across ADMONISH

Rebuke beautiful Greek husband pinching millions (8)

Definition: Rebuke · container + charade

  • beautiful Greek → ADONIS Adonis, the byword for male beauty in Greek myth
  • husband → H H = husband, a standard abbreviation
  • millions → M M = millions

ADONIS and H make the 'beautiful Greek husband', who then pinches M near the front: AD(M)ONISH.

26 across MERE

Lake shimmered, somewhat (4)

Definition: Lake · hidden word

  • shimmered → MERE hidden in shimMEREd

'Somewhat' asks for only part of 'shimmered' — and a shimmering lake makes the surface picture-perfect.

27 across SALES SLIP

Receipt of disappointing news for rep? (5,4)

Definition: Receipt · double definition

  • Receipt → SALESSLIP a sales slip is the American term for a shop receipt
  • disappointing news for rep? → SALESSLIP read as a sentence: sales slip, i.e. fall — grim news for a sales rep

One phrase, two readings: the paper in the till, or sales taking a dive — which a sales rep would rather not hear about.

Down

1 down FORTY

Score twice, like A Castle? (5)

Definition: Score twice · double definition

  • Score twice → FORTY a score is twenty, so two of them make forty
  • like A Castle? → FORTY read the answer as FORT-Y: resembling a fort, i.e. a castle

The capitals in 'A Castle' suggest a person's name, but the clue wants FORT plus the adjective ending -Y — castle-like, as the question mark concedes.

2 down RIMBAUD

French poet traumatised Vietnam veteran on film, reportedly (7)

Definition: French poet · homophone

  • traumatised Vietnam veteran on film → RIMBAUD sounds like RAMBO, Sylvester Stallone's traumatised veteran in First Blood

Arthur Rimbaud and John Rambo are pronounced alike in English — a favourite crossword pairing of poet and action hero.

3 down AGENT

American chap is spy (5)

Definition: spy · charade

  • American → A A = American, a standard abbreviation
  • chap → GENT a chap is a gent, short for gentleman

A for American is a dictionary abbreviation worth banking; the rest is a chap being a GENT.

4 down ATTRACT

Content of dustcart taken up to entice (7)

Definition: entice · hidden word + reversal

  • dustcart taken → ATTRACT dus(TCARTTA)ken holds the letters; 'taken up' in this down entry reads them bottom to top

The answer hides backwards across 'dustcart taken': lift TCARTTA up the grid and it spells ATTRACT. Fitting that a dustcart clue turns on its contents.

5 down MARAUDING

Sacking main guard after reshuffle (9)

Definition: Sacking · anagram

  • main guard → MAINGUARD anagram fodder, nine letters

A guard being sacked is the surface joke: reshuffle MAIN GUARD and the guards become the raiders they were meant to stop.

6 down HEATHEN

Hard to consume chicken for non-believer (7)

Definition: non-believer · charade

  • Hard → H H = hard, as on pencil grades
  • consume → EAT consume = eat
  • chicken → HEN a chicken is a hen

Three short pieces in a row; the pencil-grade H = hard is the convention worth remembering.

7 down SHAMBOLIC

Claims hob broken, which is chaotic (9)

Definition: chaotic · anagram

  • Claims hob → CLAIMSHOB anagram fodder, nine letters

A broken hob is a nicely domestic disaster for a word meaning total disorder.

13 down ATTRIBUTE

Virtue of watching Björn Again show, perhaps (9)

Definition: Virtue · charade

  • watching → AT watching a show means being AT it
  • Björn Again show → TRIBUTE Björn Again are an ABBA tribute act, so their show is a tribute — 'perhaps' flags the example

Watching Björn Again means you are AT a TRIBUTE — the band are the best-known ABBA tribute act, punningly named for Björn Ulvaeus.

14 down RUNABOUTS

Organise a contest with son in small cars (9)

Definition: small cars · charade

  • Organise → RUN to organise an event is to run it
  • a → A the clue's own 'a'
  • contest → BOUT a contest, especially in boxing, is a bout
  • son → S S = son, the family-tree abbreviation

Four pieces chain together; only the boxing sense of 'contest' and the genealogist's S = son need spotting.

17 down PREFACE

Walk around official introduction (7)

Definition: introduction · container

  • Walk → PACE to walk = to pace
  • official → REF a match official is the ref

'Official' does double duty: it reads as an adjective in the surface, meaning formal, and works as a noun in the wordplay, meaning referee. That switch of part of speech is where the clue hides.

18 down TOENAIL

NATO lie about providing protective cover (7)

Definition: protective cover · anagram

  • NATO lie → NATOLIE anagram fodder, seven letters

Geopolitical intrigue dissolves into anatomy once 'about' scrambles NATO LIE.

20 down ETHANOL

Loan the drunk something bad for them? (7)

Definition: something bad for them · anagram

  • Loan the → LOANTHE anagram fodder, seven letters

'Drunk' does double duty: it scrambles LOAN THE, and it is also the 'them' the definition points back at — a neat little loop.

22 down REMUS

Legendary brother beginning to raise flightless birds (5)

Definition: Legendary brother · charade + first letter

  • raise → R the beginning of Raise
  • flightless birds → EMUS emus cannot fly

Remus was one of Rome's legendary twin founders, suckled by a wolf — which makes 'raise' a sly word to take a beginning from.

23 down AESOP

A model upset Greek storyteller (5)

Definition: Greek storyteller · charade + reversal

  • A → A the clue's own A
  • model → ESOP POSE (to model, as for a portrait) turned upwards

In a down entry 'upset' flips POSE on its head. The surface's difficult model is a red herring.
