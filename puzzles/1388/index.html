Across

1 across BUTCHER

Make a mess of Schubert, missing opening when playing (7)

Definition: Make a mess of · anagram + deletion

  • Schubert, missing opening → CHUBERT SCHUBERT drops its opening S, leaving seven letters to shuffle

The composer loses his first letter and the rest is rearranged — 'playing' is the anagram signal hiding inside the musical surface.

5 across CHICAGO

US city smart in the past (7)

Definition: US city · charade

  • smart → CHIC chic = stylish, smart
  • in the past → AGO as in 'long ago'

The surface makes 'smart in the past' feel like one idea — a fashion that has dated — and that is exactly what hides the join between the two halves.

9 across GROAN

Sound of discomfort from good red horse? (5)

Definition: Sound of discomfort · charade

  • good → G a standard abbreviation
  • red horse? → ROAN a roan has a reddish-brown coat — the question mark admits it's loose

A roan is a horse with a reddish coat, so 'red horse?' is fair with its question mark. G for 'good' does the rest.

10 across COMPLAINT

Frenchman not elaborate in bed, leading to protest (9)

Definition: protest · charade + container

  • Frenchman → M M = Monsieur, the crossword's standard Frenchman
  • not elaborate → PLAIN a plain synonym
  • bed → COT the bed that wraps around M + PLAIN

M is Monsieur and COT the bed doing the wrapping. The definition 'protest' is the noun sense, as in lodging a complaint.

11 across TESTAMENT

What’s left in religious text? (9)

Definition: What’s left · double definition

  • What’s left → TESTAMENT a last will and testament sets out what someone has left
  • religious text → TESTAMENT the Bible divides into the Old and New Testaments

The split comes at 'in': a will on one side, the Bible on the other, with the question mark nodding at the wordplay-free construction.

12 across EQUIP

Provide material for joke after end of game (5)

Definition: Provide material for · charade + last letter

  • joke → QUIP a quip is a witty remark
  • end of game → E the last letter of 'game'

Watch the order: 'after' places QUIP behind the E, so the single letter leads despite being named second.

13 across DRESS

Garment rejected among lesser designs? (5)

Definition: Garment · hidden word + reversal

  • lesser designs → DRESS read backwards inside the phrase: lesSER Designs

'Among' says the answer hides in plain sight and 'rejected' turns it around — in an across entry, sent back means read right to left.

15 across TRAMLINES

Rebuilt terminals for transport routes (9)

Definition: transport routes · anagram

  • terminals → TERMINALS anagram fodder, nine letters

One word supplies all nine letters — TERMINALS 'rebuilt' — and the station surface keeps the fodder camouflaged as railway scenery.

18 across PLUTOCRAT

Cartoon dog and cat seizing tail of another fat cat? (9)

Definition: fat cat? · charade + container + last letter

  • Cartoon dog → PLUTO Disney's dog
  • cat → CAT wraps around the seized letter
  • tail of another → R the last letter of 'another'

Disney's Pluto meets a cat that has swallowed the tail of 'another'. 'Fat cat' for a moneyed magnate gives the surface its wink.

19 across HAREM

Area of New York evicting 50 women living together? (5)

Definition: women living together? · deletion

  • Area of New York → HARLEM the Manhattan district, about to lose a letter
  • 50 → L the Roman numeral for 50 — the letter evicted

HARLEM is the New York district; evicting its Roman-numeral 50 leaves the answer. Deletion clues often disguise the doomed letter as a number.

21 across LINED

End of quatrain, say, showing signs of age? (5)

Definition: showing signs of age? · charade

  • End of quatrain, say → LINED a quatrain's four lines can be labelled a to d, so its end is 'line d'

The trick is naming a quatrain's fourth line 'line d', as rhyme schemes label verses a to d — 'say' concedes it is one example of such labelling.

23 across SWEET PEAS

Multiple bloomers in having veg after dessert (5,4)

Definition: Multiple bloomers · charade

  • dessert → SWEET the sweet course, in British usage
  • veg → PEAS placed 'after' the dessert

SWEET as a noun — the pudding course — is a British usage worth knowing, and 'bloomers' means flowers here, not underwear.

25 across RESILIENT

Is taken aback by deception in lease? Readily recovering (9)

Definition: Readily recovering · reversal + charade + container

  • Is → SI IS reversed — 'taken aback'
  • deception → LIE a plain synonym
  • lease? → RENT the lease that wraps around SI + LIE

'Taken aback' reverses IS, and 'in lease' wraps RENT around the middle. Three mechanisms in one clue, each with its own signal.

26 across BRACE

Support British competition (5)

Definition: Support · charade

  • British → B a standard abbreviation
  • competition → RACE a plain synonym

As simple as charades come: B for British in front of a race. The definition is the verb, as in bracing a shelf.

27 across MALAYAN

Some south-east Asian fellow penning a song (7)

Definition: Some south-east Asian · charade + container

  • fellow → MAN split M…AN around what he pens in
  • a → A used literally
  • song → LAY a lay is an old poetic word for a song

'Penning' means enclosing — the MAN pens in A LAY. A lay as a song is archaic vocabulary that survives mainly in crosswords.

28 across CHEDDAR

Gorge in Somerset? He had to take daughter in car (7)

Definition: Gorge in Somerset? · charade + container

  • He had → HED contracted to HE'D
  • daughter → D a standard family-tree abbreviation
  • car → CAR closes around HE'D + D

HE'D — 'he had' — takes D for daughter, and the car closes around them both. Cheddar the gorge, not the cheese, is the destination.

Down

1 down BIGOTED

Substantial religious books edited, ignoring alternative views? (7)

Definition: ignoring alternative views? · charade

  • Substantial → BIG a plain synonym
  • religious books → OT the Old Testament — a crossword staple
  • edited → ED the standard abbreviation, as in 'ed.'

Two crossword staples do the work: OT for the Old Testament and ED for 'edited'. The question mark softens a definition that describes rather than names.

2 down TROUSSEAU

Uses a tour to move around? It’s for after the wedding (9)

Definition: It’s for after the wedding · anagram

  • Uses a tour → USESATOUR anagram fodder, nine letters

Nothing in the touring surface hints at weddings — the mark of a well-disguised anagram. The French spelling makes the letter run hard to see.

3 down HENNA

Hair dye Anne Hathaway initially promoted (5)

Definition: Hair dye · charade + first letter + reversal

  • Anne → ANNE the actress's first name, used literally
  • Hathaway initially → H the first letter of Hathaway

'Promoted' is a lift: in a down entry, sending ANNE H upwards reads it bottom to top. Celebrity names are favourite fodder for initial letters.

4 down ROCHESTER

List includes revolutionary romantic hero (9)

Definition: romantic hero · container

  • List → ROSTER a roster is a list — it wraps around CHE
  • revolutionary → CHE Che Guevara, the crossword's default revolutionary

CHE for 'revolutionary' is worth memorising. Here he slots into a roster, and the definition points at Jane Eyre's brooding Mr Rochester.

5 down COMET

Old aeroplane company satisfied (5)

Definition: Old aeroplane · charade

  • company → CO the standard abbreviation, as in '& Co'
  • satisfied → MET as demands or conditions are met

CO for company is standard, and 'satisfied' gives MET as in 'demands met'. The definition recalls the de Havilland Comet, the first jet airliner.

6 down ILL HEALTH

I left the hall, struggling – sickness? (3,6)

Definition: sickness · anagram

  • I → I used literally in the fodder
  • left → L l = left, joining the fodder rather than pointing a direction
  • the hall → THEHALL the rest of the fodder, seven letters

'Left' contributes its abbreviation L to the fodder rather than acting as a direction — a favourite bit of misdirection in anagram clues.

7 down ADIEU

Finish investing in a University? Farewell (5)

Definition: Farewell · container

  • Finish → DIE to finish is to die, as an engine or a battery does
  • a University? → AU A plus U for university — the shell DIE is invested in

'Investing in' is the container signal: what you invest, you clothe. DIE for 'finish' is gentler than it looks — think of an engine dying.

8 down OCTOPUS

A month’s work to get sea creature (7)

Definition: sea creature · charade

  • A month’s → OCT October abbreviated; the possessive ’s just glues the surface
  • work → OPUS a musical or literary work — Latin, and a crossword staple

OCT is the calendar abbreviation and OPUS the composer's 'work'; together they assemble something unexpectedly tentacled.

14 down SWORDPLAY

Small component of clue? It’s combative stuff (9)

Definition: combative stuff · charade

  • Small → S as on clothing labels
  • component of clue? → WORDPLAY wordplay is the mechanical half of every cryptic clue

A self-referential treat: wordplay really is a component of every cryptic clue, and adding 'small' itself (S) completes the answer.

16 down AUTHENTIC

Real gold subsequently getting mark of correctness? Not quite (9)

Definition: Real · charade + deletion

  • gold → AU the chemical symbol for gold
  • subsequently → THEN a plain synonym
  • mark of correctness? → TIC a TICK that is 'not quite' finished — the K falls off

'Not quite' clips the K off TICK. AU for gold is one of the commonest crossword conventions, straight from the periodic table.

17 down NURSEMAID

Nanny with fake surname on documents? (9)

Definition: Nanny · anagram + charade

  • surname → NURSEMA anagram of SURNAME, signalled by 'fake'
  • documents? → ID identity documents

'Fake' shuffles SURNAME, and 'on' stacks it above ID — in a down entry, 'on' means on top of. ID for 'documents' is a modern staple.

18 down PILGRIM

Large house dismissing last dour religious traveller (7)

Definition: religious traveller · charade + deletion

  • Large house → PIL a PILE — a grand mansion — dismissing its last letter
  • dour → GRIM a plain synonym

'Dismissing last' docks the E from PILE, a pile being a grand mansion in British usage. GRIM for 'dour' completes the trip.

20 down MISHEAR

Fail to understand I’m upset over cut (7)

Definition: Fail to understand · reversal + charade

  • I’m → MI I'M turned upside down
  • cut → SHEAR as sheep are shorn

'Upset' flips I'M, which is fair in a down entry; 'over' then places MI above SHEAR, just as the grid reads.

22 down NASAL

American agency taking line regarding the nose (5)

Definition: regarding the nose · charade

  • American agency → NASA the US space agency
  • line → L a standard abbreviation

NASA is the American agency and L the abbreviation for line. The answer is an adjective, matching the 'regarding' phrasing.

23 down STERN

Rear of ship is part of coaster, note (5)

Definition: Rear of ship · hidden word

  • coaster, note → STERN the letters straddle the comma: coaSTER, Note

'Part of' marks a hidden answer, and the letters straddle the punctuation — running across a break is the setter's favourite camouflage.

24 down TABLE

Furniture item not likely to wobble? Not at first (5)

Definition: Furniture item · deletion

  • not likely to wobble? → STABLE STABLE, about to lose its first letter

'Not at first' removes the initial S from STABLE — and the wobble question is the clue's own joke about tables.
