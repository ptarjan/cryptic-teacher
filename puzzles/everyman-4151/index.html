Across

1 across ABOARD

Ace meals on a ship (6)

Definition: on a ship · charade

  • Ace → A the card's own letter, the one printed in its corner
  • meals → BOARD board is the food half of room and board — what a landlady provides at table

Nothing in the surface points to the sea: 'Ace meals' reads as a compliment about the catering, which is exactly where the two pieces are standing.

5 across DOTAGE

You shouldn't have got into setter, perhaps Everyman's beginning to show feebleness (6)

Definition: feebleness · container + first letter

  • setter, perhaps → DOG a setter is one breed among many, so the general animal is wanted — 'perhaps' marks the example
  • Everyman's beginning → E the opening letter of the name this puzzle is signed with
  • You shouldn't have → TA what you say on being handed an unexpected present is thanks, and this is the two-letter British version of it

Everyman signs his own puzzles, so his name in a clue usually means him; here it is only its first letter, and the setter that matters is the animal.

8 across INK-BLOT TEST

Startled kittens bolt in psych procedure (3-4,4)

Definition: psych procedure · anagram

  • kittens bolt → INKBLOTTEST eleven letters of frightened cat, which is exactly the eleven the entry needs

The surface is a vet's waiting room and the answer is a psychologist's couch. Note where the anagram indicator sits: first word, passing for scene-setting.

11 across MAKE A WISH

Birthday announcement pathetic, a mawkish constant (4,1,4)

Definition: Birthday announcement · anagram

  • a mawkish → AMAWKISH eight of the nine letters, taken from the article and the adjective as they stand
  • constant → E e is the mathematician's constant, 2.718..., joining the shuffle as a single letter

'Pathetic' looks like a verdict on the birthday card and is the instruction instead. Everyman enjoys an anagram whose fodder reads as an insult.

12 across ACNED

Spotty old hat for a Cockney (5)

Definition: Spotty · homophone + deletion

  • old hat → HACKNEYED anything old hat has been worn out by overuse, which is what this longer word means
  • for a Cockney → ACNED with the aitch gone, what is left is said exactly as the much shorter spelling is, though the two share barely half their letters

Say 'hackneyed' without its aitch and you have said the answer. The joke is that a Cockney's 'old hat' turns out to describe a teenager's complexion.

13 across GRANDPA

He's related Greek saga's finale including, additionally, prince (7)

Definition: He's related · container + last letter

  • Greek → GR Gr is the standard short form for Greek, and Greece's international car plate
  • additionally → AND one thing additionally to another is one and another
  • prince → P P is the abbreviation for prince, as on a royal monogram
  • saga's finale → A the last letter of sagA

Everything after the definition reads like literary criticism — a Greek epic, its ending, a prince — and every word of it is an abbreviation or a single letter.

14 across UNICORN

Unlisted (not indexed) company offering riches nowadays, primarily? (7)

Definition: Unlisted (not indexed) company offering riches nowadays, primarily? · &lit + first letters

  • Unlisted (not indexed) company offering riches nowadays → UNICORN the opening letter of each of these seven words in turn, the bracketed pair counting exactly like the rest

An all-in-one: the sentence describes a private company worth a fortune and spells it at the same time. The brackets are the only thing making the count awkward.

15 across ANDORRA

Part of Finland - or, rather, another country (7)

Definition: another country · hidden word

  • Finland - or, rather → ANDORRA seven consecutive letters running from inside the first country's name through the aside that follows it

A dash and two commas fall inside the run and not one of them interrupts it: punctuation is invisible to a hidden word, which is what makes this one hard to see.

17 across TEDIOUS

Wearisome after time, editor with promises of payment (7)

Definition: Wearisome · charade

  • time → T T is the standard short form of time, as on a stopwatch
  • editor → ED Ed is the newspaper's editor, the form used on a masthead
  • promises of payment → IOUS an IOU is a scribbled promise to pay, and more than one of them takes the plural

Three abbreviations in a row, dressed as a complaint about the office. IOU is worth knowing — 'I owe you' spelled by its sound — because setters use it constantly.

20 across DROLL

On reflection, Liberal parliamentarian is amusing (5)

Definition: amusing · charade + reversal

  • Liberal → L L is the ballot paper's abbreviation for Liberal
  • parliamentarian → LORD a member of Britain's upper house sits in Parliament without ever having been elected to it

'On reflection' opens the clue as a phrase about second thoughts, which is how a reversal indicator likes to arrive: sounding like a comment on the surface.

21 across OUTGROWTH

How gut-rot's provoked? (9)

Definition: How gut-rot's provoked? · &lit + anagram

  • How gut-rot → OUTGROWTH the nine letters of the question's opening, re-sorted

An all-in-one: the same words ask the question and spell the answer. Gut-rot is British slang for cheap spirits, the sort that burn on the way down.

22 across TAXIDERMIST

Stuffy type occupied with relics of the past? (11)

Definition: Stuffy type occupied with relics of the past? · cryptic definition

  • Stuffy type → the reading the surface pushes: a pompous bore in an airless room, the sort who lectures you about antiques
  • occupied with relics of the past → the setter means it literally — the work is done on creatures that stopped being alive some time ago

'Stuffy' does two jobs at once: the adjective for a boring man, and the verb for what this trade actually does to a dead fox before it goes on the wall.

23 across FRIDAY

Good is the one that comes yearly (6)

Definition: Good is the one that comes yearly · cryptic definition

  • Good → the reading the surface pushes: an adjective praising whichever one is meant, as though several were being compared
  • the one that comes yearly → a weekday comes round every seven days; fix the right word in front of it and the name belongs to a single date in the church calendar

The joke is the capital G: as a label rather than praise, it turns an ordinary weekday into the solemn day before Easter, which comes once a year.

24 across DREARY

Gloomy and arid, capturing attention (6)

Definition: Gloomy · container

  • arid → DRY arid ground has no moisture left in it at all
  • attention → EAR to lend an ear, or to have someone's ear, is to give or be given attention

'Capturing' is the only instruction and it arrives late: the first three words read as two adjectives sharing a comma, which is how the container stays hidden.

2 down BRICKS-AND-MORTAR

Stand back! Mirror, perversely, is not online (6-3-6)

Definition: not online · anagram

  • Stand back! Mirror → BRICKSANDMORTAR an exclamation and a newspaper title, fifteen letters between them — exactly the fifteen the entry needs

The italics make Mirror a newspaper, and both it and 'Stand back!' promise a reversal. Neither delivers: they are fodder, and 'perversely' is the clue's only instruction.

3 down AWKWARD

Graceless sabre-rattler not starting to usher in war dead (7)

Definition: Graceless · charade + deletion

  • sabre-rattler not starting → AWK a hawk is the warmonger of the political aviary, and the bird is taken minus the letter it opens with
  • war → WAR the clue's own word, lifted whole and unaltered
  • dead → D d is the standard short form of dead, as printed on a family tree

Sabre-rattling is threatening war rather than making it, and the hawk is the bird that kind of talk belongs to. The definition hides at the front, looking like scene-setting.

4 down DALAI LAMA

Spiritual leader in a state, a chap's written up (5,4)

Definition: Spiritual leader · charade + reversal

  • a state → AMALI the article, then Mali, the landlocked West African republic
  • a chap → ALAD the article again, then the informal word for a young man

Read from the bottom, the four small pieces regroup entirely: the joins in the wordplay fall nowhere near the join between the answer's two words.

5 down DITCH

Drop in moat? (5)

Definition: Drop · double definition

  • Drop → the verb: to drop a plan, a habit or a passenger is to abandon it and move on
  • moat → the noun: the trench dug round a castle wall, water in it or not, is one sort of this

Three words, and the surface is one continuous picture — a drop of water in a moat — so nothing at all marks the place where the first meaning stops.

6 down THE MAGI

Those people: one soldier; three kings (3,4)

Definition: three kings · charade

  • Those people → THEM the pronoun you would use for those people if you were pointing at them
  • one → A one soldier is a soldier — the article does the counting
  • soldier → GI GI is the American enlisted man of every war film

'Those people' and 'one soldier' read as a sentence about troops, and the semicolon does a comma's work: three separate pieces, laid end to end.

7 down GET INTO HOT WATER

Experience mishap when visiting geyser? (3,4,3,5)

Definition: Experience mishap · charade

  • visiting → GETINTO to visit a place is to enter it, the phrase you would use of a house you finally managed to reach
  • geyser? → HOTWATER an Icelandic geyser is a jet of exactly that, thrown boiling out of the ground — one example standing for the general thing

The trouble idiom and the tourist attraction are the same four words, which is the whole joke: a literal visit to one would land you in the figurative other.

9 down SMOG

Foul air from small cat (4)

Definition: Foul air · charade

  • small → S S is the size on a clothing label
  • cat → MOG a mog, or moggy, is British household slang for a cat of no particular breed

Two ordinary words with nothing odd about either, which is the difficulty: nothing in 'small cat' announces that it is spelling rather than describing.

10 down ADEN

Major port in Pasadena (4)

Definition: Major port · hidden word

  • Pasadena → ADEN four consecutive letters inside the Californian city's name, beginning at its third

A Californian suburb with a Yemeni harbour buried in it, and no comma or hyphen to mark the seam — long place names are a hidden word's favourite cover.

14 down UNTUTORED

Turned out, sadly, to be ignorant (9)

Definition: ignorant · anagram

  • Turned out → UNTUTORED the nine letters of the opening phrase, re-sorted

'Turned out' wants to be the instruction — turning is exactly what setters ask for — but here it is the material, and the meek adverb beside it does the ordering.

15 down AIDE

Help with a passport, perhaps as embassy opens (4)

Definition: Help · charade + first letter

  • a passport, perhaps → AID the article, then the two letters any document proving who you are is called — a passport being one example
  • embassy → E the first letter of the building named here

Passport and embassy are both there for the travel picture, and only one of them survives as a whole piece of wordplay; the other is cut to a single letter.

16 down RELAXED

Rush around California airport that's restful (7)

Definition: restful · container

  • Rush → REED a rush is a marsh plant, and the setter wants the other tall stem that grows in the same wet ground
  • California airport → LAX LAX is the three-letter code for Los Angeles International, printed on every bag tag

'Rush' is the trap: it reads as speed and it is a plant. Airport codes are worth collecting — three letters, used the way setters use chemical symbols.

18 down DORMICE

Rodents cut up - headless worm inside (7)

Definition: Rodents · container + deletion

  • cut up → DICE to cut something up into small cubes is to dice it
  • headless worm → ORM the wriggler minus the letter it starts with

In a down entry 'cut up' promises a reversal and delivers a kitchen verb instead. The dash is Everyman separating the halves without saying anything.

19 down SOHO

I'm surprised map-maker's upset part of London (4)

Definition: part of London · charade + reversal

  • I'm surprised → OH the noise you make when something takes you unawares
  • map-maker's → OS the Ordnance Survey is Britain's national mapping agency, known everywhere by its two initials

'Upset' reads as annoyed, describing the poor cartographer, and is really the instruction: read the two pieces from the bottom of the entry upwards.

21 down ODDLY

In a weird way, how number's found in the end? (5)

Definition: In a weird way · double definition

  • In a weird way → the plain adverb: strangely, in a manner that does not fit what was expected
  • how number's found in the end → count through the letters of 'the end' and take the first, third and fifth: T, E, N, a number sitting at the positions the answer is named for

The second half is a puzzle in miniature: 'the end' holds TEN if you take alternate letters from the start, and the answer names the way you had to read.
