Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,883

Across

1 across AGONISED

San Diego quakes were really worrying (8)

Definition: were really worrying · anagram

  • San Diego → SANDIEGO the city's name supplies eight letters and no meaning; they go into the grid in a different arrangement

The surface is a news bulletin, and that is what keeps a Californian city looking like scenery rather than like the raw material the clue is handing you.

6 across REGIFT

Pass on concerning file with images left in taxi (6)

Definition: Pass on · charade + first letter

  • concerning → RE re means concerning, the word heading a memo or a letter
  • file with images → GIF a GIF is the picture file format, animated or not
  • taxi → T the letter at the front of the word, the one on its left

'Left in taxi' reads as lost property on the back seat, which is the whole misdirection: nothing has been forgotten anywhere, and the word is being pointed at rather than searched.

9 across FAST

Fixed ahead of time (4)

Definition: Fixed · double definition

  • Fixed → of a knot or a dye: held so firmly that nothing will shift it or wash it out
  • ahead of time → of a clock that gains: it shows an hour that has not arrived yet

The two senses have nothing to do with each other, and a third — going without food — is the one 26 across borrows. Four letters, three unrelated jobs.

10 across OVERGROUND

Maybe too powdery on the surface (10)

Definition: on the surface · charade

  • too → OVER over- in front of a word means too much of it: over-cautious, over-cooked
  • powdery → GROUND what has been ground fine is powder, so the two words describe one state
  • Maybe → it softens the equation that follows — something ground may be powdery rather than must be — and hands over no letters of its own

The trap is 'powdery on the surface', which reads as a single phrase about snow. The definition is only the last three words, and the wordplay stops before them.

11 across PROCRASTINATE

Poorly, so partier can't make it later (13)

Definition: make it later · anagram

  • so partier can't → SOPARTIERCANT thirteen letters, exactly what the grid asks for, waiting to be put in another order

'Poorly' does two jobs at once: the excuse in the surface story and the instruction in the wordplay, which is why it reads as scenery rather than as a signal.

13 across LEAN ON

Middle Eastern country spending billions to intimidate (4,2)

Definition: to intimidate · deletion

  • Middle Eastern country → LEBANON Lebanon sits on the Mediterranean coast, north of Israel
  • billions → B b is the usual short form for billions in a set of accounts

'Spending billions' reads as government policy right up to the moment it turns into an instruction, and the country that loses its B is left threatening somebody.

15 across PUT FIRST

Make key golf shot around trees (3,5)

Definition: Make key · container

  • golf shot → PUTT a putt is the stroke played on the green, with the flat-faced club
  • trees → FIRS firs are the evergreens, and four letters of them go inside

'Make key' is the definition and looks like the beginning of an instruction about cutting one. The golf course is the surface's own invention.

16 across WINNIPEG

Overcome feeling of cold, say, in Canadian city (8)

Definition: Canadian city · charade

  • Overcome → WIN to overcome your opponents is to win
  • feeling of cold → NIP a nip in the air is the sharp feel of cold weather
  • say → EG say introduces an example, which is what eg stands for

'Overcome feeling of cold' reads as one idea, soldiering on through the chill, and that is the join the clue is hiding: three separate pieces pretending to be a phrase.

19 across EVEN SO

Flat and incredibly still (4,2)

Definition: still · charade

  • Flat → EVEN a flat surface is an even one, with no bumps in it
  • incredibly → SO so does the work of very in speech: so good, so tired

'Still' at the end reads as part of the description, flat and motionless, when it is the definition — and the sense wanted is 'nevertheless' rather than 'not moving'.

21 across MIXED METAPHOR

Is one white elephant in the room? (5,8)

Definition: Is one white elephant in the room? · cryptic definition

  • white elephant → one stock figure of speech: a possession that costs a fortune and does nothing useful
  • elephant in the room → a quite different figure of speech, for the obvious thing nobody will mention — and the clue has welded the two together at the word they share

Two idioms have been joined at the word they have in common, and the sentence that results is an example of the very thing it is asking you to name.

23 across DRAMATISED

Shot by wayward side put in play, perhaps (10)

Definition: put in play, perhaps · charade + anagram

  • Shot → DRAM a dram is a shot of whisky
  • by → AT by and at both mean beside: waiting by the door, waiting at the door
  • side → ISED the same four letters, in a different order, finish the word

The surface is a football match, so 'put in play' reads as a ball coming back into the game rather than as a script. Only 'wayward' is an instruction.

25 across TACK

Stitch silk lining for hats back to front (4)

Definition: Stitch · charade + middle letters + reversal

  • silk → KC a senior British barrister is a King's Counsel, and being appointed one is called taking silk
  • hats → AT the two letters inside the word, with the outer pair stripped away

The misdirection is that hats and linings belong together, so you look for a fabric. The lining wanted is the middle of a word, not the inside of a hat.

26 across LENTEN

Allowed to use Elton's clothing during 9? (6)

Definition: during 9 · charade + outer letters

  • Allowed to use → LENT if you allowed somebody to use your car, you lent it to them
  • Elton's clothing → EN the first and last letters of the singer's name, the ones on the outside

The 9 is a cross-reference to 9 across, where the word means going without food as well as fixed and quick, and it is that third sense the definition needs.

27 across RIESLING

Sir Nigel turned white (8)

Definition: white · anagram

  • Sir Nigel → SIRNIGEL the knight's title and name supply eight letters between them, to be taken in another order

'Turned' looks like what happens to wine that has gone off, which is the joke: the only word in the clue meaning anything is the definition, and the knight is letters wearing a name.

Down

2 down GRAPPLE

Wrestle gross eater? (7)

Definition: Wrestle · charade

  • gross → GR gr is the standard short form of gross, the dozen dozen a wholesaler counts in
  • eater → APPLE a British greengrocer calls a variety you bite into raw an eater, as opposed to a cooker

Both words change job the moment you stop reading the surface: 'gross' turns from disgusting into a number, and 'eater' from a person into a fruit.

3 down NITRO

Stuff that explodes can upset returning troops (5)

Definition: Stuff that explodes · charade + reversal

  • can upset → NIT a can is a TIN, stood on its head
  • returning troops → RO OR is the army's other ranks, the ordinary soldiers, and it comes back to front

Two turnarounds in a row, and the surface hides both: a can of drink and a column of soldiers make one picture, and neither word looks like an instruction inside it.

4 down SCOURING PAD

Aggressive cleaner ransacking home (8,3)

Definition: Aggressive cleaner · charade

  • ransacking → SCOURING to ransack a place is to scour it, turning it over in search of something
  • home → PAD a pad is slang for the place somebody lives

The surface is a burglary, and the definition sits in front of it: 'Aggressive cleaner' looks like a person with a mop until you hear it as the thing doing the scrubbing.

5 down DRESS UP

Prepare for party with various reds to drink (5,2)

Definition: Prepare for party · anagram + charade

  • reds → DRES the four letters of the wines, in a new arrangement, open the answer
  • to drink → SUP to sup is to drink, chiefly in northern English use

'Prepare for party' looks like wordplay and the wines like the definition, because a glass of red is easier to picture than an instruction. It is the other way round.

6 down RIG

Doctor half justified claims (3)

Definition: Doctor · deletion

  • justified claims → RIGHTS a claim you are entitled to is a right, and the plural of that runs to six letters

'Doctor' looks like the person and is the verb: to doctor a result is to fiddle it. 'Justified claims' looks like a legal complaint rather than one word waiting to be cut in two.

7 down GLOBALISE

Damaging middle of smelly bag, soil spread everywhere (9)

Definition: spread everywhere · anagram + middle letters

  • middle of smelly → EL the word has six letters, so its centre is the third and fourth
  • bag, soil → BAGSOIL seven more letters join that pair, and the nine are then rearranged together

'Spread everywhere' reads as part of the mess the surface describes — soil over the floor — which is exactly why it takes a while to see it as the definition.

8 down FINGERS

Loud sopranos perhaps wanting top piano players? (7)

Definition: piano players · charade + deletion

  • Loud → F f is forte, the direction printed in a score to play loudly
  • sopranos perhaps → INGERS sopranos are one kind of singer, and that word turns up with its first letter gone

'Perhaps' is the tell that sopranos are an example rather than a synonym: the word you want is the general one they belong to.

12 down IN THE SADDLE

500 diamonds to pack among discounted items, mounted (2,3,6)

Definition: mounted · container

  • discounted items → IN THE SALE goods with money off are in the sale, and this phrase opens up to take something
  • 500 → D D is the Roman numeral for five hundred
  • diamonds → D D is the card player's shorthand for diamonds

The surface is a shoplifting story, so 'mounted' at the end reads as stock piled up rather than as the definition. Two Ds arrive from two quite different conventions.

14 down NON-EXEMPT

Gas rising and depleted, almost bound to be taxed (3-6)

Definition: bound to be taxed · reversal + charade + deletion

  • Gas rising → NONEX xenon is the noble gas, and here it is climbing the column
  • depleted → EMPT a depleted store is empty, and that word arrives one letter short

The hyphen falls in the wrong place for the wordplay: the join between the two pieces is two letters later than the grid's, which is what makes the halves hard to see.

17 down IMMERSE

Swimmer seen grabbing duck (7)

Definition: duck · hidden word

  • Swimmer seen → IMMERSE seven letters run straight on across the gap between these two words, already in the order you need

'Duck' looks like the bird being grabbed, which is the whole disguise: it is the definition, and 'Swimmer' is not a definition at all but half the hiding place.

18 down GREASER

Gang member John Travolta plays as Romeo, after cast agrees (7)

Definition: Gang member John Travolta plays · anagram + charade

  • agrees → GREASE these six letters rearrange into the title of the film the surface is talking about
  • Romeo → R Romeo is R in the radio alphabet

The film turns up twice: once as the role Travolta played, once as the six letters 'agrees' rearranges into. One of those is the definition, the other the wordplay.

20 down SARACEN

Old Arab hospital admitting certain type of people? (7)

Definition: Old Arab · container

  • hospital → SAN a san is a sanatorium, the sick bay at a British boarding school
  • certain type of people → RACE one particular kind of people, taken as a group, is a race

The surface is a hospital admissions story, so 'admitting' reads as ordinary English right up to the moment it becomes an instruction. The question mark covers the vagueness of 'certain type'.

22 down HOTEL

This sending up a little tipple to honeymooners? (5)

Definition: This sending up a little tipple to honeymooners? · hidden word + reversal + &lit

  • tipple to honeymooners → HOTEL five letters sit in a row across these words, waiting to be read the other way up

Every word does both jobs at once: read straight, the clue describes what the place does for its guests; read as instructions, it points at five letters running upwards through its last two words.

24 down TIN

Reduced very small part of bronze (3)

Definition: part of bronze · deletion

  • very small → TINY very small is tiny, and that word loses its tail

Bronze is an alloy of copper and one other metal, which is where the definition comes from, while 'Reduced very small' reads as a description of a shrunken statue.
