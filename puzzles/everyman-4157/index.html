Across

1 across TSETSE

Fly - or endlessly, repeatedly to sea (6)

Definition: Fly · charade + deletion

  • to sea → TSE TO docked of its last character leaves T, and SEA docked of its last leaves SE
  • to sea → TSE the same trimmed pair a second time, since one copy is only half the length wanted

The surface is a grumble about a long voyage, and the trap is reading 'or' as the start of the wordplay: it is only the word joining the definition to it.

5 across ABBOTS

Brothers who may give prior approval (6)

Definition: Brothers · double definition

  • Brothers → ABBOTS the men who head a monastery are members of its brotherhood, monks like the rest of them
  • who may give prior approval → ABBOTS a prior is the deputy who runs a monastery under its head, so approving one is a job for the rank above

'Prior approval' is the sort of phrase a form asks for, which is the misdirection: the prior here is a person, a monastery's second in command, and the approval is his superior's.

8 across ABRACADABRA

A supporter twice defending scoundrel, it might be said - for a spell (11)

Definition: a spell · charade + container

  • A supporter → ABRA the letter A followed by the undergarment that does the supporting
  • scoundrel → CAD a cad is an ungentlemanly rotter, three letters long
  • twice → ABRA the same four letters again, this time on the far side
  • it might be said → surface only: it points at a magic word being uttered, and hands over no letters

'For a spell' means 'for a while' in the surface and 'as a magic word' in the answer, and the supporter is underwear rather than a fan in the stands.

11 across ROBIN HOOD

One in the green helping those in the red? (5,4)

Definition: One in the green · double definition

  • One in the green → ROBINHOOD the outlaw of the ballads wore Lincoln green and lived in the greenwood of Sherwood Forest
  • helping those in the red? → ROBINHOOD to be in the red is to be in debt, and this man took from the rich and handed it to the poor

Two colours and two idioms: Lincoln green is what the legend wore, being in the red is owing money, and the modern banking phrase is put to medieval work.

12 across LEARY

LSD enthusiast allegedly revolutionising youth in their heads? (5)

Definition: LSD enthusiast allegedly revolutionising youth in their heads? · first letters + &lit

  • LSD enthusiast allegedly revolutionising youth → LEARY the opening letter of each of the five words, read in the order given

Timothy Leary was the Harvard psychologist sacked over his LSD experiments, who told the sixties to turn on and drop out — so the sentence is a fair description as well as a recipe.

13 across MARCONI

Study hard about ... about ... primarily Italian ... Italian engineer (7)

Definition: Italian engineer · charade + reversal + first letter

  • Study hard → MARC to cram is to study hard, and turned round those four letters read the other way
  • about → ON a book about a subject is a book on it, so the one preposition stands in for the other
  • primarily Italian → I the opening letter of the nationality

The ellipses are stage business, making the surface stumble like somebody groping for a name; they carry no letters and nothing hangs on them.

14 across PINK GIN

Kingpin mixed drink (4,3)

Definition: drink · anagram

  • Kingpin → KINGPIN seven letters of fodder: the boss of the outfit, taken as one word
  • mixed → anagram indicator

'Mixed drink' is a phrase you read as a unit, which is what hides the seam: 'mixed' belongs to the word in front of it, and 'drink' stands alone.

15 across HOUSING

Accommodation husband finding stimulating - run away (7)

Definition: Accommodation · charade + deletion

  • husband → H the standard abbreviation on a form, and a crossword staple
  • run → R a run is entered as R on a cricket scorecard, so the single letter is what goes
  • stimulating → OUSING something stimulating is rousing, and that word minus its R is what remains

'Run away' looks like flight in the surface; in the wordplay it is a cricketer's single letter and the order to lose it.

17 across NIGERIA

With head cleared, migraine treated somewhere in W Africa (7)

Definition: somewhere in W Africa · deletion + anagram

  • head → M the head of the ailment's name is its opening letter, and that is what gets cleared
  • migraine → IGRAINE the headache word minus that opening letter, seven letters left to shuffle

The joke is medical: the headache loses its head and is then treated. Both instructions are dressed as symptoms, which is why neither looks like an instruction.

20 across ACHES

Causes of discomfort hidden in tea chest (5)

Definition: Causes of discomfort · hidden word

  • tea chest → ACHES five consecutive letters bridge the gap between the two words

A tea chest is the plywood box tea was shipped in, a real thing rather than a random pairing, and that is what makes the surface sit still enough to hide letters.

21 across TECHNIQUE

Method with which you prepare the quince (9)

Definition: Method · anagram

  • the quince → THEQUINCE nine letters of fodder, the article included
  • prepare → anagram indicator

A quince is a hard, sour fruit that has to be cooked before it is edible, so 'prepare the quince' reads as a recipe line and hides the instruction in plain sight.

22 across ANGLOPHILIA

At first, Aston Villa (without No 1) hoping to animate emotion of England fans (11)

Definition: emotion of England fans · anagram + first letter + deletion

  • Aston → A the opening letter of the first half of the club's name
  • Villa (without No 1) → ILLA the second half gives up the letter standing first in it, leaving four for the mix
  • hoping → HOPING six more letters of fodder, taken as they stand

Aston Villa are a Birmingham football club, so the surface is a supporter's daydream; England fans are a different crowd, and that switch of allegiance is where the definition hides.

23 across GRATIS

Good: musical instrument's flipping free (6)

Definition: free · charade + reversal

  • Good → G the standard one-letter abbreviation, as on a school report
  • musical instrument's → RATIS the Indian long-necked lute is a sitar, and turned over it reads backwards

'Flipping' is also the mild expletive an exasperated speaker reaches for, which is what keeps the surface sounding like a sentence about a bargain.

24 across ORATED

Speechified, valued by none (6)

Definition: Speechified · charade

  • none → O nothing is written as a nought, and a nought on the page is the letter O
  • valued → RATED to rate a thing is to put a value on it

The surface reads as a slight — a speech nobody thought much of — and the two halves are simply laid end to end, with 'by' doing nothing but joining them.

2 down SCARBOROUGH FAIR

Traditional number of herbs? (11,4)

Definition: Traditional number of herbs? · cryptic definition

  • Traditional number → a 'number' in the music trade is a song, and a traditional one is a folk tune with no known author
  • of herbs? → not a count of them: the herbs are the four the refrain lists, parsley, sage, rosemary and thyme

The question mark carries the whole clue. Scarborough Fair is an English folk ballad, and its herb-listing refrain is probably the best-known line in the tradition.

3 down TORONTO

What may be taken from Raptor on tour somewhere in Ontario (7)

Definition: somewhere in Ontario · hidden word

  • Raptor on tour → TORONTO seven consecutive letters straddle all three words, beginning inside the first

The Raptors are Toronto's basketball team, so the city is sitting inside a phrase about its own side travelling — the surface names the answer twice over.

4 down ENCLOSING

Penning rubbish long since (9)

Definition: Penning · anagram

  • long since → LONGSINCE nine letters of fodder, taken exactly as they stand
  • rubbish → anagram indicator

Read 'rubbish' as a noun and the clue is a complaint about old junk; read it as a verb and it is the instruction. That switch of part of speech is the whole difficulty.

5 down ADDED

On social media, befriended a journalist bored by Double Dutch (5)

Definition: On social media, befriended · charade + container

  • a → A the article is taken as the single letter it is written with
  • Double Dutch → DD Dutch is taken as D, and the word in front of it calls for a second copy
  • journalist → ED the editor is the journalist crosswords reach for, shortened as it is on a masthead

'Double Dutch' means gibberish and 'bored' means fed up, and neither reading is the working one: the first is a pair of letters, the second a drill.

6 down BABYLON

Continue to divulge secrets, overheard in city of sin (7)

Definition: city of sin · homophone

  • Continue to divulge secrets → BABBLE ON to babble is to let secrets out in a rush, and 'continue' supplies the ON that keeps it going
  • overheard → BABYLON said aloud, those two words run together and come out spelt as one

The definition is scriptural: the ancient city became the Bible's byword for corruption and luxury, and later for any place thought to be both.

7 down TRAFALGAR SQUARE

A larger squat, far off in London location (9,6)

Definition: London location · anagram

  • A larger squat, far → ALARGERSQUATFAR fifteen letters of fodder, taken exactly as they stand
  • off → anagram indicator

'A larger squat, far off' reads as a description of an illegal occupation somewhere distant, and only the last word of it is an instruction; the other five are raw material.

9 down GRIM

Vacuous 'gagster', Everyman's leaving you stony-faced (4)

Definition: stony-faced · charade + outer letters

  • 'gagster' → GR hollow out the joker's word and its first and last letters are what is left
  • Everyman's → IM this paper's setter signs himself Everyman, so his own 'I am' contracts to two letters

Everyman is the name this crossword's setter writes under, and 'Everyman's' is him saying I'm — a signature trick worth knowing, because he uses himself as fodder most weeks.

10 down HYMN

What has holy musician turned out? (4)

Definition: What has holy musician turned out? · &lit + outer letters

  • holy → HY empty the middle of the word and its first and last letters stay put
  • musician → MN the same treatment for the player: outside letters kept, the six inside dropped

'Turned out' does double duty: in the surface it means produced, in the wordplay it means emptied, which is why the clue is true read either way.

14 down PINOCCHIO

His dishonesty increases the bill (9)

Definition: His dishonesty increases the bill · cryptic definition

  • His dishonesty → not a restaurant scam: the lying itself sets the change off, and the change happens to the liar
  • increases the bill → a bill is a beak, and by extension a nose, so what grows is a face rather than a total

Pinocchio's nose lengthens every time he lies, which is the picture behind the pun, and 'bill' is the pivot: a restaurant total in the surface, a beak in the answer.

15 down HEAL

In hearing, ne'er-do-well is to improve (4)

Definition: to improve · homophone

  • ne'er-do-well → HEEL a heel is a shabby, untrustworthy man, the word a P G Wodehouse character would use
  • In hearing → HEAL spoken, there is nothing to choose between the two spellings, so the ear reaches for the wrong one

Two words that sound identical and share nothing else: one is a scoundrel, the other is what a wound does. The instruction is the only bridge between them.

16 down IN SIGHT

Imminent aperçu (2,5)

Definition: Imminent · double definition

  • Imminent → INSIGHT split after two letters, the phrase says a thing is near enough to be seen coming
  • aperçu → INSIGHT a French loanword for a brief flash of understanding, a glimpse into how something works

The enumeration is the giveaway: (2,5) says two words, and only one of the two meanings needs the gap. Seven letters do both jobs.

18 down GANGLIA

Criminals and perjurer throwing off tail: some nerve! (7)

Definition: some nerve! · charade + deletion

  • Criminals → GANG a band of criminals working together goes by this four-letter word
  • perjurer → LIA someone who lies on oath is a liar, and that word arrives four letters long and leaves three
  • tail → R the tail of a word is its final letter, and it is the one thrown off

'Some nerve!' is an exclamation about cheek in the surface and a piece of anatomy in the answer, and that pivot is what the clue turns on.

19 down AXES

Fires X and Y? (4)

Definition: Fires · double definition

  • Fires → AXES to axe a job or the person doing it is to cut them, the verb every headline uses for redundancies
  • X and Y? → AXES the two reference lines of a graph are labelled with exactly those letters

Two plurals with nothing in common: one is a verb for sacking people, the other the pair of lines you plot a graph against. The question mark flags definition by example.

21 down TOOLS

Mechanically produces seat for cycling (5)

Definition: Mechanically produces · cycling

  • seat → STOOL a stool is a backless seat, five letters ready to be rotated

'Seat for cycling' pictures a bicycle saddle, and that is the trap: the last word is not describing the seat, it is telling you what to do with its letters.
