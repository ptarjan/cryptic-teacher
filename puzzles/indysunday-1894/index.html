Across

1 across EVENING DRESS

Uniform in East Germany Wessis regularly wear for parties (7,5)

Definition: wear for parties · charade + alternate letters

wear for parties -> EVENING DRESS: wear is the noun for clothing, and evening dress is the formal outfit a party invitation asks for.

  • Uniform → EVEN a uniform surface is an even one, level and unvarying
  • in → IN the little word joins the assembly whole, exactly as printed
  • East Germany → GDR the German Democratic Republic, the communist state that lasted until 1990, is GDR in English
  • Wessis regularly → ESS Wessis is German slang for West Germans; its second, fourth and sixth letters are wanted
  • regularly — Letters taken at a steady beat means every second one, so only the even-numbered characters of the word before are picked up.

'Uniform' opens the clue looking like army kit for a parade, and it is an adjective meaning even. The two Germanies are there only to hand over letters.

9 across FLAGON

Weary having consumed jug of wine (6)

Definition: jug of wine · charade

jug of wine -> FLAGON: a flagon is the big-bellied vessel wine or cider is served from, so the definition names container and contents together.

  • Weary → FLAG to flag is to grow weary, as runners do late in a race
  • having consumed → ON somebody on drink or on drugs has consumed them

'Weary' reads as an adjective describing whoever has been drinking, and it is the verb — the one that means to droop.

10 across GUERNSEY

What's certainly near EU and close to Cherbourg, going west? (8)

Definition: What's certainly near EU and close to Cherbourg, going west? · &lit + charade + last letter + reversal

Read straight, the whole clue asks what lies outside the EU and just west of Cherbourg, and the Channel Island is its own description.

  • close to Cherbourg → G the last letter of the French port
  • EU → UE the union's own two letters, written the other way
  • near → RN nr is the short form of near used in addresses, turned round
  • certainly → SEY the plainest three-letter word of agreement, reversed
  • close to — The close of a word is where it stops, so only the final letter of the name beside it is taken.
  • going west — West is leftwards on a map, and an across entry read leftwards runs from its last letter to its first.

An all-in-one: read straight, the question describes the island, and those very same words spell it out backwards. Cherbourg is the French port opposite.

11 across SEDATEST

Most sober people you see set limits (8)

Definition: Most sober · container

Most sober -> SEDATEST: sedate means calm and unhurried rather than merely off the drink, and the superlative ending matches 'most'.

  • set → SET a set is a circle of people, and these three letters part after the second
  • people you see → DATES the people you see socially are your dates
  • limits — To limit a thing is to fence it at both ends, so the word doing the limiting opens up and takes the other one inside.

The whole clue reads as advice about the company you keep, and 'set limits' looks like one phrase. The two words are doing different jobs.

12 across COBALT

Dog rolled over in bed, kind of blue (6)

Definition: kind of blue · container + reversal

kind of blue -> COBALT: cobalt blue is the deep shade the metal's compounds give to paint and glass, so the answer names one blue among many.

  • bed → COT a cot is a small bed, and its three letters open up after the second
  • Dog rolled over → BAL a lab is a retriever, and the breed's short name is turned
  • rolled over — A dog rolling over ends up the other way round, and so do the three letters it stands for.
  • in — What is in a thing sits inside it, so the letters just turned go between those of the word that follows.

Lab is the crossword's usual dog, short for Labrador. 'Kind of blue' is the definition: the metal gives its name to one particular shade.

13 across SPAD

Political aide, one in black suit mostly (4)

Definition: Political aide · deletion

Political aide -> SPAD: British shorthand for a special adviser, the party appointee who works to a minister outside the civil service.

  • one in black suit → SPADE a card belonging to the black suit that is not clubs
  • mostly — Most of a word is all of it bar the final letter, so the last character is dropped before anything else happens.

A SpAd is a special adviser, the politically appointed staffer who works for a minister rather than for the civil service.

15 across CURMUDGEON

Dancing cured Mungo's grump (10)

Definition: grump · anagram

grump -> CURMUDGEON: both words name the person rather than the mood, a bad-tempered old soul who complains on principle.

  • cured Mungo → CUREDMUNGO anagram fodder, ten letters
  • Dancing → anagram indicator
  • Dancing — Dancers keep changing places on the floor, so the letters that follow will not stay in the order they are given.

The surface promises that a cure worked, which is the opposite of what the answer describes. That pull in the wrong direction is what hides ten easy letters.

16 across INTOXICANT

A fan of 11 is unable to drink, perhaps (10)

Definition: drink, perhaps · charade

drink, perhaps -> INTOXICANT: definition by example, since an alcoholic drink is one instance of the wider class of things that intoxicate.

  • A fan of → INTO somebody into jazz is a fan of it
  • 11 → XI eleven in Roman numerals, not a pointer to another clue
  • is unable → CANT can't, with the apostrophe dropped as crosswords allow

Every crossword trains you to look up at clue 11 when a number appears, and this grid has one. Here the digits are only a Roman numeral.

19 across CEDE

Report of grain yield (4)

Definition: yield · homophone

yield -> CEDE: to cede territory is to give it up under pressure, which is the surrender sense of yield rather than the harvest one.

  • grain → CEDE a grain of wheat is a seed, and that word spoken has another spelling
  • Report of — A report is what somebody heard and then passed on, so what counts is how the word sounds and not how it is spelt.

'Yield' pulls two ways — a crop's yield and giving ground — and the farming reading is the false one, planted by the grain beside it.

20 across MULLET

Currently popular hairstyle, say, that's iffy from behind (6)

Definition: Currently popular hairstyle · charade + reversal

Currently popular hairstyle -> MULLET: business at the front and party at the back, a 1980s cut that came back into fashion in the 2020s.

  • that's iffy → MU um is the noise a speaker makes when unsure, turned round
  • say → LLET to tell somebody something is to say it, and the word is written backwards
  • from behind — Seen from behind, an across answer is read right to left, so both pieces are written out back to front.

The cut is short at the front and long at the back, so 'from behind' plays as description as much as instruction.

21 across DARK MEAT

Withdrawn advert affected market for cheaper poultry (4,4)

Definition: cheaper poultry · anagram + reversal

cheaper poultry -> DARK MEAT: the butcher's term for the legs and thighs, richer in colour than the breast and sold at a lower price.

  • Withdrawn advert → DA an advert is an ad, and those two letters come back the other way
  • market → MARKET anagram fodder, six letters
  • affected → anagram indicator
  • Withdrawn — Something withdrawn is drawn back the way it came, so the two letters beside it are written in the other order.
  • affected — An affected manner is one altered from its natural state, so the word after it is rearranged rather than left as printed.

'Withdrawn advert' looks like a phrase to shuffle whole. Only the ad's two letters move, and they simply turn round before the market is jumbled.

23 across OUTSMART

At a social event maybe wearing a suit's best (8)

Definition: best · charade

best -> OUTSMART: as a verb, to best a rival is to get the better of them, here specifically by cleverness rather than force.

  • At a social event → OUT somebody out for the evening is at a party or a dinner
  • maybe wearing a suit → SMART smart means well turned out, and a suit is one way of being it

'Best' closes the clue looking like the top of a range, and it is a verb: to best somebody is to beat them.

25 across ETHNIC

Cycling finishes in sprint, French kind of race (6)

Definition: of race · charade + last letters + cycling

of race -> ETHNIC: ethnic describes belonging to a people defined by shared descent and culture, so the phrase is the adjective's plain sense.

  • finishes in sprint, French → TH the final letters of those two words, in the order given
  • kind → NICE a kind person is a nice one
  • Cycling — Letters that cycle travel round from one end of the assembly to the other without changing their order.
  • finishes in — A word's finish is its last letter, so the ends of the two words named next are what is taken.

The surface is a bike race, which lets 'Cycling' sit in plain sight as the instruction: one letter rolls off the back of the assembly to the front.

26 across SUPERSTARDOM

Celebrity sprouted arms in different places (12)

Definition: Celebrity · anagram

Celebrity -> SUPERSTARDOM: celebrity in the abstract sense, the state of being famous rather than a famous person, which is what the -dom ending names.

  • sprouted arms → SPROUTEDARMS anagram fodder, twelve letters
  • in different places → anagram indicator
  • in different places — Put the same letters in different places and you have the word rearranged, an instruction disguised here as geography.

The picture is a statue growing extra limbs, and the instruction hides inside it. Twelve letters is long enough for a surface to swallow them whole.

2 down VALUE

Playing against Hearts in tears, club get prize (5)

Definition: prize · charade + middle letters

prize -> VALUE: to prize something is to set worth on it, the verb sense of both words rather than the trophy the surface suggests.

  • Playing against → V v is the abbreviation for versus, as printed in a fixture list
  • tears → A the centre of a five-letter word is its third letter
  • club → LU an even-length word has two letters at its middle, here the second and third
  • get → E the middle letter of the three
  • Hearts in — The heart of a word is the letter or two at its centre, so what is wanted from each word listed after this is its middle.

Hearts and club look like a hand of cards, and Hearts even looks like a Scottish football fixture. No suit is wanted: 'hearts' means middles.

3 down NIGGARD

Silver Spirit on drive upset miser (7)

Definition: miser · charade + reversal

miser -> NIGGARD: an old word, now rare outside crosswords, for someone grudging with money, which is exactly what a miser is.

  • Spirit → NIG the spirit poured as a measure is gin, tipped over here
  • Silver → GA AG is the chemical symbol for silver, and it arrives the other way round
  • drive → RD Dr is the street-sign short form of Drive, again turned
  • on — In a down entry whatever sits on something is written above it, so the pieces clued first end up higher in the grid.
  • upset — Something upset has been tipped over, so each piece is written out the other way up.

Silver Spirit is a Rolls-Royce, so the surface is all motoring and the two words are a metal and a drink. Every piece lands back to front.

4 down NONDESCRIPT

Anonymous text appended to edition of Donne (11)

Definition: Anonymous · anagram + charade

Anonymous -> NONDESCRIPT: not the unnamed-author sense but the other one, a face or a building so lacking in features that nothing marks it out.

  • edition of Donne → NONDE the five letters of the poet's name, re-set in a different order
  • text → SCRIPT a script is the written text of a play or a speech
  • appended to — To append is to tack on at the end, so the piece named before it is written after everything else.
  • edition — A fresh edition is the same material set out again in a new arrangement, so the letters that follow will not keep their order.

John Donne is here only as five letters to rearrange, and 'edition of' passes as bibliography rather than as an instruction. Nothing in the clue is about poetry.

5 down DOG STAR

500 traitors shot after revolution as shining example (3,4)

Definition: shining example · charade + reversal

shining example -> DOG STAR: Sirius outshines every other star, so it is both literally the shining one and idiomatically the model to follow.

  • 500 → D D is 500 in Roman numerals
  • shot → OG a shot at something is a go, turned round
  • traitors → STAR traitors are rats, and the word is written backwards
  • after revolution — A revolution turns a thing full circle, so the words before it are written out from their last letter to their first.

Sirius is the brightest thing in the night sky, so the definition works as a straight description and as the idiom at the same time.

6 down EYE

Detective agreed, on the record? (3)

Definition: Detective · homophone

Detective -> EYE: short for private eye, the American term for a hired investigator, which crosswords treat as a standing sense.

  • agreed → EYE a voter who agrees says aye, the parliamentary yes
  • on the record — What goes on the record is spoken first and written down afterwards, so it is how the word is heard that matters, not how it is spelt.

A private eye is the detective of American fiction, and 'on the record' does double duty in the surface as a politician's phrase.

7 down SANDBAG

Spooner's crew decline help with water coming in (7)

Definition: help with water coming in · spoonerism

help with water coming in -> SANDBAG: the thing stacked in a doorway when a river rises, so the definition names its single job.

  • crew → BAND a band is a crew, a group working together
  • decline → SAG sales or spirits that decline sag
  • Spooner's → SANDBAG trade the opening sounds of those two words, B for S, and read the result
  • Spooner's — The Reverend Spooner famously tripped over his own speech, so the opening sounds of the two words named change places.

Spooner clues run backwards: you look for two words whose front sounds, once traded, describe the thing being defined. Here the definition is a flood defence.

8 down FEELGOOD

Giving cheer, Scooby-Doo gleefully lifted masks (8)

Definition: Giving cheer · hidden word + reversal

Giving cheer -> FEELGOOD: the adjective for a film or a song that leaves its audience happy, which is what cheering somebody up does.

  • Scooby-Doo gleefully → FEELGOOD eight letters in a row read the wrong way, starting inside the second word and running back across the gap
  • lifted — A down entry read upwards climbs the grid, so this run of letters is picked up from the bottom end first.
  • masks — A mask keeps the face behind it out of sight, so these words are holding a longer run of letters hidden.

The cartoon dog is decoration: his name is in the clue because the letters wanted happen to run through it backwards, and a hyphen is no barrier.

12 down COUNTERFEIT

Pirate well-trained behind bar, collecting empties primarily (11)

Definition: Pirate · charade + container + first letter

Pirate -> COUNTERFEIT: to pirate a film or a handbag is to make and sell an unauthorised copy, which is counterfeiting it.

  • bar → COUNTER the bar you are served across is a counter
  • well-trained → FIT someone well-trained is fit, and the word splits after its first letter
  • empties primarily → E the word for drained glasses starts with E
  • behind — What stands behind something comes after it, so the trained one's letters follow those of the place named next.
  • collecting — A bar collecting glasses takes them in, so the letter named afterwards is gathered inside the letters already assembled.
  • primarily — A word's primary letter is the one it starts with, so only the initial of the word before is wanted.

'Pirate' looks like a swashbuckler propping up the bar, and it is a verb. The whole pub scene is built out of wordplay pieces.

14 down PENDULUM

Purple robes do for upper-class swinger (8)

Definition: swinger · container + charade

swinger -> PENDULUM: a plain description of the object by what it does, since a pendulum's whole business is swinging on its pivot.

  • Purple → PLUM a plum is a deep purple, and the four letters open up after the first
  • do for → END to do for someone is to finish them off
  • upper-class → U U is the old label for upper-class speech and manners, as in U and non-U
  • robes — To robe someone is to wrap cloth right round them, so the word before goes round the letters clued after it.

'Purple robes' reads as a bishop's vestments. The colour is a fruit and 'robes' is the instruction to wrap it round what follows.

17 down OBLASTS

Former Soviet regions love sound of bombing (7)

Definition: Former Soviet regions · charade

Former Soviet regions -> OBLASTS: an oblast is an administrative province of Russia and its neighbours, the tier the USSR was governed through.

  • love → O love is nothing in tennis, and nothing is written as a nought
  • sound of bombing → BLASTS the noise bombing makes, the word itself rather than a word resembling it

'Sound of' is the standard signal for a word said aloud, and here it means the actual noise instead.

18 down AUDITOR

Money expert Ed swaps capital for gold (7)

Definition: Money expert · substitution

Money expert -> AUDITOR: the accountant who inspects a company's books and signs them off, so the definition names the job by what it handles.

  • Ed → EDITOR Ed is the newsroom's short form for the editor, and the full job title is what the letters spell
  • capital → E his capital is the letter he begins with, the only upper-case one in the name
  • gold → AU AU is the chemical symbol for gold, from the Latin aurum
  • swaps — A swap puts one thing where another was, so the letters named last stand in the place vacated by the letter named first.

Read the surface and Ed is a colleague; read it again and he is the job. 'Capital' is the letter he starts with, not a city.

19 down CAMPHOR

Sleep outside house with right insect repellent (7)

Definition: insect repellent · charade

insect repellent -> CAMPHOR: the white waxy stuff in mothballs, whose smell keeps moths and other insects out of stored clothes.

  • Sleep outside → CAMP to sleep outside under canvas is to camp
  • house → HO ho is the standard abbreviation for house on a nameplate
  • right → R R is the standard abbreviation for right

'Outside' begs to be read as an instruction to put one word round another, and it belongs to the sleeping instead.

22 down AMIGO

Friend's promise to leave in the morning? (5)

Definition: Friend · charade

Friend -> AMIGO: the Spanish word, borrowed into English as a breezy way of addressing a mate.

  • in the morning → AM am marks the hours before noon on a clock
  • promise to leave → IGO the two words somebody undertaking to depart would use

The question mark apologises for the whimsy: 'I go' is no stock phrase, just what a person promising to leave might say.

24 down ALP

Height bagged by phenomenal pole-vaulter (3)

Definition: Height · hidden word

Height -> ALP: one peak of the range that crosses Europe, a named mountain standing in for high ground in general.

  • phenomenal pole-vaulter → ALP three letters in a row, the last two of the first word and the first of the second
  • bagged by — Whatever is bagged is carried unseen inside the bag, so the letters lie unbroken within the words that follow.

An athlete clearing a height is such a tidy picture that nothing in it looks like an instruction, which is exactly how a short hidden word survives.
