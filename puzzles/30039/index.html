Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,039

Across

1 across SPRITE

Special ceremony for fairy (6)

Definition: fairy · charade

  • Special → SP sp = special (a standard abbreviation)
  • ceremony → RITE a rite is a ceremony

'Special ceremony' reads as one phrase — a wedding, a coronation — and that is what hides the seam between the two pieces. 'For' is only glue.

5 across CORNROWS

Hairy arrangement faced by harvester? (8)

Definition: Hairy arrangement · double definition

  • Hairy arrangement → cornrows: hair braided in tight rows
  • faced by harvester? → a harvester literally faces rows of corn

The pleasure is the second reading: split the word and the harvester really is facing rows of corn. The question mark is the setter apologising for it.

9 across ACT THREE

Halfway through play perhaps catheter fails (3,5)

Definition: Halfway through play perhaps · anagram

  • catheter → CATHETER anagram fodder, eight letters
  • fails → anagram indicator

In a five-act play the middle is act three, and 'perhaps' is the setter conceding that not every play has five acts — that little word is what makes the definition fair.

10 across VANITY

Such a case of conceitedness? (6)

Definition: Such a case of conceitedness? · cryptic definition

  • case → points to the phrase 'vanity case'
  • conceitedness → vanity = conceitedness

The trick is 'case': it reads as 'an instance of' and it means the cosmetics bag. Both senses are true at once, which is what a cryptic definition lives on.

11 across PURR

Express pleasure, turning up with classy car (4)

Definition: Express pleasure · reversal + charade

  • up → PU UP 'turning' (reversed)
  • classy car → RR RR = Rolls-Royce

The sly bit is that 'up' is not an instruction but the word to be turned. And a purr belongs equally to a contented cat and a Rolls-Royce engine.

12 across HUMILIATED

Mortified, having to sing wordlessly to one particular backing (10)

Definition: Mortified · charade + reversal

  • sing wordlessly → HUM to hum is to sing without words
  • one → I the Roman numeral one
  • particular → LIATED a particular = a DETAIL, 'backing' (reversed)

'Backing' reads as musical accompaniment, which is why it hides so well as a reversal instruction — and DETAIL turned round is the least visible of the three pieces.

13 across STAMEN

In tin, weak fertiliser (6)

Definition: fertiliser · container

  • tin → SN Sn is the chemical symbol for tin
  • weak → TAME tame = weak, feeble

'Fertiliser' sounds like something you buy in a sack, but a stamen is what fertilises a flower. The garden-centre surface is the trap; the definition is exact.

14 across DON'T KNOW

Pass reply to pollster (4,4)

Definition: Pass · double definition

  • Pass → in a quiz, saying 'pass' means 'don't know'
  • reply to pollster → pollsters record undecided answers as 'don't know'

'Pass' is quiz-show jargon and 'don't know' is polling jargon. The clue works by setting two specialist idioms side by side with nothing to mark the join.

16 across REHOUSED

Wounded hero accustomed to being moved elsewhere (8)

Definition: moved elsewhere · anagram + charade

  • Wounded hero → REHO 'wounded' scrambles HERO
  • accustomed → USED accustomed (to) = used (to)

Only 'hero' is fodder — the rest is a straight charade piece. Anagrams that eat just part of a clue are the ones solvers walk past.

19 across UPPITY

Arrogant but promoted – shame (6)

Definition: Arrogant · charade

  • promoted → UP promoted = up (moved up)
  • shame → PITY 'what a shame' = 'what a pity'

'Promoted – shame' reads as one wry line about office politics, so nothing announces itself as wordplay. The dash is punctuation, not an instruction.

21 across NINETEENTH

Drinking hole where one may buy round after round (10)

Definition: Drinking hole where one may buy round after round · cryptic definition

  • Drinking hole → the 'nineteenth hole' is the golf clubhouse bar
  • round after round → a round of drinks after a round of golf

The joke turns on 'round' meaning both a circuit of the course and a set of drinks, and on 'drinking hole' being literally true: the clubhouse bar is the nineteenth hole.

23 across EWES

Farm animals’ exploit recounted (4)

Definition: Farm animals · homophone

  • exploit → USE to exploit = to use; 'recounted' means said aloud

'Recounted' means said out loud, not retold, and spotting that is the whole clue. The possessive apostrophe belongs to the surface only; the answer is a plain plural.

24 across SCREED

Tedious speech, a statement of belief by saint (6)

Definition: Tedious speech · charade

  • a statement of belief → CREED a creed is a statement of belief
  • saint → S S = saint, as in S. Francis

'By' tells you the two pieces are adjacent but not in which order — here the saint comes first, and only the enumeration and the crossing letters settle it.

25 across BRINGS IN

Yields items of jewellery to be thrown away? (6,2)

Definition: Yields · container

  • items of jewellery → RINGS rings are items of jewellery
  • to be thrown away? → BIN things to be thrown away go in the BIN, so RINGS goes inside B…IN

'To be thrown away' clues BIN by description rather than synonym, which is the hard step. And 'yields' is the financial sense — what an investment brings in.

26 across ASK FOR IT

To seek computer assistance is to invite trouble (3,3,2)

Definition: To seek computer assistance · double definition

  • To seek computer assistance → asking for IT — the Information Technology department
  • to invite trouble → 'you're asking for it' = you're inviting trouble

The join is invisible because both halves are idioms and IT changes from pronoun to department. 'You're asking for it' is the parental version.

27 across SODDEN

Two types of earth, very wet (6)

Definition: very wet · charade

  • Two types of earth → SODDEN SOD (a piece of turf) + DEN (a fox's home, called an 'earth')

A fox's lair is called an earth — that is the sense solvers miss, and the clue needs 'earth' to mean two different things at once.

Down

2 down PICK UP THE PIECES

Recover from shock of upsetting the board? (4,2,3,6)

Definition: Recover from shock · double definition

  • Recover from shock → the everyday sense: pick up the pieces after a disaster
  • upsetting the board? → knock over a chessboard and you must literally pick up the pieces

'Upsetting the board' first reads as annoying the directors; the question mark is the setter admitting he means it literally, with a chessboard on the floor.

3 down INTERIM

During school time, accepting one is temporary (7)

Definition: temporary · container

  • During school time → INTERM during term = 'in term'
  • one → I the Roman numeral one

The hard step is hearing 'during school time' as the phrase IN TERM rather than as scene-setting. Once it is written out, the insertion is the easy part.

4 down EARPHONES

US lawman perfects hearing aid (9)

Definition: hearing aid · charade

  • US lawman → EARP Wyatt Earp, of the O.K. Corral
  • perfects → HONES to hone is to perfect, sharpen

Wyatt Earp is one of a small cast of names setters keep on hand for their letters. The surface, a lawman with a hearing problem, gives nothing away.

5 down CREAMED

Like some potatoes? Made huge fuss, denied starter (7)

Definition: Like some potatoes? · deletion

  • Made huge fuss → SCREAMED screamed = made a huge fuss
  • denied starter → remove the first letter (the 'starter') of Screamed

The joke is on the menu — 'denied starter' sounds like a diner going without a first course, and it is also the instruction to drop the opening S.

6 down REVEL

Delight as joystick is pulled up (5)

Definition: Delight · reversal

  • joystick → LEVER a joystick is a lever

'Pulled up' is doing double duty: it suits a joystick, and in a down clue it means read the letters upwards.

7 down RANSACK

Hurried with big bag for plunder (7)

Definition: plunder · charade

  • Hurried → RAN hurried = ran
  • big bag → SACK a sack is a big bag

The surface is already the crime, told innocently — a man hurrying off with a big bag. 'With' and 'for' are only glue.

8 down WITHER ON THE VINE

Excitedly invite whore, then fail through inanition (6,2,3,4)

Definition: fail through inanition · anagram

  • invite whore → INVITEWHORE eleven letters of anagram fodder
  • then → THEN four more letters join the fodder
  • Excitedly → anagram indicator

Inanition is exhaustion from lack of nourishment, so the definition is exact rather than loose. The startling surface is there to stop you counting letters.

15 down NOUGHTIES

Ten years at the start of century? It’s enough mess (9)

Definition: Ten years at the start of century? · anagram

  • It’s enough → ITSENOUGH anagram fodder, nine letters
  • mess → anagram indicator

The surface sounds like a fed-up parent, which is why 'mess' never looks like an instruction. The question mark covers a definition that fits only one particular century.

17 down ONESELF

Me and my little helper? (7)

Definition: Me · double definition

  • Me → oneself = me (in a rather royal register)
  • my little helper? → split the word as ONE'S ELF — my own little helper

The gag is the apostrophe you cannot hear: one's elf. Splitting a solid word into a possessive is a favourite trick, and the question mark is the wink.

18 down DINGBAT

Special symbol indicating ingredients of pudding batter (7)

Definition: Special symbol · hidden word

  • pudding batter → DINGBAT hidden inside pud-DING BAT-ter

Hidden words live or die by the surface, and 'pudding batter' is entirely innocent. A dingbat is a typographic ornament — the little flowers and pointing hands in fonts like Zapf Dingbats.

20 down PLEDGED

Paid across the counter as promised (7)

Definition: promised · container

  • Paid → PD pd = paid (the bookkeeping abbreviation)
  • the counter → LEDGE a counter is a ledge, a shelf

'Paid across the counter' is a whole plausible transaction, and that is the disguise — 'across' is the instruction hiding in the middle of it.

22 down EIDER

Monarch formerly eating fish and duck (5)

Definition: duck · container

  • Monarch formerly → ER ER = Elizabeth Regina, the late Queen
  • fish → IDE the ide, a freshwater fish beloved of setters

'Fish and duck' reads as a menu, which is why the seam is invisible. ER and IDE are both worth memorising — setters reach for them constantly.
