Across

1 across SHATTERING

Straighten out - but it's exhausting! (10)

Definition: exhausting · anagram

  • Straighten → SHATTERING ten letters, and exactly the ten wanted once they are dealt out again

The dash invites you to read two definitions, tidying and tiring. There is only one: the instruction to straighten is itself the thing put out of order.

6 across YETI

Still, India's supposed mountain-dweller (4)

Definition: supposed mountain-dweller · charade

  • Still → YET both words mean up to now, as in 'is he here still?'
  • India → I I is India in the NATO spelling alphabet

The comma makes the first word look like a discourse marker opening a sentence about the subcontinent. It is the first chunk, and the country is only a radio-alphabet letter.

9 across ALGORITHMS

Procedures for solving problems as clattering hailstorm overwhelms golf (10)

Definition: Procedures for solving problems · anagram

  • hailstorm → HAILSTORM nine letters of weather, waiting to be dealt out again
  • golf → G G is golf in the NATO spelling alphabet

'Clattering hailstorm' is one picture, which makes it easy to read both words as scenery. Only the noun is fodder; the participle in front of it is the instruction.

10 across GRIT

Determination regularly exhibited by Geraint (4)

Definition: Determination · alternate letters

  • Geraint → GRIT the first letter of the name and then every other one after it

'Regularly exhibited by' sounds like a phrase about character, which is what makes it easy to read past as padding. Geraint is a Welsh name, here only a supply of letters.

11 across CHOPPED LIVER

Axed before 50, Everyman's at end of career, an irrelevance (7,5)

Definition: an irrelevance · charade + last letter

  • Axed → CHOPPED to axe something is to chop it, and the past tense carries across
  • 50 → L fifty in Roman numerals
  • Everyman's → IVE the setter talking about himself: Everyman's here is I've, short for I have
  • career → R the last letter of this word

Everyman signs every puzzle, so his name in a clue is the setter saying 'I'. The definition is the aside tacked on at the end, and the comma is the only warning of the switch.

15 across HOTHEAD

Madman seen in Psycho (the adaptation) (7)

Definition: Madman · hidden word

  • Psycho (the adaptation) → HOTHEAD seven letters in a row starting in the middle of the film title and running on past the bracket

The italics and the brackets dress this as a note about which version of the film is meant, and that is the disguise: punctuation never interrupts a hidden run.

16 across SPONSOR

North, then south, track circling: back! (7)

Definition: back · container

  • track → SPOOR a spoor is the trail of prints and scent an animal leaves for a hunter to follow
  • North, then south → NS the two compass initials, taken in the order the clue sets them out

The exclamation mark makes the last word read as a shout at a dog on a trail. It is the definition, and it means to fund something rather than to retreat.

17 across THEOREM

Some loathe - or embrace - idea (7)

Definition: idea · hidden word

  • loathe - or embrace → THEOREM seven letters in a row, beginning inside the first word and finishing inside the last

'Embrace' is doing everything it can to look like an instruction to put one thing inside another. It is fodder, and the two dashes are there to break the run up.

19 across TADPOLE

I'm grateful to get support embracing prince who'll become a frog (7)

Definition: who'll become a frog · charade + container

  • I'm grateful → TA ta is the everyday British word for thanks
  • support → DOLE the dole is British unemployment benefit, state support for those out of work
  • prince → P P is the standard abbreviation for prince

'Support' wants to be a pole or a prop, and it is the benefit money instead. In the fairy tale of the surface a frog turns into a prince, which is the wrong way round here.

20 across UNDERDELIVER

Rashly endure drivel; fail to make good on promise (12)

Definition: fail to make good on promise · anagram

  • endure drivel → UNDERDELIVER twelve letters between the two words, which is the whole supply needed

The semicolon is the join: wordplay to the left of it, definition to the right. 'Rashly endure' reads as one act of impatience, and that is what hides the instruction.

23 across TONG

Ultimately prudent to learn mingling; one would lose grip without partner at the barbecue (4)

Definition: one would lose grip without partner at the barbecue · last letters

  • prudent → T the last letter of this word
  • to → O the last letter, which in a two-letter word is half of it
  • learn → N the last letter of this word
  • mingling → G the last letter, from the word planted here to look like a shuffling instruction

The semicolon marks the switch to the definition, which is long and jokey enough to look like the whole clue. Tongs come in pairs, so a single one drops the sausage.

24 across SKYLARKING

Broadcaster backing poet (German) gallivanting (10)

Definition: gallivanting · charade

  • Broadcaster → SKY Sky is the British satellite television company
  • poet → LARKIN Philip Larkin, the Hull librarian who became one of Britain's best-known post-war poets
  • (German) → G G is the dictionary abbreviation for German

The surface is a broadcaster funding a German poet, a sentence plausible enough to stop you looking any further. The definition is the single word at the end.

25 across REEL

It may be a Fisherman's Dance (4)

Definition: It may be a Fisherman's · double definition

  • It may be a Fisherman's → 'may be' flags an example: the thing meant belongs to an angler, who winds his line back onto it
  • Dance → a brisk Scottish and Irish country dance in which the couples wheel round one another

The capital letters make the clue look like the title of a piece of music, which is the whole of the misdirection. Both halves are ordinary senses of one short word.

26 across STATUETTES

Laws covering alien little figures (10)

Definition: little figures · container

  • Laws → STATUTES statutes are laws as written down and passed by a parliament
  • alien → ET the extraterrestrial of the film, and the crossword's standard alien

'Laws covering alien' reads as immigration policy, which is exactly the sentence the setter wants in your head. The definition is the two words at the end.

Down

1 down SEAR

Heard prophet sizzle (4)

Definition: sizzle · homophone

  • prophet → SEAR a seer is one who sees what is to come, which is a prophet's trade, and aloud it can be written the other way

Three words and four letters, so nothing is being assembled. 'Heard prophet' reads as a sentence, which hides that the first word is an instruction rather than part of it.

2 down ARGO

Slowly, learner's lost mighty ship (4)

Definition: mighty ship · deletion

  • Slowly → LARGO largo is the musical direction to play at a slow, broad tempo
  • learner → L L is the plate a British driving learner has to display on the car

The comma dresses the first word as an adverb attached to the sentence, when it is the whole of the fodder. Both conventions here are standing ones: the tempo marking and the L-plate.

3 down TORCHBEARER

Race brother pathetically - he's seen in the lead (11)

Definition: he's seen in the lead · anagram

  • Race brother → TORCHBEARER eleven letters from a sprint and a sibling, to be dealt out again

'Race' looks like a definition and 'in the lead' like a finishing position, so the athletics stays in your head throughout. Both belong to the surface only.

4 down RETAPED

Made another recording of French father that's stuck up (7)

Definition: Made another recording · charade + reversal

  • of French → DE de is the French word for of
  • father → PATER pater is Latin for father and survives in British public-school slang as a name for one's dad

'French father' pulls you straight to PERE, and that is the trap. Split the words the other way — 'of French', then 'father' on its own — and two pieces appear.

5 down NUMBERS

Songs in book (7)

Definition: Songs · double definition

  • Songs → a number is one item on a programme — a single song or dance performed as one turn
  • book → the fourth book of the Old Testament, titled for the two censuses of the Israelites it records

Three words, and the middle one only joins the other two. Nothing is spelled out here, which is why so short a clue can hold so long an answer.

7 down EUROVISION

Air battle across the Continent? (10)

Definition: Air battle across the Continent? · cryptic definition

  • Air battle → the reading the surface pushes: fighters and dogfights over occupied Europe, war in the sky
  • across the Continent → the reading the setter meant: an air is an old word for a tune, and the fight is between countries each sending one song

Nothing comes apart into letters here; the question mark is the tell. Once 'air' stops being sky and starts being melody, the whole clue settles into one annual televised contest.

8 down INTERBREED

Cross Italian footballers given Belgium: maybe buck up (10)

Definition: Cross · charade + reversal

  • Italian footballers → INTER Internazionale of Milan, known everywhere by the first half of its name
  • Belgium → B B is Belgium's international vehicle registration letter
  • buck → DEER a buck is the male of the species, so the general word for the animal is what is wanted

The surface is a transfer story and the colon is punctuation for it, nothing more. 'Cross' is the definition and means to mate one strain with another, not to be annoyed.

12 down LLOYD GEORGE

Statesman: Gee! Old Glory aflutter! (5,6)

Definition: Statesman · anagram

  • Gee! Old Glory → LLOYDGEORGE an exclamation plus the flag's nickname, eleven letters between them

Old Glory is the nickname of the flag of the United States, so 'aflutter' reads as bunting on a pole. The colon after 'Statesman' is doing the same job a dash would.

13 down SHOT-PUTTER

Sportsperson damaged golf-club (4-6)

Definition: Sportsperson · charade

  • damaged → SHOT a knee or an engine described as shot is wrecked past use
  • golf-club → PUTTER the club used on the green, the one that rolls the ball at the hole

Three words and a story about a broken golf club, which is exactly the wrong sport. The hyphen in the clue also sits in a different place from the hyphen in the answer.

14 down ATTENDANCE

Servants in the auditorium: but how many in the audience? (10)

Definition: how many in the audience · homophone

  • Servants → ATTENDANCE attendants are the servants who wait on someone, and spoken aloud that plural is indistinguishable from the word wanted

The colon and the question mark make this look like two separate thoughts about a theatre. The middle phrase belongs to the first half, as an instruction to listen.

18 down MEERKAT

At sea, ark teems with small flying kind of a mammal (7)

Definition: kind of a mammal · anagram + deletion

  • small → S S is the size printed on a clothing label for small
  • ark teems → MEERKAT eight letters, less the one named just before, leaving seven to deal out again

'With small flying' reads as insects over the water, which is the picture the surface wants. It is an instruction: one letter goes before any shuffling happens.

19 down TRIPLET

Unusual issue characteristic of Pitt the Elder? (7)

Definition: Unusual issue · double definition

  • Unusual issue → issue in its old legal sense of a person's offspring, and three arriving at once is well out of the ordinary
  • characteristic of Pitt the Elder? → stop reading the name as an eighteenth-century prime minister and count the letter T as the two words run together

Pitt the Elder was a British prime minister, and the clue is banking on you searching his family history. Write the words out instead: PiTT THe puts three Ts in a row.

21 down GIFT

Present the northern fruit that's offered up (4)

Definition: Present · charade + reversal

  • the northern → T in northern English speech 'the' is clipped to a single t', as in down t'pit
  • fruit → FIG one of the shortest fruits in the language, which is why setters reach for it so often

'Present the northern fruit' reads as one instruction to hand something over, so the definition looks like it runs on. It stops after the first word.

22 down EGOS

Exhausting grandeur of showbusiness primarily displayed here? (4)

Definition: Exhausting grandeur of showbusiness primarily displayed here? · &lit + first letters

  • Exhausting grandeur of showbusiness → EGOS the opening letters of these four words, read straight off in the order they stand

An all-in-one: the same four words spell the answer out of their initials and describe it at the same time, with 'displayed here' pointing back at the clue itself.
