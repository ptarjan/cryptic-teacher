Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,091

Across

9 across DIME NOVEL

‘Evil demon vanquished!’ makes sensational reading (4,5)

Definition: sensational reading · anagram

  • Evil demon → DIME NOVEL the nine letters of EVIL DEMON, redealt

The exclamation mark keeps the indicator inside the shouted quotation, where it reads as triumph rather than as an instruction standing outside it.

10 across CAVER

Sporty type beneath the surface, but one who’ll give in? (5)

Definition: Sporty type beneath the surface · double definition

  • Sporty type beneath the surface → CAVER potholing is a sport, and it happens underground
  • one who’ll give in → CAVER somebody who caves in yields, and the setter makes a person out of the phrasal verb

'Beneath the surface' is literal, not figurative: the sport happens underground. The question mark covers the second half, where a phrasal verb is squeezed into a single noun.

11 across OCTOBER

Court award presented in alternative month (7)

Definition: month · container

  • alternative → OR or is the alternative in any either/or
  • Court → CT Ct is how Court is abbreviated in a street address
  • award → OBE the Order of the British Empire, the crossword's standard honour

The surface pictures a prize handed over on a tennis court, and every word of it is quietly doing a different job in the parse.

12 across GALILEO

Astronomer’s western partner, one with a sign (7)

Definition: Astronomer · charade

  • western partner → GAL in a western the hero's sweetheart is his gal
  • one → I the Roman numeral
  • a sign → LEO the lion, one of the twelve signs of the zodiac

'Western' is the film genre rather than a compass point, and its hero always has a gal; 'sign' is the zodiac kind, which is the only astronomy the parse needs.

13 across INCH

Tongue-in-cheek, creep inside (4)

Definition: creep · hidden word

  • Tongue-in-cheek → INCH the run crosses a hyphen: the whole of 'in' and the front of 'cheek'

'Tongue-in-cheek' means ironic in the surface and is a hiding place in the parse. The answer sits astride a hyphen, which is exactly why the eye skips over it.

14 across MONTEVERDI

Composer of divertimento managed without it (10)

Definition: Composer · anagram + deletion

  • divertimento → DIVERTMENO DIVERTIMENTO less the I and the T of 'it', leaving the ten letters that get shuffled

A divertimento really is a piece of music, which is what makes the fodder sit so quietly. Note that the deletion happens before the shuffle, not after it.

16 across TANGENT

Treasure’s almost buried by explosive – touching (7)

Definition: touching · container + deletion

  • explosive → TNT TNT is the crossword's standard explosive, and it opens up to take the other piece
  • Treasure → ANGEL 'she's an angel' is the same compliment as calling somebody a treasure

The dash makes 'touching' look like the end of a sentimental sentence, and 'treasure' look like buried gold rather than a term of endearment.

17 across WATTEAU

Artist’s Power Unit and Evian Water (7)

Definition: Artist · charade

  • Power Unit → WATT the watt is the SI unit of power
  • Evian Water → EAU Evian is French, and water in French is eau

The capitals are a bluff: 'Power Unit and Evian Water' reads as a delivery note, and the second half only works once Evian is read as French rather than as a brand.

19 across EMBODIMENT

Model person appearing in NME, Time Out (10)

Definition: Model · anagram + container

  • NME, Time → EMIMENT the seven letters of NME and TIME, dealt out again
  • person → BOD bod is casual British for a person

Two magazine titles in italics look like one list, and the cut falls between them: Time keeps its letters while Out turns into the instruction.

22 across BRAT

Child has right to enter club (4)

Definition: Child · container

  • club → BAT a bat is the club you swing at a ball
  • right → R R is the standard abbreviation for right

'Club' looks like the sort you need the right connections to get into, which is exactly the reading the parse throws away.

24 across CENTRED

Focused on getting money – shamefaced (7)

Definition: Focused · charade

  • money → CENT a cent is the smallest coin in a dollar
  • shamefaced → RED a shamefaced person goes red in the face

The dash hides the join: 'money' and 'shamefaced' are two separate pieces of wordplay, not one phrase about being caught with somebody else's cash.

25 across SEDUCER

Deceiver, foreign nobleman, featured in Observer (7)

Definition: Deceiver · container

  • Observer → SEER one who observes is a seer
  • foreign nobleman → DUC duc is the French duke, which is what 'foreign' points at

The italics make it the Sunday paper, and the parse wants the ordinary noun instead: somebody who observes.

26 across ARSON

Minister without Portfolio’s capital offence (5)

Definition: offence · deletion

  • Minister → PARSON a parson is the minister of a parish
  • Portfolio’s capital → the capital letter of Portfolio is P, and P is the letter that goes

Minister without Portfolio is a real cabinet post, which is what holds the surface together, and the crime really was once a capital offence. Both are bluffs.

27 across MERCILESS

Very hard as level of gratitude abroad reduced? (9)

Definition: Very hard · charade

  • gratitude → MERCI merci is how you say thanks in France
  • reduced → LESS reduced means made less

The question mark covers the joke: a traveller whose thanks have run short is being called hard, and two languages end up jammed into one word.

Down

1 down A DROP IN THE OCEAN

Not much of an experience for the Artemis II crew at the end (1,4,2,3,5)

Definition: Not much · double definition

  • Not much → A DROP IN THE OCEAN the idiom for an amount so small it makes no difference to the whole
  • an experience for the Artemis II crew at the end → A DROP IN THE OCEAN read absolutely literally: a moon capsule finishes its flight by splashing down at sea

Artemis II loops the Moon and comes home by splashdown, so the second half is a plain statement of fact dressed up as a cryptic.

2 down EMOTICON

Smiley book’s going up I learn? (8)

Definition: Smiley · charade + reversal

  • book → EMOT a TOME is a weighty book, climbing the entry rather than running down it
  • I → I the letter stands for itself, lifted straight out of the clue
  • learn → CON to con a part is to learn it by heart, an old sense the grids keep alive

Smiley is le Carre's spymaster in the surface, which is what makes 'book' sit so naturally beside him.

3 down SNUBS

Insults marrow in seconds (5)

Definition: Insults · container

  • seconds → SS s is the abbreviation for second, and the plural asks for two of them
  • marrow → NUB the nub of a matter is its marrow, the pith at the centre of it

'Marrow' is not the vegetable but the core of a thing, the sense preserved in 'the marrow of the argument'. Two seconds make the shell it sits in.

4 down OVERCOAT

Company actor initially wearing plain attire (8)

Definition: attire · container + first letter

  • plain → OVERT overt means open and undisguised, plain to anyone looking
  • Company → CO Co is how company is abbreviated on any letterhead
  • actor → A the opening letter of actor

'Plain' is the trap: it means overt here, out in the open, rather than undecorated. The surface dresses a nervous understudy.

5 down BLIGHT

Black, easily portable mould (6)

Definition: mould · charade

  • Black → B B is the abbreviation on a pencil and on a chess score sheet
  • easily portable → LIGHT something easily portable is light to carry

Nothing here signals a build: 'Black, easily portable' reads as one description of a suitcase, and the comma is the only sign that it is two pieces.

6 down ACCLIVITY

Incline to quash civil act by end of day (9)

Definition: Incline · anagram + last letter

  • civil act → ACCLIVIT the eight letters of CIVIL ACT, dealt out again
  • day → Y the last letter of daY

'Incline' has to be read as a noun, though the surface offers it as a verb, being inclined to quash something. That switch is the whole disguise.

7 down OVULAR

Valour shown after detonation of part of a plant (6)

Definition: of part of a plant · anagram

  • Valour → OVULAR the six letters of VALOUR, blown into a new order

The definition is doing something unusual: it opens with 'of', which is what lets an adjective answer where a noun seems to be wanted.

8 down ARBORICULTURIST

Tree expert’s concoction of citrus and oil-tar rub (15)

Definition: Tree expert · anagram

  • citrus and oil-tar rub → ARBORICULTURIST the fifteen letters of CITRUS, OIL, TAR and RUB, stirred together

Fifteen letters is a lot of fodder to take on trust, so count them before committing: the four ingredients supply exactly the letters wanted, no more.

15 down DEODORANT

It refreshes dictionary’s backing – and work by Bellow (9)

Definition: It refreshes · charade + reversal

  • dictionary → DEO the OED is the dictionary a crossword means, turned round
  • work → DO to do a job is to work at it
  • Bellow → RANT to bellow at somebody is to rant

Bellow is Saul the novelist in the surface, where 'work by Bellow' is a book, and an ordinary verb in the parse.

17 down WINDSURF

Travel on board Chinook, say, seated on foam (8)

Definition: Travel on board · charade

  • Chinook → WIND the chinook is the warm dry wind that comes down off the Rockies
  • foam → SURF surf is the foam breaking along a shore

The Chinook in the surface is the twin-rotor helicopter, and 'on board' is what you are once you have climbed into one. Both readings have to go.

18 down ENRICHED

Buddy possibly needs energy to be put in goal and improved (8)

Definition: improved · container + charade

  • goal → END the end you are working towards is your goal, and it splits to take the rest
  • Buddy → RICH Buddy Rich, the big-band drummer
  • energy → E E is the physicist's symbol for energy

The surface is a football substitution, right down to 'in goal'. Buddy is a first name rather than a pal, which is what 'possibly' quietly admits.

20 down BANISH

Elected, elevated and taken to party’s heart – then shut out (6)

Definition: shut out · container + reversal

  • party → BASH a bash is a party, and it opens up to take the other piece
  • Elected → NI a candidate who is elected is IN, and this entry climbs

A political career in one sentence: elected, elevated, embraced, expelled. All of it is wordplay, and none of it is the definition until the dash.

21 down MADAME

Nice woman gets a daughter into musical? (6)

Definition: Nice woman · container

  • musical → MAME Mame, the Broadway musical of 1966
  • a daughter → AD the article A, then D, the family-tree abbreviation for daughter

The capital N is the whole disguise: Nice is the city on the Riviera, not the adjective, and the question mark covers the leap from a place to its language.

23 down ADMIT

Sing and entertain (5)

Definition: Sing · double definition

  • Sing → ADMIT underworld slang: a suspect who sings owns up
  • entertain → ADMIT to entertain a doubt is to let it in and give it room

Two rooms in three words: the interview room where a suspect sings, and the drawing room where a host entertains. Both come down to letting something in.
