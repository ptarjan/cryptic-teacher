Across

8 across RIFLE SHOT

Spinning round? (5,4)

Definition: Spinning round? · cryptic definition

  • Spinning → the surface offers a pirouette or a wheel turning; a bullet spins too, because of the spiral grooves cut inside a gun barrel
  • round? → a round is a single cartridge, and the question mark is the setter admitting these two words name one object

Rifling is the spiral cut inside a barrel, and it spins the bullet so that it flies true. Read 'round' as ammunition rather than as a shape and the clue is one picture.

9 across NEWEL

Post just arrived left on end of table (5)

Definition: Post · charade + last letter

  • just arrived → NEW something that has just arrived is new
  • end of table → E TABLE gives up its closing letter and nothing else
  • left → L L is the standard abbreviation for left

'Post' pulls towards the mail and 'just arrived' keeps that delivery picture going. Nothing in the surface survives the parse — the answer is a piece of joinery.

11 across IDEAL

Model observed fish using ears (5)

Definition: Model · homophone

  • observed fish → IDEAL observed is EYED and a fish is an EEL; run the pair together and one quite differently spelt word comes out

Say the two words quickly and the join between them disappears, which is the whole device — 'using ears' is the only thing telling you to stop reading and start listening.

12 across RIDICULES

Clear sporting clue is for guys (9)

Definition: guys · charade + anagram

  • Clear → RID to clear a place of something is to rid it of that thing
  • clue is → ICULES six letters shaken into a new order and parked behind the first three

'Clear' looks like the definition and turns out to be the first three letters. The definition is the last word, and it has to be read as a verb.

13 across PRINCES

Charges covering any number of people like Andrew in the past (7)

Definition: people like Andrew in the past · container

  • Charges → PRICES what a shop charges are its prices, and the word opens up after three letters
  • any number → N N is the mathematician's letter for a number left unspecified

'Charges covering' wants to be a verb about accusations, which is what makes the sentence read as a news bulletin rather than as a price list.

15 across ANALYST

Therapist at gathering votes against admitting learner (7)

Definition: Therapist · container

  • at → AT the two letters are taken as they stand and then pulled apart to make room
  • votes against → NAYS a nay is a vote against, and the plural splits after two letters
  • learner → L L is the plate a learner driver must display in Britain

Three small words do all the work and none of them looks like wordplay. The trap is reading 'at gathering' as a place rather than as an instruction.

17 across WAGON

Truck came first loaded with silver (5)

Definition: Truck · container

  • came first → WON whoever came first in the race won it, and the word opens after one letter
  • silver → AG AG is the chemical symbol for silver, from the Latin argentum

The race picture runs right through the clue, and the medal is the joke: silver here is the element on the periodic table, not second place.

18 across THE

One already mentioned mother's embraces (3)

Definition: One already mentioned · hidden word

  • mother's → THE three consecutive letters sit inside this word, starting at its third

Tiny answers hide in plain sight, and 'mother's' is the only word here with room for one. The definition is a grammar lesson: the definite article points back at something already introduced.

19 across BAIZE

Green surface of parking spaces reported (5)

Definition: Green surface · homophone

  • parking spaces → BAIZE a parking bay is a marked space, and the plural said aloud is spelt another way entirely

The green is snooker's, not a golf course or a village lawn — this puzzle keeps returning to the game, and this is the cloth it is played on.

21 across REFEREE

Official dealing with loose pockets back in time (7)

Definition: Official · charade + container + last letter

  • dealing with → RE re at the head of a memo means concerning, or dealing with
  • loose → FREE an animal that is loose is free, and the word opens after one letter
  • in time → E only TIME's closing letter is wanted

'Loose pockets' reads as one phrase about baggy trousers and is two instructions: the loose thing is what does the pocketing.

23 across CUE BALL

Line you wait for, to shout out loud "4D 18A ___ 25A?" (3,4)

Definition: "4D 18A ___ 25A?" · charade + homophone

  • Line you wait for → CUE an actor waits for a cue, the line that tells them to speak
  • to shout → BALL to bawl is to shout at full volume, and once you have only the sound it is spelt this way

Fill the gaps and the quotation is a snooker commentator's catchphrase, asked while the white rolls on after the strike.

24 across SEASCAPES

Frames featuring the blue? (9)

Definition: Frames featuring the blue? · cryptic definition

  • Frames → in snooker a frame is one complete game, which is the reading the surface wants; a frame is also what a painting hangs in
  • featuring the blue? → on a table the blue is a ball; in a gallery it is water, and a picture of water is mostly water

The puzzle has been playing snooker throughout, so 'frames' and 'the blue' land on the table first. Take them into a gallery instead and the answer is a wall of pictures.

25 across GOING

On and off (5)

Definition: On · double definition

  • On → a match that is still on is still being played, and an engine on is running
  • off → 'I'm off' is what you say as you leave, so this is departure rather than continuation

Two words, one answer twice over. The clue works because 'on and off' is itself a phrase, and that is what stops you splitting it into halves.

26 across VIRGO

Sign name for 17 18 & 19 across (5)

Definition: Sign · double definition

  • Sign → the zodiac reading — one of the twelve star signs, the one running from late August
  • name for 17 18 & 19 across → those three answers run together as a wag on the baize, a joker at the snooker table, and one man made his name being exactly that

Read 17, 18 and 19 across as one phrase and the answer is John Virgo, the snooker player turned commentator whose catchphrase 23 across quotes.

27 across SOAP OPERA

... thus time takes a popular TV institution (4,5)

Definition: TV institution · charade + container

  • thus → SO so means thus, the plainest substitution in the clue
  • a → A the article goes in as the single letter it is
  • popular → POP pop is the everyday short form of popular, as in pop music
  • time → ERA an era is a stretch of time

The three dots carry the surface on from 26 across and do no work at all, while 'time takes' looks like a proverb and is really two separate pieces.

1 down BRAINPOWER

Ability to calculate shower pressure in boudoir (10)

Definition: Ability to calculate · container + charade

  • boudoir → BOWER a bower is a lady's private room, which is what boudoir means, and it splits open after its first letter
  • shower → RAIN a shower of rain, read as weather rather than as plumbing
  • pressure → P P is the standard symbol for pressure in physics

The bathroom is the trap: 'shower pressure in boudoir' reads as one plumbing complaint, and not a word of it is doing that job.

2 down OFFERING

Gift from fiancé on vacation, the traditional one (8)

Definition: Gift · charade + outer letters

  • from → OF a present from someone is a present of theirs, so this preposition swaps for its two-letter twin
  • fiancé → FE only the outer pair survives the emptying, and the accented é is read as a plain E
  • the traditional one → RING on getting engaged, the traditional thing to hand over is a ring

The engagement story is doing double duty: the ring genuinely is the traditional gift, so the last piece of the wordplay and the surface picture are the same object.

3 down FELL

High ground dropped away (4)

Definition: High ground · double definition

  • High ground → up north a bare moorland hill goes by this word — Scafell and Bowfell carry it in their names
  • dropped away → the past tense of fall, said of land sloping down or of a level dropping

Read as one phrase it sounds like a landslip, and it is two unrelated senses. The northern hill is the half a solver outside Britain is least likely to have met.

4 down WHERE'S WALLY

Finding book for kids excited Welsh lawyer (6,5)

Definition: Finding book for kids · anagram

  • Welsh lawyer → WELSHLAWYER eleven letters, exactly what the two-part enumeration asks for
  • excited → anagram indicator

A Welsh lawyer is a person, not a piece of machinery, which is what makes the surface hold — the letter count is the only thing marking the phrase as fodder.

5 down STUD-MARE

Mother in stable steps in dirt according to Spooner (4-4)

Definition: Mother in stable · spoonerism

  • dirt → STUD dirt is mud, and after the swap its M has gone next door and an ST has arrived in exchange
  • steps → MARE steps are a stair, and with its ST traded away it takes the M and is written out to match the sound

A mother in a stable pulls hard towards the nativity, and there is no manger here at all — the stable is a horse's and the mother is a breeding animal.

6 down UNSCRAMBLE

Republican in crooked club means to straighten out (10)

Definition: to straighten out · anagram

  • Republican → R R is the letter printed beside a Republican politician's name in American usage
  • club means → CLUBMEANS nine more letters of fodder, taken exactly as they stand
  • crooked → anagram indicator

'Means to' reads as joinery announcing the definition, and the letter count is what gives it away: 'means' supplies five of the ten letters being shuffled.

7 down PLUS

Velvety fabric nearly quality that's desirable (4)

Definition: quality that's desirable · deletion

  • Velvety fabric → PLUS plush is the deep-piled velvety cloth, and it hands over everything except its closing H

Four words that read as one sentence about upholstery, and 'nearly' is the hinge: it belongs to the fabric behind it, not to the quality in front of it.

14 down CONTRACTOR

Charlie driving across fields perhaps promising party (10)

Definition: promising party · charade

  • Charlie → C Charlie is C in the NATO alphabet, the one pilots and police spell with
  • driving across fields perhaps → ONTRACTOR anyone driving across fields is perhaps on a tractor, and those words are written down as they are said

'Charlie driving' reads as one person at a wheel, and the split falls between the two words: one is a single letter, the other a picture to be named.

16 down THEOLOGIAN

Hotel in Goa boiling? God, I know! (10)

Definition: God, I know! · anagram

  • Hotel in Goa → HOTELINGOA ten letters, exactly the length wanted, taken as they stand
  • boiling → anagram indicator

Goa is a holiday coast, so the first half reads as a complaint about the heat. Nothing here is an abbreviation — all ten letters go straight into the mix.

18 down TRESPASS

Bath covered with hair gives offence (8)

Definition: offence · container

  • hair → TRESS a tress is a long lock of hair, and it splits after three letters
  • Bath → SPA Bath is the English city built on hot springs, and a spa is what such a place is

The capital B does the misleading: this is the Somerset city rather than a tub, and 'gives' is only joinery between the wordplay and the definition.

20 down IMAGINED

Setter's opposed to editor's thought (8)

Definition: thought · charade

  • Setter's → IM the setter is the person who wrote the puzzle, so 'setter's' comes out as I'm
  • opposed to → AGIN agin is the dialect spelling of against, as in agin the government
  • editor → ED ED is the usual short form for an editor, and the apostrophe-s after it only holds the sentence up

'Setter' is the convention worth keeping: it names the person writing the clue, so I, me and I'm are all in play wherever the word appears.

22 down FLAIR

Large breaks show talent (5)

Definition: talent · container

  • show → FAIR a fair is a show — a trade fair, a county fair — and it splits after one letter
  • Large → L L is the size printed on a clothing label for large

The snooker sense of 'breaks' is what the surface is selling. Here the word is an instruction, and 'show' has to be read as a noun.

23 down CO-STAR

A hundred and two sailors share the bill (2-4)

Definition: share the bill · charade

  • A hundred → C C is a hundred in Roman numerals
  • two sailors → OSTAR an ordinary seaman is an OS in the Navy and an old hand at sea is a tar, and the two are written one after the other

'Share the bill' looks like splitting a restaurant tab, which is the whole joke — the bill meant is the poster outside a theatre.

24 down SAVE

Put money by bar (4)

Definition: Put money by · double definition

  • Put money by → putting money by is what you do with whatever you have not spent
  • bar → the preposition meaning except, as in 'all bar one' — nothing to do with drink

Both halves are everyday words and neither is the pub: 'bar' is the preposition meaning except, and the answer carries that same sense in 'all save one'.

25 down GLOW

Down under ginger's first to turn red, perhaps (4)

Definition: turn red, perhaps · charade + first letter

  • ginger's first → G ginger gives up its opening letter and nothing else
  • Down → LOW low means down, as in low spirits or a low ceiling

'Down under' is Australia in the surface and two separate pieces in the parse, and 'ginger' is a hair colour there rather than a spice.
