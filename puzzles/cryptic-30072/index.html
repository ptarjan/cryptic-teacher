Across

1 across KENNINGTON OVAL

Accompanied by star, left behind with knowledge of Glasgow sports venue (10,4)

Definition: sports venue · charade

  • knowledge of Glasgow → KENNING Scots — a Glaswegian's 'kenning' is knowledge, as in 'beyond my kennin'
  • Accompanied by → TO as in singing to a piano — 'to' an accompaniment
  • star → NOVA an exploding star
  • left → L L = left; 'behind' puts the whole TO-NOVA-L run after KENNING

Don't hunt for a Glasgow ground: the Oval is in London, and 'Glasgow' is only there to flag KENNING as Scots for knowledge. 'Behind' tells you the star-and-left assembly follows it.

8 across RUING

Regretful of £1000 deposited after bankruptcy (5)

Definition: Regretful of · charade

  • bankruptcy → RUIN financial ruin
  • £1000 → G a grand — standard crossword currency

G for grand — £1000 — is a staple abbreviation; 'deposited after' banks it at the end of the ruin.

9 across SET-ASIDE

Discarded top class team (3-5)

Definition: Discarded · charade

  • top class → SETA school streaming: the top class is Set A
  • team → SIDE a side, as in a football side

School streaming does the work — in many schools the top class is called Set A, and a side is a team in sport.

11 across CADENZA

Solo turn by crossword guru touring centre of 1ac? (7)

Definition: Solo · reversal + container + middle letter

  • crossword guru → AZED Jonathan Crowther — Azed of the Observer, the setters' setter
  • centre of 1 → N the middle letter of ONE, which Azed 'tours' (wraps round): AZ-N-ED
  • ac? → AC the 'ac' tags along as letters; then the whole AZNED-AC does a turn

Azed took his pseudonym from Deza, a Spanish Grand Inquisitor, spelt backwards — so sending him round a turn is a fitting tribute. The question mark owns up to the liberties taken with '1ac'.

12 across EXTREMA

Maximum and minimum values taken out of context, remarkably (7)

Definition: Maximum and minimum values · hidden word

  • context, remarkably → EXTREMA spelled out across the two words: contEXT REMArkably

'Taken out of context' sounds like a complaint about quotation, but it is a literal instruction — the answer sits inside those letters.

13 across ALTER

Change location of union meeting in telephone call? (5)

Definition: Change · homophone

  • location of union meeting → ALTER the union is a wedding, and the altar is where it happens

Weddings are the 'union' here, and 'in telephone call' tells you to listen rather than read: altar and alter sound the same.

15 across OPPRESSED

Under a great burden, Ms Viner perhaps, with work (9)

Definition: Under a great burden · charade

  • with work → OP op. — an opus; 'with' bolts it on at the front
  • Ms Viner perhaps → PRESSED Katharine Viner edits the Guardian: a press ed.

'Perhaps' concedes Ms Viner is only one example of a press ed. — any newspaper editor would do.

17 across LAMPLIGHT

An essential for burner of midnight oil hit spot (9)

Definition: An essential for burner of midnight oil · charade

  • hit → LAM to lam = to hit, as in 'lam into'
  • spot → PLIGHT a spot — the tight kind

Both halves are rougher than they look: to LAM is to hit, and the spot here is a PLIGHT. Burn the midnight oil and this is what you read by.

20 across CLEAN

Guiltless about Jack Sprat’s diet, it’s implied (5)

Definition: Guiltless · charade

  • about → C c = circa, about
  • Jack Sprat’s diet, it’s implied → LEAN he could eat no fat — so what he did eat, by implication, was the lean

The nursery rhyme is the key: Jack Sprat could eat no fat, so his diet — never stated outright — was the lean.

21 across NARWHAL

Arctic resident worked in retirement with supercomputer (7)

Definition: Arctic resident · charade + reversal

  • worked → NAR worked = RAN, sent backwards 'in retirement'
  • with → W w = with
  • supercomputer → HAL HAL 9000, the computer in 2001: A Space Odyssey

The tusked whale of the Arctic. HAL is cinema's most famous computer, and 'in retirement' — withdrawing — is a direction-neutral way to reverse RAN.

23 across SAPSAGO

Cheese production initially stops its run (7)

Definition: Cheese · first letter + container + charade

  • production → P just its first letter, which 'stops' — plugs — the gap
  • its → SASA 'it' = SA (sex appeal, the 'It' in It girl); the plural gives a pair of them
  • run → GO a run at something = a go at it

Sapsago is a hard green Swiss cheese. 'It' = SA is vintage crosswordese for sex appeal; Enigmatist pluralises it to get two, then the P stops the gap between them.

25 across SIDESTEP

Dodge section that is recording (8)

Definition: Dodge · charade

  • section → S s. — as in a legal citation
  • that is → IDEST id est — 'that is' before it was abbreviated to i.e.
  • recording → EP an extended-play record

The trick is 'that is' expanded to its full Latin, id est, rather than the usual two-letter i.e.

26 across TAIGA

Forest dweller there – fearfully symmetrical, according to recital (5)

Definition: Forest · homophone

  • dweller there – fearfully symmetrical → TAIGA Blake's 'Tyger Tyger, burning bright… thy fearful symmetry' — and Siberian tigers really do live in the taiga

The taiga is the great northern forest, and its most famous dweller arrives via Blake's poem — 'fearful symmetry' is the Tyger's own phrase, delivered 'according to recital'.

27 across KENSINGTON GORE

Planet’s reporter takes chorus on leave, on the way to the Proms? (10,4)

Definition: the way to the Proms? · container + charade

  • Planet → italicised because it is a title: the Daily Planet, Superman's paper
  • reporter → KENT Clark Kent, the Planet's reporter — split round the chorus
  • chorus → SING to chorus = to sing together; it sits inside KENT
  • on → ON the first 'on' spells itself
  • leave → GO to leave = to go
  • on → RE the second 'on' = re, about

Kensington Gore is the road at the Royal Albert Hall, where the Proms play — and the name later became stage slang for fake blood. The italics flag Planet as a newspaper: Clark Kent's Daily Planet.

Down

1 down KERB CRAWLING

Charles and his mum in dust-up about constant sleazy activity (4,8)

Definition: sleazy activity · container + charade

  • Charles → KING King Charles — K…ING wraps round everything else
  • his mum → ER Elizabeth Regina, the late Queen's royal cypher
  • dust-up → BRAWL split round the constant
  • constant → C c — the speed of light, physics' favourite constant

Two containers at once: KING (Charles) wraps everything, and inside it the BRAWL wraps the C. ER = Elizabeth Regina is a convention worth learning — setters lean on royal cyphers constantly.

2 down NAIAD

Water nymph transformed by Diana (5)

Definition: Water nymph · anagram

  • Diana → DIANA the goddess supplies her own five letters

Diana's own letters rearrange into one of the nymphs who attended the goddess of the hunt — surface and wordplay are the same five letters.

3 down IN GENERAL

Mostly where soldiers are trained to place their trust (2,7)

Definition: Mostly · double definition

  • Mostly → the everyday sense: in general = mostly, on the whole
  • where soldiers are trained to place their trust → whimsically, troops put their faith in (their) general

One word of definition, then the joke: soldiers are drilled to place their trust in their general.

4 down GESTAPO

Harsh and unscrupulous activity, seizing racy tapes (7)

Definition: Harsh and unscrupulous · container + anagram

  • activity → GO on the go = active; G…O seizes the rest
  • racy tapes → ESTAP the letters of TAPES, made 'racy' — shuffled

GO as in 'on the go'. The definition is really the Gestapo's reputation — and the surface, an outfit seizing tapes, is uncomfortably close to the literal truth.

5 down ON THE UP

Awfully sick after leaving new soup kitchen, getting better (2,3,2)

Definition: getting better · anagram + deletion

  • sick → the letters S, I, C, K — scattered ('awfully') through SOUP KITCHEN — depart
  • soup kitchen → OUPTHEN what is left of SOUP KITCHEN once SICK has gone

A deletion inside an anagram: 'awfully' licenses taking SICK's letters from wherever they sit in SOUP KITCHEN, and 'new' stirs what remains.

6 down ONSET

Start rolling the cameras here (5)

Definition: Start · double definition

  • Start → an onset is a start — the straight sense
  • rolling the cameras here → read it as ON SET — where a film crew rolls the cameras

A start, or where filming starts: on set. The second reading is the whimsical one, which is why it carries the cameras.

7 down ADDRESSEE

Notices surrounding hip-hop doctor’s name on envelope (9)

Definition: name on envelope · container

  • Notices → AD the first of the two 'notices' — a small ad
  • hip-hop doctor’s → DRES Dr Dre's — sandwiched by the notices
  • Notices → SEE …and the second: to see something is to notice it

'Notices' is plural for a reason: the one word supplies two separate pieces, one at each end of the answer. Spotting that a single word does double duty is the step most solvers miss.

10 down MAIDEN VOYAGE

Sorcerer welcomes assistance getting diplomat initial crossing? (6,6)

Definition: initial crossing? · container + charade

  • Sorcerer → MAGE M…AGE, wrapped round the rest
  • assistance → AID help
  • diplomat → ENVOY an envoy

The sorcerer swallows both the help and the diplomat. 'Initial crossing' is the definition — a ship's first trip across the water.

14 down TOM CRUISE

Actor suggesting a spot of 1dn? (3,6)

Definition: Actor · charade

  • a spot of 1dn? → TOM CRUISE 'tom' is old slang for a prostitute, and to 'cruise' is to drive around looking for custom — a bit of kerb crawling (1 down)

The actor's name reads as a little kerb crawling: a tom on the cruise. The cross-reference to 1 down is the whole engine of the clue.

16 down EL CAPITAN

Sort of alpine act a daring climb? (2,7)

Definition: a daring climb? · anagram

  • alpine act → ALPINEACT nine letters to shuffle

El Capitan in Yosemite is the daring climb — famously scaled without ropes in Free Solo. 'Sort of' quietly does the shuffling.

18 down GELATIN

It’s a bit gluey getting up on a cycle (7)

Definition: It’s a bit gluey · anagram

  • getting up → ELATING elating = uplifting, getting your spirits up

A rare device: 'on a cycle' means the letters rotate — push the G of ELATING from the back round to the front. Gelatin genuinely is the gluey bit: glue is made from it.

19 down TOSSPOT

Old drunkard beats wino up (7)

Definition: Old drunkard · charade + reversal

  • wino → TOS a wino is a SOT, sent climbing
  • beats → SPOT beats = TOPS, also sent climbing

This is a down entry, so 'up' sends TOPS and SOT bottom-to-top together. 'Old' flags that tosspot is an archaic word for a drunkard.

22 down HEELS

Spurs are improving, reportedly (still in need of work) (5)

Definition: Spurs · homophone

  • are improving → HEELS to heal is to mend, to get better
  • (still in need of work) → a parenthetical dig — even improving, Tottenham remain a work in progress — keeping you on the football reading of Spurs

A heel is the spur a fighting cock wears — the sense behind 'well-heeled', originally meaning armed for the pit. The football club is pure decoy.

24 down AMIGO

Friend is one shot (5)

Definition: Friend · charade

  • is → AM first person: is = am
  • one → I the Roman numeral
  • shot → GO have a shot = have a go

Read it as a tiny sentence: is = AM, one = I, shot = GO — and a Spanish friend appears.
