Guardian Quiptic Crossword No 1,398

Across

1 across GARBAGE

For example, a crow returning for rubbish (7)

Definition: rubbish · charade + reversal

  • For example → EG EG is the Latin exempli gratia, the standing shorthand for 'for example'.
  • a → A The clue's own single letter, taken exactly as it stands.
  • crow → BRAG To crow about something is to brag about it.

A crow flapping back to pick over the bins.

'Crow' looks like the bird the surface has just put in the air; it is the verb, to boast. The three pieces are built in clue order and only then turned round.

5 across WAILERS

Those serving time ignored and left inside, they sound really upset! (7)

Definition: they sound really upset! · deletion + container

  • Those serving → WAITERS Waiters are the people serving at table.
  • time → T T is the abbreviation for time, and it is the letter that gets ignored.
  • left → L L is the abbreviation for left, and it takes the vacated place.

Prisoners locked up, forgotten, and making a terrible racket about it.

'Those serving time' reads as one phrase about convicts. The wordplay splits it: the servers come from the first two words and an abbreviation from the third.

9 across NO DOUBT

Money? Nothing for Spooner, surely (2,5)

Definition: surely · spoonerism

  • Money → NO Dough is slang for money, and it is said "doh". Hand that D over to the other word, take the N coming back, and "doh" becomes "noh".
  • Nothing → DOUBT A nought is a zero, and it is said "nawt". It gives up its N, collects the D the other word let go of, and "dawt" is spelt with a silent B.

Somebody asked about money and answering that there is none of it.

Spooner trades sounds, not letters, which is why the spellings change out of all recognition: "doh" and "nawt" swap their opening consonants and come back as two words that share almost no letters with them. Setters allow the vowels to drift a little on the way.

10 across NIRVANA

Unit stripped, gunners commandeer vehicle to find ultimate destination? (7)

Definition: ultimate destination? · charade + container + middle letters

  • Unit → NI The two middle letters of this four-letter word, once the U and the T are stripped off.
  • gunners → RA RA is the Royal Artillery, the British army's gunners.
  • vehicle → VAN A van is the crossword's plainest three-letter vehicle.

Soldiers seizing a lorry and driving off towards the last stop.

The question mark is the setter admitting the definition is a stretch: NIRVANA is the Buddhist release from rebirth, not a place on anybody's timetable.

11 across SPINELESS

Yellow Skittle eaten by tennis’s Monica, before beginning to serve (9)

Definition: Yellow · container + first letter

  • tennis’s Monica → SELES Monica Seles won nine grand slam singles titles in the 1990s.
  • Skittle → PIN The skittles you knock down in a bowling alley are pins.
  • serve → S The opening letter of this word, and nothing else.

A yellow sweet swallowed by a tennis champion moments before she serves.

Skittle wears a capital to send you to the sweets, but the bowling sense is the one that pays, and 'Yellow' is an accusation of cowardice rather than a colour.

12 across GOUDA

Nibbles of grapes, olives, unagi, dips and cheese (5)

Definition: cheese · first letters

  • grapes, olives, unagi, dips and → GOUDA The opening letter of each of these five words, in the order the clue lists them.

A tapas table being picked at: grapes, olives, eel, dips and something from the cheeseboard.

The list reads as a buffet, so 'and' looks like it is joining the items rather than standing in the queue with them — and it is the letter most solvers leave out.

13 across SCRUB

Cancel massage after ‘50% off’ scam (5)

Definition: Cancel · charade + deletion

  • scam → SC The first half of this four-letter word, the other half discounted away.
  • massage → RUB To rub someone's shoulders is to massage them.

A half-price spa offer that turns out to be a con.

The sale sign in quotation marks is an instruction, not scenery, and 'after' is the only word telling you which of the two pieces comes first.

15 across THRILLERS

Sensational stories of farm workers smuggling heroin end in disaster (9)

Definition: Sensational stories · container + last letter

  • farm workers → TILLERS Tillers are the people who work the soil.
  • heroin → H H is the standing abbreviation for heroin.
  • disaster → R The final letter of this word.

Farmhands caught running drugs, and the whole business ending badly.

'End in disaster' is the sentence's natural finish and an instruction at the same time, which is what makes it easy to read straight past. H for heroin is worth learning once.

17 across APPLAUDED

Showed appreciation of papal dude dancing (9)

Definition: Showed appreciation · anagram

  • papal dude → APPLAUDED Nine letters of fodder — P, A, P, A, L, D, U, D and E — reshuffled.

A cardinal cutting loose on the dance floor, to general approval.

Counting the fodder is the whole check: those two words hold exactly nine letters. The definition is in the past tense, which the answer has to match.

19 across RATED

Esteemed renegade journalist (5)

Definition: Esteemed · charade

  • renegade → RAT A rat is somebody who turns on their own side.
  • journalist → ED ED is the editor, the crossword's standard journalist.

A turncoat reporter who is nonetheless well thought of.

Nothing in the clue announces that a charade is happening — there is no indicator and no link word, so three bare words have to be tried as a definition plus two pieces.

22 across OASIS

Old donkey circling island watering hole (5)

Definition: watering hole · charade + container

  • Old → O O is the standing abbreviation for old.
  • donkey → ASS An ass is a donkey.
  • island → I I is the map-maker's abbreviation for island.

An elderly donkey plodding rings round an island.

'Watering hole' is normally a joke name for a pub, and here it is meant literally: water in a desert.

23 across SCORPIONS

Contempt surrounding Greek character, Oscar, and the introduction of stinging arachnids (9)

Definition: arachnids · container + first letter

  • Contempt → SCORN Scorn is contempt.
  • Greek character → PI Pi is the sixteenth letter of the Greek alphabet.
  • Oscar → O Oscar is O in the NATO radio alphabet.
  • stinging → S The opening letter of this word.

Sneering that has built up around a Greek, a man called Oscar and a stinging remark.

'Stinging arachnids' looks like a two-word definition. The adjective belongs to the wordplay and only the noun defines, which is why the letters run one short if you take both.

25 across SQUEEZE

Grasp Camilla, almost ousting individual from mass (7)

Definition: Grasp · substitution + deletion

  • mass → SIZE The mass of a thing is its size.
  • individual → I I is the crossword's individual, the single self, and it is the letter that gets turned out.
  • Camilla, almost → QUEE Camilla is the Queen, wife of King Charles III, and almost all of that title is four letters.

Elbowing one person out of a crowd to get hold of the Queen.

'Ousting X from Y' is a swap rather than a plain deletion: the letter leaves and something else moves straight into the space it kept warm.

26 across EDITORS

Newspaper bosses, those we’re indebted to, giving up career, exhausted (7)

Definition: Newspaper bosses · deletion + outer letters

  • those we’re indebted to → CREDITORS The people you owe money to are your creditors.
  • career → CR The first and last letters of this word, everything between them spent.

Newspaper chiefs walking out of the job, worn out and owing money.

'Career, exhausted' reads as the end of a sad story and is really the instruction: a word with its middle used up leaves two letters, and those two come off the front of the longer one.

27 across DEEPEST

Dutch saint, stabbed with twisted sword, as far in as possible (7)

Definition: as far in as possible · anagram + container

  • Dutch → D D is the abbreviation for Dutch.
  • sword → EEPE An epee is a fencing sword, and its four letters are wrenched about.
  • saint → ST ST is the standing abbreviation for saint.

A Dutch saint run through with a bent blade.

'Dutch saint' is not a person but two abbreviations standing side by side, and the definition is the four-word phrase at the end that reads like the rest of the story.

28 across DEAD END

No way out of here, badly need Dad (4,3)

Definition: No way out of here · anagram

  • need Dad → DEAD END Seven letters — N, E, E, D, D, A and D — rearranged across two words.

A child desperate for their father, with nowhere left to turn.

The enumeration is the tell: seven letters split four and three, out of two short words that hold exactly seven between them.

Down

1 down GENESIS

Low-down on Earth, Sun, first person and Sabbath in here? (7)

Definition: in here? · charade

  • Low-down → GEN The gen on something is the low-down, the inside information.
  • Earth → E E is the standing abbreviation for Earth.
  • Sun → S S is the abbreviation for Sun.
  • first person → I The first-person pronoun is a single letter.
  • Sabbath → S S is the abbreviation for Sabbath.

The inside story on the Earth, the sun, the first man and the day of rest.

The definition is the two small words at the end, which point at a book instead of naming one. 'First person' is the pronoun I, not Adam.

2 down RUDDIER

More impolite, I’d turned up, interrupting, becoming increasingly crimson (7)

Definition: increasingly crimson · container + reversal

  • More impolite → RUDER Ruder is the comparative of rude.
  • I’d → DI The clue's own contraction I'D, its two letters climbing the grid.

Somebody's cheeks going redder and redder as a rude remark keeps butting in.

'More impolite' sits where a definition usually sits, at the front, and it is the wordplay. The definition is the reddening at the far end of the sentence.

3 down AMUSE

Entertain, dressed up as Emu (5)

Definition: Entertain · anagram

  • as Emu → AMUSE Five letters of fodder — A, S, E, M and U — rearranged.

Turning up to do a turn in an emu costume.

The capital E on Emu makes it look like a name to be looked up, but it is fodder, and so is the little word 'as' — both go into the shuffle.

4 down ENTREATED

Pleaded – hospital department responded with no heart (9)

Definition: Pleaded · charade + deletion

  • hospital department → ENT ENT is a hospital's ear, nose and throat department.
  • responded → REATED To respond is to have REACTED, and the C sitting at its centre is the heart that goes.

Begging at the hospital and getting a heartless answer back.

ENT is worth learning once and owning forever — three letters that turn up whenever a clue mentions a hospital department. The dash is doing the work of an equals sign.

5 down WINGS

Moves fast and has casual sex with other partners, topless (5)

Definition: Moves fast · deletion

  • has casual sex with other partners → SWINGS Swinging is the term for couples who swap partners.

A description of someone with a quick car and a very relaxed private life.

'Topless' does two jobs at once: it keeps the racy surface going and it is a plain instruction about the first letter of a word running down the grid.

6 down IRREGULAR

Current Rolls Royce, mega dull and dark on the inside – unusual (9)

Definition: unusual · charade + middle letters

  • Current → I I is the physicist's symbol for electric current.
  • Rolls Royce → RR The car maker's own initials, as they appear on the badge.
  • mega → EG The two letters sitting between the M and the A.
  • dull → UL The two letters sitting between the D and the final L.
  • dark → AR The two letters sitting between the D and the K.

A road-test verdict on the latest Rolls Royce: huge, boring and gloomy inside.

'Current' is electricity here, not the present day. The dash marks the join: everything to its left builds the answer, and the single word on its right defines it.

7 down ERASURE

Destruction of extract from dossier a surety (7)

Definition: Destruction · hidden word

  • dossier a surety → ERASURE Seven consecutive letters run across these three words, starting partway into the first.

Paperwork being pulled out of a file to back up a guarantee.

'Destruction of' at the front reads like an anagram is about to be ordered. It is the definition plus a link, and the letters are already lying in order.

8 down SHAMANS

They offer spiritual healing without consuming meat (7)

Definition: They offer spiritual healing · container

  • without → SANS SANS is the French for without, long since borrowed into English.
  • meat → HAM Ham is an everyday meat, and three letters is all it takes.

Faith healers who happen to be vegetarian.

'Without' looks like an instruction to take something away and is in fact four letters of the answer, while 'consuming meat' is one word swallowing another rather than anybody eating.

14 down BRASSIERE

Money I waste, ultimately, on underwear (9)

Definition: underwear · charade + last letter

  • Money → BRASS Brass is northern British slang for money — 'where there's muck there's brass'.
  • I → I The clue's own letter, taken exactly as it stands.
  • waste → E The last letter of this word.
  • on → RE RE means on the subject of, as at the head of a memo.

Someone totting up how much cash they have frittered away on lingerie.

'On' looks like the joinery holding the sentence upright and is really two of the nine letters, which is why the count comes up short if you skip past it.

16 down RADIOHEAD

Group study covering commercial Moon Hotel (9)

Definition: Group · container + charade

  • study → READ To study a text is to read it.
  • commercial → AD A commercial is an ad.
  • Moon → IO Io is one of Jupiter's four largest moons.
  • Hotel → H Hotel is H in the NATO radio alphabet.

Someone poring over an advert for a hotel called the Moon.

Capitals on Moon and Hotel make them look like the name over a door; both are ordinary wordplay, and the definition is the single word standing at the front.

17 down AROUSED

Woke road use cultivated (7)

Definition: Woke · anagram

  • road use → AROUSED Seven letters — R, O, A, D, U, S and E — turned right over.

A fashionably right-on transport policy, carefully nurtured.

'Woke' is the modern political adjective in the surface and the past tense of wake in the parse, and only the second reading gives a word the answer can replace.

18 down PASTURE

Grazing land on the other side of river (7)

Definition: Grazing land · charade

  • on the other side of → PAST To be past something is to be beyond it, on its far side.
  • river → URE The Ure is a Yorkshire river.

A field of grass on the far bank.

British setters keep a stock of very short rivers — Ure, Exe, Dee, Po — and an unqualified 'river' is an invitation to try them. Four words of the clue turn out to be four letters.

20 down TWOSOME

Couple Mummy Pig brought up in large book (7)

Definition: Couple · container + reversal

  • large book → TOME A tome is a big, heavy book.
  • Mummy Pig → SOW A sow is a female pig, so a mummy pig is one — and these three letters then climb the grid.

A children's-book pig being lifted out of a fat volume.

The capitals send you to Mummy Pig of the cartoon Peppa Pig, a fixture of British children's television. Read as plain English the phrase is just a mother pig.

21 down DISUSED

Abandoned South American was no longer around (7)

Definition: Abandoned · container + charade

  • was no longer → DIED A person who was, and no longer is, died.
  • South → S S is the compass abbreviation for south.
  • American → US US stands in for American.

A South American who is no longer with us, and long forgotten.

'South American' reads as one nationality and comes apart into two abbreviations, while 'around' sits at the very end looking like a description of wandering.

23 down SWEET

You and I set about making dessert (5)

Definition: dessert · container

  • set → SET The clue's own three letters, used exactly as they stand.
  • You and I → WE You and I together are we.

Two people setting about a pudding together.

The definition leans on British dining-room usage, where the sweet is the pudding course after the main one. 'Set' is one of the rare fragments a clue uses without altering it at all.

24 down PRIMA

First Muslim Prince rising to power (5)

Definition: First · reversal + charade

  • power → P P is the physicist's symbol for power.
  • Muslim Prince → RIMA An amir is a Muslim prince, and those four letters climb the grid.

The first Muslim prince to climb his way to power.

'First' standing alone at the front is the definition, and the answer is the Italian word English borrowed for prima ballerina and prima donna.
