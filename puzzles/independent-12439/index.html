Independent Cryptic Crossword No 12,439

Across

1 across PROJECTIONIST

I jettison crop that's failed one screening (13)

Definition: one screening · anagram

  • I jettison crop → PROJECTIONIST thirteen letters to rearrange: I JETTISON CROP

The surface is a grower dumping a ruined harvest, and nothing in it suggests a cinema until the last two words arrive.

8 across WATT

Slow at thinking, displays a bit of power (4)

Definition: a bit of power · hidden word

  • Slow at thinking → WATT lying across sloW AT Thinking

The joke is the mismatch: a phrase about being dim is where a unit of power turns out to be sitting. James Watt gave the unit its name.

9 across TEXT EDITOR

How setter might contact boss on a mobile application (4,6)

Definition: application · charade

  • How setter might contact → TEXT to text somebody is to send them a message
  • boss → EDITOR the setter's boss is the crossword editor, who commissions and checks the puzzle
  • on a mobile → surface only — the phone is where texting happens and it contributes no letters

'Setter' means the person who wrote this crossword, and their boss really is an editor: a rare clue whose wordplay is autobiographical.

10 across COMPARED

On air, was a show host judged against somebody else? (8)

Definition: judged against somebody else · homophone

  • was a show host → COMPARED to compere is to host a show, so this is COMPERED; say it aloud and you write the other spelling

A compere is the person who introduces the acts, from the French for godfather — two spellings that sound identical and mean nothing like each other.

12 across COMPLY

Go along with firm parliamentarian - lobby must be eviscerated (6)

Definition: Go along with · charade + outer letters

  • firm → CO CO is the abbreviation for company, a firm
  • parliamentarian → MP a Member of Parliament
  • lobby → LY L…Y is all that is left of LOBBY once the middle is out

'Lobby' is chosen with care — it is the parliamentary word for the corridor where members are buttonholed, so the surface never breaks character.

13 across BANKRUPT

Bust rioting punk brat (8)

Definition: Bust · anagram

  • punk brat → BANKRUPT the eight letters of PUNK BRAT, rearranged

Three words and nothing else. Short clues are often the hardest, because there is no surface to tell you which end the definition is at.

15 across ALWAYS

Haul a weary ass regularly, or every time? (6)

Definition: every time · alternate letters

  • Haul a weary ass → ALWAYS every second letter of hAuL A wEaRy AsS

The donkey is scenery. What matters is that the phrase was built letter by letter to land on the answer, small words counted in with the rest.

17 across PHONIC

Husband about to crash photo of speech (6)

Definition: of speech · charade + container

  • Husband → H H is the standard abbreviation for husband
  • about → ON a book about cats is a book on cats
  • photo → PIC a photo is a pic, and it splits P…IC to let the other two in

'About' meaning ON is worth banking — it turns up constantly — and here it also keeps the surface reading as a husband barging into a picture.

19 across INSCRIBE

Carve manger in Xmas decoration, avoiding the edges (8)

Definition: Carve · container + middle letters

  • Xmas decoration → INSE the decoration is TINSEL; without its edges it reads INSE, and it opens to take a passenger
  • manger → CRIB a crib is the manger in a nativity scene

The crib and the tinsel keep the surface entirely Christmassy, so the two instructions buried in it — go inside, drop the edges — read as decoration.

21 across VIAGRA

Roman street artist swallows good drug (6)

Definition: drug · charade + container

  • Roman street → VIA via is Latin, and modern Italian, for a road
  • good → G G is the standard abbreviation for good
  • artist → RA a Royal Academician, the crossword's usual artist

RA for artist and G for good are two of the abbreviations that come up most often; 'Roman street artist' is three separate pieces standing in a row.

23 across SURGEONS

Encourage onboard medical professionals (8)

Definition: medical professionals · container

  • board → SS a steamship is SS, and it splits S…S around its passengers
  • Encourage on → URGEON to urge somebody on is to encourage them

One word has to be read as two doing two jobs: the ON that completes the phrasal verb, and the vessel that wraps itself round the lot.

25 across SEQUENCING

Ordering mashed quince in shells of Senegalese nutmeg (10)

Definition: Ordering · anagram + container + outer letters

  • Senegalese → SE the shell of SenegalesE
  • quince → QUENCI the six letters of QUINCE, mashed
  • nutmeg → NG the shell of NutmeG

Two different words supply the shell, one pair of letters each, which is what makes the container hard to see behind an entirely edible surface.

27 across BAAL

Heartless, trite ancient god (4)

Definition: ancient god · deletion

  • trite → BANAL something trite is banal
  • Heartless → the central N comes out and the four remaining letters close up

Baal is the Canaanite god the Old Testament rails against, and the comma after the first word invites you to read it as describing a person.

28 across STEADFASTNESS

Stan's deaf - Tess possibly shows loyalty (13)

Definition: loyalty · anagram

  • Stan's deaf - Tess → STEADFASTNESS the thirteen letters of STANS DEAF TESS, rearranged

'Possibly' is a mild anagram signal that reads as ordinary hedging, and the two names exist only to hand over letters.

Down

1 down PIANOLA

Epic want solo pared down on one instrument (7)

Definition: instrument · charade + middle letters

  • Epic → PI ePIc with its outer letters pared away
  • want → AN wANt loses its shell the same way
  • solo → OL sOLo, down to its middle two letters
  • one → A 'one' is the indefinite article A

The clue reads as a description of a stripped-back epic, but 'pared down' is an instruction, and it governs three words in a row — that repetition is what hides it.

2 down OUTSPOKEN

Happy to share opinions upon Stoke undergoing an earthquake (9)

Definition: Happy to share opinions · anagram

  • upon Stoke → OUTSPOKEN the nine letters of UPON STOKE, shaken into a new order

Stoke puts a real town in the surface and 'upon' reads as a preposition joining it to something — both words are material, which is the hard thing to see.

3 down ENTER

Record tenants removing walls (5)

Definition: Record · deletion

  • tenants → RENTERS people who pay rent are renters
  • removing walls → the R and the S at either end come away

Two steps, and the first is the buried one: the clue never prints the word you are meant to demolish, only a synonym for it.

4 down TUX

Fancy jacket time for everyone on social media site (3)

Definition: Fancy jacket · charade

  • time → T T is the scientist's symbol for time
  • for everyone → U a U certificate passes a film for everyone, whatever their age
  • social media site → X X is the site that used to be Twitter

Three abbreviations from three unrelated worlds — physics, film classification and a rebranded website — so no part of the sentence sounds like wordplay.

5 down OVERCOATS

Finished performing Tosca in protective gear? (9)

Definition: protective gear · charade + anagram

  • Finished → OVER when something is finished it is over
  • Tosca → COATS the five letters of Puccini's opera, rearranged

'Finished performing' reads as one phrase about the end of a run, and that false join is exactly the seam the clue wants you to miss.

6 down IDIOM

Buddhist chant supports old Ugandan saying (5)

Definition: saying · charade

  • old Ugandan → IDI Idi Amin, Uganda's dictator in the 1970s
  • Buddhist chant → OM om is the syllable chanted in Buddhist and Hindu practice

Idi Amin is the setter's standing source for three letters whenever Uganda turns up — worth filing away with the rest of the crossword's cast.

7 down TROLLEY

Internet troublemaker initially explains young loony's off this? (7)

Definition: loony's off this? · charade + first letters

  • Internet troublemaker → TROLL a troll is someone who stirs up trouble online
  • explains young → EY the opening letter of each of those two words

'Off one's trolley' is the phrase the question mark points at, so the definition is a whole idiom with its noun missing rather than a word for mad.

11 down DETAILS

Small detachments of troops severely criticised about Italy’s uprising (7)

Definition: Small detachments of troops · container + reversal

  • severely criticised → SLATED to slate something is to criticise it savagely; it opens between the L and the A
  • Italy → I I is Italy's international vehicle registration letter

Two things happen to one word: it takes a passenger and is then read upwards. On the surface, an uprising in Italy is simply the news.

14 down UNCHAINED

Liberated a French church Diane renovated (9)

Definition: Liberated · charade + anagram

  • a French → UN 'a' in French is UN
  • church → CH CH is the standard abbreviation for church
  • Diane → AINED the five letters of DIANE, rebuilt

'A French' is the setter asking for the French word for 'a' rather than describing anything Gallic — a construction worth recognising on sight.

16 down WORKER BEE

Colonist's religious outfit - tiny according to Spooner (6,3)

Definition: Colonist · spoonerism

  • religious outfit → WORKER a burka is the religious garment; it hands its B over and takes a W in exchange
  • tiny → BEE tiny is wee, and it collects the B the first word gave up

'Colonist' is the misdirection — it suggests settlers and empire, when the colony in question is a hive.

18 down HEIFERS

European condition found in that woman's cows, ... (7)

Definition: cows · charade + container

  • European → E E is the abbreviation for European
  • condition → IF an 'if' is a condition attached to a promise
  • that woman's → HERS what belongs to that woman is hers; it opens up after the H

'Condition' as IF is the step to spot. Everything else reads as an ordinary sentence about somebody's livestock, which is what makes it hard.

20 down BANTAMS

... chickens, bats and man out of sorts (7)

Definition: chickens · anagram

  • bats and man → BANTAMS BATS and MAN, seven letters shuffled together

The clue finishes the sentence 18 down started, and 'bats' looks like an instruction to shuffle in its own right. It isn't — it is material.

22 down GAUZE

Look around you for light dressing (5)

Definition: light dressing · container

  • Look → GAZE to look steadily is to gaze; it opens between the A and the Z
  • you → U U is the text-message spelling of 'you'

'Look around you' is a complete instruction in ordinary English, which is why it slides past — and the setter means it letter by letter.

24 down RIGHT

Correct audio for ceremony (5)

Definition: Correct · homophone

  • ceremony → RIGHT a ceremony is a rite; spoken aloud it arrives as the other spelling

English keeps three of this sound in play — rite, wright and write — so it is the crossing letters, not the ear, that decide which the grid wants.

26 down IDA

Cretan's height of impertinence, disregarding Athenian's introductions (3)

Definition: Cretan's height · first letters

  • impertinence, disregarding Athenian's → IDA the opening letters of those three words

'Disregarding' looks like an instruction to drop something and is really fodder — the initials run straight through it, which is the trap.
