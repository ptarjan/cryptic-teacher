7 across TOFF

Aristocrat's sweet, giving drugs away (4)

Definition: Aristocrat · deletion

Aristocrat -> TOFF: British slang for someone of the upper class, said with a sneer at the accent and the inherited money.

  • sweet → TOFFEE a chewy sweet in a twist of paper, six letters before the trim
  • drugs → E is ecstasy in crossword shorthand, and the plural means there are two of them to lose
  • away — letters given away are parted with altogether, removed from the word rather than shuffled about inside it

In the surface a rich boy is handing pills out; in the parse the clue confiscates them. The plural matters, because one E would leave a letter too many.

8 across EARBASHING

He bargains about carpeting (10)

Definition: carpeting · anagram

carpeting -> EARBASHING: both are informal nouns for a long scolding, one from being summoned onto the carpet, the other from the noise inflicted on your ear.

  • He bargains → HEBARGAINS ten letters of fodder, the whole phrase lifted from the clue
  • about — 'about' in the sense of turned about, facing another way, and letters turned about come out in another order

'Carpeting' is not floor covering: being called onto the carpet in front of the boss is where the word comes from, and the haggling surface hides that entirely.

10 across ASSESS

Tax dope and hash if lacking millions (6)

Definition: Tax · charade + deletion

Tax -> ASSESS: to tax someone is to work out and impose the sum they owe, which is precisely what a revenue officer does to a return.

  • dope → ASS an ass is a fool, which is what calling someone a dope means
  • hash → MESS to make a hash of a job is to make a mess of it
  • millions → M is the standard abbreviation for millions, and it is the letter that goes
  • lacking — a word lacking something is short of it, so the letter named goes missing before the rest is used

Two drug words in a row and neither is a drug: 'dope' is the idiot and 'hash' the muddle. That double bluff is the whole joke of the surface.

11 across LAMENTED

City folk retired detective deplored (8)

Definition: deplored · charade + reversal

deplored -> LAMENTED: both are past-tense verbs for regretting something publicly, a death or a decision, rather than merely disliking it.

  • City → LA LA is the two-letter short form of Los Angeles
  • folk → MEN folk in the old sense is people generally, and here it wants the three-letter word for them
  • detective → TED DET is the rank abbreviation on a police detective's name, and it comes in backwards
  • retired — a piece that has retired has gone back the way it came, so its letters read in the opposite order

'Retired' reads as a pensioned-off policeman and works as an instruction at the same time: one word carrying the surface and the mechanism together.

12 across PLAYTIME

Quiet period in which set gets a break (8)

Definition: a break · charade + container

a break -> PLAYTIME: the school interval in the middle of the morning, when the class is turned out of the room to run about.

  • Quiet → P p is the musical mark for piano, the instruction to play softly
  • set → LAY to set a table is to lay it
  • period → TIME a period of history is a time
  • in which — what goes in something is buried among its letters, and the phrase names the gap the piece drops into

The break wanted is the school one, out in the yard mid-morning, which is about as far from a quiet period in an office as the clue could get.

13 across NESTLE

Home by latest eleven, primarily to snuggle up (6)

Definition: to snuggle up · charade + first letters

to snuggle up -> NESTLE: to settle yourself into a warm comfortable position against somebody, the verb a cat uses on a lap.

  • Home → NEST a bird's home, built of twigs
  • latest eleven → LE the initial letters of the two words, in the order the clue prints them
  • primarily — what a word is primarily is the letter it opens with, so each of these gives up its first and nothing else

An eleven is a cricket or football side, so the surface reads as late team news. That British sports idiom does nothing here except disguise two initials.

15 across ACUPUNCTURIST

Needleworker cut up curtains for alteration (13)

Definition: Needleworker · anagram

Needleworker -> ACUPUNCTURIST: someone who treats patients by putting fine needles into them, so the needles are the tools and no sewing is involved.

  • cut up curtains → CUTUPCURTAINS thirteen letters of fodder, matching the enumeration exactly
  • for alteration — an alteration recuts a garment out of the cloth already there, and the letters are recut the same way

'Needleworker' promises a seamstress and 'alteration' a fitting, so the whole sentence points at a sewing room. The needles are the medical kind.

18 across STOATS

Beastly types love to interfere with data (6)

Definition: Beastly types · container

Beastly types -> STOATS: definition by example, a beast being any animal, and these are the slim brown hunters that turn white in winter.

  • data → STATS stats are the figures a data set boils down to, and they open up after the second letter
  • love → O love is a score of nothing at tennis, written as the round O
  • to interfere with — to interfere with something is to get into it and meddle, so the letter lands inside rather than alongside

'Beastly' reads as an adjective of complaint, a beastly cold day, and is really pointing at animals: small vicious ones out of the weasel family.

20 across ACTUALLY

Truly pretend to be posh to get partner (8)

Definition: Truly · charade

Truly -> ACTUALLY, since both adverbs insist that what follows is the case, though the longer one often carries a hint of correcting somebody.

  • pretend → ACT somebody pretending to feel something is putting on an act
  • posh → U U is the crossword letter for upper class, from the old British talk of U and non-U speech
  • partner → ALLY an ally is a partner in a war or a deal

The surface is a social climber putting on a voice. The definition is the single word at the front, which is easy to miss because it reads as part of the sentence.

22 across HEDONIST

Job in bank filled by academic playboy? (8)

Definition: playboy? · container

playboy? -> HEDONIST: definition by example — a playboy is one kind of pleasure-seeker, and the question mark admits the wider word is wanted.

  • Job in bank → HEIST a bank job is a heist, five letters that split after the second
  • academic → DON a don is a senior member of an Oxford or Cambridge college
  • filled by — a space filled by something has that thing put within it, so the shorter piece goes inside the longer

The question mark is doing real work. A playboy is not the definition so much as one specimen of it, and the robbery in the surface never happens.

24 across MOTION

Medium-size, unopened concoction for evacuation (6)

Definition: evacuation · charade + deletion

evacuation -> MOTION: a motion is the polite clinical word for a bowel movement, one narrow sense of the general word for moving.

  • Medium-size → M M is the letter on the label in the collar of a medium shirt
  • concoction → OTION a concoction in a bottle is a potion, arriving here with the P at its front still shut away
  • unopened — a word left unopened keeps its lid on, so the letter it would have opened with never appears

'Evacuation' sounds like clearing a burning building and is medical here: the doctor's word for what the bowels do, which the concoction in the surface has brought on.

25 across FORSWEARER

One who perjures or frees war criminal (10)

Definition: One who perjures · anagram

One who perjures -> FORSWEARER: to forswear is to swear falsely or renounce upon oath, so the noun names whoever does it.

  • or frees war → ORFREESWAR ten letters of fodder, the three words sitting between definition and indicator
  • criminal — a criminal is somebody bent out of true, and letters described that way come out bent out of their order

'War criminal' is one phrase in the surface and two separate jobs in the parse: the last word is the instruction, not part of the picture it paints.

26 across OSLO

Go slowly ... jams in capital city abroad (4)

Definition: capital city abroad · hidden word

capital city abroad -> OSLO: Norway's capital, at the head of a long fjord, and abroad from the London paper this puzzle appears in.

  • Go slowly → OSLO four consecutive letters, the run starting at the end of the first word and carrying on into the next
  • jams in — to jam something in is to force it into a space it barely fits, which is how tightly these letters are packed

The ellipsis and the traffic make it look like a sentence about a jam. The letters have been sitting in plain sight since the first two words of the clue.

1 down CONSULTANT

Specialist can stun lot with new presentation (10)

Definition: Specialist · anagram

Specialist -> CONSULTANT: in a British hospital the consultant is the most senior specialist doctor, the rank a registrar spends years working towards.

  • can stun lot → CANSTUNLOT ten letters of fodder, three short words straight from the clue
  • with new presentation — to present something anew is to set the same material out in a fresh arrangement, which is all that happens to these letters

The surface sounds like a sales pitch about dazzling a crowd. Counting those three little words against the enumeration is what gives them away as material rather than description.

2 down AFTER YOU

Without leader, more silly people lead the way (5,3)

Definition: lead the way · deletion + charade

lead the way -> AFTER YOU: the phrase used to let somebody else go first, so it is the invitation to lead the way rather than a synonym for it.

  • more silly → AFTER DAFTER means more silly, and the D at its front is what the clue throws away
  • people → YOU the pronoun addresses a whole roomful at once, so it stands in for a crowd
  • Without leader — the leader of a word is the letter standing at its front, and a word deprived of one has that first letter stripped off

Politeness is the joke. The surface calls a crowd stupid, while the phrase itself is what you murmur at a doorway to hold it open for them.

3 down SENSEI

Faculty with tip for inexperienced Japanese teacher (6)

Definition: Japanese teacher · charade + first letter

Japanese teacher -> SENSEI: the Japanese form of address for a master or instructor, borrowed into English mostly for martial-arts and karate schools.

  • Faculty → SENSE a faculty is a power of perception — sight and hearing are two of the five
  • inexperienced → I the opening letter of the word, sitting at the top of a down entry where its tip is
  • tip for — the tip of a word written downwards is its topmost letter, so only the opening letter of what follows is taken

'Faculty' looks like a university department, which is exactly what a teacher would belong to. The wordplay wants the older sense, a power of the mind.

4 down KATMANDU

Two dudes work on radio in foreign capital (8)

Definition: foreign capital · charade + homophone

foreign capital -> KATMANDU: the capital of Nepal, high in the Himalayas, and foreign from the London newspaper the puzzle is printed in.

  • Two dudes → CATMAN a cat and a man are both dudes — 'cat' for a bloke comes out of jazz slang
  • work → DO to do a job is to work at it
  • on radio → KATMANDU those three scraps said out loud in one breath are written down as the city
  • on radio — a word reaching you over the radio arrives as sound alone, so how it is spelt is left entirely to the listener

Nepal's capital is spelt Kathmandu in most atlases; the older eight-letter spelling is the one that fits, and it is the sound the clue gives you, never the letters.

5 down THANKS

Tracked vehicles carrying head of Hull's TA (6)

Definition: TA · container + first letter

TA -> THANKS: 'ta' is the everyday British word for thank you, said in a shop or on a bus rather than ever written down.

  • Tracked vehicles → TANKS tanks run on caterpillar tracks, and these five letters open up after the first
  • head of Hull → H the front letter of Hull, a port on the Humber in the east of Yorkshire
  • carrying — a vehicle that carries something holds it inside itself rather than towing it along behind
  • head of — the head of a word is the letter it starts with, and that single letter is all this asks for

TA in the surface is the Territorial Army, which is why the tanks are there. It is really the word you say for a small favour, and the military reading is scenery.

6 down KNEE

Joint nurse rolled that Keef regularly inhales (4)

Definition: Joint · container + alternate letters + reversal

Joint -> KNEE: a joint is any place where two bones hinge against each other, and this is the big one halfway down a leg.

  • Keef regularly → KE Keith Richards is Keef to the music press, and every second letter of that nickname is wanted
  • nurse → NE an EN is an enrolled nurse in Britain, and those two letters are turned round
  • rolled — something rolled over comes to rest the other way round, so its letters are read from the far end back
  • regularly — letters picked regularly are taken at a fixed interval, here every second one from the start
  • inhales — to inhale is to draw something in, so the outer piece takes the other one inside it

The whole surface is a Rolling Stone rolling a joint, so 'joint' arrives already meaning something else. The definition wants the anatomical one.

9 down ROLLER-COASTER

One having frightful ups and downs? (6-7)

Definition: One having frightful ups and downs? · cryptic definition

The whole clue reads straight: a fairground ride whose entire existence is steep climbs and terrifying plunges, and nothing in the sentence is wordplay.

  • frightful ups and downs → the surface reading is figurative: the reversals of fortune in a bad year, a life lurching from crisis to crisis
  • One having → the setter means a thing that owns its climbs and plunges literally, being built out of them rather than suffering them

The idiom is figurative — a rollercoaster of a week — and the clue quietly takes it back to the fairground, where the ups and downs are made of steel.

14 down LITTLE OWLS

We give a hoot if swell lot messes about with it (6,4)

Definition: We give a hoot · anagram

We give a hoot -> LITTLE OWLS: owls hoot, and the idiom for caring is the trap; the adjective picks out one particular small species.

  • swell lot → SWELLLOT eight letters of fodder; a swell lot is a fine crowd in the surface
  • it → IT the pronoun joins the mix, two more letters of fodder
  • messes about — to mess something about is to disturb the arrangement it was in, which is exactly what befalls these letters

The little owl is a small speckled species brought to Britain in the nineteenth century. 'We give a hoot' has the birds introduce themselves, and caring is not the sense.

16 down UNSUITED

Inappropriate as one going skinny-dipping? (8)

Definition: Inappropriate · double definition

Inappropriate -> UNSUITED, not suited to the job or the person; and one going skinny-dipping is literally un-suited, wearing no costume at all.

  • Inappropriate → the plain sense: not fitting the occasion, the job or the person
  • as one going skinny-dipping? → somebody swimming naked is wearing no costume, and the word splits into a negative prefix and exactly such a garment

Eight letters, no wordplay and a question mark to warn you. The joke is that the second reading needs no rearranging at all, only a hyphen you supply yourself.

17 down INACTION

Idleness at work (8)

Definition: Idleness · double definition

Idleness -> INACTION, doing nothing at all; and 'at work' -> IN ACTION, the same letters respaced into a phrase meaning busy.

  • Idleness → doing nothing whatever, the state of a machine that has been switched off
  • at work → busy and operating: the same eight letters with a space dropped into the middle

Three words and no mechanism, which is why it reads as one phrase. Idleness and industry turn out to be the same spelling with the gap in a different place.

19 down AROUSE

Turn on a banker that's embezzled Rand (6)

Definition: Turn on · charade + container

Turn on -> AROUSE: to excite or awaken somebody, with 'turn on' the everyday phrase for producing that same effect.

  • a → A the article is taken at face value, one letter straight from the clue
  • Rand → R R is the letter on a South African price tag, the rand being the currency there
  • banker → OUSE a banker in crosswords is a river, because a river is the thing with banks; the Ouse flows through York
  • that's embezzled — to embezzle is to take money quietly into your own keeping, so the letter named is tucked away inside

The surface is a fraud story from first word to last, and the only banker in the parse is a stretch of water. That pun is a setter's standby worth learning early.

21 down TIMBRE

Quality wood needing to be delivered (6)

Definition: Quality · homophone

Quality -> TIMBRE: the quality of a sound that lets you tell a violin from a flute playing the very same note.

  • wood → TIMBRE the wood a builder orders by the yard is timber, and spoken aloud that word can be written a second way
  • delivered — a speech or a line is delivered out loud, so what counts is the sound of the word and not its spelling

'Quality wood' sits together so comfortably that the definition looks like both words. Only the first is it, and the second is there to be heard rather than read.

23 down EROS

Men in extremes of strife flipping love God (4)

Definition: love God · container + outer letters + reversal

love God -> EROS: the Greek god of desire, the Romans' Cupid, whose winged statue stands above Piccadilly Circus.

  • extremes of strife → SE the letters at the two ends of the word, in the order they stand
  • Men → OR OR is the army's abbreviation for other ranks, the soldiers who are not officers
  • in — a piece put in another sits between its letters rather than before or after them
  • extremes of — the extremes of a word are the letters at either end of it, and everything between them is left behind
  • flipping — something flipped lands the other way up, so the assembly is read from its far end back

Assembled, the wordplay spells a word for aching, and only then does it turn over. The men and the strife make a battlefield that has nothing to do with the answer.
