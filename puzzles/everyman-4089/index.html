Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,089

Setter: Everyman · Published: Sunday 2 March 2025 · Difficulty: Moderate (harder than 28% of the puzzles here) · Grid: 15×15

Across

1 across DISH

Meal from Germany, skipping starter of herring perhaps (4)

Definition: Meal · charade + deletion

  • Germany → D D is Germany's international vehicle plate letter, short for Deutschland
  • herring perhaps → ISH a herring is one kind of fish, and FISH minus its opening letter is ISH

'Perhaps' is the setter owning up: a herring is only an example of a fish, and it is the general word the wordplay wants, never the particular one.

3 across DUMBSTRUCK

Sweep your hand across mallard: Spooner’s lost for words (10)

Definition: lost for words · spoonerism

  • Sweep your hand across → DUMB to strum is to sweep your hand across the strings; it hands its STR over for a D, and the spelling picks up a silent B on the way
  • mallard → STRUCK a mallard is a duck, and it collects the STR that strum gave up

Say the plain phrase first — a strum, a duck — and the swap does the rest. The silent B is the one thing the sound cannot tell you.

9 across LOUT

Yobbo in defeat changing sides (4)

Definition: Yobbo · substitution

  • defeat → ROUT a rout is a crushing defeat, the sort that sends an army running
  • sides → L the two sides are left and right, written L and R, so the R at the front gives way to an L

'Changing sides' reads as treachery on a battlefield and means something much smaller: one letter of the alphabet standing down for another.

10 across LOSE WEIGHT

See darn rowers successfully diet (4,6)

Definition: successfully diet · charade

  • See → LO lo is the archaic word for see, as in lo and behold
  • darn → SEW to darn a hole in a sock is to sew it up
  • rowers → EIGHT an eight is a racing crew, a whole boat named by the number in it

Three plain words in a row, each swapped for a synonym and run together with no indicator anywhere — 'rowers' as EIGHT is the crossword convention worth banking.

12 across AFGHANISTAN

Ha! Giants fan lost in the country (11)

Definition: country · anagram

  • Ha! Giants fan → AFGHANISTAN the eleven letters of HA GIANTS FAN rearranged

'Giants' reads as a sports team, so the surface is a jeer across a stadium — about as far from the answer's part of the world as the setter could get.

15 across SURGEON

One in theatre’s rushin’ to be heard (7)

Definition: One in theatre · homophone

  • rushin’ → SURGEON to rush forward is to surge, and the clue's dropped g asks for the same clipped form — say SURGIN’ aloud and this is what you write

'Rushin'' is dressed as Russian and sends you hunting a nationality. The apostrophe is the real instruction: whatever you sound out has lost its g too.

16 across ACHIEST

Son becoming cold, most pale and most painful (7)

Definition: most painful · substitution

  • most pale → ASHIEST ashen is pale, so the palest of all is the ashiest
  • Son → S S is the family-tree abbreviation for son, and it is the letter leaving
  • cold → C C is the letter on the cold tap, and it takes the vacant place

The whole clue turns on 'becoming'. Without it, 'Son' and 'cold' would be two pieces to add on; with it they are one letter leaving and another arriving.

17 across GALAHAD

Swanky do took in virtuous knight (7)

Definition: virtuous knight · charade

  • Swanky do → GALA a gala is a grand do, the sort with a dress code
  • took in → HAD to take somebody in is to have them, in the sense of being had, deceived

The surface promises a party and delivers Arthurian romance. 'Took in' means deceived here, a sense that survives mostly in 'you were had'.

19 across AL DESKO

As lunch, desultorily eaten, still keeping officebound, principally? (2,5)

Definition: As lunch, desultorily eaten, still keeping officebound, principally? · &lit + first letters

  • As lunch → AL the leading letters of the first two words
  • desultorily eaten, still keeping officebound → DESKO the leading letters of the next five words, taken in the order they stand

Every word works twice over: read straight, the sentence describes a lunch picked at while the office refuses to let you leave.

20 across FORTHCOMING

Communicative, as expected (11)

Definition: Communicative · double definition

  • Communicative → FORTHCOMING a witness of this kind tells you what they know instead of holding back
  • as expected → FORTHCOMING money that arrives exactly when it should is described this way — produced at the point it is due

Two words, one comma, and no letters to move at all. The senses have nothing to do with each other: one is about a person talking, the other about a thing turning up.

23 across PRECOCIOUS

Of value to protect company, that’s obvious early on (10)

Definition: obvious early on · container

  • Of value → PRECIOUS a precious stone is one of great value
  • company → CO CO is the standard short form of company, as in Smith & Co

The takeover story in the surface is a decoy — nothing about firms survives into the answer, which is about children who get there years before anyone else.

24 across FLEA

Abruptly: Run for it! A biting beastie! (4)

Definition: biting beastie · charade + deletion

  • Run for it → FLE to run for it is to flee, and cut short that is three letters
  • A → A the clue's own A, taken at face value and joined on the end

The exclamation marks sell it as panic in a comic strip, which is the point: 'Run for it!' is a phrase to be swapped for a synonym, not an instruction to obey.

25 across REMORTGAGE

Got a merger in trouble? Borrow more (10)

Definition: Borrow more · anagram

  • Got a merger → REMORTGAGE the ten letters of GOT A MERGER rearranged

The question mark marks the seam: everything before it is letters to be shuffled, everything after it is the definition, and the boardroom story is only a surface.

26 across FRET

Pine feature of guitar (4)

Definition: Pine · double definition

  • Pine → FRET to pine for something is to worry at its absence, turning it over anxiously
  • feature of guitar → FRET the metal ridges across a guitar's neck that fix where each note falls carry this same name

'Pine' is a verb here, not the timber a guitar might be built from, and that quiet change of part of speech is the entire clue.

Down

1 down DOLLAR SIGN

$500 girl loans foolishly (6,4)

Definition: $ · charade + anagram

  • 500 → D D is the Roman numeral for five hundred
  • girl loans → OLLARSIGN the nine letters of GIRL LOANS reset in a new order behind the numeral

The definition is printed rather than written out — one typed character doing a word's job, which is why the eye reads it as a price and walks straight past it.

2 down SLUGGARDLY

Workshy mended rugs – gladly? (10)

Definition: Workshy · anagram

  • rugs – gladly → SLUGGARDLY the ten letters of RUGS GLADLY rearranged

The dash makes 'gladly?' look like an afterthought about willingness when it is simply more material — and being glad to help is the one thing the answer is not.

4 down UTOPIAN

Somewhat froufrou top I antiqued, expecting impossibly good outcome? (7)

Definition: expecting impossibly good outcome · hidden word

  • froufrou top I antiqued → UTOPIAN the answer runs straight through the ends of four words, from the tail of froufrou to the head of antiqued

'Froufrou' is a real word — frills and fuss — so it reads as description rather than a hiding place, and the join sits inside a word rather than at a space.

5 down BEEF TEA

Busy type churning feta for hearty beverage (4,3)

Definition: hearty beverage · charade + anagram

  • Busy type → BEE busy as a bee — the insect is the byword for industry, and 'type' points at a creature rather than a person
  • feta → FTEA the four letters of the Greek cheese reset in a new order

The surface offers a hard-working cook and a fridge; the answer keeps neither. 'Hearty' is quietly literal here, since what is in the cup came off an animal.

6 down TOE IN THE DOOR

Opportunity to hide ore, not refined (3,2,3,4)

Definition: Opportunity · anagram

  • to hide ore, not → TOE IN THE DOOR the twelve letters of TO HIDE ORE NOT rearranged

The comma is the misdirection: 'not' looks like it belongs to the sentence, arguing about smuggling, when it belongs to the letters.

7 down URGE

15 suppressing this impulse (4)

Definition: impulse · deletion

  • 15 → SURGEON 15 across is the one in theatre, and its letters are what this clue works on
  • this → URGE hold down the S at the front and the ON at the back, and the four letters left in the middle are the answer

A cross-reference: solve 15 first and this one is already written inside it. The surface's joke about a doctor resisting temptation is exactly what the letters are doing.

8 down KITS

Assemblages in humorous burlesque, cycling (4)

Definition: Assemblages · cycling

  • humorous burlesque → SKIT a skit is the short comic sketch that sends something up

Only one letter actually travels, and it travels the whole way: the front letter goes to the back while everything else keeps its place and its order.

11 down TAKE THE FLOOR

Book large hotel suite? Give us an address (4,3,5)

Definition: Give us an address · charade

  • Book → TAKE to book a table is to take it, holding it for yourself
  • large hotel suite? → THE FLOOR a suite big enough fills a whole storey of the hotel, and the question mark admits the stretch

The surface is a reservation and a request for directions. Both halves change job in the answer: nobody is booking anything and the address is a speech.

13 down BEN STILLER

Hollywood funnyman seeing Scottish mountain, increasingly serene (3,7)

Definition: Hollywood funnyman · charade

  • Scottish mountain → BEN ben is the Gaelic word for a peak, as in Ben Nevis
  • increasingly serene → STILLER more still is stiller, and stillness is what serenity comes down to

Bank the first piece: a Scottish mountain in a clue is a ben, every time. The surface pretends the comedian is out walking and admiring the calm.

14 down AT LONG LAST

Finally ants all got slaughtered (2,4,4)

Definition: Finally · anagram

  • ants all got → AT LONG LAST the ten letters of ANTS ALL GOT rearranged

'Finally' looks like an instruction to take a last letter and is doing no such thing — it is the definition, standing exactly where a solver expects an indicator.

18 down DARLING

Dear Director, Rugby player Will will miss opening (7)

Definition: Dear · charade + deletion

  • Director → D D is the standard abbreviation, as it appears on a film's credits
  • Rugby player Will → ARLING Will Carling captained England at rugby union, and his surname without its opening C leaves ARLING

The capitals do the misleading: 'Dear Director' reads as the top of a letter of complaint, and only one of the two people named in this clue actually exists.

19 down ACHTUNG

Warning from German: sadly can’t hug (7)

Definition: Warning from German · anagram

  • can’t hug → ACHTUNG the seven letters of CAN'T HUG rearranged, apostrophe discarded

The surface paints a German who is bad at affection. Note where the language sits: it is the answer that is German, not the person the warning is aimed at.

21 down SPAR

Pole performs hiphop about box (4)

Definition: Pole · reversal + double definition

  • performs hiphop → RAPS somebody who performs hiphop raps, and those four letters read from the back are the answer

Two definitions with the wordplay parked between them, so not one word is spare: a ship's pole, a bout of practice punches, and raps read backwards, all in four letters.

22 down TEAM

Group working together rejected Everyman, tense, hiding hint of anger (4)

Definition: Group working together · charade + reversal + container + first letter

  • Everyman → ME the setter signs himself Everyman, so he is ME
  • tense → T T is the grammar book's abbreviation for tense
  • anger → A the letter anger opens on, dropped into the middle of the reversed pair

Bank the convention: a setter who names himself in his own clue means ME, and Everyman does it most weeks. The reversal takes both pieces together, not just the second.
