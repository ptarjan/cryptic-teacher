Across

1 across HOLIDAYS

Breaks one in possession, say, being upset (8)

Definition: Breaks · container + anagram

A holiday is time off, and 'breaks' is the everyday British plural for the same thing, from a long weekend to a fortnight away.

  • possession → HOLD possession of something is a hold over it
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one
  • say → AYS the three letters of SAY in a new order
  • in — here it is the placing sense: the short word before it goes inside the word after it, not beside it
  • being upset — upset a thing and it no longer sits the way it was set down, which is what happens to the three letters beside it

'Say' almost always means 'for example' in a cryptic, so being asked for its letters instead is the surprise here, and the quarrel in the surface is what hides the instruction.

5 across SUN GOD

Dynasty celebration recalled deity (3,3)

Definition: deity · charade + reversal

Definition by class: Ra and Helios are gods of the sun, and each of them is a deity, so the clue names the family rather than the member.

  • Dynasty → SUNG the Sung ruled China for three centuries
  • celebration → OD a celebration is a do, turned round
  • recalled — to recall something is to call it back, and letters called back arrive in the opposite order

Two conventions worth banking: the Sung are the crossword's favourite Chinese dynasty because the letters behave, and a party is nearly always a 'do'.

9 across FAREWELL

Females initially exhibit health for so long (8)

Definition: so long · charade + first letter

'So long' is the casual parting phrase and the answer its formal cousin, both being what you say to someone on leaving.

  • Females initially → F the opening letter of Females
  • exhibit health → AREWELL people who exhibit health ARE WELL, and the two words run straight together
  • initially — at the outset of a word there is one letter only, and that is what this asks for

The sleight is grammatical: 'exhibit health' is not a noun to swap for letters but a verb phrase, and saying it another way spells seven of the eight.

10 across DENIAL

Failure to accept looking old may be reversed with injection of article (6)

Definition: Failure to accept · reversal + container

Denial is refusing to admit a fact is true, so the definition spells out the psychological sense the word now carries.

  • looking old → LINED a lined face is one that looks old
  • article → A a is the indefinite article
  • may be reversed — reversing a thing is reading it the other way round, and an across entry read that way runs right to left
  • injection of — an injection goes into whatever it is given to, so the letter named next is pushed inside

The surface is about the face and the definition about the mind, and 'failure to accept looking old' is one phrase doing both jobs until you decide where to cut it.

12 across MANOR

Staff officer cleared out base of operations (5)

Definition: base of operations · charade + outer letters

In British police slang your manor is the patch you work out of, so the definition catches that sense rather than the lord's country house.

  • Staff → MAN to staff a desk is to man it
  • officer cleared out → OR OfficeR emptied of everything between its ends
  • cleared out — a room cleared out keeps only its walls, so the word keeps only the letters at its two ends

'Staff' is the verb here rather than the people, which is what keeps 'staff officer' looking like one rank instead of two separate instructions.

13 across SCINTILLA

Wrong about start of clue - work to get a hint (9)

Definition: hint · container + charade + first letter

A scintilla is the tiniest detectable trace of something - 'not a scintilla of evidence' - so 'hint' catches it as the smallest amount there could be.

  • Wrong → SIN a wrong done is a sin
  • start of clue → C the first letter of clue
  • work → TILL to till the land is to work it
  • a → A the clue's own a, taken at face value
  • about — writing about a subject means going round it, so the word before wraps the letter after
  • start of — the start of a word is its first letter and nothing more

The dash makes 'clue' and 'work' read as the setter chatting about the puzzle, which is exactly why you skim past two words that are both raw material.

14 across NOTEPAD

Well-known to involve secretary where comments may be made (7)

Definition: where comments may be made · container

A notepad is the block of paper kept for jotting remarks down, so the definition points at the answer by the job it does.

  • Well-known → NOTED a noted figure is a well-known one
  • secretary → PA a PA is a personal assistant, the crossword's standing secretary
  • to involve — to involve somebody is to draw them in, so the piece named next is drawn inside the other

The definition never names an object at all - it describes what one is for, which is why the whole clue can pass as a sentence about an office.

16 across PENCIL

A lot of change applied to one Latin writer (6)

Definition: writer · charade + deletion

A writer here is a thing rather than a person - the implement that does the writing - which is the crossword's favourite use of the word.

  • A lot of change → PENC loose change is pence, all but its final letter
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one
  • Latin → L l is the dictionary's abbreviation for Latin
  • A lot of — a lot of a thing is most of it but not all, so its last letter is left behind

'One Latin' reads as a Roman citizen and is really two abbreviations stacked up, i then l, which is the step that keeps this one out of sight.

18 across ILL

Not right to occupy village (3)

Definition: Not right · hidden word

Someone not right is ill in the health sense, and an ill deed is not right in the moral one, so the definition holds either way you take it.

  • village → ILL the run lodged inside vILLage
  • to occupy — to occupy a place is to sit inside it, and the letters wanted are sitting inside the word that follows

Three-letter hidden answers are the hardest to see, because almost any word could be concealing one; the giveaway is an instruction dressed up as part of the story.

20 across HEROES

This is keeping nothing for the lions (6)

Definition: the lions · container

A lion is the standard figure for a person of great courage, so the definition points at the answer through an image rather than a synonym.

  • This is → HERES 'this is' presents something exactly as 'here's' does
  • nothing → O nothing is the round zero, O
  • keeping — to keep a thing is to hold on to it, so the word before holds the next one inside

The surface throws somebody to the lions in a Roman arena; the definition wants the other lions, the brave ones, and that flip is the whole clue.

22 across EARNEST

Serious attention given to collection of machine guns (7)

Definition: Serious · charade

Earnest means meaning what you say with no lightness in it, so 'serious' is the sense that describes a person rather than a situation.

  • attention → EAR to give someone your ear is to give them your attention
  • collection of machine guns → NEST a nest is the military's word for a cluster of machine guns

No instruction word anywhere: the clue is two synonyms laid end to end, and 'given to' is joinery holding them together rather than telling you to do anything.

25 across AMBROSIAL

Arab limos going around? Sweet (9)

Definition: Sweet · anagram

Ambrosia was the food of the Greek gods, so anything ambrosial tastes divine and 'sweet' is the everyday word for that praise.

  • Arab limos → AMBROSIAL the nine letters of ARAB LIMOS in a new order
  • going around — things that go around end up somewhere other than where they set off, and so do these letters

The instruction does double duty: it pictures cars circling a block and tells the letters to do the same, and the question mark covers the second job.

28 across ERASE

French and English are backing excise (5)

Definition: excise · charade + reversal

To excise is to cut a passage out and to erase is to rub it out, so both leave a text with something taken away.

  • French → ES 'are' in French is es, as in tu es
  • English are → ARE and in English it is simply are
  • backing — a thing that is backing is travelling the other way, and an across entry read the other way runs right to left

One word asked for in two languages: that is why 'and' sits between two nationalities, and why nothing else in the clue does any work at all.

29 across MILTON

Poet’s mostly gentle expression? Mostly (6)

Definition: Poet · charade + deletion

Definition by example: Milton wrote Paradise Lost, so one particular poet stands in for the job description.

  • mostly gentle → MIL gentle is mild, without its final letter
  • expression → TON a tone of voice is an expression, docked of its e
  • mostly — most of a word is all of it bar the last letter
  • Mostly — the same instruction a second time, docking the word that comes before it

The same instruction twice, one in lower case and one capitalised at the end, which is what makes the second read as an afterthought rather than a second order.

30 across REKINDLE

Type line in section of film that’s cut? Start again (8)

Definition: Start again · container + charade + deletion

To rekindle a fire is to get it going once more, and the figurative sense - a romance, an old interest - is precisely starting again.

  • section of film that’s cut → REE a reel is a section of film, cut short by its last letter
  • Type → KIND a type of thing is a kind of thing
  • line → L l is the standard abbreviation for line
  • in — the two pieces before it go inside the word after it rather than alongside it
  • that’s cut — a length of film cut is a shortened one, and the shortening costs this word its final letter

'Cut' belongs to the cutting room as much as to the wordplay, so the film loses a letter at the exact moment the surface starts talking about editing.

31 across COSMOS

Everything to be found in quiet comments accepting kinky sex? (6)

Definition: Everything · container

The cosmos is the universe considered as one ordered whole, so 'everything' is the plainest available name for the same total.

  • quiet comments → COOS to coo is to murmur softly, so quiet comments are coos
  • kinky sex → SM S&M is the shorthand for the kinky sort
  • accepting — to accept a thing is to take it in, so the word before makes room inside for the word after

The question mark is an apology for the shorthand: S&M is one kind of kinky sex rather than the whole of it, which is fair enough in a clue this playful.

32 across MYTHICAL

Imaginary gracious story largely about drunken comment? (8)

Definition: Imaginary · charade + container + deletion

A mythical creature never existed outside the stories told about it, so 'imaginary' is the everyday word for what myth does to a thing.

  • gracious → MY 'my!' and 'goodness gracious!' are the same exclamation
  • story largely → TAL a story is a tale, most of it being TAL
  • drunken comment → HIC hic is the hiccup a drunk is always drawn saying
  • largely — largely means for the most part, so the word beside it arrives short of its last letter
  • about — one piece is written about another when it wraps all the way round it

Two comic-strip noises do the heavy lifting here, 'my!' for gracious and 'hic' for the drunk, and both are standard material once you have met them.

Down

1 down HOFFMANN

German writer of opera not working with husband after husband’s indiscretion, ultimately (8)

Definition: German writer · charade + last letter

Hoffmann is the German Romantic whose fantastical stories became a staple of the fiction shelf, so the definition simply states his nationality and his trade.

  • husband → H h is the standard abbreviation for husband
  • opera not working → OFF a show that is not running is off
  • with husband → MAN a woman's man is her husband
  • indiscretion, ultimately → N the last letter of indiscretion
  • after — what comes after something stands behind it, so the pieces named first end up second
  • ultimately — at the very end of the word beside it there is a single letter, and that is all this asks for

E.T.A. Hoffmann wrote the tales Offenbach later set to music. The clue asks for husband twice over, once as its abbreviation and once as the plain word, which lets the marriage in the surface read so smoothly.

2 down LORGNETTE

Spectacles with rotten leg getting repair (9)

Definition: Spectacles · anagram

A lorgnette is a pair of eyeglasses held up on a handle, so 'spectacles' names it by the family it belongs to rather than by its stick.

  • rotten leg → LORGNETTE the nine letters of ROTTEN LEG in a new order
  • getting repair — something sent away for repair comes back with its parts put together again, and the parts here are letters

The surface and the answer are the same object: a lorgnette is the pair of glasses on a stick, so the leg being mended is also a spectacle's arm.

3 down DEWDROP

Climbing school joined up around top of range - that’s a sight for the morning (7)

Definition: a sight for the morning · container + charade + reversal + first letter

Dew forms overnight and burns off by mid-morning, so the definition places the answer by the hour you can see one rather than naming it.

  • school → POD a school of whales is also called a pod
  • top of range → R the first letter of range
  • joined up → WED a couple joined in marriage are wed
  • Climbing — a down entry is written downwards, so anything climbing in it is read the other way, from the bottom square up
  • around — going around a thing means passing every side of it, so one piece closes over the letter that follows
  • top of — the top of a word is the end you meet first, so only its opening letter is wanted

'School' is the whale sense rather than the classroom the surface offers, and the dash is the join: everything before it builds letters, everything after it defines them.

4 down YELLS

Cries "Absolutely!," embracing Lord Lieutenant (5)

Definition: Cries · container

To yell is to cry out at volume, so 'cries' is the plain synonym in its shouting sense rather than its weeping one.

  • Absolutely → YES an emphatic 'absolutely!' is a yes
  • Lord Lieutenant → LL the initials of the Lord Lieutenant, the monarch's representative in a county
  • embracing — an embrace closes round whoever is held, so the word before it takes the next one inside

A doubled letter is nearly always the initials of a two-word title, and this is the commonest of them; the exclamation mark makes the quoted word look like the point of the sentence.

6 down UNEATEN

Trim after last item on menu not yet served? (7)

Definition: not yet served? · charade + last letter

Nothing that has not been served has been eaten, so the definition arrives by consequence rather than as a straight synonym.

  • last item on menu → U the final letter of menu
  • Trim → NEATEN to trim a hedge or a beard is to neaten it
  • last item on — the last thing on a list is the one at the bottom, so the closing letter of the word beside it is taken
  • after — a piece placed after another goes behind it, leaving the single letter to lead

The question mark admits the definition is a step away from the word: a dish that never reached the table cannot have been finished either.

7 down GRILL

Cook finally receiving burn (5)

Definition: Cook · charade + last letter

To grill is to cook under a fierce direct heat, so the definition is the general verb and the answer one particular way of carrying it out.

  • finally receiving → G the last letter of receiving
  • burn → RILL a rill is a small stream, which is what a Scot means by a burn
  • finally — it asks for how the neighbouring word ends, and a word ends in exactly one letter

Two streams and no water in sight: 'burn' is the Scottish word and 'rill' the English one, while the surface keeps you in a kitchen where a burn is an injury.

8 down DOLLAR

Everyone in the staff picked up money (6)

Definition: money · container + reversal

A dollar is a unit of currency, so 'money' defines it by the wider class and leaves whose dollar it is entirely open.

  • staff → ROD a rod is a staff you can lean on
  • Everyone → ALL everyone is all of them
  • in — the placing sense again: one word sits within another rather than joining it end to end
  • picked up — a down answer is written downwards, so a piece picked up is read the other way, from below

'Picked up' is the right instruction for a down clue and 'back' would not be: there is no backwards on a vertical axis, only up.

11 down SIMPLE

Not hard: soft in comparison I deleted (6)

Definition: Not hard · container + deletion

Simple is the opposite of hard in the sense of difficulty rather than texture, which is the sense a puzzle uses when it calls a clue hard.

  • comparison → SIMILE a simile is a comparison - 'like a lion'
  • I → I the I taken out of SIMILE, leaving SIMLE
  • soft → P p is piano, the musician's mark for soft
  • in — the letter named before it goes inside the word that follows rather than in front of it
  • deleted — what is deleted never reaches the answer, so the letter named beside it is struck out first

The colon invites you to read 'soft' as more of the definition, a gentler way of saying not hard, when it is really the single letter p.

15 down DISTIL

Condense source in dictionary, one even now incomplete (6)

Definition: Condense · charade + first letter + deletion

To distil is to boil something down until only its essence is left, which is what condense means in both the chemical and the figurative sense.

  • source in dictionary → D the first letter of dictionary
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one
  • even now → STIL even now means still, docked of its final L
  • source — a river's source is where it begins, so the letter wanted is the one its word begins with
  • incomplete — a word left incomplete stops short, giving up the letter at its end

'One' sitting between two other instructions is the easiest piece to read past, because the sentence wants it as a number rather than as the letter I.

17 down ICELANDIC

Reserve, after cut, repeatedly caging antelope from northern country (9)

Definition: from northern country · container + deletion

Icelandic means belonging to Iceland, which sits far enough north for the definition to describe the place instead of naming it.

  • Reserve, after cut → IC reserve is ice, the coolness of manner, cut short
  • antelope → ELAND the eland is the big African antelope crosswords keep coming back to
  • repeatedly → IC the same two letters a second time, closing the cage
  • after cut — a cut takes something off the end, so what is left is the word without its final letter
  • repeatedly — doing a thing repeatedly means doing it more than once, so that short piece is written out twice
  • caging — a cage holds its occupant on every side, so the animal named next is shut in between them

Being asked for one short piece twice over is rare enough that the word ordering it reads as filler, and 'reserve' is icy manner rather than a booking.

19 down ETHEREAL

Somewhat here, there, always immaterial (8)

Definition: immaterial · hidden word

Something ethereal seems too light to have any substance, which is what immaterial means at its root: not made of matter.

  • here, there, always → ETHEREAL the run spans all three words: herE THEREALways
  • Somewhat — it means to a degree rather than wholly, and a degree of a phrase is a run of its letters

The little words are chosen to look like a list of nowheres, and the commas fall exactly where the eye wants a break - punctuation hides a run of letters better than anything.

21 down OLOROSO

Old John, swallowing a lot of pink wine and sherry (7)

Definition: sherry · charade + container + deletion

Oloroso is one of the sherry styles, the dark nutty one from Jerez, so the definition names the drink of which the answer is a variety.

  • Old → O o is the standard abbreviation for old
  • John → LOO the john is the American name for the loo
  • a lot of pink wine → ROS rosé is the pink wine, all but its last letter
  • swallowing — what you swallow ends up inside you, so the word before takes the next one in
  • a lot of — a lot of something is most of it, so the word turns up without its final letter

Three conventions in one short clue: 'Old' at the front of a clue is nearly always o, the john is the loo, and rosé loses its accent along with its final letter.

23 down RHENISH

Former PM, not one consuming chicken and wine (7)

Definition: wine · container + deletion

Rhenish is the old English name for the wine of the Rhine, the word Shakespeare uses, so the definition gives the class and the answer one region's version.

  • Former PM → RISHI Rishi Sunak led the government until 2024
  • one → I the I that comes out of the prime minister's first name
  • chicken → HEN a hen is a chicken
  • not one — 'not' says a letter has to go and the word beside it names which: the Roman numeral for a single unit
  • consuming — to consume something is to take it right in, so what follows disappears inside

A prime minister's first name as raw material is fresh vocabulary, and 'not one' is the setter naming a letter to remove rather than counting anything.

24 down TARMAC

River grass rising to surface (6)

Definition: surface · charade + reversal

Tarmac is the bound stone-and-tar layer a road or a runway is finished with, so 'surface' names the answer by what it is laid down to be.

  • River → CAM the Cam runs through Cambridge
  • grass → RAT to grass on someone is to rat on them
  • rising — a down entry is written from the top down, so whatever rises in it is read the other way, from the bottom

'Grass' is the informer rather than the lawn, which is the whole misdirection: read straight, the clue is a riverbank and nothing else.

26 down BOLUS

Lump raised above me is rounded mass (5)

Definition: rounded mass · charade + reversal

A bolus is a rounded lump: the ball of chewed food a swallow sends down, or a drug dose given all at once.

  • Lump → LOB a lob is a lump, the old word for a clumsy shapeless thing
  • me → US the editorial we, so a setter speaking of themselves becomes us
  • raised — a down entry runs downwards, so a piece raised inside it is read upwards instead
  • above — in a down clue whatever sits above comes first, because the entry is written from the top

Both halves are conventions rather than definitions: a setter is 'me' and also 'us', and 'lump' is an archaic noun the surface keeps hidden in plain sight.

27 down LEERY

Dodgy look, unknown quantity? That’ll make you cautious (5)

Definition: cautious · charade

To be leery of something is to be wary of it, so 'cautious' is the plain synonym for a word that usually turns up with 'of' attached.

  • Dodgy look → LEER a leer is a sly sideways look
  • unknown quantity → Y algebra's unknowns are x, y and z

The clue names the answer as a state it puts you into rather than defining it outright, which is why four words of joinery stand in front of the definition.
