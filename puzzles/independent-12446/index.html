1 across LOHENGRIN

Rheingold mostly replaced by new Wagner opera (9)

Definition: Wagner opera · anagram + deletion

Wagner opera -> LOHENGRIN: definition by example — Wagner wrote thirteen operas, and this is the 1850 one about the knight who arrives in a swan-drawn boat.

  • Rheingold → RHEINGOL the title of the first opera in Wagner's Ring cycle, handing over all but its last letter
  • new → N N is the standard abbreviation for new
  • mostly — most of a word is all of it bar the last letter, so the final one never arrives
  • replaced — to be re-placed is to be put somewhere else, and letters put somewhere else spell something else

Both titles are Wagner's, so the italicised one reads as a fact to recognise rather than raw material — and it is a letter short of what is needed, which is where 'new' comes in.

6 across CLIMB

Aim of mountaineering clubs attracting member ... (5)

Definition: Aim of mountaineering · charade

Aim of mountaineering -> CLIMB: what a mountaineer sets out to make is an ascent, and the noun names both the act of going up and the route taken.

  • clubs → C C is the card table's letter for clubs
  • member → LIMB a limb is a member of the body, the older sense of the word
  • attracting — whatever attracts a thing draws it up against itself, so the two pieces finish side by side

The dots at the end run the sentence into the next clue and carry no letters with them. A card suit and a leg are the two senses hiding in ordinary words.

9 across GAMUT

Definition: range · reversal + container

range -> GAMUT: the whole sweep from one end of a scale to the other, originally the complete musical scale, now any full span of possibilities.

  • draw → GUT to draw a rope is to tug it, and that word is set down the other way about
  • morning → AM AM marks the hours before noon on a clock
  • recalled — to recall a thing is to call it back, and letters called back come the other way
  • around — a piece set around another wraps outside it, leaving a gap in the middle to fill

The dots carry the sentence on from the clue before and bring no letters with them. 'Draw' is a tug of war here, not a picture, which is the pivot of the whole clue.

10 across RESHUFFLE

Change of membership upset Liberal in new arrangement, mostly (9)

Definition: Change of membership · container + charade + deletion

Change of membership -> RESHUFFLE: a prime minister who reshuffles the cabinet refills the same posts with different people, which changes who belongs to it.

  • new arrangement → RESE to arrange things anew is to reset them, and the last letter is left off
  • upset → HUFF somebody in a huff is upset and sulking about it
  • Liberal → L L is the party's letter on a ballot paper
  • in — the plainest container word there is: what is 'in' something has the other letters standing on either side of it

'Upset' sits right beside a political party and looks like an instruction to jumble it. It is a word to translate instead, and the party gives up a single letter.

11 across THROTTLE

Definition: Suppress · charade + container + deletion

Suppress -> THROTTLE: to throttle is to choke off a flow — a voice, fuel to an engine — so both words mean holding something down until it stops.

  • trio → THRE a trio is three, with the last letter of the number cut off
  • outrageous → OTT over the top, OTT for short, is what outrageous behaviour gets called
  • Latin → L L is the dictionary's tag for a Latin word
  • penned by — a pen is what livestock are shut into, so whatever is penned finishes up enclosed
  • cutting end — the end of a word is the letter it finishes on, and cutting takes that letter away

The surface is three people writing something outrageous in Latin. 'Penned' is the farmyard sense, and 'trio' is a number waiting to be spelled out.

Definition: Hispanic mountains · reversal + container

Hispanic mountains -> SIERRA: the Spanish word for a saw-toothed mountain range, at home in Spain and Latin America — Sierra Nevada, Sierra Madre.

  • America is → AIS A is the short form for America, with the two letters of the word beside it

  • backtracking — to backtrack is to retrace your steps, so these letters are read the way they came
  • about — a piece set about another goes round it, with the second one nested inside

'America is backtracking' reads as a headline about a retreat, and that is exactly what the letters do. 'Hispanic' is pointing at a Spanish word rather than a place.

14 across MICRO-MINIS

Definition: Revealing garments · anagram + container

Revealing garments -> MICRO-MINIS: skirts cut even shorter than a miniskirt, so brief that the name doubles up on how little cloth is involved.

  • I’m → IM the two letters of the contraction, as printed
  • crimson → CRIMSON seven more letters, the colour of a blush
  • one → I one is the Roman numeral I, a single stroke
  • turning — turning into something else means changing form, and here it is the letters that change theirs
  • randomly — at random means with no order at all, so no letter keeps the place the clue gave it
  • clutching — to clutch a thing is to grab it and hold it close, so it ends up gripped in the middle

Turning crimson is blushing on the surface, and the clue means it literally of the letters. The number in the last word is the piece they close around.

17 across TOME

Definition: a big book · charade

a big book -> TOME: a tome is a weighty volume, the word you reach for when a book is thick enough to prop a door open.

  • in → TO 'in my opinion' and 'to my mind' say the same thing, so this little word turns into that one
  • my opinion → ME the one whose opinion it is, shrunk to the speaker alone

'In my opinion' looks like the setter hedging about how big the book is. It is not a comment on the definition at all — it is the whole of the wordplay.

'Try a holiday' reads as advice from a kind friend, and every word of it is wordplay: the definition is the single word at the front.

25 across ALLEVIATE

'A large rise' sounds like a pay claim and 'ignoring first time' like a lenient magistrate. Both are instructions about letters, and neither is about money or mercy.

28 across THREATENS

Promises danger to thirty, wanting one Earl replaced by Australian (9)

Definition: Promises danger · substitution

Promises danger -> THREATENS: a sky that threatens rain promises trouble rather than a gift, so 'promises' is taken in its ominous sense.

  • thirty → THREE TENS thirty counted out as three lots of ten, written in words rather than figures
  • one Earl → E Earl is E among the peerage abbreviations, and this is the letter that leaves
  • Australian → A A is the standard short form for Australian, as on a cricket scorecard
