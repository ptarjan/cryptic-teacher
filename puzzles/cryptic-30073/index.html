Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,073

Across

1 across HIGH TEA

Spooner’s man from Bangkok makes meal (4,3)

Definition: meal · spoonerism

  • from Bangkok → HIGH somebody from Bangkok is Thai, and the swap gives its T away and takes an H in exchange
  • man → TEA a man is a he, and it collects the T that Thai has just given up

Say the plain phrase — a Thai he — and Spooner does the rest. The step to spot is that 'man' and 'from Bangkok' are two words to be sounded, not one description.

5 across TRIGGER

Bouncer defending right cause (7)

Definition: cause · container

  • Bouncer → TIGGER Tigger is the bouncing tiger of the Hundred Acre Wood, so bouncing is what he does
  • right → R R is the standard abbreviation for right

'Bouncer' suggests a nightclub doorman or a short-pitched ball, and the wordplay wants A. A. Milne instead. The definition is the last word, sitting there as a plain verb.

10 across FOUR

Square meal left out (4)

Definition: Square · deletion

  • meal → FLOUR meal is ground grain, the sense that survives in oatmeal, and flour is what that gives
  • left → L L is the standard abbreviation for left, and it is the letter that goes

'Square meal' is a real phrase and the setter has split it down the middle, which is why the clue reads as one idea when it is two. Neither word does what the phrase suggests.

11 across GRANDSTAND

Show off magnificent stomach (10)

Definition: Show off · charade

  • magnificent → GRAND grand = magnificent, as in a grand house
  • stomach → STAND to stomach something is to stand it: 'I can't stomach him', 'I can't stand him'

Both halves are verbs dressed as something else. Stomach for STAND is the harder one, and it works mainly in the negative — you rarely hear that somebody can stomach a thing.

12 across GIDEON

Christian Bale finally doing wrong (6)

Definition: Christian · anagram + last letter

  • doing → DOING five of the six letters, waiting to be reordered
  • Bale finally → E the last letter of BalE

Christian Bale is an actor and neither of his names does what it appears to: the first is the definition and the second yields a single letter.

13 across TRUSSING

PM briefly in front of No.11 preparing joint (8)

Definition: preparing joint · charade + first letter

  • PM briefly → TRUSS Liz Truss held the job for seven weeks, the shortest premiership there has been
  • in → IN the word in is taken straight off the page, two letters as they stand
  • No.11 → G the answer to 11 across begins with a G

'In front of' looks like one phrase saying where a piece goes, and it is really the word IN followed by an instruction. The number sends you to another clue for a single letter.

14 across STUD FARMS

Dr Faust confused by manuscript showing birthplaces of 6 and 28 (4,5)

Definition: birthplaces of 6 and 28 · anagram

  • Dr Faust → DRFAUST seven of the nine letters, taken from the name as it is written
  • manuscript → MS MS is the standard abbreviation for a manuscript

Marlowe's doctor is a red herring and his name is only letters. The definition cannot be checked until 6 and 28 are in, so this one is easier late than early.

16 across SENSE

Soundness of judgement is some change for Donald Trump in speech (5)

Definition: Soundness of judgement · homophone

  • some change for Donald Trump → SENSE an American's loose change comes in cents rather than pence, and CENTS is what you hear

Naming a president is the setter's way of saying 'American money' without saying it, and the currency is the whole reason the coins are cents and not pennies.

17 across BOGGY

3 whimsically entertained by youth’s description of Slough (5)

Definition: description of Slough · container

  • youth → BOY a boy is a youth, and he is the one doing the entertaining
  • 3 → GG the nursery name for a horse is a gee-gee, written as two Gs

Slough is a town in Berkshire and also an ordinary word for a patch of swamp. The capital letter belongs to the town and the definition belongs to the swamp.

19 across SMACKDOWN

3D wrestling match (9)

Definition: wrestling match · charade

  • 3 → SMACK horse and smack are both street names for heroin, so one leads across to the other
  • D → DOWN D is how a crossword abbreviates down, written out here in full

'3D' reads as a cross-reference or as a film format and is neither: it is two separate pieces, and the second is a letter asked to spell itself out.

23 across STEEPLED

3 taking pulse oddly inspired? (8)

Definition: inspired · container + alternate letters

  • 3 → STEED a steed is a horse, the mounted-knight sort
  • pulse → PLE the odd letters of PuLsE, first, third and fifth

'Taking pulse' is a doctor's action in the surface and an instruction in the wordplay, which is the neatest thing here. The definition is a pun: read it as in-spired.

24 across LIVERY

Organ Symphony’s finale is uniform (6)

Definition: uniform · charade + last letter

  • Organ → LIVER the liver is the organ, and the largest one inside you
  • Symphony’s → Y the last letter of SymphonY

Saint-Saëns wrote an Organ Symphony, so the two words look like one title. Splitting a real phrase across the join is this setter's favourite move; 'square meal' at 10 does the same.

26 across HIPPOCAMPI

Bits of grey matter in fish (10)

Definition: Bits of grey matter · double definition

  • Bits of grey matter → the pair of curled ridges deep in the brain that lay down memory
  • fish → Hippocampus is also the genus of the sea horse, which is a bony fish

Read as one sentence it describes fish brains, which is a real thing and a red herring. The two halves are separate: the brain's ridges, and the sea horse they were named after.

27 across SHOE

Weeds cycling in Oxford? (4)

Definition: Oxford · cycling

  • Weeds → HOES to weed a border is to hoe it, so the verb weeds gives HOES

This is a rotation, not a reversal: one letter travels from the back to the front and the others hold their places. 'Weeds' as a verb rather than a noun is the other half of the trick.

28 across SHERGAR

That woman in tattered clothing coming round for 3 (7)

Definition: 3 · container + reversal

  • tattered clothing → RAGS rags are tattered clothing, and they come round to read SGAR
  • That woman → HER that woman is her, and she is the one going inside

The turned word is split after a single letter, which is the hardest kind of insertion to see — the eye expects the inserted piece to sit somewhere near the middle.

29 across CHARGER

Spice Girl cut by tea tray (7)

Definition: tray · charade + deletion

  • tea → CHAR char is the old slang for tea, as in a cup of char
  • Spice Girl → GERI Geri Halliwell, Ginger Spice, is the Spice Girl
  • cut → I the letter taken off the end of Geri, leaving GER

The tea comes second in the clue and first in the answer, so the pieces have to be assembled the other way round — 'by' only says that the two of them stand together.

Down

2 down IRONIST

Club is ultimately perfect for George Bernard Shaw? (7)

Definition: George Bernard Shaw? · charade + last letter

  • Club → IRON an iron is the golf club, the one with the metal head
  • is → IS the little verb is taken at face value, two letters straight off the page
  • perfect → T the final letter of perfecT

Golf gives crosswords three clubs worth filing away — IRON, WOOD and PUTTER — and 'Club is' reads instead like the start of a sentence about a nightclub.

3 down HORSE

Hard rock capturing the heart of Mason Mount (5)

Definition: Mount · charade + container + middle letter

  • Hard → H H is the pencil-grade abbreviation for hard
  • rock → ORE ore is the rock you mine metal out of
  • Mason → S the middle letter of MaSon

Mason Mount is a footballer, which is the whole point of the surface: his surname is doing the defining and his first name is only there to be cut down to a letter.

4 down EUGENIA

Plant from eastern New Guinea (7)

Definition: Plant · anagram

  • eastern → E E is the compass-point abbreviation for eastern
  • Guinea → GUINEA the six letters that get shuffled around the E

'New Guinea' is one place on the map and two pieces of wordplay, which is the whole deception — a capital letter hides an instruction inside a proper name.

6 down RED RUM

3 makes a killing after receiving a tip (3,3)

Definition: 3 · reversal

  • a killing → MURDER a killing is a murder, and it is the word that gets turned over

The racing surface does two jobs at once: a tip is what you get before a bet and also a shove that turns something over. The name read backwards is the joke The Shining used.

7 down GATESHEAD

Billionaire boss in Northern town (9)

Definition: Northern town · charade

  • Billionaire → GATES Bill Gates, the Microsoft founder, is the billionaire meant
  • boss → HEAD the head of an organisation is its boss

Two names-and-jobs in a row, both reading as ordinary words. The clue never says 'Bill', so the billionaire has to be worked out from the letters that follow him.

8 down EL NINOS

Climatic events during climbing season in Lesotho (2,5)

Definition: Climatic events · hidden word + reversal

  • season in Lesotho → SONINLE the unbroken run seaSON IN LEsotho, before it is turned round

Lesotho is landlocked and thousands of miles from the Pacific, so the sentence reads as geography and gives no hint that it is a place to look for letters.

9 down PANTOMIME DAME

Emma made point about cross-dresser (9,4)

Definition: cross-dresser · anagram

  • Emma made point → EMMAMADEPOINT the thirteen letters that get rearranged

Thirteen letters is a lot to juggle by eye, so work from the enumeration: 9,4 with a theatrical flavour narrows it fast. 'Emma made point' is a sentence, not a description of anyone.

15 down DIG DEEPER

Pedigree dog’s heading off to investigate more thoroughly (3,6)

Definition: investigate more thoroughly · anagram + first letter

  • Pedigree → PEDIGREE eight of the nine letters
  • dog’s heading → D the first letter of Dog

A pedigree dog and its heading belong to the same picture, so nothing in the surface announces that one letter is being borrowed from one word and the rest from another.

18 down OSTRICH

Bird not up for courtship dancing (7)

Definition: Bird · deletion + anagram

  • courtship → COURTSHIP the nine letters to start from
  • up → UP 'not up' takes away the U and the P, leaving CORTSHI

Ostriches really do dance at each other in the mating season, so the surface is a plain fact about birds and gives no sign that two of its letters are about to leave.

20 down COLTISH

3 is hot and frisky (7)

Definition: frisky · charade

  • 3 → COLT a colt is a young male horse, so the cross-reference is answered by one particular kind
  • is → IS the verb is lifted off the page as two letters
  • hot → H H is the standard abbreviation for hot, as on a tap

The surface describes a horse and the wordplay uses one, which is unusually generous: most cross-references fight the picture rather than fitting into it.

21 down WAR ZONE

Spin bowler containing Australia up front? (7)

Definition: front · container + reversal

  • Spin bowler → WARNE Shane Warne, the leg-spinner, is the bowler meant
  • Australia → OZ Oz is the familiar nickname for Australia, and it goes in climbing

Warne was Australian himself, so the sentence holds together as cricket talk while its two halves pull in different directions.

22 down FLICKA

Movie about mustang (6)

Definition: Movie · double definition

  • Movie → the 2006 picture about a wild mustang, based on a classic book and earlier film and TV adaptations of the same story
  • mustang → the wild-blooded filly the story is named after

Both halves point at one title, which is the joke: the film and the horse in it share a name, so 'about' is telling the truth as well as joining the two definitions.

25 down VISOR

Counsellor spending a day in the shade (5)

Definition: shade · deletion

  • Counsellor → ADVISOR an advisor is a counsellor
  • a → A the first of the two letters spent
  • day → D D is the standard abbreviation for day, and it is the second letter to go

Spending a day in the shade is a holiday, which is why nothing in the sentence sounds like an instruction. The two letters leave together from the front of the word.
