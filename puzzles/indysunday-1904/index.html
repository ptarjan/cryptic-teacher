Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,904

Across

1 across STRIPE

Band tour on back of success on-line? (6)

Definition: Band · charade + last letter

  • success → S the last letter of success, standing at the back of the word
  • tour → TRIP a tour is a trip
  • on-line → E e- is the prefix for anything done online, as in email and e-commerce

'Band' looks like the musicians whose tour this is, and means a coloured strip. The e- prefix for online is worth banking — setters use it constantly.

4 across SPUTUM

Propose splitting amount that's coughed up (6)

Definition: that's coughed up · container

  • amount → SUM an amount of money is a sum
  • Propose → PUT to put a motion to a meeting is to propose it

A committee-room surface, where proposals and amounts belong, hides a word from the sickbed; nothing sounds like an instruction until 'splitting' turns literal.

9 across CONFORMIST

Sheep anti/pro low cloud (10)

Definition: Sheep · charade

  • anti → CON of the pros and cons, con is the anti side
  • pro → FOR to be pro something is to be for it
  • low cloud → MIST cloud down at ground level is mist

The slash makes 'anti/pro' look like one hesitation rather than two words pulling opposite ways, and each of them is a separate piece of the build.

10 across NASA

Backing lost for nosy US agency (4)

Definition: US agency · deletion

  • Backing → L the backing is the last letter, and here that letter is L
  • nosy → NASAL nosy means of the nose — nasal — and without that L the agency is left

'Backing lost' reads as a sponsor pulling out of a deal, and is an instruction to drop a final letter — the only operation in the clue.

11 across ANYTHING GOES

Gay in Ghent so upset there are no bars (8,4)

Definition: there are no bars · anagram

  • Gay in Ghent so → ANYTHINGGOES those twelve letters, knocked over and resettled

The surface is a night out in Flanders. Four short words carry the whole fodder, which is why so little of the sentence is left for the definition.

13 across HERNIA

Protruding part goddess squeezes back in (6)

Definition: Protruding part · container + reversal

  • goddess → HERA Hera is the Greek queen of the gods
  • in → NI the word IN, taken back to front

Hera is the crossword's default goddess, alongside Isis and Nut, and worth banking: the clue says only 'goddess', so the name has to come from you.

14 across HECATOMB

Explosive combat involved great sacrifice (8)

Definition: great sacrifice · charade + anagram

  • Explosive → HE HE is the army's marking for high explosive
  • combat → CATOMB the six letters of combat, tangled into a new order

The battlefield surface is doing its job: 'Explosive' reads as an adjective describing the combat, and is really a two-letter noun.

16 across YUGOSLAV

Five plus Burgess, say, turned communist once (8)

Definition: communist once · charade + reversal

  • Five → V V is the Roman numeral for five
  • plus → ALSO plus in the sense of and also
  • Burgess, say → GUY Guy Burgess was the Cambridge spy, and 'say' warns that the first name is only one example of the surname

Burgess is a decoy and a gift at once: the spy is real, and all the clue wants from him is a first name that doubles as a word for a man.

18 across ARMANI

Somewhat peculiar man in expensive clothes (6)

Definition: expensive clothes · hidden word

  • peculiar man in → ARMANI the run begins inside peculiAR and finishes on the I of in

Three ordinary words hide a designer label across their joins, and 'Somewhat' is the only hint that nothing needs rearranging.

20 across COLLYWOBBLES

Walter fixes shoes for Spooner's runs (12)

Definition: runs · spoonerism

  • Walter → COLLY Walter shortens to Wally, who hands his W over and takes a C in exchange
  • fixes shoes → WOBBLES to cobble is to mend shoes, and cobbles gives up its C for Wally's W

A Spooner clue needs the plain phrase first: find the nickname and the trade, and only then trade their opening sounds.

23 across FILE

Tool store (4)

Definition: Tool · double definition

  • Tool → the rasp in the toolbox, for smoothing metal
  • store → papers put away where they can be found again

Two words, two definitions and no joinery at all: the shortest clue in the puzzle, and the seam between its halves is the only thing to find.

24 across LIEUTENANT

US cop that is blocking tunnel at ground (10)

Definition: US cop · anagram + container

  • tunnel at → LUTENANT the eight letters of tunnel at, ground into a new order
  • that is → IE ie is the Latin id est, that is

'At ground' reads as a location beside the tunnel and then splits in two: 'at' is a pair of the letters, 'ground' is what to do with them all.

25 across YELLOW

Cry quietly having dispatched one large lemon? (6)

Definition: lemon · charade + deletion

  • Cry → YELL a cry is a yell
  • quietly → LOW speaking low is speaking quietly
  • one large → L large is L on a clothing label, and one of the two Ls is sent away

Run the two pieces together and there are three Ls in a row; the clue sends one of them away, which is all 'one large' is there for.

26 across ENZYME

Stimulating stuff from lead characters in naughty novel, I'm told (6)

Definition: Stimulating stuff · first letters + homophone

  • naughty novel → NN the initial letters of the two words, a pair of Ns
  • I'm → ENZYME two Ns spoken are 'N's', and running that into the clue's own I'm gives the sound of the answer

'I'm told' looks like one modest aside and is really split in half: 'I'm' is material, 'told' the instruction. The lead characters are letters, not lovers.

Down

1 down STOP

Break crockery up (4)

Definition: Break · reversal

  • crockery → POTS crockery is pots, and a down clue reads them from the bottom up

'Break' is doing noun duty — a break in play — while the surface has you smashing plates. A down entry has no backwards, which is why the clue says up.

2 down REFRAIN

Those stopping football and cricket desist (7)

Definition: desist · charade

  • Those stopping football → REF the man who stops a football match is the ref
  • cricket → RAIN what stops a cricket match is rain

One phrase, 'those stopping', serves two utterly different stoppers — a man in black and the weather — and the answer they build is a third kind of stopping.

3 down PARTY WALL

Maybe Labour overturned Liberal legislation's divisive feature (5,4)

Definition: divisive feature · charade + reversal

  • Maybe Labour → PARTY Labour is one party among several, and 'maybe' is what sends you up to the general word
  • Liberal → L L is the standard abbreviation for Liberal
  • legislation → LAW legislation is the law, and it follows the L before the pair are turned

The Westminster surface is a decoy — nothing political is overturned here, and the second half of the answer is a builder's word, not a parliament's.

5 down PITON

Advice from below about where to fix rope (5)

Definition: where to fix rope · charade + reversal

  • Advice → PIT a piece of advice is a tip, climbing from below
  • about → ON a book about birds is a book on birds

'About' is the trap: it looks like an instruction to wrap one piece round another, and is quietly just a small word meaning on.

6 down TONIGHT

Stretched nurses down for shifts this evening (7)

Definition: this evening · container

  • Stretched → TIGHT a stretched rope is tight
  • down for shifts → ON anyone down for shifts is on — the crossword's ON for working or on duty

Nothing here is medical: 'nurses' means holding rather than caring, and ON for working is a piece of shorthand worth banking early.

7 down MASTERMIND

Chief planner furious about M1, forbidding bridges (10)

Definition: Chief planner · container

  • furious → MAD furious is mad, and it opens after its MA
  • forbidding → STERN a forbidding look is a stern one
  • M1 → MI the motorway's 1 is the Roman numeral I, so M1 is written MI

A road scheme with a bridge over the motorway is a complete decoy, and reading the 1 as a Roman numeral is the step that unlocks it.

8 down MIGHT-HAVE-BEEN

Drone maybe brought into harbour after power failure (5-4-4)

Definition: failure · charade + container

  • power → MIGHT might is power, as in might is right
  • harbour → HAVEN a harbour is a haven, and it opens after HAVE
  • Drone maybe → BEE a drone is one kind of bee, which is what 'maybe' signals

'Power failure' reads as one phrase and is really two jobs: the first word is wordplay, the second the definition. Spotting that seam is the clue.

12 down NEBULOSITY

Shapelessness misrepresented by outlines (10)

Definition: Shapelessness · anagram

  • by outlines → NEBULOSITY the ten letters of by outlines, redrawn

'By' looks like the joinery holding the sentence together and is really two of the letters, which is where this one hides.

15 down CHARLATAN

Fake tea the French turn brown (9)

Definition: Fake · charade

  • tea → CHAR char is the old British slang for a cup of tea
  • the French → LA 'the' in French is la
  • turn brown → TAN to tan is to turn brown in the sun

'The French' for LA or LE is a workhorse of British cryptics. 'Turn brown' is a verb where the surface offers it as a description of the tea.

17 down OIL WELL

Fuel supply cook uncapped successfully (3,4)

Definition: Fuel supply · charade + deletion

  • cook → OIL to cook is to BOIL, and uncapped it loses its first letter
  • successfully → WELL doing something successfully is doing it well

The joke is that the answer caps itself: uncapping is exactly what happens to a real one when it blows, and the surface is a kitchen.

19 down MASONRY

Building work that near bores old PM (7)

Definition: Building work · container + charade

  • old PM → MAY Theresa May was prime minister until 2019
  • that → SO not that big and not so big say the same thing
  • near → NR nr is the map-maker's short form of near

'That' as SO is the piece to spot: the smallest word in the clue, doing the most work, hidden in plain grammar.

21 down WILCO

OK without sealing airlock regularly (5)

Definition: OK · container + alternate letters

  • without → WO w/o is the everyday written shorthand for without
  • airlock → ILC the second, fourth and sixth letters of aIrLoCk

The airlock and the seal belong to one picture, which is what makes them so hard to read as instructions instead of scenery.

22 down ONCE

Somebody catching cold at any time (4)

Definition: at any time · container

  • Somebody → ONE somebody, an unnamed person, is one
  • cold → C C is the standard abbreviation for cold, as marked on a tap

'Catching cold' is a single idea on the surface and two instructions underneath, which is why nothing in the sentence sounds mechanical.
