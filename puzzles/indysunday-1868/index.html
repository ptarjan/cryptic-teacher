Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,868

Across

1 across BRIDESMAID

Bachelor travels on motorway to help girl taking train (10)

Definition: girl taking train · charade

  • Bachelor → B the standard abbreviation, as in Bachelor of Arts
  • travels → RIDES someone who travels by horse, bike or bus rides
  • motorway → M British roads are numbered this way: the M1, the M25
  • help → AID aid is help, and the two swap freely as noun or verb

The surface is a journey by road, so 'taking train' invites you to add a leg by rail. It is the trailing part of a dress instead.

7 across BIFF

Thump boyfriend admitting condition (4)

Definition: Thump · container

  • boyfriend → BF BF is how a boyfriend is abbreviated in a text message
  • condition → IF an if is the condition attached to a promise

Three words with nothing to skip past, which is what makes a short clue hard: 'boyfriend' looks like the subject of a sentence and is really two initials.

9 across QUISLING

Collaborator who French support when injured (8)

Definition: Collaborator · charade

  • who French → QUI qui is the French for who, with the language named after the word it translates
  • support when injured → SLING the cloth loop that holds a broken arm across the chest

Vidkun Quisling ran Norway on Germany's behalf from 1940, and within months his surname had become the ordinary English word for anyone who does the same.

10 across MUTINY

Mother curtailed baby's insubordination (6)

Definition: insubordination · charade + deletion

  • Mother → MU mum is the British word for mother, and its tail comes off
  • baby → TINY a baby portion or a baby car is a tiny one

The surface has a parent cutting short some backchat, so the two halves of the wordplay read as one continuous sentence about a family row.

11 across DOUBTING THOMAS

Sceptic bishop feeds tailored version of God to humanist (8,6)

Definition: Sceptic · anagram

  • bishop → B B is the bishop on a chess scoresheet
  • God to humanist → GOD TO HUMANIST thirteen letters, which with the bishop's one make up the full fourteen

'Sceptic bishop' sounds like a churchman with private doubts, and that smooth picture is what buries the seam: only the first word is the definition.

13 across MAI TAI

Would it be OK if I said "cocktail"? (3,3)

Definition: cocktail · homophone

  • Would it be OK if I → MAI TAI 'Might I?' is the polite way of asking whether something would be allowed, and aloud the t slides across to open the second word

The quotation marks are the tell: 'cocktail' is being held up as a word to be defined, and everything before it is a phrase you say out loud.

15 across ENLISTED

Nurse leant on a roll (8)

Definition: on a roll · charade

  • Nurse → EN EN is the British qualification of enrolled nurse, the grade below a registered one
  • leant → LISTED a ship that leant over to one side listed

'On a roll' looks like a run of good luck, or a filling in a bap. A roll is a register of names, the muster an army keeps.

16 across NOT A PEEP

Silence, a lack of records? (3,1,4)

Definition: Silence · charade

  • a lack of → NO a lack of something amounts to none of it
  • records → TAPE one recording format, the magnetic ribbon inside a cassette
  • records → EP the other, an extended-play disc carrying more tracks than a single

The question mark flags the definition by example: two named formats stand in for records in general, and their joins fall nowhere near the answer's.

19 across ACUITY

Certainly, carrying knife one goes in for sharpness (6)

Definition: sharpness · container

  • Certainly → AY ay is the old spelling of aye, the word for yes certainly
  • knife → CUT to knife someone is to cut them, so the verb stands for the noun
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one, a single stroke

The comma after the first word reads as a pause in speech, as though the setter were agreeing with you. It is there to disguise wordplay as conversation.

21 across REPRESENTATIVE

Ambassador among others here at four dealing with English sandwiches (14)

Definition: Ambassador · charade + container

  • dealing with → RE re heads a memo about its subject: re your letter of the third
  • among others here → PRESENT to be present is to be here, in the room with the rest of the company
  • at → AT the little word is taken exactly as it stands
  • four → IV four written in Roman numerals
  • English → E E is the usual abbreviation on a form or a scoreboard

'Ambassador among others' invites you to picture a room full of diplomats, and that smooth opening is what buries the seam after the very first word.

23 across WALLOP

One standing at the side of the room by work buffet (6)

Definition: buffet · charade

  • One standing at the side of the room → WALL the upright at the edge of a room, described at length rather than named
  • work → OP op is opus, a composer's numbered work

'Buffet' at the end looks like a spread of food. Said the other way, to rhyme with tuffet, it means to knock about with blows.

25 across PUSHOVER

Happy turning barge right, one managed easily (8)

Definition: one managed easily · charade + reversal

  • Happy → UP someone feeling up is in high spirits, and these two letters do not stay in that order
  • barge → SHOVE to barge into someone is to shove them out of the way
  • right → R R is right on a map or in a stage direction

'Barge' is the trap: with a river of a surface around it, it reads as a boat rather than as the verb for shouldering through a crowd.

26 across G'DAY

Neighbours' greeting, perhaps not straight across front of drive? (4)

Definition: Neighbours' greeting, perhaps · container

  • not straight → GAY straight is the slang for heterosexual, so its opposite gives the three letters
  • drive → D the letter it begins with, and nothing more

Neighbours is the Australian soap that ran for decades on British afternoon television, so the greeting wanted is the one heard on Ramsay Street.

27 across NETHERMOST

Flask in bag right at the bottom (10)

Definition: right at the bottom · container

  • bag → NET a net is the bag a shopper or a fisherman carries things home in
  • Flask → THERMOS the vacuum flask that keeps tea hot, known by its brand name

'Right at the bottom' reads as the last thing left in the bag, which is the joke: the words that sound like a position in the surface are the definition.

2 down ROUND

One piece of toast ample? (5)

Definition: One piece of toast · double definition

  • One piece of toast → a British cafe counts toast in slices, and one slice is one of these
  • ample → the kindly word for a full, well-padded figure

Two definitions butted straight together with no joinery, which is why five words read as one breakfast order. The question mark marks the second, looser sense.

3 down DISGUST

Revolt suits leaders of guerrillas desperate for reform (7)

Definition: Revolt · anagram + first letters

  • suits → SUITS five of the seven letters, handed over as a word before being broken up
  • guerrillas desperate → GD the opening letter of each of the two words joins the pool

Guerrillas and revolt paint one political picture, and that is what hides the seam: the first word is the definition and the second is raw material.

4 down SAINTLINESS

Aren't ropes aboard ship very good quality? (11)

Definition: very good quality · charade + container

  • Aren't → AINT read as the colloquial ain't, and the apostrophe drops out as it always does in a grid
  • ropes → LINES at sea a rope that has a job to do is called a line
  • ship → SS SS is the prefix on a steamship's name, and the pair splits to sit at either end

'Aren't' looks like grammar holding the question together. Read it as the slangy contraction instead and it stops being a word and becomes four letters.

5 down ARGONNE

WWI battleground Nivelle evacuated with gas coming over (7)

Definition: WWI battleground · charade + outer letters

  • gas → ARGON argon is the inert gas used in light bulbs and welding
  • Nivelle → NE the outer pair from the name of the French general who commanded in 1917

The Argonne is a forest in north-eastern France, and the Meuse-Argonne offensive of autumn 1918 was the largest battle the American army had then fought.

6 down DAMPEN

Restrain mother hen (6)

Definition: Restrain · charade

  • mother → DAM a dam is the mother of a foal or a calf, the breeder's word that pairs with sire
  • hen → PEN a pen is a female swan, and hen serves for the female of a bird generally

Three words long, and the two after the definition both mean a female animal, which is what lets them read as one phrase about poultry.

7 down BUTTONS

Remote controls, absolutely loads (7)

Definition: Remote controls · charade

  • absolutely → BUT but carries the sense of purely or no more than: it is but a trifle
  • loads → TONS tons of it means loads of it, the same everyday exaggeration

'Remote controls' wants to be a verb phrase about operating something from a distance. It is a plural noun: the controls on a remote.

8 down FUNDAMENT

Kitty met an awful arse (9)

Definition: arse · charade + anagram

  • Kitty → FUND the kitty is the pot of money a group all chips into
  • met an → AMENT five letters across two little words, to be reassembled

FUNDAMENT is the formal, faintly medical word for the buttocks, and it shares a root with foundation: what you sit on is what you rest on.

12 down TALLAHASSEE

Florida city high-rise enjoys view across area (11)

Definition: Florida city · charade + container

  • high-rise → TALL a high-rise is a tall building, and it is the adjective that is wanted
  • area → A A is the standard abbreviation for area
  • enjoys → HAS someone who enjoys good health has it
  • view → SEE to view something is to see it

Not Miami and not Orlando: the state capital of Florida is a small city up in the northern panhandle, which is why the skyline in the surface misleads.

14 down AFOREHAND

American shot by Fritz, perhaps earlier (9)

Definition: earlier · charade

  • American → A A abbreviates American, and it does double duty as the nationality in the surface
  • shot by Fritz, perhaps → FOREHAND Taylor Fritz is a tennis player, so a stroke of his is one example of this shot

'Fritz' wants to be a German, the old wartime nickname for one. The 'perhaps' is what redirects it towards a particular named sportsman.

17 down AURALLY

Recover gold crowns by listening (7)

Definition: by listening · charade

  • gold → AU AU is gold's symbol on the periodic table, from the Latin aurum
  • Recover → RALLY a patient who rallies recovers strength, and so does a falling share price

'Recover gold crowns' reads as dentistry or treasure, and it is that picture which disguises the middle word as loot rather than as an instruction.

18 down PINT POT

Container of two halves, name seen in mine with lid flipped (4,3)

Definition: Container of two halves · container + reversal

  • mine → PIT a pit is a coal mine, the word used across the British coalfields
  • name → N N is the abbreviation for name on any form
  • lid → TOP the lid of a jar is its top, and this one does not stay that way up

The definition is a hint as well as a definition: two half-pints fill one of these, so the vessel is named by the measure it holds.

20 down UNIFORM

Showing equality, further education to benefit millions (7)

Definition: Showing equality · charade

  • further education → UNI uni is the everyday British short form of university
  • to benefit → FOR to be for something is to be in its favour, working to its benefit
  • millions → M M stands for millions on a balance sheet

The surface is an argument about funding, and the trap is the noun everyone knows: school clothes never come into it, because the definition wants an adjective.

22 down EVENS

Half chance rugby doesn't start (5)

Definition: Half chance · deletion

  • rugby → SEVENS sevens is the seven-a-side form of the game, an Olympic sport since 2016
  • doesn't start → S the letter that would have opened it is the one taken away

Naming a sport by the number of players a side is what lets the setter treat a whole game as a word, and the surface hides it as a scoreline.
