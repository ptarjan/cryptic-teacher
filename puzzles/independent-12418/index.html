1 across WOMBAT

Marsupial reproductive organ initially astounded taxonomists (6)

Definition: Marsupial · charade + first letters

Marsupial -> WOMBAT: definition by example, since the wombat is one particular Australian pouched animal and the clue names only the family.

  • reproductive organ → WOMB the organ a mammal carries its unborn young in
  • astounded taxonomists → AT the initial letters of the two words, in the order the clue prints them
  • initially — what is done initially is done at the very start, so only the opening letter of each word beside it is taken

A marsupial's young finish growing in the pouch rather than the womb, so the organ the wordplay borrows is the one this animal makes least use of.

5 across GREMLINS

Troublemakers playing REM single endlessly (8)

Definition: Troublemakers · anagram + deletion

Troublemakers -> GREMLINS: a gremlin is the mischief-maker held responsible when a machine misbehaves, so the word names a whole class of small deliberate trouble.

  • REM → REM the band's three letters join the pile exactly as printed
  • single → SINGL a single stopped before its last letter supplies five more
  • playing — a band plays a song, but letters played with are letters handled loosely enough to end up somewhere else
  • endlessly — something endless never reaches its end, so the final letter of the word beside it is never written down

Gremlins are the imaginary imps RAF crews blamed for machinery failing in the 1940s, and the name has outlived the aircraft it was invented for.

9 across BEWAIL

Lament extremely bizarre rule about nursing institute (6)

Definition: Lament · charade + outer letters + reversal + container

Lament -> BEWAIL: to bewail a thing is to weep and cry out over it, an old-fashioned verb that survives mainly in writing and in crosswords.

  • bizarre → BE the first and last letters of the word, its two extremes
  • rule → LAW a rule laid down for everyone is a law, and here it is written backwards
  • institute → I I is the abbreviation for institute, as in a body's initials; it drops in between the A and the L
  • extremely — the extremes of a word are its two far ends, so this one contributes only its outermost letters
  • about — something turned about is facing the other way, and these three letters are written in the opposite order
  • nursing — a nurse keeps a patient in her care, so the piece beside it is being held inside

Four small operations in six words, and the sentence still reads as a grumble about red tape — that smoothness is the disguise, and 'nursing' is what gives it away.

10 across MACHISMO

Chairman maintains church is centre of harmful masculinity (8)

Definition: masculinity · container + charade + middle letter

masculinity -> MACHISMO: the Spanish word for aggressive male pride, taken into English for masculinity of the strutting, something-to-prove kind.

  • Chairman → MAO Chairman Mao ruled China from 1949, and the title alone is enough to name him
  • church → CH CH is what an Ordnance Survey map prints for a church
  • is → IS the verb is taken at face value, two letters as written
  • harmful → M the middle letter of the seven, with three on each side
  • maintains — to maintain something is to keep hold of it, so this word keeps the others inside itself
  • centre of — the centre of a word is the letter with the same number standing either side of it

'Harmful masculinity' is the phrase the surface wants read whole, and prising it apart is the solve: only the second word is the definition.

11 across MEANDERS

Turns nasty colour, repulsed by sulphur (8)

Definition: Turns · charade + reversal

Turns -> MEANDERS: a river that meanders turns this way and that across flat ground, so the verb names continual wandering rather than one bend.

  • nasty → MEAN a mean trick is a nasty one
  • colour → RED the plainest colour of them all, and it arrives back to front
  • sulphur → S S is sulphur's symbol in the periodic table
  • repulsed — an attack that is repulsed is driven straight back the way it came, and so are these three letters

'Turns nasty' is a phrase, and separating the two words is the whole clue: one of them is the definition and the other is four letters of the answer.

12 across AVERSE

Opposed state surveillance on vacation (6)

Definition: Opposed · charade + outer letters

Opposed -> AVERSE: to be averse to a thing is to be set against it, and the word almost always arrives with 'to' in front of what is opposed.

  • state → AVER to aver something is to state it firmly, a word that survives mostly in court
  • surveillance → SE everything between its first letter and its last has gone away, leaving the two ends
  • on vacation — a house whose owners are on vacation is empty in the middle, with only its outside left standing

'State surveillance' is one idea on the surface and two separate jobs underneath, which is why a clue about spying reads so much like a headline.

13 across ZONE

District of Cadiz on escarpment (4)

Definition: District · hidden word

District -> ZONE: a zone is an area marked off and treated as a single unit, so it is the planner's word for what everyday speech calls a district.

  • Cadiz on escarpment → ZONE the four letters run across the joins, from the end of the Spanish port into the start of the slope
  • of — a district of a place is a piece taken from inside it, and this piece is taken from inside the words that follow

Cadiz and an escarpment make a plausible piece of geography, and geography is the one thing they are not doing here: they are only lending letters.

15 across TIMOROUS

Fearful rodent discovered on Southeast Asian island (8)

Definition: Fearful · charade + middle letters

Fearful -> TIMOROUS: timorous is built on timor, Latin for fear, and describes the one shrinking from a fright rather than the fright itself.

  • Southeast Asian island → TIMOR the island at the far end of the Indonesian chain, shared with East Timor
  • rodent → OUS the rodent is a MOUSE, and stripping a letter off each end leaves the middle three
  • discovered — read it as dis-covered: the covering comes off, and a word's covering is the letter at each end
  • on — in an across entry one thing sits on another by following it, so this piece goes at the back

Burns addressed a mouse as a 'wee, sleekit, cow'rin, tim'rous beastie', so the rodent and the definition have been sharing a line of verse since 1785.

18 across HERDSMAN

Detective entertained by Melville's name for bucolic character (8)

Definition: bucolic character · container

bucolic character -> HERDSMAN: bucolic means of the countryside and its herding life, so the figure the word conjures is the one driving the cattle.

  • Melville's name → HERMAN the author of Moby-Dick was Herman Melville, and his first name parts after three letters
  • Detective → DS DS is a detective sergeant's rank as the police abbreviate it
  • entertained by — a host entertains a guest inside the house, so the letters beside it take the other piece in

Whale-hunting is nobody's idea of a pastoral scene, which makes Melville's own name a good place to keep a man who looks after cattle.

19 across SOLO

Piece one played very softly in auditorium (4)

Definition: Piece one played · homophone

Piece one played -> SOLO: a solo is written for a single performer, so the definition names it by counting the people on stage.

  • very softly → SOLO to play very softly is to play so low, and the two words run together into one when you say them
  • in auditorium — an auditorium is a room for hearing, so whatever happens there reaches you through the ear rather than the eye

Music sits on both sides of this clue, a dynamic marking on one and a performance on the other, so the ear is the only thing keeping them apart.

21 across RIBALD

Funny bone with a large diameter (6)

Definition: Funny · charade

Funny -> RIBALD: ribald humour is the coarse, rude sort, so the definition offers the mildest available word for a joke that is anything but.

  • bone → RIB one of the twelve pairs curving round the chest
  • a → A the article is taken exactly as printed
  • large → L L is the size printed on a clothing label
  • diameter → D d stands for diameter in a geometry formula

'Funny bone' is the phrase the surface offers and its two words belong to opposite halves of the clue, which is as neat a join as this grid has.

23 across MISQUOTE

Times desperately stifling question over inaccurate citation (8)

Definition: inaccurate citation · anagram + container + charade

inaccurate citation -> MISQUOTE: to misquote is to report someone's words wrongly, with MIS- doing its usual job of turning an action into a botched one.

  • Times → MISTE the paper's five letters dealt out again, then opened after three
  • question → QU qu. is the short form of question on a dictionary page or an exam paper
  • over → O O is an over on a cricket scorecard
  • desperately — a desperate person will try anything in any order, and letters handled that way come out in one
  • stifling — to stifle someone is to smother them by closing over their face, so these letters close over the ones named next

The italics mark the newspaper rather than the plural of time, so the surface is a paper getting a quotation wrong — and lending five of the eight letters to do it.

25 across INHERENT

Entire broadcast about New Hampshire native (8)

Definition: native · anagram + container

native -> INHERENT: an inherent quality is one born in the thing itself, which is the older sense of native, belonging by birth rather than by nationality.

  • Entire → IERENT the six letters dealt out again, and they part after the first
  • New Hampshire → NH NH is the state's two-letter postal code
  • broadcast — to broadcast seed is to scatter it by hand, so what is broadcast lands in no particular order
  • about — one thing set about another is wrapped round it, so these letters open to let the next piece in

'Native' is the definition hiding in plain sight, because a native of New Hampshire is the obvious reading and the state is only there for two letters.

26 across INFAMY

Popular fellow beginning to appreciate setter's notoriety (6)

Definition: notoriety · charade + first letter

notoriety -> INFAMY: infamy is fame gone bad, so the definition is the neutral-sounding noun for being widely known for the wrong reasons.

  • Popular → IN whatever is in this season is what is popular
  • fellow → F F is the abbreviation for fellow, as after the name of a Royal Society member
  • appreciate → A the first letter of the word
  • setter's → MY the setter is the voice writing the clue, so anything of the setter's is mine
  • beginning to — the beginning of a word is the letter it starts with, and that is all this one contributes

'Setter's' means 'my' wherever a crossword says it — the setter speaks the clue in the first person — and it is one of the earliest conventions worth owning.

27 across RELAUNCH

A filling meal after essentially free promotional event (8)

Definition: promotional event · charade + container + middle letters

promotional event -> RELAUNCH: a product sent back to market with new packaging, and the party thrown to announce it takes the same name.

  • free → RE the two letters at the heart of the word, with the F and the last E taken off
  • meal → LUNCH the midday meal, and it opens up after its first letter
  • A → A the article is taken exactly as printed, and it is what goes into the gap
  • filling — a filling goes into the hole in a tooth, so this letter drops into the word rather than sitting beside it
  • essentially — the essence of a word is what sits at its heart once the outsides are taken away
  • after — what comes after a thing is written behind it, so the piece named first takes the front

'A filling meal' is one idea on the surface and three instructions underneath, which is how a clue about lunch turns out to be about marketing.

28 across EJECTS

Releases tip of willie caught in flies! (6)

Definition: Releases · charade + container + last letter

Releases -> EJECTS: a machine ejects a cassette by pushing it out, so the definition is the gentler word for the same forcible letting-go.

  • willie → E the letter at the very end of the word
  • flies → JETS jets are aircraft, so 'flies' is a noun here and not a verb
  • caught → C c is how a cricket scorecard records a batsman caught, and it drops in after the second letter
  • tip of — the tip of a thing is its far end, so what is wanted is the letter the word finishes on
  • in — whatever is caught in something is held inside it, so this letter sits among the others rather than beside them

The seaside-postcard surface is the joke, and every word of it turns out respectable: a trouser zip, some aircraft and a line of a cricket score.

2 down OBESE

Burmese boarding houses served up super roly-poly (5)

Definition: super roly-poly · hidden word + reversal

super roly-poly -> OBESE: roly-poly is the nursery word for comfortably round, and pushing it to 'super' reaches the clinical term for dangerously overweight.

  • Burmese boarding → OBESE the tail of the first word and the head of the second give five letters in a row, and they read the right way only from the far end
  • houses — to house something is to keep it within your walls, so these words have the answer indoors
  • served up — a dish comes up from the kitchen, and in a down entry coming up means reading from the bottom square towards the top

A roly-poly is a suet pudding as well as a shape, which is what lets a boarding-house dinner and a description of a diner share one sentence.

3 down BRAIN-DEAD

Stupid Scandinavian spawned Spoonerism (5-4)

Definition: Stupid · spoonerism

Stupid -> BRAIN-DEAD: the medical term for irreversible loss of brain function, borrowed by everyday speech as an insult for someone showing no sign of thought.

  • Scandinavian → BRAIN a Dane is a Scandinavian, and Spooner takes his D away and hands him a BR instead
  • spawned → DEAD spawned means bred, and it collects the D the Dane gave up
  • Spoonerism — the Reverend Spooner traded the opening sounds of two words, so naming the trick is an instruction to swap them over

Say the setter's two words in his order first and swap afterwards; the hyphen in the enumeration is the promise that what comes out is one compound word.

4 down TALKER

Hunter S dismissed as gossip? (6)

Definition: gossip · deletion

gossip -> TALKER: a gossip is named for the single thing they do, and the plain agent noun for someone who does a lot of it says the same.

  • Hunter S dismissed → TALKER the hunter is a STALKER, one who follows game unseen, and sacking the S it starts with leaves the six letters after it
  • dismissed — a servant who is dismissed is sent away and does not come back, and the letter beside it goes the same way

Hunter S. Thompson is the whole misdirection: the middle initial belongs to the wordplay rather than to the journalist, and it is the letter being sacked.

5 down GAME SET AND MATCH

Enraged MAGA chants meet defending Democrat's victory statement (4,3,3,5)

Definition: victory statement · anagram + container

victory statement -> GAME SET AND MATCH: the umpire's words as a tennis match ends, used far beyond the court to announce a contest is settled.

  • MAGA chants meet → GAMESETANMATCH fourteen letters dealt out again, and they part after nine to take something in
  • Democrat → D on a US ballot a Democrat's name carries a (D) after it
  • Enraged — rage is the loss of all self-control, and letters that have lost control of themselves will not stay in the order given
  • defending — a defender stands all round the thing being protected, so the rearranged letters close over the piece named next

A rally between MAGA chanters and a Democrat is the picture; the answer is called from the umpire's chair instead, which is where a clue about a contest least wants you to look.

6 down EXCLAIMS

Cries of former international comprehended by reticent people (8)

Definition: Cries · charade + container

Cries -> EXCLAIMS: to exclaim is to cry out suddenly, so the definition is the everyday verb for the same short burst of speech.

  • former → EX an ex is a former partner, and EX- is the standing prefix for anything former
  • reticent people → CLAMS someone who clams up refuses to say anything, so a clam is a reticent person
  • international → I an international is a player capped for their country, and I is the abbreviation for it; it lands between the A and the M
  • comprehended by — in its older sense to comprehend is to take something in and hold it, so the piece beside it swallows the other

Reticent people who make cries of any kind are a contradiction, and the clue survives it because the two halves never meet: one is the definition, the other the fodder.

7 down LOIRE

Odd characters fleeing flood in Rye Valley (5)

Definition: Valley · alternate letters

Valley -> LOIRE: the Loire Valley is the chateau-lined stretch of central France, known to everyone by the river's name alone.

  • flood in Rye → LOIRE ten letters, of which the second, fourth, sixth, eighth and tenth stay behind
  • Odd characters fleeing — number the letters along and let the first, third and fifth run away; every second one is left standing

Rye is a Sussex town where flooding is a real worry, so the surface reads as local news while the letters are quietly being counted off in French.

8 down NUMBSKULL

Drunken bum slunk around Lima? Idiot! (9)

Definition: Idiot · anagram + container

Idiot -> NUMBSKULL: literally a skull gone numb, so the word calls someone stupid by picturing a head that cannot feel anything.

  • bum slunk → NUMBSKUL eight letters dealt out again, and they part before the last of them
  • Lima → L Lima is how a radio operator says the letter L
  • Drunken — a drunk cannot walk a straight line, and letters in that state keep to no line either
  • around — to go around a thing is to pass on every side of it, so the rearranged letters close over the one named next

The question mark after the city is doing real work: it warns that Peru is not wanted at all, only the letter the word stands for on the airwaves.

14 down OBEDIENCE

Deference award one kept in study overlooking empty countryside (9)

Definition: Deference · charade + container + outer letters

Deference -> OBEDIENCE: both name yielding to another's will, obedience being the doing of what you are told where deference is the respect behind it.

  • award → OBE the Order of the British Empire, handed out at the palace twice a year
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one
  • study → DEN a den is the small room at home where the work gets done, and the I goes in after its D
  • countryside → CE its first and last letters, with everything between them cleared out
  • kept in — a pupil kept in stays inside the building after everyone else has gone, and this letter stays inside the word
  • overlooking — a house overlooking a field stands above it, and in a down entry standing above means coming first
  • empty — an empty room has nothing between its walls, so only the first and last letters of the word remain

An award, a study and the countryside are the furniture of a settled English life, which is what stops the surface sounding like the list of instructions it is.

16 down RESHUFFLE

Drag one's feet following concerning shake-up (9)

Definition: shake-up · charade

shake-up -> RESHUFFLE: a cabinet reshuffle moves the same people into different jobs, which is exactly what a shake-up of an organisation amounts to.

  • concerning → RE RE heads a memo to mean concerning, from the Latin for 'in the matter of'
  • Drag one's feet → SHUFFLE to shuffle is to walk without lifting your feet clear of the floor
  • following — what follows a thing comes after it, and in a down entry after means further down the column

'Shake-up' sits at the end of the clue looking like an instruction to rearrange letters. It is the definition, and nothing in this one gets rearranged.

17 down SMIDGEON

Pinch child squashing tiny insect (8)

Definition: Pinch · container

Pinch -> SMIDGEON: a pinch of salt is as much as finger and thumb will hold, and a smidgeon is the same vague, very small helping.

  • child → SON a son is somebody's child, and the word opens up after its first letter
  • tiny insect → MIDGE the biting fly of Scottish summers, small enough to fit inside
  • squashing — to squash a thing is to press down until it is flat under whatever is on top, so the piece named next ends up buried

A child squashing an insect is a complete small scene, and it does the opposite of what it says: the insect ends up inside the child, entirely intact.

20 down ASPIRE

Seek over-the-counter drug when lacking in energy (6)

Definition: Seek · deletion + charade

Seek -> ASPIRE: to aspire to something is to seek it as an ambition, the sense of seeking that is about wanting rather than searching.

  • over-the-counter drug → ASPIR ASPIRIN is the painkiller sold without a prescription, and it turns up here without its IN
  • energy → E E is the physicist's symbol for energy, as in E=mc2
  • lacking — to lack a thing is to be without it, so whatever is named next is missing from the word

'Lacking in energy' reads as one weary phrase and has to be heard as two separate instructions, which is the whole of the disguise here.

22 down ARENA

An epoch reflected in entertainment venue (5)

Definition: entertainment venue · charade + reversal

entertainment venue -> ARENA: the Roman arena was the sanded floor where the spectacle took place, and the word now covers the whole building a tour plays in.

  • An → AN the article is taken exactly as printed, two letters
  • epoch → ERA an era is a stretch of history, the epoch of a geologist or a historian
  • reflected — a reflection shows a thing the wrong way round, and these five letters are read back out of the mirror

Five letters that read as an ordinary scrap of English forwards and a concert venue backwards; the surface is doing nothing but supplying them.

24 down TEMPT

Seduce office worker close to retirement (5)

Definition: Seduce · charade + last letter

Seduce -> TEMPT: both mean drawing someone towards what they should resist, tempt being the older and milder of the two, as in tempting fate.

  • office worker → TEMP a temp is the agency worker brought in to cover a desk for a week
  • retirement → T the letter the word closes on
  • close to — the close of anything is its end, so only the final letter of the word beside it is wanted

An office worker close to retirement is a whole person in five words, and the clue needs you to stop seeing them and start counting letters.
