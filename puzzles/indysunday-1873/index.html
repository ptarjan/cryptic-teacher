Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,873

Across

1 across CARESS

Pet minds Sam leaving in the morning (6)

Definition: Pet · charade + deletion

  • minds → CARES to mind something is to care about it, and the -s ending carries straight across
  • Sam → SAM the man's name is handed over whole, before anything is taken out of it
  • in the morning → AM am is the clock's label for the hours before noon, and that pair is what departs

The surface has Sam catching an early train. 'Pet' is the verb, not the animal — you pet a dog rather than keep one.

4 across ALIENS

Film creatures right inside eviscerated astronauts? (6)

Definition: Film creatures · container + outer letters

  • astronauts → AS the outermost letter at either end of that word, once the middle is gone
  • right → LIEN a lien is a creditor's legal right to hold property until a debt is paid

'Right' is nearly always a single letter in a cryptic, which is the trap. Here it is the legal sort, and it fills the space the astronauts' insides left.

8 across RANDOMISER

Creator of lottery managed by complete Scrooge (10)

Definition: Creator of lottery · charade

  • managed → RAN to run a business is to manage it, and the past tense matches the clue's
  • complete → DO to do a job is to complete it, so the two verbs swap in that sense
  • Scrooge → MISER Dickens's tight-fisted Scrooge has given his name to the type

Three plain synonyms laid end to end. The only trap is reading 'complete' as an instruction to finish something rather than as two letters.

10 across OGRE

Giant boob at Wembley, possibly concerning (4)

Definition: Giant · charade

  • boob at Wembley, possibly → OG an own goal is a footballer's classic blunder, and og is how it goes down on the scoresheet at England's national stadium
  • concerning → RE re means about or concerning, the heading at the top of a memo

'Giant boob' reads as one phrase and is two. The definition stops after the first word, and everything else is the mistake.

11 across BASENJI

Dog willing to hump anyone in designer jeans? On the contrary (7)

Definition: Dog · container + anagram

  • willing to hump anyone → BI bi is short for bisexual, and the clue's leer is that such a person is willing either way
  • jeans → JEANS five letters taken as printed, ready to be laid out again

'Designer jeans' looks like a label to be recognised and is really fodder plus an instruction. Then the last three words turn the container inside out.

12 across HOOVER

President's house knocked down (6)

Definition: President · charade

  • house → HO ho is the standard abbreviation for house, the one an estate agent prints in an address
  • knocked down → OVER knock something over and you have knocked it down, so the little word carries the toppled sense by itself

Nothing in the clue tells you to join the halves — they simply sit side by side, which is a charade at its plainest. 28 across borrows the same surname.

13 across BRUNEI

Monarchy, British one, stuck with old German character (6)

Definition: Monarchy · container

  • British one → BI b for British and i for one, the Roman numeral, taken in that order
  • old German character → RUNE a rune is a letter of the old Germanic alphabet, cut into stone or wood

'Character' wants to be a person in a story. It is a written sign, and the alphabet in question was carved rather than penned.

15 across BUMP INTO

Tramp and horse meet (4,4)

Definition: meet · charade

  • Tramp → BUM a bum is a tramp or vagrant in American usage
  • horse → PINTO a pinto is a horse patched in white and one other colour, the American word for a piebald

Three words, and 'meet' is the whole definition. Two nouns stacked end to end come out as a phrasal verb, which is the small surprise here.

17 across CHAINSAW

Husband trapped by murderer noticed weapon in horror (8)

Definition: weapon in horror · container + charade

  • murderer → CAIN Cain killed his brother Abel, and he is the murderer every cryptic reaches for
  • Husband → H h is the standard abbreviation for husband, as w is for wife
  • noticed → SAW to notice something is to see it, and the past tense is what the clue offers

Two conventions in one clue, both worth keeping: the Bible's first killer and the single letter for a husband. The horror film supplies the definition.

19 across DO TELL

Coming back through Lille today, really? (2,4)

Definition: really? · hidden word + reversal

  • Lille today → DOTELL six consecutive letters here, spanning the gap between the two words, read from the right-hand end

An innocent line about travel, and the letters sit across the join between the two words, which is where a hidden run is hardest to see.

21 across AENEAS

Sean Bean stripped off playing Trojan hero (6)

Definition: Trojan hero · anagram + middle letters

  • Sean → SEAN the first name is handed over entire, all four letters of it
  • Bean → EA the middle pair of the surname, once the outer letter at each end has been taken off

Sean Bean has played a great many parts, though never this one. 'Playing' is the shuffle instruction and the actor is only there to lend letters.

23 across IMITATE

Ape that is holding article in hand (7)

Definition: Ape · container

  • that is → IE i.e. is short for the Latin id est, which translates as 'that is'
  • hand → MITT a mitt is a hand, the boxer's and the baseballer's word for it
  • article → A a is the indefinite article, the shortest of the three in English

Two insertions, one nested in the other, and 'i.e.' for 'that is' is the convention to keep. What makes it hard is that i.e. is split apart rather than used whole.

25 across DONE

Agreed starting point for white queen? (4)

Definition: Agreed · charade

  • white queen → D set the pieces out and she stands on the d-file, the fourth column from the left
  • starting point → ONE white's men line up along the first rank, so the square she starts on carries that number

A chessboard is lettered a to h across and numbered one to eight up, so every square has a name of exactly this shape. The question mark admits it is a definition by example.

26 across LOGGERHEAD

Lake George had various species of turtle (10)

Definition: species of turtle · charade + anagram

  • Lake → L l is the map-maker's abbreviation for lake
  • George had → GEORGEHAD nine letters taken exactly as printed, before they are put in a new order

Lake George is a real place in New York State, and that is what makes the fodder read as a proper noun rather than as letters waiting to be shuffled.

27 across NO-SHOW

Today cops nearly used guns, booking waster (2-4)

Definition: booking waster · container + deletion

  • Today → NOW today is the day that is happening now, so the three letters stand for the present moment
  • used guns → SHOT guns are shot when they are used, and only part of that word survives

'Cops' looks like police the moment guns are mentioned, and it is the instruction instead. The grid hyphenates the answer, which the enumeration tells you.

28 across FORCED

Given no option, monster punches 12's agent (6)

Definition: Given no option · container

  • monster → ORC an orc is Tolkien's foot-soldier monster, three letters a setter can rely on
  • 12's agent → FED a fed is a US federal agent, and the Hoover of 12 across directed the FBI for decades

The cross-reference does two jobs at once: it sends you to 12 across for a surname, and it expects you to know which Hoover had agents working for him.

Down

1 down CHAMBER

Slope around hospital room (7)

Definition: room · container

  • Slope → CAMBER a camber is the slope built into a road so the rain runs off it
  • hospital → H h is the standard abbreviation for hospital, the letter on the road sign

A slope on a road has a name of its own, which is the piece of vocabulary this clue is really testing; the hospital letter is the easy half.

2 down RIDES

Fair features of women marrying with face hidden? (5)

Definition: Fair features · deletion

  • women marrying → BRIDES the women at a wedding, in the plural the clue asks for
  • face → B the face of a word is its front, so this is the letter it begins with

'Fair' is the funfair rather than fairness, and 'features' are the attractions it puts on. The question mark owns up to the surface, where a veil is what you picture.

3 down SEMANTICS

Almost half opposed to crosswords on vacation — language issues? (9)

Definition: language issues · charade + deletion + outer letters

  • half → SEMI semi- is the prefix for half, as in semicircle or semi-final
  • opposed to → ANTI anti is the everyday word for being against something
  • crosswords → CS the first and last letters of that word, all that survives the clear-out

Setters reach for semi- whenever they want half, and the trick here is that only part of it survives. The dash marks where the wordplay stops.

5 down LARCH

Structure supporting weight beneath large tree (5)

Definition: tree · charade

  • large → L l is the standard abbreviation for large, the size on a clothing label
  • Structure supporting weight → ARCH an arch is the curve that carries the load of everything built above it

The clue never says 'arch', it describes what an arch does. Definition by description rather than by synonym is a setter's habit worth learning early.

6 down ECONOMIST

Marx among others once mucked around with duck film (9)

Definition: Marx among others · charade + anagram

  • once → ONCE four letters taken exactly as printed, before anything is done to them
  • duck → O a batsman out without scoring has made a duck, written as a nought on the card
  • film → MIST a film of vapour on a window is a mist, the two nouns naming the same faint covering

Marx plus a duck plus a film points hard at Groucho and Duck Soup, and the definition wants Karl instead. Only 'once' is fodder here.

7 down SURFEIT

Too much sport on waves, with cup match coming up (7)

Definition: Too much · charade + reversal

  • sport on waves → SURF to surf is to ride the breaking waves, and the sport takes the same name
  • cup match → TIE a cup tie is the knockout fixture a British club is drawn into, nothing to do with knots

'Cup match' is a phrase in British football, and the clue's smooth talk about sport and waves is there to keep you reading it as weather.

9 down SPIN BOWLING

Maybe leg breaks cast that accommodates leg bend (4,7)

Definition: Maybe leg breaks · charade + container

  • cast → SPIN a cast is a throw, and a throw given a turn of the wrist is a spin
  • bend → BOWING to bow is to bend at the waist, and the -ing form is the noun for doing it
  • leg → L l is the standard abbreviation for leg, as on a scorecard or a tailor's chart

The surface builds a broken leg in plaster: 'breaks' and 'cast' belong together there. Both words are working for cricket instead, where a break is a ball that turns off the pitch.

14 down NOISELESS

Having no hooter keeps one quiet (9)

Definition: quiet · container

  • Having no hooter → NOSELESS hooter is British slang for the nose, from the sound it makes when blown, so a face without one is described here
  • one → I i is one in Roman numerals, the commonest single letter in the language

The surface plays on the other hooter, the horn, which is exactly what a silent vehicle would lack. The clue is happy for you to think of both.

16 down MADRILENO

Spaniard crazy with anger, wouldn't you say? (9)

Definition: Spaniard · charade

  • crazy → MAD mad is the plain three-letter word for crazy
  • anger → RILE to rile someone is to anger them, the verb sense the clue needs
  • wouldn't you say? → NO tack 'no?' onto the end of a statement and it invites agreement, exactly as the English tag question does

Spanish spells this word with a tilde over the n; British grids drop accents, as they do with cafe and neé. The tag question at the end supplies the last two letters.

18 down HOEDOWN

Did weed have a dance? (7)

Definition: dance · charade

  • Did weed → HOED to hoe is to weed a bed with a long-handled blade, and 'did' asks for the past tense
  • have → OWN to own a thing is to have it, both of them plain verbs of possession

The question mark flags the odd question the surface asks about weed. Both halves are ordinary verbs, one past and one present, laid end to end.

20 down LEEWARD

Pull fish up to one side of the boat (7)

Definition: one side of the boat · charade + reversal

  • Pull → DRAW to draw a cart is to pull it, the verbs matching exactly
  • fish → EEL the eel is the fish a setter reaches for when three letters are wanted

Both pieces go down in clue order and the whole assembly then climbs the column. In a down entry 'up' is the only reversal word available.

22 down SALVO

Sudden burst of five entering hairdressers' wanting trim (5)

Definition: Sudden burst · container + deletion

  • hairdressers' → SALON the salon is where hairdressers work, and its last letter is snipped before anything goes in
  • five → V v is five in Roman numerals

'Wanting trim' sits so comfortably in the hairdresser's surface that it reads as description rather than instruction, which is the whole of the setter's trick here.

24 down AD HOC

Case of delish locally certified bottles for the occasion (2,3)

Definition: for the occasion · container + outer letters

  • locally certified → AOC AOC is appellation d'origine controlee, the French mark guaranteeing a wine comes from the district on the label
  • delish → DH the letter at each end of that word, the outside of the case

'Case of' is the outside of a word rather than a box of wine, though the surface would much rather sell you the wine. Latin phrase, French label, English clue.
