Across

9 across CHARD

Vegetable patch ardently covered (5)

Definition: Vegetable · hidden word

  • patch ardently → CHARD the run crosses the gap: the tail of 'patch' and the front of 'ardently'

Hidden answers are hardest to see when they straddle the gap between two words, and a gardening surface gives you no reason to go looking for one.

10 across CHOCOLATE

Sweet coke, hot drinks in cafe with espresso at first (9)

Definition: Sweet · charade + container + first letters

  • cafe → C the first letter of CAFE, which leads off
  • hot → HOT the word is used as it stands, and it opens up to take something in
  • coke → COLA a coke is a cola, and it goes inside
  • espresso → E the first letter of ESPRESSO, closing the answer off

Every word of the surface is something you could order at a counter, which is what hides the fact that two of them are wanted for one letter each.

11 across SUSTAINED

Confirmed fronts of service uniforms dirty (9)

Definition: Confirmed · charade + first letters

  • service → S the first letter of SERVICE
  • uniforms → U the first letter of UNIFORMS
  • dirty → STAINED dirty clothing is stained

The answer is courtroom English — what a judge says when an objection is upheld — while the surface stands in a barracks looking at somebody's kit.

12 across KNIFE

Drunk Nelson at last provided English cutter (5)

Definition: cutter · charade + last letters

  • Drunk → K the last letter of DRUNK
  • Nelson → N the last letter of NELSON
  • provided → IF 'provided that' and 'if' introduce the same condition
  • English → E E is the standard abbreviation for English

One instruction reaches back over two words at once, which is the step that hides the parse: 'Drunk Nelson at last' looks like a scene and is a pair of final letters.

13 across COTTAGE

Extravagant Co-op surrounds home (7)

Definition: home · container

  • Co-op → CAGE a coop is a cage for poultry, and here it opens up to take something in
  • Extravagant → OTT over the top shortens to OTT

The Co-op of the surface is the grocer's; underneath it is a hen coop, and that pun is where all the clue's difficulty lives.

15 across ERITREA

Sufferer I treated internally in a state (7)

Definition: a state · hidden word

  • Sufferer I treated → ERITREA the run starts near the end of 'sufferer', crosses the whole of 'I', and continues into the front of 'treated'

The hidden run starts five letters into 'Sufferer' and crosses two gaps, and the medical surface is there to keep your eye travelling instead of looking.

17 across WATCH

View sample that is trimmed at the front (5)

Definition: View · deletion

  • sample → SWATCH a swatch is a sample of cloth, and its leading S is trimmed away

Tailoring is all over this clue and none of it matters: the sample and the answer differ by a single letter, which is the entire mechanism.

18 across SAG

Sink in which neon reflected? (3)

Definition: Sink · reversal

  • neon → GAS neon is one of the noble gases, and it is turned round

The question mark is doing real work: it owns up to the fact that naming neon is definition by example, one member standing in for the whole family.

20 across GUARD

17, you say, in type of carriage coming back (5)

Definition: 17 · container + reversal + homophone

  • type of carriage → DRAG a drag is a four-horse coach, and it is turned round
  • you → U the letter U is called exactly what the word 'you' sounds like

A cross-reference is the kindest clue in the grid once its partner is solved. A drag really is a carriage: the four-in-hand coach a Victorian gentleman drove himself.

22 across DUCHESS

Peer from jet, disregarding old ship (7)

Definition: Peer · charade + deletion

  • jet → DOUCHE a douche is a jet of water, straight from the French for a shower
  • old → O O is the usual abbreviation for old, and it is the letter dropped
  • ship → SS SS is the steamship prefix on a vessel's name

'Jet' is the spurt of water, not the aircraft and not the black stone, and that is where the clue does all of its hiding.

25 across AIRFARE

Sky-high price of cockney’s locks sounds reasonable (7)

Definition: Sky-high price · charade + deletion + homophone

  • cockney’s locks → AIR locks are hair, and the cockney's version has lost its aitch
  • reasonable → FARE a reasonable price is a fair one, and 'fair' is what you hear — the grid takes the other spelling

Two dialect tricks in one sentence: the dropped aitch, and a fair/fare pair that is among the crossword's favourite soundalikes.

26 across ARENA

Directions with extra finally given after short arm bowl (5)

Definition: bowl · charade + deletion + last letter

  • arm → AR ARM cut short loses its last letter
  • Directions → EN east and north, two compass points run together
  • extra → A the last letter of EXTRA

The surface is a cricket field — a short arm, an extra, a bowl — and not one of those words is doing underneath what it appears to be doing on top.

27 across OWNERSHIP

Possession of pier shown to be condemned (9)

Definition: Possession · anagram

  • pier shown → PIERSHOWN nine letters to rearrange
  • condemned → anagram indicator

Seaside piers really do get condemned, so nothing in the sentence announces itself as an instruction — which is what makes this anagram so hard to spot at all.

30 across MUSTINESS

One packing essential cape that’s showing mould (9)

Definition: showing mould · charade + container

  • essential → MUST an essential is a must, the thing you cannot do without
  • One → I the Roman numeral for one
  • cape → NESS a ness is a headland or cape — Dungeness, Sheerness

'Must' is doing two jobs at once: the essential the clue asks for, and the old word for the very smell of a damp shut-up room. That is the neatest touch here.

31 across LEMON

Colour in retrospective Tarantino melodrama (5)

Definition: Colour · hidden word + reversal

  • Tarantino melodrama → NOMEL the run straddles the gap in tarantiNO MELodrama, waiting to be turned round

'Retrospective' is doing double duty: a film season on the surface, and the instruction to read backwards underneath. Lemon here is the pale yellow, not the fruit.

Down

1 down ACES

Court serves claims, primarily making sound lawsuits with these (4)

Definition: Court serves · deletion + first letter

  • sound lawsuits → CASES lawsuits are cases, and sound ones are cases that stand up in court
  • claims → C the first letter of CLAIMS, and it is the letter that has to come off the front
  • with these → 'these' is the entry itself: the clue says what the answer turns into rather than what it is built from

Kite clues this one the other way round, telling you what the answer becomes once a letter is stuck on the front, so you work back from the lawsuits.

2 down MARSH TIT

Blemish on this wobbly bottom of frequent flyer (5,3)

Definition: flyer · charade + anagram + last letter

  • Blemish → MAR to mar something is to blemish it
  • this → SHTI the four letters of THIS, reset in another order
  • frequent → T the last letter of FREQUENT

'Frequent flyer' is one phrase in the surface and two separate jobs underneath, which is the whole misdirection. A marsh tit is the small brown cousin of the blue tit.

3 down IDEA

Ignoring the last perfect theory (4)

Definition: theory · deletion

  • perfect → IDEAL perfect is ideal, and the L on the end is left out

One letter separates the two words, which is why the sentence reads as an ordinary remark about a flawless theory rather than as an instruction.

4 down SCANNERS

First of cantilever bridges crossed having no parking inspectors (8)

Definition: inspectors · anagram + deletion + first letter

  • bridges → SPANNERS a spanner is anything that spans, so bridges are spanners
  • parking → P P is the road sign for parking, and it is the letter that goes
  • cantilever → C the first letter of CANTILEVER joins what is left

A spanner here is not the tool but anything that spans, and the P is a road sign — two everyday words used in senses the surface keeps well out of sight.

5 down FONDUE

Pet Labrador’s initially absent after eating posh food (6)

Definition: food · container + deletion

  • Pet → FONDLE to pet an animal is to fondle it
  • Labrador’s → L the first letter of LABRADOR, which is the one that goes missing
  • posh → U U is the crossword's shorthand for upper class, out of Nancy Mitford's U and non-U

'Pet' is the verb, not the animal, and the surface's dog is there only so that a Labrador is standing by when the clue asks for a single letter.

6 down ROCK SINGER

Plant maybe in Spooner’s garment dryer (4,6)

Definition: Plant maybe · spoonerism

  • garment → ROCK a sock is the garment, and Spooner takes its S away in exchange for an R
  • dryer → SINGER a wringer squeezes the water out of washing, and it collects the S the sock gave up

Say the plain phrase first and the swap does the rest. A wringer as a dryer is the loosest link in the clue, so it is the piece to trust last.

7 down CAVIAR

Troops I fed left yankee wanting eggs (6)

Definition: eggs · container + deletion

  • Troops → CAVALRY the cavalry are mounted troops
  • left → L L is the abbreviation for left, and it is one of the two letters removed
  • yankee → Y Y is yankee in the NATO alphabet, and it goes as well
  • I → I the letter is lifted straight out of the clue

The NATO alphabet is worth learning whole: yankee is Y, and any of its other letters can be asked for in exactly this way.

8 down FETE

Nice summer, fine at the beginning for festival (4)

Definition: festival · charade

  • fine → F F is the pencil-maker's grade for fine
  • Nice summer → ETE Nice is in France, and summer in French is été

'Nice' with a capital N is the city, and once you see that, the clue is asking for a French word. A village fête is that same word borrowed back into English.

13 down COWED

Join firm, starts scared (5)

Definition: scared · charade

  • firm → CO CO is company, the standard two letters on a firm's name
  • Join → WED to join two people in marriage is to wed them

'Firm' is the noun, not the adjective, and the surface's job offer is decoration — nothing in it has anything to do with fear.

14 down ASHKENAZIM

Jews ask him a Zen problem (10)

Definition: Jews · anagram

  • ask him a Zen → ASKHIMAZEN ten letters to rearrange
  • problem → anagram indicator

A Zen problem is a koan, which is what holds the surface together. The answer names the Jews of central and eastern Europe, as against the Sephardim of Spain.

16 down ADDLE

Bad religious scholar opens beer (5)

Definition: Bad · container

  • beer → ALE ale is beer, and it splits after its first letter
  • religious scholar → DD a Doctor of Divinity is a DD

DD for Doctor of Divinity is worth banking — crosswords reach for it whenever a churchman or a scholar turns up — and an addle egg is where the definition comes from.

19 down GLASNOST

Last song performed in Moscow, it shows sincerity (8)

Definition: in Moscow, it shows sincerity · anagram

  • Last song → LASTSONG eight letters to rearrange
  • performed → anagram indicator

Gorbachev's word reached English in the 1980s alongside perestroika, and the definition here is a whole phrase: it places the answer in Russia and glosses it at once.

21 down ANATHEMA

Three articles by mother providing curse (8)

Definition: curse · charade

  • Three articles → ANATHE English has exactly three articles — AN, A and THE — and all three are used, in that order
  • mother → MA ma is mother

Counting is the solve: English has only three articles, and setting them end to end gets you six of the eight letters before the clue asks for anything else.

23 down CHEESE

Bad luck if it’s hard (6)

Definition: Bad luck if it’s hard · cryptic definition

  • Bad luck → read as ordinary English this is somebody commiserating, which is the sense the surface pushes you towards
  • if it’s hard → the qualifier is the point: put 'hard' in front of the answer and you have the old exclamation of rueful sympathy

There is nothing to take apart here. The clue works by naming a foodstuff that only means bad luck while one particular adjective is standing in front of it.

24 down SHOWER

One that displays useless lot (6)

Definition: One that displays · double definition

  • One that displays → SHOWER the agent noun for someone who exhibits or displays something, formed by adding '-er' to the base verb
  • useless lot → SHOWER British slang for a hopeless, disorganised bunch — the kind of insult barked at a chaotic team

The two halves are not even pronounced alike: the one who shows rhymes with 'lower', the hopeless lot with 'power'. Only the spelling is shared, which is all this type needs.

26 down ARMY

Adams entertaining republican soldiers (4)

Definition: soldiers · container

  • Adams → AMY Amy Adams, the actress, supplies the first name
  • republican → R R is the standard abbreviation for Republican

A surname on its own is often a first name in disguise, and 'republican' sitting next to Adams points you firmly at Gerry rather than at Amy.

28 down ROLL

A bit funny Penny’s missing something needed for toilet (4)

Definition: something needed for toilet · deletion

  • A bit funny → DROLL droll is funny in a dry, mild way
  • Penny’s → D the old penny was d, and that is the letter that goes

The old penny was d, from the Roman denarius, which is why 'penny' in a clue so often means a letter that looks nothing like a P.

29 down PINE

Yearn to stick close to fiancée (4)

Definition: Yearn · charade + last letter

  • stick → PIN to stick a notice up is to pin it
  • fiancée → E the last letter of FIANCÉE

'Stick close to' reads as one phrase about devotion and the clue cuts straight through the middle of it. The tree has nothing to do with anything.
