Across

1 across PRESENCE OF MIND

Appearance of Bond’s boss in Germany shows quick thinking (8,2,4)

Definition: quick thinking · charade

  • Appearance → PRESENCE an appearance is a presence
  • of → OF sits in the answer as itself
  • Bond’s boss → M M runs MI6 in the James Bond films
  • in → IN also stands as itself
  • Germany → D D is the international vehicle code for Germany (Deutschland)

Two little words — 'of' and 'in' — spell themselves, a trick worth spotting: not every clue word is a code for something else.

9 across EXISTENCE

Life perturbed sixteen attending church (9)

Definition: Life · anagram + charade

  • sixteen → EXISTEN 'perturbed' shuffles its seven letters
  • church → CE Church of England

CE for church — the Church of England — is a fixture of British crosswords; here it 'attends' the scrambled SIXTEEN at the end.

10 across THREE

In the Bible, you will accept King or triumvirate (5)

Definition: triumvirate · container

  • In the Bible, you → THEE thee is the biblical 'you'
  • King → R R = Rex, Latin for king

Two conventions in one small grid entry: THEE as the scriptural 'you', and R for Rex on coins and royal ciphers.

11 across IDIOM

Way of speaking I’d associated with Man? (5)

Definition: Way of speaking · charade

  • I’d → ID the contraction, letters as printed
  • Man? → IOM the Isle of Man, abbreviated IoM — the question mark flags the abbreviation

'Man' with a question mark is the Isle of Man, whose abbreviation IOM supplies most of the answer.

12 across OVERHEARD

Picked up source of reek entering near the ceiling? (9)

Definition: Picked up · container + first letter

  • reek → R 'source of' takes its first letter
  • near the ceiling? → OVERHEAD what's near the ceiling is overhead

The R from 'reek' slips into OVERHEAD just before the final D. The surface smell is a distraction; the answer is about ears, not noses.

13 across UNSUBTLE

Blunt? Use ‘blunt’ to anagram this answer (8)

Definition: Blunt? · anagram

  • Use ‘blunt’ → USEBLUNT take these eight letters literally — they are the fodder

A self-referential joke: the instruction means exactly what it says. Anagram the words 'use blunt' and the answer appears — the setter being anything but subtle.

14 across SOLEMN

Glum, single fellow losing heart (6)

Definition: Glum · charade + deletion

  • single → SOLE sole = single, only
  • fellow → MN MAN 'losing heart' drops his middle letter A

'Losing heart' is the standard signal to remove a word's middle letter — the poor fellow is left as just M and N.

17 across MEDLEY

By the sound of it, inclined to interfere in the mix (6)

Definition: the mix · homophone

  • inclined to interfere → MEDLEY sounds like 'meddly' — apt to meddle

Someone inclined to interfere might whimsically be called 'meddly', and said aloud that is the answer. Homophones of invented words are fair when the base word — meddle — is real.

19 across LITERACY

On fire and sexy, absorbing English writing skill? (8)

Definition: writing skill? · charade + container

  • On fire → LIT lit = on fire, also slang for exciting
  • and → surface glue joining the two outer pieces
  • sexy → RACY racy = risqué, sexy
  • English → E E = English, absorbed between LIT and RACY

LIT and RACY together absorb the E — 'absorbing' is the container signal. The steamy surface is a long way from the classroom.

22 across TALL ORDER

Monks requiring long robes, a difficult request (4,5)

Definition: Monks requiring long robes · double definition

  • Monks requiring long robes → TALL ORDER read literally: a religious order made up of tall monks
  • a difficult request → TALL ORDER the everyday idiom for a demanding ask

One definition is literal-minded whimsy — a religious order of tall monks, hence the long robes — and the other is the everyday idiom.

24 across TERSE

Short extract from Easter service (5)

Definition: Short · hidden word

  • Easter service → TERSE hidden: easTER SErvice

'Extract from' tells you to pull the answer straight out of the letters — it spans the two words of the churchy surface.

25 across LEVEL

Flat party needing change of direction at the outset (5)

Definition: Flat · charade + deletion + first letter

  • party → REVEL a revel is a lively party — about to change its first letter
  • change of direction → L the opening R (right) becomes the other direction, L (left)

'Direction' here means the everyday kind: R for right and L for left. Swap one for the other at the front of REVEL and the party flattens out.

26 across ESPERANTO

No repeats after translating language (9)

Definition: language · anagram

  • No repeats → NOREPEATS the anagram fodder, nine letters

'After translating' is a pointed indicator for a clue about an invented international language — the fodder rearranges with nothing left over.

27 across CRASH-TEST DUMMY

Vehicle experimenter’s tool wrecked the cars way before misleading move (5-4,5)

Definition: Vehicle experimenter’s tool · anagram + charade

  • the cars → CRASHTE 'wrecked' — apt for cars — scrambles the seven letters
  • way → ST st = street, a way
  • misleading move → DUMMY in rugby or football, a dummy is a feinted pass — a misleading move

Wrecked cars, then ST for a way, all 'before' the DUMMY — a sporting feint, not a fool. The whole surface is one long staged collision.

Down

1 down PRECIOUS METALS

One probing clumpiest ores dug up gold, silver, etc. (8,6)

Definition: gold, silver, etc. · anagram

  • One → A one = A, probing (going inside) the mix
  • clumpiest ores → CLUMPIESTORES the main anagram fodder, 13 letters

'Dug up' disturbs the letters — apt for ores — and 'probing' pushes the A from 'one' in among them. The surface stays in the mine all the way.

2 down ELICITS

Extracts what’s allowed between bridge opponents? (7)

Definition: Extracts · container

  • what’s allowed → LICIT licit means lawful — allowed
  • bridge opponents? → ES East and South sit on opposing partnerships at the bridge table, and they split to the two ends

Bridge partnerships are North–South against East–West, so East and South are genuinely opponents — a convention setters lean on for stray letters.

3 down ESTIMABLE

Excellent meals with bite, possibly (9)

Definition: Excellent · anagram

  • meals with bite → MEALSBITE the anagram fodder — 'with' simply joins the two words and adds no letters

'Possibly' is the quiet kind of anagram indicator: it just admits the letters could be otherwise. The food surface hides an adjective of praise.

4 down CANOODLE

Surrounded by low light, loves to snog (8)

Definition: to snog · container

  • low light → CANDLE a candle gives a low, dim light — it wraps round the OO
  • loves → OO love = O (a zero score, as in tennis), so loves = OO

'Love' for O is one of the oldest conventions in cryptics — from the tennis score — and the plural doubles it. The candlelit surface is doing exactly what the answer describes.

5 down OPENER

I bat first – training at school brought in single run (6)

Definition: I bat first · charade + container

  • training at school → PE physical education — school training
  • single → ONE a single is one
  • run → R r = run on a cricket scorecard

PE is 'brought in' to ONE + R, landing after the O. The cricket surface is honest for once: single, run and batting first all belong to the same game as the answer.

6 down MATCH

Striker in soccer game? (5)

Definition: Striker · double definition

  • Striker → MATCH a match is a striker — the light you strike
  • soccer game → MATCH a game of football is a match

The surface wants you picturing a centre-forward, but the 'striker' here lives in a matchbox — a match is what you strike.

7 down NARRATE

Hurried up, supported by judge, to tell story (7)

Definition: to tell story · charade + reversal

  • Hurried → RAN hurried = ran; it climbs the grid to read NAR
  • judge → RATE to rate something is to judge it

In a down entry, 'up' turns RAN on its head and 'supported by' puts RATE underneath, holding it up — both indicators only make sense on a vertical answer.

8 down READ-ONLY MEMORY

Rely on army demo to spoil computer data (4-4,6)

Definition: computer data · anagram

  • Rely on army demo → RELYONARMYDEMO the anagram fodder, 14 letters

A single clean anagram: the military surface dissolves into a chip. Fourteen letters of fodder with nothing left over — count them when a long answer looks daunting.

15 down OVERTIRED

Clear anger shown by daughter – exhausted? (9)

Definition: exhausted? · charade

  • Clear → OVERT overt = open, clear to see
  • anger → IRE ire is anger
  • daughter → D d = daughter, a genealogy abbreviation

Three synonyms and abbreviations stacked in clue order — the family row in the surface never happened.

16 down DISRUPTS

Puts out? Duke puts Sir out (8)

Definition: Puts out? · charade + anagram

  • Duke → D d = duke, from chess and peerage abbreviations
  • puts Sir → ISRUPTS the second 'out' scrambles these seven letters

The setter uses 'puts out' twice — once as the definition, once split so that 'out' scrambles 'puts Sir'. Seeing one phrase do two jobs is the whole trick here.

18 down DELIVER

Work as postman? Hated getting up (7)

Definition: Work as postman? · reversal

  • Hated → REVILED reviled = hated; read upwards it spells the answer

REVILED and DELIVER are exact reverses — a classic pair. 'Getting up' works because this is a down entry: the word climbs.

20 down ACRONYM

A Member bringing in friend for quango, for example (7)

Definition: quango, for example · container

  • A → A stands as itself
  • Member → M m = member, as in club listings
  • friend → CRONY a crony is a close friend

A and M — the Member — bring CRONY in between them. The parliamentary surface of members and friends is pure misdirection.

21 down ADVENT

Be emotional after promotional item in Church season (6)

Definition: Church season · charade

  • promotional item → AD an ad — an advertisement
  • Be emotional → VENT to vent is to let feelings out

'After' puts VENT after AD — in a down entry, that means below it. Note the answer also means an arrival, which is exactly what the season awaits.

23 down OGLES

Looks longingly at bit of ginger in battered sole (5)

Definition: Looks longingly at · anagram + container + first letter

  • ginger → G 'bit of' takes its first letter
  • sole → OLES 'battered' — apt for a fish — jumbles it around the G

'Battered' doing double duty — cooking term on the surface, anagram indicator underneath — is the joke; the G from 'ginger' lands inside the shaken SOLE.
