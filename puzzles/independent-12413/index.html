7 across ELGIN

English composer avoiding a river in Moray town (5)

Definition: Moray town · deletion + charade

  • English composer → ELGAR Sir Edward Elgar wrote the Enigma Variations and the Pomp and Circumstance marches
  • a river → A and R are the letters to lose: r. is the map abbreviation for river
  • in → IN the word is used as itself, two letters straight from the clue

'In' looks like the join between wordplay and definition and is actually part of the build, which is why the composer alone comes up short.

8 across HIGHLANDS

Husband with dashing looks initially wandering in mountainous area (9)

Definition: mountainous area · anagram + first letter

  • Husband → H H is the standard abbreviation for husband, as on a form
  • dashing → DASHING seven letters of fodder, straight from the clue
  • looks initially → L the opening letter of looks

'Husband with dashing looks' is a compliment in the surface and three separate scraps in the parse; splitting that phrase up is the whole trick.

11 across NORTHERN LIGHTS

British PM and his majesty live in dark times - this may brighten up the sky (8,6)

Definition: this may brighten up the sky · charade + container

  • British PM → NORTH Lord North was prime minister from 1770 to 1782, the one who lost the American colonies
  • his majesty → ER ER is Edwardus Rex, the Latin cypher of a King Edward
  • dark times → NIGHTS the dark hours are nights, and the word opens up after its first letter
  • live → L L marks the live wire in household wiring, the brown one

'PM' invites a modern prime minister and gets a Georgian one; 'his majesty' wants a king's Latin cypher rather than any living monarch.

12 across HADRIAN'S WALL

Deceived, Iran's in a fight with everyone which is sort of borderline? (8,4)

Definition: sort of borderline? · charade + anagram

  • Deceived → HAD to be had is to be deceived, as in 'you've been had'
  • Iran's → RIANS the country plus its apostrophe-s, five letters rearranged
  • with → W w/ is the everyday shorthand for with
  • everyone → ALL everyone present is all of them

The fight in the surface is a news bulletin; in the parse it is an instruction, and what it builds has stood in the north of England since 122 AD.

15 across OUTWARD

Mostly better to keep hostilities away from the centre (7)

Definition: away from the centre · container + deletion

  • Mostly better → OUTD to better a rival is to outdo them, and the last letter is not wanted
  • hostilities → WAR open hostilities between countries are war

Read as English the clue is about keeping a fight at arm's length; read as wordplay it insists on shutting one in. That reversal is the joke.

17 across CYPRIOT

Deranged Trump's latest 'victory': having Venezuela's leader removed for one from Europe (7)

Definition: one from Europe · anagram + deletion + last letter

  • Trump's latest → P the last letter of Trump
  • 'victory' → ICTORY the quoted word supplies its letters once the opening one has gone
  • Venezuela's leader → V is the letter Venezuela leads with, and it is the one that leaves

The quotation marks are the surface's sarcasm about the claimed win, not an instruction: nothing in the parse depends on them.

20 across SMOG

Very good to receive money for something that is breathtaking (4)

Definition: something that is breathtaking · container

  • Very → SO so means very, as in 'so good'
  • good → G G is the standard abbreviation for good, as on a school report
  • money → M M stands for a million in sums of money, as in a £5m transfer

'Breathtaking' is a compliment everywhere except here, where the definition means it literally: air you would rather not breathe.

22 across PEMBROKE

Parking space was revealed in Welsh town (8)

Definition: Welsh town · charade

  • Parking → P P is the letter on the road sign and the gearstick for parking
  • space → EM an em is a printer's space, the width of a capital M
  • was revealed → BROKE a story that was revealed broke, as in the news broke overnight

'Parking space' is one idea in the surface and two separate abbreviations in the parse. EM is the typesetter's unit and is worth keeping.

25 across HANNIBAL

At first hungry maneater beheaded an enemy of Rome (8)

Definition: an enemy of Rome · charade + first letter + deletion

  • At first hungry → H the opening letter of hungry
  • maneater beheaded → ANNIBAL a maneater is a cannibal, and the front letter is struck off

The surface is a horror film, and the film's cannibal borrowed his name from the general, which is exactly why the misdirection works so smoothly.

26 across UMBRIA

Capital of Campania is not in part of England but somewhere in Italy (6)

Definition: somewhere in Italy · deletion

  • part of England → CUMBRIA Cumbria is the county in England's north-west that holds the Lake District
  • Capital of Campania → C is the letter Campania starts with, and it is the one that goes

Naples is the capital of Campania and is not wanted at all; the clue means the letter C. Two Italian regions and an English county in one sentence.

27 across APENNINES

Type of pasta mostly found in lively Siena, a feature of Italy (9)

Definition: a feature of Italy · anagram + container + deletion

  • lively Siena → AINES the five letters of the Tuscan city shaken up, then opened out to take a filling
  • Type of pasta mostly → PENN penne are the short quill-shaped tubes, and the final letter is left behind

A Tuscan city, a Roman pasta and a definition in Italy: the surface is a holiday, and everything in it except the definition is raw material.

28 across TREND

Fashion that would be causing great sadness if displayed during hearing (5)

Definition: Fashion · container

  • hearing → HEARING is the word doing the containing: it splits as HEAR and ING with a gap between them
  • causing great sadness → fill that gap and HEARTRENDING appears, which is the phrase this fragment defines

The wordplay is inside out: it does not build the answer, it tells you what the answer builds once you post it into a word for listening.

1 down ULTIMATUM

Pressing demand from internet pioneer for friends at university in German city (9)

Definition: Pressing demand · charade + container

  • German city → ULM Ulm stands on the Danube in southern Germany, and these three letters open up after the second
  • internet pioneer for friends → TIM Sir Tim Berners-Lee invented the web, and 'for friends' asks for the first name rather than the surname
  • at → AT the little word is taken at face value, two letters lifted from the clue
  • university → U U is the standard abbreviation for university

The surface promises Silicon Valley and student days; every one of those words is really a separate scrap of letters, and all of them end up inside the city.

2 down WITHDRAW

Pull out with no win or lose then (8)

Definition: Pull out · charade

  • with → WITH the word is used as itself, four letters straight from the clue
  • no win or lose → DRAW a match that is neither won nor lost has been drawn
  • then → surface only: it makes the clue sound like a shrug and contributes no letters

'No win or lose' reads as a run of bad luck at the bookmaker's; in the wordplay it is the sporting result that is neither one nor the other.

3 down WITNESS

See red perhaps more than once, beginning to struggle with tension inside (7)

Definition: See · container + first letter

  • red perhaps more than once → WINES red is one kind of wine, and the plural gives five letters that split after the second
  • beginning to struggle → S the opening letter of struggle, which goes on the end
  • tension → T T is the physics symbol for tension, the pull along a rope or a string

'See red' is one idiom in the surface and two jobs in the parse: the definition stops after the first word, and 'red' is already fodder.

4 down THIRD WAY

What Heathrow wants: less control, neither left or right-wing policies (5,3)

Definition: neither left or right-wing policies · deletion

  • What Heathrow wants → THIRDRUNWAY Heathrow is London's largest airport, and its long-fought expansion plan is a third runway
  • control → to run a business is to control it, and those three letters are the ones taken out

Both halves of the clue are politics, which is the disguise: the airport row supplies the letters and the centrist slogan supplies the meaning.

5 down WARHOL

Artist's conflict with Picasso surreal ultimately (6)

Definition: Artist · charade + last letters

  • conflict → WAR an armed conflict between states is a war
  • with Picasso surreal → HOL the last letters of these three words, in the order the clue sets them down

Picasso and 'surreal' point at the wrong movement entirely; both are there for their final letters and neither painter is wanted.

6 down ADORE

Love Austria being ahead of Germany or Spain (5)

Definition: Love · charade

  • Austria → A A is Austria's international vehicle code, the oval sticker on the back of a car
  • Germany → D D for Deutschland is Germany's international vehicle code
  • or → OR the conjunction is used as itself, two letters from the clue
  • Spain → E E for Espana is Spain's international vehicle code

Three country codes in a row with one plain word between them: these are the letters a driver sees on number plates all over Europe.

9 down ASIA

Extremely ambitious area including India? (4)

Definition: area including India? · container + outer letters

  • Extremely ambitious → AS the two outermost letters of ambitious, first and last
  • area → A a is the abbreviation for area, the one a geometry formula uses
  • India → I India is I in the NATO alphabet that radio operators spell with

'Area' and 'India' each do two jobs at once, defining and handing over a letter; that double duty is what makes four letters this hard.

14 down COCKAIGNE

England striker talked about after Colombia initially criticised life of luxury here (9)

Definition: life of luxury here · charade + first letter + homophone

  • Colombia → CO CO is Colombia's two-letter country code, the one on its web addresses
  • initially criticised → C the opening letter of criticised
  • England striker → KAIGNE Harry Kane is England's record goalscorer, and this spelling of the sound ends the way campaign does

The spelling is the joke: -AIGNE says 'ayn', exactly as it does in campaign and champagne, so a footballer's name comes out looking medieval.

16 down RAGE BAIT

New SEAT oddly lacking airbag - it's designed to deliberately make you angry (4,4)

Definition: designed to deliberately make you angry · anagram + alternate letters

  • SEAT oddly lacking → ET drop the first and third letters of the four and its even ones are left
  • airbag → AIRBAG six letters of fodder, straight from the clue

SEAT in capitals is the Spanish car maker, which is why the whole surface reads as a motoring review rather than a note about the internet.

18 down RARE BIRD

Something unusual like bloody chicken (4,4)

Definition: Something unusual · charade

  • like bloody → RARE a steak cooked rare is still red in the middle
  • chicken → BIRD a chicken is one kind of bird

Both halves come off the same dinner plate, the cooking of the meat and the creature it came from, which is why the clue reads as one phrase.

19 down APPLIED

Non-theoretical program spread fake news? (7)

Definition: Non-theoretical · charade

  • program → APP a program on a phone is an app
  • spread fake news → LIED whoever spread fake news lied

The question mark is doing real work: 'spread fake news' is a loose gloss for the second chunk rather than a dictionary synonym for it.

21 down MANANA

Bit of mango fruit topped for later (6)

Definition: later · charade + first letter + deletion

  • Bit of mango → M the opening letter of mango
  • fruit topped → ANANA the fruit is a banana, and this entry runs downwards so its top letter comes away

Two fruits in a row keep the surface in the greengrocer's, and the answer is a Spanish word English borrowed for putting things off.

23 down MAUL

Every now and then Emmanuel gets badly hurt (4)

Definition: badly hurt · alternate letters

  • Emmanuel → MAUL the second, fourth, sixth and eighth letters of the name, read in order

A long name hides its alternate letters well: count them off in pairs rather than trying to read anything out of the word itself.

24 down HAPPY

One of seven slightly intoxicated (5)

Definition: One of seven · double definition

  • One of seven → Snow White's seven dwarfs are each named for a mood or a habit, and this is the cheerful one
  • slightly intoxicated → two drinks leave you merry rather than drunk, and English has a mild word for that state

Four words, no wordplay, and the join is invisible because a dwarf's name and a state of mind are the same word in the same tone.
