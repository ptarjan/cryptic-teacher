Independent Cryptic Crossword No 12,421

Across

1 across ROBUST

Hold up, crossworders, the first of these is tough (6)

Definition: tough · charade + first letter

  • Hold up → ROB a hold-up at the bank is a robbery, so to hold up is to rob
  • crossworders → US the setter is addressing the people solving this, and we crossworders are US
  • these → T the head of the word 'these' is T

The comma is the disguise: 'Hold up, crossworders' reads as the setter asking us to wait a moment, and is really an instruction plus a word for the people addressed.

4 across AFFLUENT

Loaded footballers turned voluble (8)

Definition: Loaded · reversal + charade

  • footballers → AF the FA is the association that runs the English game and stands in for its players; turned round it reads AF
  • voluble → FLUENT someone voluble talks readily and easily, which is what fluent means

'Loaded' is the misdirection. Beside talkative footballers it wants to mean drunk, and the sense the answer needs is rich.

9 across TOURISM

Travel business to ancient city is minute (7)

Definition: Travel business · charade

  • to → TO the preposition is taken at face value, letter for letter
  • ancient city → UR Ur in Mesopotamia is the crossword's standing ancient city
  • is → IS the verb is lifted straight out of the clue as two letters
  • minute → M m is the standard abbreviation for minute

Only the city is a synonym here. Everything else is built from the small grammatical words the clue is written with, which is why they read as joinery.

11 across ROTATOR

Rubbish art mixed up with old Turner (7)

Definition: Turner · anagram

  • Rubbish → ROT rot is what you call something rubbish - he talks utter rot
  • art → ART the word goes into the mix exactly as it is printed
  • old → O o is the standard abbreviation for old, as in OAP

'Old Turner' is a painter on the surface and the clue would love you to hunt for JMW; the word it wants is the everyday one for anything that goes round.

12 across LIGHT

Weak understanding (5)

Definition: Weak · double definition

  • Weak → LIGHT weak in intensity, as in a mild drizzle or a lenient sentence
  • understanding → LIGHT the flash of comprehension when something finally makes sense, or what a good explanation sheds on a problem

Two words, two definitions and no wordplay at all - the pair reads as one phrase about a feeble grasp of something, and that is the whole disguise.

13 across THESAURUS

So keeps bloke from Genesis at front of record book (9)

Definition: book · container + charade + first letter

  • So → THUS so and thus are interchangeable - 'so it follows', 'thus it follows' - and the word parts in the middle
  • bloke from Genesis → ESAU Esau, Jacob's hairy elder brother, is a man out of the book of Genesis
  • record → R the front letter of 'record'

The italics make 'So' a Peter Gabriel album and the bloke from Genesis his old band; the setter means the plain word and the Bible's first book.

14 across SCANDALMONGER

Scrutinise modern gal spreading gossip (13)

Definition: gossip · charade + anagram

  • Scrutinise → SCAN to scan a document is to scrutinise it
  • modern gal → DALMONGER the nine letters of MODERN GAL, scattered into a new order

'Spreading gossip' is the phrase the surface wants read whole, and prising the two words apart is the solve: one is an instruction, the other the definition.

18 across SCROOGE MCDUCK

Disney toon to inspire about half of crooner's rock album (7,6)

Definition: Disney toon · container + charade

  • to inspire → SUCK to inspire is to breathe in, and to suck is to draw in the same way; the word parts after its first letter
  • crooner's → CROO the first four of the eight letters of CROONER'S
  • rock → GEM a rock is a jewel - the enormous rock on her finger
  • album → CD the album you buy in a shop is a CD

Inspire means breathe in long before it means encourage, and it is that older sense which hands over the word that swallows everything else.

20 across PAPETERIE

Case favoured Pennsylvania city beyond its state (9)

Definition: Case · charade

  • its state → PA Pennsylvania's postal abbreviation is PA
  • favoured → PET a pet theory or a pet subject is a favoured one
  • Pennsylvania city → ERIE Erie stands on the lake of the same name in the north-west corner of the state

Pennsylvania is clued twice over. It is named once for the city that sits in it, then pointed at again as 'its state', where only the two-letter form fits.

22 across TWILL

Material desire follows lust in the end (5)

Definition: Material · charade + last letter

  • lust → T the last letter of 'lust'
  • desire → WILL will is desire as a noun - doing something against your will

'Desire follows lust' reads as a single idea about appetite and is really two separate instructions, which is how the answer's one-letter opening stays hidden.

23 across OIL LAMP

Light reflected through strip mall I operate (3,4)

Definition: Light · hidden word + reversal

  • strip mall I operate → OIL LAMP the run striP MALL I Operate carries PMALLIO, which read backwards is the answer

A shopping-centre surface with the answer lying across four words of it, and 'reflected' is the only thing telling you which way to read.

24 across FITNESS

Fine to watch stripping with good health (7)

Definition: good health · charade + deletion

  • Fine → F F is the pencil grade for fine, and the standard crossword abbreviation
  • to watch → WITNESS to witness an event is to watch it happen
  • with → W w is the everyday abbreviation for with, and it is the letter stripped off the front

'Stripping with' reads as one phrase about undressing. Split it and 'with' stops being a preposition, becoming the letter that has to go.

25 across BROOKLYN

Bear left at backwards New York borough (8)

Definition: borough · charade + reversal

  • Bear → BROOK to brook no argument is to bear none, so brook is bear in the sense of tolerate
  • left → L L is the map and stage abbreviation for left
  • New York → YN New York shortens to NY, which is turned round to read YN

'Bear left at' is a driving instruction on the surface and three separate scraps of wordplay underneath it.

26 across PLAY AT

Casually try produce coated in bit of butter (4,2)

Definition: Casually try · container

  • bit of butter → PAT a pat of butter is the small slab served beside the bread
  • produce → LAY a hen produces eggs by laying them

'Bit of butter' looks like an instruction to take a piece of a word and is not one: the whole phrase yields a single three-letter word.

Down

1 down RUTHLESS

Savage without one book? (8)

Definition: Savage · charade

  • one book → RUTH Ruth is one of the books of the Old Testament, and the question mark owns up to naming a single example
  • without → LESS coffee without sugar is sugarless, so without does the same job as less

Read as English the clue pictures a brute with nothing to read; read as wordplay, 'without' stops being a preposition and becomes a word in its own right.

2 down BLUEGRASS

Arranged rags in one music genre, and another (9)

Definition: another · anagram + container

  • one music genre → BLUES the blues is a music genre, and the word opens up after its fourth letter
  • rags → GRAS the four letters of rags, reordered, drop into that gap

Rags earns its place on the surface because ragtime sits beside the blues as a genre, which is exactly why it does not look like fodder.

3 down SHIFT

Key change (5)

Definition: Key · double definition

  • Key → SHIFT the key on every keyboard held down to type capitals or alternate symbols
  • change → SHIFT a change in the weather or in opinion, often gradual rather than sudden

Two definitions with nothing between them, and the join is hidden because 'key change' is a phrase in its own right, the musical one.

5 down FORGET ONESELF

Puck perhaps tacked on to make characters behave improperly (6,7)

Definition: behave improperly · charade

  • make → FORGE to forge a horseshoe or an alliance is to make it
  • characters → TONES the tone of a place is its character, and both words take a plural the same way
  • Puck perhaps → ELF Puck is the elf of A Midsummer Night's Dream, and 'perhaps' admits he is only one example

The sentence points the wrong way: 'make characters behave' reads as a stage direction, and only 'tacked on to' gives away that Puck is a chunk of letters.

6 down LETHARGIC

Low on energy, pureed the garlic (9)

Definition: Low on energy · anagram

  • the garlic → LETHARGIC the nine letters of THE GARLIC, stirred into a new order

The surface is a cook explaining why the garlic got pureed rather than chopped, which hides the fact that those two words are the raw material.

7 down ENTER

Go in, letting person cut in front (5)

Definition: Go in · deletion

  • letting person → RENTER a renter is the person who lets a property out, and its leading R is about to go

'Cut in front' does two jobs at once: the queue-jumping the surface describes, and the instruction to lop a letter off the start.

8 down THRUSH

Singer starts to truly hate prog band (6)

Definition: Singer · charade + first letters

  • truly hate → TH the initial letters of those two words are T and H
  • prog band → RUSH Rush are the Canadian trio, one of prog rock's best-known bands

The surface sounds like a music snob's verdict, which is what keeps you from noticing that 'prog band' wants an actual group's name.

10 down METALLOGRAPHY

In which data plotter probes gathered material like steel? (13)

Definition: In which data plotter probes gathered material like steel? · &lit + container + charade

  • gathered → MET a crowd that gathered met, so gathered gives MET
  • material like steel → ALLOY steel is an alloy of iron and carbon, and the word splits before its final letter
  • data plotter → GRAPH a graph is what plots data against a pair of axes

An all-in-one: read it as a sentence and it describes a laboratory, read it as instructions and every word is also doing a job on letters.

15 down DIRT TRACK

Cleared up stretch with races on? (4,5)

Definition: Cleared up stretch with races on? · &lit + reversal + charade

  • Cleared → DIR to be cleared of a debt is to be rid of it, and RID climbing the grid reads DIR
  • races → TT the TT is the Isle of Man Tourist Trophy, the most famous motorcycle races there are
  • stretch → RACK a rack is the frame that stretches whatever is tied to it

An all-in-one, and an unusually literal one: the racing in the surface really is racing, so nothing here has to be re-read except 'cleared' and 'stretch'.

16 down ROUTINELY

Regularly see defeat? (9)

Definition: Regularly · charade

  • defeat → ROUT a rout is a defeat so complete that the losers run
  • see → IN ELY a bishop's see is his cathedral city and Ely is the stock one; a defeat there is a defeat IN ELY, which supplies the little word as well

The question mark is doing the work. 'See defeat' is not a synonym but a place to put one, and the answer splits into exactly that small scene.

17 down SKILL SET

Catching show's opening, pan range of talent (5,3)

Definition: range of talent · container + first letter

  • pan → SKILLET a skillet is a frying pan, and it takes the newcomer in after its fifth letter
  • show's → S the opening letter of 'show's'

'Pan' as a verb - to review something harshly - is what the surface offers, and the noun is what the wordplay needs: a heavy iron frying pan.

19 down APLOMB

Cool doctor under retro dad look (6)

Definition: Cool · charade + reversal

  • dad → AP dad is PA, and PA turned round is AP
  • look → LO lo is the old word for look - lo and behold
  • doctor → MB MB, Bachelor of Medicine, is the degree a doctor holds

'Retro dad' sounds like a man in a vintage shirt, and is really the reversal instruction sitting next to the two letters it acts on.

21 down PALEO

Pasty and doughnut diet for Americans? (5)

Definition: diet for Americans? · charade

  • Pasty → PALE a pasty complexion is a pale one
  • doughnut → O a ring doughnut is a circle with a hole in it, which draws an O

Both wordplay words are things you eat, which is what holds the surface still; only 'pasty' has to be re-read, as an adjective rather than a Cornish supper.

22 down TOTAL

Absolute child verges on animal (5)

Definition: Absolute · charade + outer letters

  • child → TOT a tot is a small child
  • animal → AL the two edges of ANIMAL are A and L

'Verges on animal' looks like a judgement about somebody's behaviour and is a pair of tweezers picking off the first and last letters.
