Across

1 across ASIDES

Remarks to audience: 'So, a bad day for Caesar' (6)

Definition: Remarks to audience · charade

  • So → AS in a comparison the two little words swap freely — not so tall, not as tall — so one stands in for the other
  • a bad day for Caesar → IDES the Roman calendar's mid-month date, and the one in March is when Caesar was assassinated after being told to beware it

The quotation marks make the second half look like a line of dialogue somebody speaks, when it is really two pieces laid end to end — one a tiny synonym, one a date from the Roman calendar.

5 across LETS GO

'Releases'? Come on! (4,2)

Definition: Releases · double definition

  • Releases → to release your grip on something is to let it go, and the quotation marks warn that the answer is a phrase rather than one word
  • Come on! → the chivvying shout that gets a group moving, an invitation to set off together

One set of letters read two ways, and an apostrophe is the only difference between them: the first is a verb about a hand opening, the second is somebody impatient at the door.

8 across NOW AND AGAIN

Last of ill-gotten wonga Diana wasted from time to time (3,3,5)

Definition: from time to time · anagram + last letter

  • Last of ill-gotten → N the closing letter of the hyphenated word
  • wonga Diana → WONGADIANA wonga is British slang for cash, money; the princess supplies the other five letters

'Ill-gotten wonga' is such a complete picture of dodgy cash that it takes some doing to see only its final letter is wanted, and that most of the fodder is a name.

11 across TRIATHLON

Ran, hot, 'til knackered - as part of this? (9)

Definition: Ran, hot, 'til knackered - as part of this? · &lit + anagram

  • Ran, hot, 'til → RANHOTTIL nine letters from the three words before the dash, which is the length required

'Ran' looks like the definition and is fodder instead. Every word before the dash is letters, and the sentence read a second time describes the event those letters spell.

12 across FEWER

Fine water jug, no less! (5)

Definition: less · charade

  • Fine → F F is the pencil-grading letter for fine, from the same table that gives H for hard
  • water jug → EWER an ewer is the wide-mouthed jug that stands with a basin on an old washstand

'No less!' is the surface's flourish and only the last word defines. The pedant's rule is the joke underneath: the word wanted is the one used of things that can be counted.

13 across HAIRNET

Cook's convenience: mop to take home (7)

Definition: Cook's convenience · charade

  • mop → HAIR a mop of curls is a thick unbrushed head of the stuff
  • to take home → NET take-home pay is what is left after tax — the net figure rather than the gross

'Mop' wants to be the thing you clean a floor with, and the colon makes the rest read as a shopping errand. Both halves are figures of speech instead.

14 across EXCITED

Roused, seeing old flame mentioned (7)

Definition: Roused · charade

  • old flame → EX an ex is the partner you used to have, and the two letters stand for a former anything
  • mentioned → CITED to cite a source is to mention it by name, the way a judgment quotes an earlier case

'Mentioned' looks like the standard instruction to listen for a sound-alike, and it is not: here it is a plain synonym, sitting after the two letters a solver meets constantly.

15 across BITTERN

Sullen Everyman finally gets the bird (7)

Definition: bird · charade + last letter

  • Sullen → BITTER bitter describes somebody soured by resentment, which is a sullen frame of mind
  • Everyman finally → N Everyman is the name this paper's setter signs, and its last letter is the one wanted

'Gets the bird' is an idiom for being booed off the stage, which is the trap. Only the last word defines, and it defines a species.

17 across TANK TOP

What has a turret but isn't armed? (4,3)

Definition: What has a turret but isn't armed? · cryptic definition

  • What has a turret → the surface wants a fighting vehicle, since a turret is the revolving gun housing on one — and the first half of the answer is that vehicle
  • but isn't armed? → armed changes sense between the two readings: carrying no weapons, and also having no arms, which is to say no sleeves

The whole joke lives in 'armed'. A sleeveless summer garment borrows an armoured vehicle's name, so it comes with the turret and none of the guns.

20 across CZECH

Language Bill's spoken (5)

Definition: Language · homophone

  • Bill → CZECH Americans ask a waiter for the check where the British ask for the bill, and said aloud that word is spelled quite differently

The capital B makes Bill look like a man's name, which is the misdirection. What you hear belongs to a country in central Europe rather than to a diner.

21 across ALARM BELL

Marble all shattered: this may be because of a drill (5,4)

Definition: this may be because of a drill · anagram

  • Marble all → MARBLEALL nine letters from the two words before the colon

A drill sounds like a power tool, especially standing next to shattered marble. It is the practice-evacuation kind, and the definition is everything after the colon.

22 across ANDRE AGASSI

A (as in grades) reassigned to sports star (5,6)

Definition: sports star · anagram

  • A (as in grades) → AASINGRADES the single letter plus the three words bracketed after it — eleven letters, the length required

The bracket looks like the setter explaining which A is meant, the one on a school report. It is really telling you that those words are letters too.

23 across ON HAND

Convenient, like an oven glove (2,4)

Definition: Convenient · double definition

  • Convenient → available at the moment it is wanted, close enough to reach for without going to fetch it
  • like an oven glove → read absolutely literally — a glove is worn over the fingers, so this says where it sits

The second half takes the phrase word by word, which is the joke, and a kitchen glove is the one object that answers both senses at the same time.

24 across SPEEDO

After upset, learned to wear outsize swimwear brand (6)

Definition: swimwear brand · container + reversal

  • outsize → OS OS is the clothing label for outsize, and these are the two letters that wrap round
  • learned → DEEP a deep thinker is a learned one — profound rather than shallow — and it goes inside

'After upset' reads as the aftermath of a row in the surface story. It is the instruction that turns the whole assembly round once the wrapping has been done.

2 down SINGIN' IN THE RAIN

Song from Spooner suggestin' Underwater Quasimodo? (6,2,3,4)

Definition: Song · spoonerism

  • Underwater Quasimodo → RINGIN' IN THE SEINE Quasimodo is Notre-Dame's bell-ringer, and Notre-Dame stands on an island in the Seine, so a submerged one would be pulling his ropes in that river
  • suggestin' Underwater Quasimodo? → SINGIN' IN THE RAIN swap the R at the front of the first word with the S at the front of the last, and the same sounds come out as an altogether wetter phrase

The italics dress 'Underwater Quasimodo' up as a title, but it is a picture to be described. An English speaker says Seine to rhyme with rain, which is what lets the swap land so neatly.

3 down DOWNTON

Sad to remain, ultimately, in storied Abbey (7)

Definition: storied Abbey · charade + last letter

  • Sad → DOWN the everyday word for low spirits — feeling down, a bit down in the mouth
  • to → TO taken at face value, the two letters written as they stand
  • remain, ultimately → N the closing letter of the verb before the comma

The capital A is the tell: this is a name rather than a monastery. 'Sad to remain' reads as one wistful phrase, and that is exactly what disguises it as three separate pieces.

4 down SINGLETON

One description of wearing a vest? (9)

Definition: One · charade

  • a vest → SINGLET a singlet is the sleeveless undershirt or running top that British shops sell as a vest
  • wearing → ON you have a thing on when you are wearing it

A vest here is the British sleeveless undergarment, not an American waistcoat. 'One description of' reads as a single phrase, and only that first word is doing the defining.

5 down LEARN

Study section of trochlear nerve (5)

Definition: Study · hidden word

  • trochlear nerve → LEARN five consecutive letters beginning six in, crossing the space into the second word

The trochlear nerve is one of the cranial nerves that swivel the eye — an anatomical phrase chosen for the run of letters straddling its two words, not for what it means.

6 down TRAFFIC

Trade in what may be used in jam-making (7)

Definition: Trade · double definition

  • Trade → the buying and selling of a commodity, and particularly the illicit kind — a dealer in arms or ivory
  • what may be used in jam-making → not fruit and sugar: the nose-to-tail queue of cars that makes the other sort of jam

Jam-making pulls the mind straight into a kitchen, and the answer belongs on a motorway. The setter keeps both halves in the same register so neither gives the other away.

7 down GONE WITH THE WIND

Classic movie, Swept Away? (4,4,3,4)

Definition: Classic movie · double definition

  • Classic movie → the 1939 Civil War epic with Vivien Leigh and Clark Gable, still among the most watched films ever made
  • Swept Away? → carried off by a gale and not seen again — the plain English meaning of the italicised phrase

The italics dress the second half as another film title, and there is one. Read it as ordinary English instead and it says exactly what a gale does to whatever is loose.

9 down ITCH

Yearning, in East End, to ride free (4)

Definition: Yearning · deletion

  • to ride free → HITCH to hitch a lift is to thumb a ride you do not pay for
  • East End → H the aitch the Cockney accent leaves off, and the letter that goes

'Free' belongs with 'ride' rather than with the yearning: the phrase to find is the unpaid lift, and the East End then gets at its first letter.

10 down GRAD

Such is primarily gowned, radiant, accepting diploma! (4)

Definition: Such is primarily gowned, radiant, accepting diploma! · &lit + first letters

  • gowned, radiant, accepting diploma → GRAD the opening letters of the four words, taken in the order they are written

Read straight, the clue is a photograph of one person at a ceremony; read again, those same words hand over their initials. Doing both jobs at once is what makes it an &lit.

14 down ESTRANGES

Alienates sergeants, foolishly (9)

Definition: Alienates · anagram

  • sergeants → SERGEANTS the nine letters of the rank, exactly the length the grid asks for

Nine letters sitting in plain sight beside a word saying they are out of order. The only real difficulty is that the definition looks just as shufflable as the fodder.

15 down BACK

Second tail (4)

Definition: Second · double definition

  • Second → to second a proposal at a meeting is to give it your support, the verb the chair waits to hear
  • tail → the tail of anything is its hindmost part, the end that comes last

Two words and nothing else, so there is nowhere for wordplay to hide. One is a verb of support, the other a noun for a rear end, and the answer covers both.

16 down ECHIDNA

Aussie animal had nice bananas (7)

Definition: Aussie animal · anagram

  • had nice → HADNICE seven letters from the two words before the instruction

The surface pictures a snack, and one word tells you otherwise. The two innocent little words in front of it are there for their letters, not for their sense.

18 down NAMASTE

Asian greeting in Vietnam, as teacher taking part (7)

Definition: Asian greeting · hidden word

  • Vietnam, as teacher → NAMASTE seven letters running on from halfway through the country, over the comma and into the word after next

A greeting concealed inside the name of a country it has nothing to do with: namaste is Sanskrit and belongs to India and Nepal, while the letters simply happen to sit here.

19 down PALS

Friends: small circle shrinking (4)

Definition: Friends · charade + reversal

  • small → S S is the size printed on a clothing label for small
  • circle → LAP one circuit of a running track is a lap

'Small circle' sounds like a definition of a tight group of friends, which is the disguise: they are two separate pieces, and the assembly turns round before it is written in.

21 down AHEAD

A teacher's coming! (5)

Definition: coming · charade

  • A → A the article is used as the single letter it already is
  • teacher → HEAD the head is what a British school calls the person in charge, short for head teacher

The apostrophe-s means 'is' in the surface story and does no work at all in the wordplay, where the two pieces simply sit side by side in the order given.
