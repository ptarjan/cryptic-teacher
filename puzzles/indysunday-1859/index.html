Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,859

Setter: Filbert · Published: Sunday 12 October 2025 · Difficulty: Tough (harder than 55% of the puzzles here) · Grid: 15×15

Across

1 across LAPDOG

Little tyke Beverly Hills cops shot from behind (6)

Definition: Little tyke · charade + reversal

  • Beverly Hills cops → LAPD the LAPD is the Los Angeles Police Department, the force of the city Beverly Hills is surrounded by
  • shot → OG a shot at something is a go at it, and this go is written the other way round

A tyke is also a naughty child, which is the reading the surface wants you to take. Filbert's police force comes straight off a cinema poster and hands over four letters.

4 across SCUPPERS

Scotch back in barrels which the drink escapes from? (8)

Definition: which the drink escapes from? · charade + last letter

  • Scotch → SCUPPER to scotch a rumour and to scupper a plan are the same move: both stop it dead
  • barrels → S only the rearmost letter of the word is wanted, and that is an S

Barrels are what drink escapes from in the surface, and sailors call the sea the drink. The word itself is not a container here: it gives up one letter and no more.

9 across SHORT IRON

Republican in Troon, his new golf club (5,4)

Definition: golf club · anagram

  • Republican → R R is the standard abbreviation for a member of the American party
  • Troon, his → TROONHIS eight more letters for the mix, and Troon is the Ayrshire town whose links host the Open

Royal Troon has held the Open ten times, so the surface reads as a story about a course and a player. Count the letters against the (5,4) and it turns into raw material.

11 across REEVE

Female ruff's beak (5)

Definition: Female ruff · double definition

  • Female ruff → the ornithological sense: the ruff is a wading bird whose male grows a collar of feathers in spring, and the hen has her own name
  • beak → the slang sense: in British usage the beak is the magistrate you are hauled up in front of

Two definitions with only an apostrophe holding them together, and the surface pretends a bird has a bill. The word lives on in Canada, where a reeve chairs a rural council.

12 across AFOREMENTIONED

Area supervisors note ID with alterations written above (14)

Definition: written above · charade + anagram

  • Area → A A is the standard abbreviation for area, in a formula or on a plan
  • supervisors → FOREMEN the foremen on a site are the workers who supervise the rest of it
  • note ID → TIONED six letters remade from the two short words

Fourteen letters and only six of them shuffle, so the count is the way in: the first eight are handed over straight. Two small words carry the whole of the fodder.

14 across EXPORT

Cautionary rating on English wine to send to France? (6)

Definition: send to France? · charade

  • English → E E is the usual abbreviation for English, as on a school timetable
  • Cautionary rating → X X was the British film certificate that barred anyone under sixteen, the warning label of its day
  • wine → PORT port is the fortified wine of the Douro, drunk after dinner

The X certificate was retired in 1982, replaced by the 18. 'Cautionary rating' points at that piece of British cinema history rather than at anything a wine merchant would say.

16 across GUNMETAL

Unsatisfied girl has bagged bronze for shooting (8)

Definition: bronze for shooting · container

  • girl → GAL gal is the informal spelling of the way some speakers say the word
  • Unsatisfied → UNMET a demand nobody has satisfied is an unmet one

'Bronze for shooting' wants to be an Olympic medal in the pistol event, and that is the trap: it is a metal here, not a placing.

18 across ESPOUSED

Did champion model speedos around university? (8)

Definition: Did champion · anagram

  • speedos → SPEEDOS seven letters for the mix, taken from the trunks the surface is wearing
  • university → U U is the standard abbreviation for university

'Did champion' is the definition and reads as a boast about a swimmer's record. Count the letters of the trunks against the (8) and the U has to be joining them.

20 across PRENUP

Contract groom listened to in court (6)

Definition: Contract · homophone + charade

  • groom → PREN a bird grooming its feathers is preening, and that sound opens the answer with a spelling of its own
  • in court → UP somebody appearing before a magistrate is up in court, or up before the beak

The surface has a bridegroom giving evidence, and neither half of it survives: one is a bird tidying its feathers, the other the idiom for standing before a magistrate.

23 across CARDINAL WOLSEY

Lord Chancellor is wary called on mobile (8,6)

Definition: Lord Chancellor · anagram

  • is wary called on → ISWARYCALLEDON fourteen letters, the whole middle of the clue going into the mix

The surface is a phone call to a nervous minister, and one word at the end turns all of it into raw material. Count the letters against the (8,6) before starting.

26 across ADAGE

Saw time on notice (5)

Definition: Saw · charade

  • notice → AD an ad in the paper is a notice placed there to be read
  • time → AGE the age of steam is the time of steam, a stretch of history

'Saw' is a noun here rather than the past tense of see, and that single switch is the whole difficulty of a three-word clue.

27 across GO WALKIES

With old Golf pulled over, people needing the AA vanish (2,7)

Definition: vanish · charade + reversal

  • With old Golf pulled over → GOW Golf is G in the radio alphabet, old is O and with is W, and the three are then turned round
  • people needing the AA → ALKIES alkies are alcoholics, and the AA they need is Alcoholics Anonymous rather than the breakdown service

The Golf and the AA both belong to the road, and neither is doing motoring work: one is a letter of the radio alphabet, the other a fellowship of drinkers.

28 across MATADORA

Bullfighter with no cojones a bit yellow, mother admits (8)

Definition: Bullfighter with no cojones · container + charade

  • mother → MA ma is the nursery word for a mother
  • a bit → ATAD a tad late and a bit late are the same amount of late
  • yellow → OR in heraldry or is gold, which on a shield is painted yellow

The rude aside is the definition: Spanish marks a female bullfighter by changing the ending, and cojones is Filbert's way of saying which ending is wanted.

29 across ORIGIN

Doctor home after round where he started (6)

Definition: where he started · charade

  • round → O a round drawn on paper is the letter O
  • Doctor → RIG to doctor a result and to rig it are the same piece of cheating
  • home → IN somebody who is at home is in

'Doctor' opens the clue like a title and is really the verb for tampering. The definition is the last three words, which read like a question and are not one.

Down

1 down LASSA FEVER

African bug left, because harmless? Not quite so (5,5)

Definition: African bug · charade + deletion

  • left → L L is the standard abbreviation for left, as in a stage direction
  • because → AS as it was raining and because it was raining say the same thing
  • harmless → SAFE something harmless is safe to handle, eat or say
  • so → VER so tired means very tired, and the word arrives one letter short

'Harmless? Not quite so' reads as somebody correcting themselves about a danger. It is really two of the four pieces, plus the instruction that trims the last of them.

2 down PHOTO OP

Press engagement quiet band keeps to (5,2)

Definition: Press engagement · charade + container

  • quiet → P p is the musician's mark for piano, the instruction to play softly
  • band → HOOP a hoop is a band of wood or metal, round a barrel or a skirt
  • to → TO the little word is taken at face value and hands over both its letters

'Press engagement quiet' reads as a hush falling over a newsroom. Filbert's band is not the kind that plays, and the smallest word in the clue is doing real letter work.

3 down OUTRE

Public touching not the done thing (5)

Definition: not the done thing · charade

  • Public → OUT a secret made public is out, and a person who is out has declared themselves openly
  • touching → RE re means concerning: a memo touching on expenses is a memo re expenses

Two everyday words sit end to end with nothing to signal the join. French spells it with an accent on the E; the grid, like most English writing, drops it.

5 down CONSTRUE

Infer fraud's honest (8)

Definition: Infer · charade

  • fraud's → CONS a con is a swindle, and the apostrophe s of the surface hands over the fourth letter
  • honest → TRUE a true friend is an honest one, not pretending to be anything else

Three words, no indicator and no joining word: the first is the definition and everything after it is spelling. The possessive belongs to the wordplay, not to the grammar.

6 down PERFORMER

Artiste first noted after agent given raise (9)

Definition: Artiste · charade + reversal

  • agent → PER an agent who acts for you is your rep, and that rep is then read back up the column
  • first → FORMER the former of two things is the first one mentioned

The surface has an agent winning a client a pay rise, and none of it survives. 'First' is the adjective for the earlier of two, not the front of anything.

7 down EVEREST

At any time, cryptic set difficult challenge (7)

Definition: difficult challenge · charade + anagram

  • At any time → EVER if you have ever done it, you have done it at any time at all
  • set → EST the three letters of the word arrive in a different order

'Cryptic set' looks like a compiler at work on a grid, and is really an instruction with its three letters attached. The definition is a mountain used as a measure.

8 down SEEK

Comb for long-haired adherent caught (4)

Definition: Comb for · homophone

  • long-haired adherent → SEEK a Sikh follows the religion founded in the Punjab, whose members leave their hair uncut, and the name is said this way

'Long-haired adherent' is a straight description rather than a joke, and the whole mechanism is in the ear: one sound with two quite different spellings.

10 down REEL

Drive off without parking to keep looping around (4)

Definition: keep looping around · deletion

  • Drive off → REPEL to repel an attacker is to drive them off
  • parking → P P is the letter on the road sign for a car park, and it is the letter that leaves

Every word of the surface belongs to a car: drive off, parking, looping around. Only the last three are the definition, and the rest is one word losing a letter.

13 down CLAP EYES ON

See incomplete clone, taking copy exactly (4,4,2)

Definition: See · deletion + charade + container

  • clone → CLON the word arrives one letter short, its final E left off
  • copy → APE to ape somebody is to copy their manner
  • exactly → YES agreeing with somebody by saying exactly is agreeing by saying yes

The surface is a laboratory story about a half-grown clone, and every word of it is doing letter work instead. 'Exactly' is the agreeing exclamation, not the adverb.

15 down ROUNDHEAD

Harry looked at text from Spooner, Charles's old foe (9)

Definition: Charles's old foe · spoonerism

  • Harry → HOUND to harry somebody is to hound them, chasing and pestering without let-up
  • looked at text → READ looking at text is reading it
  • Harry looked at text from Spooner → ROUNDHEAD swap the sounds the two words start with and the pair comes out as the grid entry

Two ordinary verbs, and the man supplying the instruction was an Oxford don whose slips of the tongue became a noun. Note the capital H: it is misdirection, not a name.

17 down TEENAGER

One staying out later, probably after 12? (8)

Definition: One staying out later, probably after 12? · cryptic definition

  • One staying out later → the reading the surface pushes: somebody out past midnight, later than the hour the rest of the clue seems about to name
  • probably after 12? → the reading meant: 12 is an age, not an hour, and the years that come after it are the ones being counted

Everything turns on what 12 is. Read as a clock it makes the clue a curfew story; read as an age it is the boundary the answer begins at, and 'later' means later than they used to.

19 down PEASANT

Poor worker nice giving away pound (7)

Definition: Poor worker · deletion

  • nice → PLEASANT a nice afternoon is a pleasant one
  • pound → L the pound sign is a curly capital L, from the Latin libra

'Poor worker' looks like a judgement on somebody's effort and is really a description of their station: the word names a rank in the old land system.

21 down NESTING

Removing eggs, e.g. nits spread after November (7)

Definition: Removing eggs · charade + anagram

  • November → N November is the international radio alphabet's word for N
  • e.g. nits → ESTING six letters for the mix, the two of the abbreviation among them

'E.g.' looks like it is introducing an example and is not: its two letters are fodder like any others, which is the hardest thing to spot here.

22 down BLOW

Knock black and blue (4)

Definition: Knock · charade

  • black → B B is the abbreviation for black, on a pencil and on a chess scoresheet
  • blue → LOW somebody feeling blue is feeling low, and both mean downhearted

'Black and blue' is the phrase for bruised, which is what a knock leaves behind, so the surface and the definition tell one story in two vocabularies.

24 down OILER

Cargo ship's steam generator failing to start (5)

Definition: Cargo ship · deletion

  • steam generator → BOILER a boiler is what raises steam, in a ship's engine room or a house

'Cargo ship's steam generator' reads as one item on a ship's inventory and is two things: the definition stops after the second word, and the rest is a word losing its head.

25 down FARM

House with a lot of land, miles and miles (4)

Definition: House with a lot of land · charade

  • miles → FAR somewhere miles away is somewhere far away, the distance measured loosely
  • miles → M m is the abbreviation printed on a road sign for the unit

One word does two different jobs in a row, which is the joke of the clue: the same four letters give a word for a long way and then a unit of measurement.
