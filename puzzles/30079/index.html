Across

1 across BENEFIT

Advantage of computerised identikit picture when pursuing crook virtually (7)

Definition: Advantage · charade + deletion

  • crook → BEN a crook is BENT; 'virtually' (= almost) stops it one letter short
  • computerised identikit picture → EFIT an E-fit is the digital identikit UK police issue

'Virtually' means almost, and it is doing surgery rather than describing: it docks the final letter. An E-fit is the digital identikit UK police issue, hidden here behind ordinary-sounding police work.

5 across CHEETAH

Spotted predator – shark is caught (7)

Definition: Spotted predator · homophone

  • shark → CHEETAH a shark is a CHEATER, which 'caught' (overheard) sounds like CHEETAH

'Caught' means caught by the ear. The shark plays cards — a cheater — and saying it aloud releases the big cat.

9 across LADEN

Young male nurse is loaded (5)

Definition: loaded · charade

  • Young male → LAD a lad
  • nurse → EN Enrolled Nurse

'Young male nurse' begs to be read as one job title — resist. EN, the old Enrolled Nurse grade, is a crossword regular.

10 across TARANTULA

Tea, cream – not full fat – oddly it’s a Cambodian delicacy (9)

Definition: a Cambodian delicacy · alternate letters

  • Tea, cream – not full fat → TARANTULA its odd letters — first, third, fifth and so on, punctuation ignored

Run through 'Tea, cream – not full fat' and take every odd-numbered letter, punctuation ignored. Fried tarantula genuinely is Cambodian street food.

11 across WATERMELON

Call Musk with Washington opening that ought to bear fruit (10)

Definition: fruit · charade

  • Washington → WA the state's postal code; 'opening' sends it to the front
  • Call → TERM to call something is to term it
  • Musk → ELON first name of the Tesla owner

WA is clued last but 'opening' promotes it to the front of the answer. The Musk-in-Washington surface is pure news-cycle noise.

12 across AGA

Two Americans holding back on driving range (3)

Definition: range · container + last letter

  • Two Americans → AA A = American, twice — one either side
  • driving → G its back — the last letter

'Driving range' wants to be golf — split it. An Aga is the great British kitchen range, and A for American serves twice.

14 across BILLIE EILISH

Pop singer’s ill – lie about swallowing pill in trashed Ibis hotel (6,6)

Definition: Pop singer · anagram

  • ill – lie → ILLLIE churned by 'about'
  • pill → E an E — an ecstasy tablet
  • Ibis → IBIS the budget hotel chain, trashed
  • hotel → H H in the phonetic alphabet

Everything goes in one pot: ILL, LIE, an E — the clubber's pill — and IBIS with hotel's phonetic H. The trashed-hotel surface suits the pop-star story.

18 across TRACEY ULLMAN

TV star barking cruelly at crew (6.6)

Definition: TV star · anagram

  • cruelly at → CRUELLYAT nine letters of fodder
  • crew → MAN to crew a ship is to man it

'Barking', as in barking mad, does the mixing — but 'crew' must first become MAN, to crew a ship being to man it. One synonym step hides inside the anagram.

21 across ARC

Bow and scrape while retreating to some extent (3)

Definition: Bow · hidden word + reversal

  • scrape → ARC hiding backwards in sCRApe

'Bow and scrape' reads as one grovelling idiom, but only 'scrape' carries the answer — read it in retreat. 'To some extent' is the classic hidden-word flag.

22 across THINK PIECE

Hunk needing reason to start op-ed? (5,5)

Definition: op-ed? · charade

  • reason → THINK 'to start' sends it to the front
  • Hunk → PIECE a hunk of bread is a piece

Like 8 down, the charade arrives out of order: 'to start' sends the reason to the front of the hunk.

25 across PUTREFIED

Corrupt state maybe does if cutting back (9)

Definition: Corrupt · charade + container + reversal

  • state → PUT to state a case is to put it
  • maybe does → DEER does are deer — 'maybe' flags the definition by example
  • if → IF cuts into DEER before everything reverses

'Maybe' flags does as one example of DEER. IF cuts in, the whole DEIFER assembly reverses, and PUT — to state a case is to put it — leads off.

26 across ISLET

Initially in sonatas, Liszt enjoyed this minor key (5)

Definition: minor key · first letters

  • in sonatas, Liszt enjoyed this → ISLET the first letters of the five words

'Initially' harvests the first letters of five words. The definition is the delight: a key, as in the Florida Keys, is a small island, so a minor key is no music at all.

27 across DECLARE

Expensive – taking 150 Euros, say (7)

Definition: say · charade + container

  • Expensive → DEAR it takes the numerals in
  • 150 → CL in Roman numerals
  • Euros → E E for euro, closing the word

The throwaway 'say' is the definition — to say is to declare. DEAR takes Roman-numeral CL, and the euro's single E closes the word.

28 across RETIRED

In bed, weary, drinking wine – quite the opposite (7)

Definition: In bed · container

  • weary → TIRE as a verb — to weary is to tire
  • wine → RED it ends up doing the drinking

'Quite the opposite' is an instruction, not a comment: it inverts the container the clue has just described, so the drinker becomes the drunk. The clue and its answer describe the same sleepy evening.

Down

1 down BYLAWS

On vacation Bradley Walsh ultimately ignored resort’s in-house rules? (6)

Definition: in-house rules? · charade + outer letters + deletion + anagram

  • Bradley → BY 'on vacation' vacates the word, keeping only B(radle)Y
  • Walsh → WALS 'ultimately ignored' drops the last letter; 'resort' then re-sorts WALS into LAWS

Bradley Walsh of The Chase is pure misdirection. 'On vacation' vacates a word — only the outside letters stay — and 'resort' quietly re-sorts what is left of Walsh.

2 down NUDITY

Finally – given you identified graffiti artist – Banksy’s in exposed state (6)

Definition: exposed state · last letters

  • given you identified graffiti artist – Banksy → NUDITY the final letters of each of the six words

'Finally' governs all six words at once — each donates its last letter. The dashes fence the fodder off from the definition at the end.

3 down FUND-RAISER

In France and in Germany, shaver sounded 1 across? (4-6)

Definition: 1 across? · charade + homophone

  • In France → F F is the international vehicle code for France
  • and → UND 'and' as said in Germany
  • shaver → RAISER a shaver is a razor, which 'sounded' gives RAISER

Two languages in one charade: France's international car-sticker F, then 'and' as a German would say it. Say RAISER aloud and the shaver appears.

4 down TITHE

Tax article without it (5)

Definition: Tax · container

  • article → THE the definite article
  • it → IT sits inside THE

'Without' wears its old meaning of 'outside': THE stands without IT rather than lacking it. Three tiny words, one neat trap.

5 down CORPOREAL

My friend entertains men with essentially new material (9)

Definition: material · charade + container + middle letter

  • My → COR 'cor!' and 'my!' are the same exclamation
  • friend → PAL it does the entertaining
  • men → OR other ranks — the army's men
  • new → E the essential middle of nEw

COR and 'my!' are both exclamations of surprise, and OR is the army's 'other ranks' — two conventions worth banking. PAL swallows them both.

6 down EONS

Ages in prison’s manure shed (4)

Definition: Ages · deletion

  • prison’s → DUNGEONS a dungeon is a prison; the possessive S stays on
  • manure → DUNG shed from the front of DUNGEONS

The prison is a DUNGEON — keep its possessive S — and shedding the manure from its front leaves the answer. No outbuilding involved.

7 down TSUNAMIS

A film star cycles and waves (8)

Definition: waves · charade + cycling

  • A → A itself
  • film → MIST a film over the eyes is a mist
  • star → SUN our nearest star

Cycling moves letters round without changing their order — the tail comes to the front, like cutting a deck of cards. It is rarer than a reversal and easy to miss, and 'cycles' is its usual signal.

8 down HEADACHE

Pine nut’s beginning to be problem (8)

Definition: problem · charade

  • nut → HEAD slang for the head; it begins the answer
  • Pine → ACHE to pine is to ache

The order is the trick: 'nut's beginning' announces that HEAD starts the answer, so the ACHE clued first goes second.

13 down DISAPPOINT

School paid to spin results in mock (10)

Definition: mock · anagram

  • paid to spin → PAIDTOSPIN anagram fodder — ten letters, schooled into shape

'School' is the anagram indicator: schooling knocks raw material into shape. 'Mock' defines the answer in its old sense of deceiving hopes.

15 down LAUGH LINE

Conducted lessons in English, with students covering tenses – one might appear with a smile (5,4)

Definition: one might appear with a smile · charade + substitution

  • Conducted lessons → TAUGHT to conduct lessons is to teach
  • in English → INE IN plus E for English
  • students → LL two learner-plate Ls
  • tenses → TT T = tense; both Ts of TAUGHT-IN-E get covered

'Covering' as in covering someone's shift: the student Ls stand in for the two tense Ts of TAUGHT IN E, and the wrinkle appears.

16 down STRAPPED

Leaves power drills on the counter, securely fastened (8)

Definition: securely fastened · container + reversal

  • Leaves → DEPARTS a verb here, not foliage
  • power → P drilled into the middle

P for power 'drills' into DEPARTS, and 'on the counter' — running against the flow — turns the whole assembly around.

17 down NARCOTIC

Sedative not applicable on piercing in the middle of the ear (8)

Definition: Sedative · charade + middle letters

  • not applicable → NA the form-filler's abbreviation
  • piercing → RC its two middle letters: pie-RC-ing
  • of the ear → OTIC the anatomical adjective

PIERCING surrenders its exact centre, and OTIC — 'of the ear' — turns an ear-piercing surface into anatomy.

19 down CELLAR

Stock of wine merchant delivered (6)

Definition: Stock of wine · homophone

  • merchant → CELLAR a merchant is a SELLER; 'delivered' aloud it becomes CELLAR

'Delivered' means spoken, as a line is delivered — so it is the wine merchant's homophone that goes in the grid, not the merchant.

20 down HEATED

Gossipy magazine journalist is excited (6)

Definition: excited · charade

  • Gossipy magazine → HEAT the UK celebrity weekly
  • journalist → ED the editor

Heat is Britain's celebrity-gossip weekly and ED crosswordland's hardest-working journalist. Two conventions, one join.

23 down NADIR

Lowest point of brain drain with regular emigration climbing (5)

Definition: Lowest point · alternate letters + reversal

  • brain drain → RIDAN what remains — b(R)a(I)n(D)r(A)i(N) — once every other letter emigrates

'Regular emigration' is the joke: every other letter of BRAIN DRAIN emigrates, and what stays behind climbs the down entry.

24 down MEGA

Excellent, for example, to be Morocco-bound (4)

Definition: Excellent · container

  • for example → EG e.g.
  • Morocco → MA the international vehicle code; it does the binding

'Morocco-bound' looks like travel but means bound in morocco, the goatskin leather of fine bookbinding — so Morocco's car-code MA wraps the example.
