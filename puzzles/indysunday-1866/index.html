Across

1 across SPEAKING

Giving lecture, do numbers that prime divides (8)

Definition: Giving lecture · container

Giving lecture -> SPEAKING: the sense of the word that means addressing an audience from a platform, not merely talking to somebody.

  • do numbers → SING a performer does numbers on stage, the numbers being the songs in the set
  • prime → PEAK an athlete in their prime is at their peak
  • divides — A thing that divides comes down in the middle of what it lands on, so the piece named before it splits open and the other one sits in the gap.

The clue is dressed up as arithmetic, and none of it is maths: the numbers are songs and the prime is a high point of form.

6 across ENCAMP

Get ready for a night out? (6)

Definition: Get ready for a night out? · cryptic definition

Get ready for a night out? -> ENCAMP: to encamp is to pitch a tent and settle in, so preparing for a night outdoors is the whole of it.

  • a night out → the surface sells an evening in town, dinner and a show, the sort of night you dress up for
  • Get ready → the setter means a night spent outdoors under canvas, and preparing for one means pitching the tents before dark

There is no wordplay to pick apart here: the whole clue is one definition, and all of the work is in which kind of night out is meant.

9 across EUCLID

Mathematician's prompt returning cap (6)

Definition: Mathematician · reversal + charade

Mathematician -> EUCLID: the Greek geometer whose Elements set out the axioms schools still teach, so the definition names his trade and leaves you the era.

  • prompt → EUC a prompt is the cue whispered to an actor who has dried, and it goes in backwards
  • cap → LID the cap on a jar is its lid
  • returning — Something returning comes back the way it went, so the three letters just named are written into the row the other way round.

'Prompt' looks like the adjective meaning punctual and is a theatre noun instead, the word fed from the wings to an actor who has forgotten a line.

10 across AMORALLY

Mother returned by 1 without thinking what might be wrong (8)

Definition: without thinking what might be wrong · reversal + charade

without thinking what might be wrong -> AMORALLY: acting with no sense of right and evil at all, which is different from knowingly behaving badly.

  • Mother → AM mother shortens to MA, and the next word sends those two letters the other way
  • by 1 → ORALLY the bare number points at 1 across, and doing something by that is doing it in speech rather than in writing
  • returned — A word returned has gone back where it came from, and going back means reading its two letters the other way along the row.

A number on its own in a clue points at another entry, so this one cannot be finished until 1 across is in place - a neat way of locking two answers together.

11 across BUTTERFLY KISS

Several quick strokes of the lash as foreplay? (9,4)

Definition: Several quick strokes of the lash as foreplay? · cryptic definition

Fluttering your eyelashes against someone's skin is a butterfly kiss, named for the beating wings, so the lash is an eyelash and the strokes are the flutter.

  • strokes of the lash → the surface reading is a whipping, in which a lash is a whip and the strokes are blows
  • Several quick → the setter means an eyelash instead, fluttered rapidly against a partner's cheek, which is why they come several at a time

No letters change hands anywhere in this one: the whole clue is the definition, and the only job is working out which sort of lash is meant.

12 across TAKE FRIGHT

Eccentric freak caught by mean spook (4,6)

Definition: spook · anagram + container

spook -> TAKE FRIGHT: a horse that spooks takes fright, bolting at a sudden noise, so both work as verbs for a creature startled into flight.

  • mean → TIGHT a mean person is tight with money, and the word splits after its first letter to make room
  • freak → AKEFR the five letters of freak, shaken into a new order before they go in
  • Eccentric — An eccentric does not behave in the expected order, and letters told to behave that way come out in an order nobody expects.
  • caught by — Being caught by something means being held inside it, so the shuffled letters end up trapped between the letters of the word beside them.

'Eccentric freak' reads as one phrase describing an oddball, and only one of those two words is fodder. The other is the instruction telling you what to do with it.

15 across ROOT

Bit of tooth decay multiplied internally (4)

Definition: Bit of tooth · container

Bit of tooth -> ROOT: the part below the gumline that anchors a tooth in the jaw, which is what a dentist means by a root canal.

  • decay → ROT a tooth or a fence post left to decay will rot
  • multiplied internally → O the middle letter of that three-letter word turns up a second time
  • multiplied internally — Multiplying makes more of a thing, and doing it internally means the letter standing in the middle of the word gets written twice.

'Tooth decay' offers itself as a single idea and the clue wants the two words prised apart, one for the definition and one for the letters.

16 across EMIT

Let out on reversal of prison sentence (4)

Definition: Let out · reversal

Let out -> EMIT: a chimney that emits smoke lets it out, the sense of releasing a substance rather than freeing a person.

  • prison sentence → EMIT a stretch inside is time, and those four letters are read from the back
  • reversal of — A reversal turns letters end for end, so the phrase named next is written into the row starting from its final letter.

Every word of the surface belongs in a jail except the definition, which is about giving something off: light, heat or a smell.

17 across GOBSMACKED

In shock, return edition full of nasty sex (10)

Definition: In shock · charade + container

In shock -> GOBSMACKED: utterly astonished, as though somebody had slapped you in the mouth, the British slang for the moment speech fails you.

  • return → GOBACK to return somewhere is to go back there, two short words run together
  • edition → ED ED is the standard abbreviation for edition, as on a title page
  • nasty sex → SM SM is the usual shorthand for sadomasochism
  • full of — A thing full of something has it inside, so the pair of letters named at the end goes within what has been built already.

'Return' looks like an instruction to reverse something and is only a word to translate. Being gobsmacked is being struck in the gob, the mouth.

19 across SEVENTH HEAVEN

Small flat subsequently hosts have converted, a delightful place (7,6)

Definition: a delightful place · charade + anagram + container

a delightful place -> SEVENTH HEAVEN: the phrase names a state of perfect happiness, which the definition treats as somewhere a person can be in.

  • Small → S S is the clothing-label abbreviation for small
  • flat → EVEN a flat surface is an even one, level all the way across
  • subsequently → THEN what happens subsequently happens then, and the word splits after two letters
  • have → HEAV the four letters of have, rearranged before they move in
  • hosts — A host takes guests into the house, so the word doing the hosting opens up and keeps the other letters inside it.
  • converted — Converting turns a thing into something else made of the same stuff, which for four letters means the same four in a different arrangement.

'Small flat' is an estate agent's phrase and the two words are clued separately. The place itself is the topmost of the seven heavens, the one nearest God.

23 across DIASPORA

People scattered seeing snake interrupt radio broadcast (8)

Definition: People scattered · anagram + container

People scattered -> DIASPORA: Greek for a sowing abroad, used of a population dispersed from its homeland and settled among others.

  • radio → DIORA the five letters of radio, sown into a new order
  • snake → ASP the asp is the small Egyptian viper that killed Cleopatra
  • interrupt — To interrupt is to cut in while something is already under way, so these three letters land in the middle of the word rather than beside it.
  • broadcast — Broadcasting was sowing seed by hand across a field long before it meant radio, so a word broadcast has its letters thrown about.

'Radio broadcast' reads as one thing and is two: a word to shuffle, and the instruction to shuffle it. The old farming sense of broadcast is what makes the pun work.

24 across DARING

Bold sound of operatic cycle in German (6)

Definition: Bold · homophone

Bold -> DARING: a daring raid is a bold one, both words describing a willingness to take a risk that a careful person would refuse.

  • operatic cycle → RING Wagner wrote four operas meant to be staged in sequence, known together as the Ring
  • in German → DARING German puts DER in front of a masculine noun for 'the', and the two words said one after the other run into a single English one
  • sound of — The sound of a phrase is what the ear receives rather than what the page shows, so it is spelled the way it lands, not the way it is written.

Der Ring des Nibelungen is the cycle in question, and the article in front of it supplies the first syllable. The whole answer arrives through the ear.

25 across REHASH

Claire has homework including AI's thoughts on topic? (6)

Definition: AI's thoughts on topic · hidden word

AI's thoughts on topic -> REHASH: a rehash is old material served again with a fresh surface, which is what a machine trained on other people's writing returns.

  • Claire has homework → REHASH six letters in a row, beginning inside the name and stopping just inside the last word
  • including — Including means the words on either side are carrying something within them, so the letters wanted are already written out in order.

The definition is the joke: what a chatbot hands you is old material warmed up. Nothing has to be built, because the letters are already sitting in the clue.

26 across NOWADAYS

A lack of banknotes, say, circulating currently (8)

Definition: currently · charade + anagram

currently -> NOWADAYS: in the present age, the stretch of years we are living through, rather than at this exact moment.

  • A lack of → NO a lack of something is no something at all
  • banknotes → WAD a thick fold of banknotes is a wad of them
  • say → AYS the three letters of say, tumbled into a new order
  • circulating — Something circulating is going round and round, and letters sent round like that do not come back in the order they set off in.

Banknotes circulating is the surface picture, and the word actually being circulated is the little one you would skate past: 'say' is fodder, not the usual for-example marker.

2 down PLUMB

Very desirable bachelor perfectly erect (5)

Definition: perfectly erect · charade

perfectly erect -> PLUMB: a builder's plumb line hangs true from a lead weight, so plumb means exactly vertical rather than just upright.

  • Very desirable → PLUM a plum job or a plum role is the one everybody wants
  • bachelor → B B is the standard abbreviation for bachelor, as in a BA degree

The surface reads as a personal ad, and the answer belongs on a building site: nothing about the man survives into the meaning.

3 down ALLOTMENT

Every guy in first set of books back for novelist's growing plot (9)

Definition: growing plot · container + charade + last letter

growing plot -> ALLOTMENT: a small parcel of land let out to a household for vegetables, so the plot is soil rather than storyline.

  • Every → ALL every one of a group is all of them
  • first set of books → OT the Old Testament is the first half of the Bible and shortens to two letters
  • guy → MEN every guy taken together makes a plural, and the testament drops in between the two words
  • novelist's → T the letter novelist finishes on
  • in — Guys who are in a book are inside it, so the letters of the book go between the letters of the guys rather than beside them.
  • back — The back of a word is its far end, so only the letter the following word finishes with is wanted.

Put next to a novelist, 'plot' sounds like the plot of a book. An allotment is a rented patch of council land, and the novelist contributes one letter.

4 down INDIE

Islands that are East or West, mostly free from big business (5)

Definition: free from big business · deletion

free from big business -> INDIE: a label, band or film made outside the major corporations is called indie, the trade word for working unbacked.

  • Islands that are East or West → INDIES one group of these islands lies east and the other west, and both go by the same six-letter plural, which is one letter too long
  • mostly — Mostly a word is nearly all of it, which in a crossword means everything except the letter at the end - here a final S.

East and West both point at the same six letters, which is the whole joke of the surface. The answer is short for independent.

5 down GOAT-FIG

Wild fruit tree in western state present to the north (4-3)

Definition: Wild fruit tree · charade + reversal

Wild fruit tree -> GOAT-FIG: the uncultivated fig of the Mediterranean, so named because its small hard crop is fit for livestock rather than people.

  • western state → GOA Goa is a state on the west coast of India, once a Portuguese colony
  • present → TFIG a present is a gift, and those four letters climb the grid instead of descending it
  • to the north — A down entry is written from the top downwards, so a piece sent north goes in the other way, its last letter first.

'Western state' wants to be Oregon or Nevada until you count the letters. The tree is the wild Mediterranean fig, whose fruit is left to the goats.

6 down ECOSYSTEM

Living arrangement snug during English spring (9)

Definition: Living arrangement · container

Living arrangement -> ECOSYSTEM: the plants, animals and habitat of a place taken as one working unit, which is how living things arrange themselves.

  • English → E E is the usual single-letter abbreviation for English
  • snug → COSY a snug room and a cosy one are the same warm idea
  • spring → STEM to spring from something is to stem from it, both meaning to originate there
  • during — What happens during something else falls inside it rather than before or after, so the word just clued is planted in the middle.

'English spring' looks like weather, and 'spring' is a verb here, the one hiding in 'stems from'. The arrangement is a community of organisms, not a flatshare.

7 down COATI

Organised action saving North American mammal (5)

Definition: American mammal · anagram + deletion

American mammal -> COATI: a long-snouted, ring-tailed relative of the raccoon from the New World tropics, placed here by continent alone.

  • action → ACTIO action supplies the letters, minus the one the next word holds back
  • North → N N is the compass abbreviation for north, and this is the letter that never makes it in
  • Organised — Something being organised is being put into an order it did not have, so the letters handed over are free to be rearranged.
  • saving — Saving here means excepting, the sense in 'all saving one', so the letter named next is held back from the mix.

'Saving North' reads as a rescue mission and is a subtraction, and 'Organised action' as a well-run campaign. Neither phrase means what the surface says.

8 down MILESTONE

Road sign, in case you should have motorway ahead (9)

Definition: Road sign · charade

Road sign -> MILESTONE: a marker set at the roadside telling travellers how far they have come, which is a sign by a road doing its job.

  • motorway → MI the M1 is Britain's oldest long-distance motorway, and its number doubles as a letter
  • in case → LEST lest means in case something bad happens, as in 'lest we forget'
  • you → ONE the formal impersonal pronoun stands in for you: one does not do that sort of thing
  • ahead — Whatever is ahead of the rest goes in front, so the piece named beside it opens the answer instead of trailing at the end.

The motorway gets in by shape rather than by sound, the digit borrowed as a capital I. Milestones were the original road signs, cut stones counting down to the next town.

13 down ARMISTICE

Peace - Crimea's flourishing with it (9)

Definition: Peace · anagram

Peace -> ARMISTICE: an agreed halt to the shooting, the truce signed while a treaty is still being negotiated, so peace in its narrow military sense.

  • Crimea's → CRIMEAS seven letters, the S of the apostrophe included, all of them fodder
  • it → IT two more letters join the pile before the shuffle
  • flourishing — To flourish something is to wave it about, and letters waved about like that do not settle back in the order they were given.

The dash after the first word is standing in for an equals sign, and the Crimean War is a red herring: Crimea is here only for its letters.

14 down ROGAN JOSH

Meat curry, good parts horse rib (5,4)

Definition: Meat curry · container + charade

Meat curry -> ROGAN JOSH: a slow-cooked lamb dish from Kashmir in a deep red sauce, so the definition gives the category and leaves the animal to you.

  • horse → ROAN a roan is a horse with white hairs mixed through its coat
  • good → G G is the standard abbreviation for good, as on a school report
  • rib → JOSH to rib someone is to josh them, teasing without malice
  • parts — To part two things is to come between them, so the letter named beforehand pushes into the middle of the word that follows.

The dish is a Kashmiri lamb curry, red from mild chillies rather than heat. 'Good parts horse rib' is a butcher's window and no part of the recipe.

15 down RECOVERED

Charlie finished cutting grass, green again after drought? (9)

Definition: green again after drought · charade + container

green again after drought -> RECOVERED: a lawn burnt brown and then come back is said to have recovered, the word used of anything returning to health.

  • grass → REED a reed is the tall grass that grows in wet ground, and it opens after two letters
  • Charlie → C Charlie is C in the radio alphabet, alongside Alpha and Bravo
  • finished → OVER when a match is finished it is over
  • cutting — Cutting into something goes through the middle of it with a blade, so the letters just assembled slice into the word that follows.

'Charlie' is the radio alphabet rather than a man, and 'cutting grass' sounds like mowing the lawn, which is the very picture the definition is painting.

18 down BAHRAIN

GP's location that's unsatisfactory and wet (7)

Definition: GP's location · charade

GP's location -> BAHRAIN: this small Gulf island state hosts a round of the Formula One world championship, so a Grand Prix is held there each year.

  • that's unsatisfactory → BAH the noise of contempt you make when something is no good, the one Scrooge is famous for
  • wet → RAIN what makes a day a wet one

'GP' sends you straight to a doctor's surgery, and the surface keeps you there with a grumble about the weather. The capital letters are the tell.

20 down VISTA

Landscape remains in a box upside-down (5)

Definition: Landscape · container + reversal

Landscape -> VISTA: a long view of the countryside, usually framed by trees or buildings, so it is scenery as seen rather than as painted.

  • a box → VTA the box is the television, so this is A and TV, climbing the grid rather than descending
  • remains → IS he remains happy means he is happy, so the verb shrinks to two letters
  • in — What sits in something sits among its letters, so the short word just clued is planted inside the other one.
  • upside-down — A down entry runs from the top of the grid downwards, so anything upside-down is written into it from the bottom up.

'Remains' looks like a noun - ashes, ruins, what is left behind - and it is the verb meaning stays. The box is the old British nickname for the television.

21 down ENDOW

Provide means of support for heel that hurts (5)

Definition: Provide means of support · charade

Provide means of support -> ENDOW: to endow a university chair or a hospital bed is to settle money on it so it keeps going unaided.

  • heel → END the heel of a loaf is the crusty end of it
  • that hurts → OW the two letters you shout when something stings

The surface is a complaint about a shoe, and neither half of the wordplay is about feet: one is bread and the other is a noise.

22 down NINNY

Silly hotel apparently competing with the Plaza? (5)

Definition: Silly · container

Silly -> NINNY: a ninny is a foolish, feeble creature, the mild playground insult for someone behaving daftly rather than someone genuinely stupid.

  • apparently competing with the Plaza → NY the Plaza is the grand hotel on Central Park, so a rival to it stands in New York, whose two letters go round the outside
  • hotel → INN a small hotel is an inn

No word here says 'inside': the geography does it, because a hotel taking on the Plaza has to be in the same city.
