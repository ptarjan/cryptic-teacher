Across

1 across PUNCTUAL

Partner secures uncut supply just in time (8)

Definition: just in time · container + anagram

  • Partner → PAL your partner in crime is your pal, and these three letters open up to take another piece
  • uncut → UNCTU the five letters of UNCUT in a new order, dropped into the gap

'Uncut supply' looks like a shipment nobody has trimmed. 'Supply' is the adverb of supple, so it is an instruction about the word standing in front of it.

6 across COSMIC

I'm so taken aback by volume of the Universe (6)

Definition: of the Universe · reversal + container

  • volume → CC cc is cubic centimetres, the volume marked on a syringe or an engine
  • I'm so → OSMI the four letters of the exclamation, read the other way

'Volume of the Universe' reads as one phrase and is really two jobs: a measure of capacity, then the definition. The exclamation is astonishment and raw letters at once.

9 across ENFOLD

Embrace ten times after taking the lead (6)

Definition: Embrace · deletion

  • ten times → TENFOLD multiplied by ten is tenfold, and its first letter is the one at risk

The surface offers a race, where taking the lead is the thing to want. In the wordplay it is a subtraction performed on a multiplier.

10 across TEMPORAL

Member with gold duck houses is only lasting a short time (8)

Definition: only lasting a short time · container + charade

  • duck → TEAL a teal is a small wild duck, and it splits after two letters
  • Member → MP a Member of Parliament is an MP
  • gold → OR OR is gold in heraldry, from the French for the metal

The surface remembers the MPs' expenses scandal, in which a duck house became the emblem of what members claimed for. The wordplay only wants a small wildfowl.

11 across PEREGRINE

Number one accepts erring badly will get the bird (9)

Definition: bird · container + anagram

  • Number one → PEE number one is the nursery euphemism for a pee
  • erring → REGRIN the six letters of ERRING in a new order, sitting inside

To get the bird, in British usage, is to be jeered at, so the closing phrase reads as a fate rather than a species. Only its last word is the definition.

13 across CHIC

Ken's going off yellow style (4)

Definition: style · deletion

  • yellow → CHICKEN calling someone yellow is calling them a coward, and there is a farmyard word for that
  • Ken's → KEN the man's name is the three-letter chunk dropped from the end of that longer word

Ken looks like the subject of the sentence, someone going off yellow. He is really three letters to be lifted out of a word for cowardly.

15 across BURTON

Actor's not Polish revolutionary (6)

Definition: Actor · reversal

  • Polish → BUR to polish is to rub, and those three letters are turned about
  • not → TON the small word exactly as printed, turned about as well

The capital P does all the work: 'Polish' looks like the nationality and is really the verb. Turn 'not rub' round as one string and the Welshman appears.

17 across HEARSE

What's reversing behind big black car? (6)

Definition: big black car · charade + reversal

  • What's → HE 'Eh?' is what you say when you did not catch something, and here it is turned round
  • behind → ARSE the rude word for a behind, taken as it comes

'What?' has to become the two-letter version before anything is turned round, and 'behind' is the anatomical sense, not a position in the traffic.

18 across WINNER

Show in Nero's boxing champion (6)

Definition: champion · hidden word

  • Show in Nero's → WINNER six letters run straight on from the end of the first word through the two that follow

A Roman emperor at the ringside is why 'boxing' reads as the sport. It is the plain verb, and the letters never leave the words they are printed in.

19 across CLOSET

What 18 didn't do in the middle of court recess (6)

Definition: recess · container

  • court → CT Ct is the abbreviation used for court on street signs and in case names
  • What 18 didn't do → LOSE the entry at 18 across came first, so the thing they did not do is its opposite

A cross-reference is a promise that the other clue is solved first. 'Court recess' looks like a legal adjournment, but the recess is the definition and the court is two letters.

21 across RAVE

Go naked to party (4)

Definition: party · deletion

  • Go → TRAVEL to go somewhere is to travel, and this six-letter word is the one being undressed

'Go naked' is a picture of streaking and an instruction to strip a word. Only the last word of four is the definition, which is how so short a clue hides a whole verb.

22 across SANCTIONS

Authorises international punishment (9)

Definition: Authorises · double definition

  • Authorises → the permitting sense: a governing body formally allows something to go ahead
  • international punishment → the penalising sense: trade bans and frozen assets imposed by one country on another

Three words with no join and no indicator, because there is no wordplay to signal. The trick is that one word carries two senses that flatly contradict each other.

25 across HAT TRICK

Deception by bowler might produce this? (3,5)

Definition: Deception by bowler might produce this? · &lit + charade

  • bowler → HAT a bowler is a stiff round hat, the City gent's headgear
  • Deception → TRICK a deception is a trick, and it comes second though the clue names it first

Two bowlers in one word: the cricketer the sentence describes and the hat the letters want. Reading it straight and reading it as wordplay use exactly the same words.

26 across ICE TEA

Reserve trimmed steak and refreshing drink (3,3)

Definition: refreshing drink · charade + middle letters

  • Reserve → ICE reserve in the sense of coolness — an icy manner towards someone
  • steak → TEA cut the S and the K off the ends and three letters are left in the middle

'Reserve' is neither the booking nor the substitute on the bench: it is the chilly-manner sense, which is what lets frost stand in for it.

28 across WEIRDO

Oddball rector interrupts wedding vow after draining wine (6)

Definition: Oddball · container + outer letters

  • wine → WE the first and last letters, with the middle drained away
  • wedding vow → IDO the two words said at the altar, which the next letter then splits
  • rector → R R is the abbreviation for rector, the priest who takes the service

The whole wedding is scenery. What matters is that the vow is spelt out as two short words, and that draining a word takes its middle rather than its edges.

29 across DONATION

Provide state aid (8)

Definition: aid · charade

  • Provide → DO caterers do a party — that is, they provide it
  • state → NATION a state is a nation, six letters as they come

Three words, no indicator and nothing left over. 'State aid' is the phrase the surface wants read as one thing, and only its second half is the definition.

Down

2 down URN

Link to website with pound off new vase (3)

Definition: vase · deletion + charade

  • Link to website → URL a web address is a URL, the string a link points at
  • pound → L L is the abbreviation for the pound, from the Latin libra the £ sign draws
  • new → N N is the standard abbreviation for new

Nothing in the surface suggests pottery, which is the point: 'Link to website' is doing lexical work, not scene-setting, and the £ sign is a curly L.

3 down CLOVE

Divided 100 by zero (5)

Definition: Divided · charade

  • 100 → C C is the Roman numeral for a hundred
  • zero → LOVE a score of nil in tennis is called love

The answer is also a spice, which is the reading the enumeration invites. The definition wants the older sense that survives in 'cloven hoof'.

4 down UNDERCOVER

In secret, run over code leaving nothing suspect (10)

Definition: In secret · deletion + anagram

  • run over code → RUNOVERCDE these three words supply the letters to be shuffled, one O short
  • nothing → O nothing is written as a nought, and O is the letter that drops out

'In secret' at the front looks like scene-setting for a hit-and-run, complete with a victim. It is the definition, and the accident is the raw material.

5 down LATENT

Dormant in insect net, a locust turned up (6)

Definition: Dormant · hidden word + reversal

  • insect net, a locust → TNETAL six letters in a row straddle these words, printed in the opposite order to the one wanted

The swarm in the surface invites you to shuffle the insects. Nothing is shuffled: the letters are consecutive, and only the direction of travel changes.

6 down COMB

Thoroughly search male nutcases (4)

Definition: Thoroughly search · container

  • nut → COB a cob is a hazelnut, the fat one that grows in hedgerows
  • male → M M is the standard abbreviation for male

'Nutcases' is one word on the page and two in the wordplay. Police comb an area for evidence, which is the sense the definition wants.

7 down SHOEHORNS

Squeezes weed into cut flowers at last (9)

Definition: Squeezes · container + last letter

  • cut → SHORN cut here is the past participle of shear — a sheep is shorn
  • weed → HOE to weed a bed is to hoe it, and the tool goes inside
  • flowers at last → S the closing letter of flowers, joining on at the end

'Cut flowers' is a single picture on the page and two separate jobs in the wordplay: one word hands over five letters, the other only its last.

8 down ISAAC NEWTON

Doctor Watson - a nice scientist (5,6)

Definition: scientist · anagram

  • Watson - a nice → WATSONANICE eleven letters to be rearranged — count them against the (5,6) before starting

Doctor Watson is Sherlock Holmes's companion, so the two words read as one man's name. The first is the instruction, and the man wanted is a different Englishman entirely.

12 down ELBOW GREASE

Bowler's developing with age, experience at either end, and hard work (5,6)

Definition: hard work · anagram + outer letters

  • Bowler's → BOWLERS seven letters go into the mix, the possessive S among them
  • age → AGE three more letters, taken exactly as printed
  • experience → E the outer letters of the word are the same one, so whichever end you take hands over that letter

'Bowler's developing with age' reads as one thought about a cricketer past his best. It is really an instruction plus its raw material, and the definition waits until the last two words.

14 down REFLECTION

Official left "Out of Order" notice for consideration (10)

Definition: consideration · charade + anagram

  • Official → REF the official in charge of a match is the ref
  • left → L L is the standard abbreviation for left
  • notice → ECTION the six letters of NOTICE, shuffled

'Official left' reads as a departure — someone walking out of a meeting — and that is the whole misdirection: both words are short abbreviations waiting at the front.

16 down RENOVATOR

Restorer picked up French car with tax overhead reimbursed up-front (9)

Definition: Restorer · homophone + charade + first letters

  • French car → RENO Renault is a French make, and its name said aloud is written down here in four letters
  • tax → VAT VAT is Value Added Tax, the sales tax charged on most goods in Britain
  • overhead reimbursed → OR the opening letter of each of these two words

The surface is a garage bill: a car, the tax on it, overheads and a refund. Only the first word stands outside that sum, and it is the definition.

20 down MASKED

Back in Gotham, requested description of Batman and Robin? (6)

Definition: description of Batman and Robin · charade + last letter

  • Gotham → M the last letter of the city's name
  • requested → ASKED requested is the past tense of ask

'Back in Gotham' reads as a caption — the heroes home again — and is really an instruction to take a single letter. The question mark admits the definition describes rather than defines.

23 down INEPT

Elected essentially dangerous, vacuous President? Hopeless! (5)

Definition: Hopeless · charade + middle letter + outer letters

  • Elected → IN a candidate who wins is in
  • dangerous → E the central letter of the nine
  • President → PT the two letters at the ends of the word, with everything between them gone

The question mark warns that the President is not being defined but dismantled. Every word before the last is an instruction, and the sneer at the end is the definition.

24 down SILO

Store Greek character wants up North (4)

Definition: Store · deletion

  • Greek character → UPSILON the twentieth letter of the Greek alphabet, written out in full
  • up → UP two letters that go, and they are sitting right at the front
  • North → N N is the standard abbreviation for north, and it goes too

'Wants' is the old sense, as in found wanting. The Greek letter has to be written out in full before you can see what the compass point and the little word take away.

27 down ECO

Green manure covers hedges (3)

Definition: Green · hidden word

  • manure covers → ECO three letters in a row straddle the gap between these two words

Manure and hedges make a gardening sentence, which is the disguise. The definition is the first word, and the letters wanted never move from where they are printed.
