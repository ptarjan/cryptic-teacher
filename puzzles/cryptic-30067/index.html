Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,067

Across

1 across GARBAGE

Rubbish clothes, baggier with holes at intervals (7)

Definition: Rubbish · charade + alternate letters

  • clothes → GARB garb = clothes
  • baggier with holes at intervals → AGE alternate letters of bAgGiEr

One picture of worn-out clothing from start to finish, and the instruction is hidden inside it: 'with holes at intervals' means take every other letter and let the rest be the holes.

5 across SHAPELY

Wasted help, say, to get in trim (7)

Definition: in trim · anagram

  • help, say → HELPSAY anagram fodder
  • Wasted → anagram indicator

On the surface this is a squandered gym membership. 'Wasted' is the anagram word, and it means ruined or thrown away rather than thin.

10 across PANG

Hungry feeling? Frier finally working (4)

Definition: Hungry feeling? · charade + last letter

  • Frier → PAN a frier (frying pan)
  • finally working → G last letter of workinG

'Frier' looks like a person at the stove and is the pan itself. That single re-reading is the whole clue, which is why it only needs four letters.

11 across ONE AT A TIME

Exercising, animate toes almost in sequence (3,2,1,4)

Definition: in sequence · anagram + deletion

  • animate → ANIMATE anagram fodder
  • toes almost → TOE TOES 'almost' — drop the last letter
  • Exercising → anagram indicator

The surface is a fitness class, and the answer is a phrase rather than a word. Worth remembering when an anagram will not resolve: the letters may be waiting to break into four short pieces.

12 across TASSIE

Cup from Scotland, or one from Hobart? (6)

Definition: Cup from Scotland · double definition

  • Cup from Scotland → TASSIE Scots word for a small cup (Burns: 'the silver tassie')
  • one from Hobart? → TASSIE informal for a Tasmanian — Hobart is Tasmania's capital

The 'or' is the pivot between two unrelated worlds, Burns and Tasmania, and the question mark on the second half concedes that Hobart is an example rather than a definition.

13 across NAUTICAL

Called out indecent state of the crew (8)

Definition: of the crew · charade + homophone

  • indecent → NAUGHTY indecent = naughty
  • state → CAL California, the US state, abbreviated CAL
  • Called out → NAUTICAL run the two together and say them: the indecent state of the crew becomes the answer

Say the pieces aloud and 'naughty-cal' falls out — the homophone is the whole clue. 'State' for CAL is the kind of abbreviation to expect once a charade needs three letters from nowhere.

14 across STATESMAN

New magazine from US island (9)

Definition: New magazine · charade

  • from US → STATES the States = the US
  • island → MAN the Isle of Man

The definition works by completion: the answer is not itself a magazine, but put 'New' in front and you have one. Setters use this whenever a title has a spare first word.

16 across GIRTH

Looking west, this elevated point used to measure height and circumference (5)

Definition: circumference · reversal

  • height → H h = height
  • elevated point used to measure → TRIG a trig point, the hilltop survey pillar

'Looking west' is the reversal, and it can only mean that in an across entry, west being leftwards on the page. The surveying surface holds together from start to finish.

17 across SPLIT

Peach burst open (5)

Definition: Peach · double definition

  • Peach → SPLIT both are slang for 'inform on someone'
  • burst open → SPLIT the everyday sense

The fruit is the decoy: to peach on someone is to inform on them, an old sense that survives almost nowhere outside crosswords.

19 across SNOW WHITE

Beautiful girl whose twin perished (4,5)

Definition: Beautiful girl · anagram

  • whose twin → WHOSETWIN anagram fodder, 9 letters
  • perished → anagram indicator — perished like old rubber

'Perished' means decayed, the way old rubber perishes, and that is the anagram word. The surface points you towards twins and tragedy instead.

23 across BENEDICT

Pope’s half genial proclamation (8)

Definition: Pope · charade + deletion

  • half genial → BEN genial = BENIGN; half of it is BEN
  • proclamation → EDICT an edict is a proclamation

'Pope's' is the definition with a hidden 'is' attached — the apostrophe-s joins the two halves of the clue rather than giving the Pope anything to own. Seeing that is the hard step.

24 across O'CLOCK

The precise hour business is over, close up (6)

Definition: The precise hour · reversal + charade

  • business → CO Co. = company, business; 'is over' reverses it to OC
  • close up → LOCK to lock = to close up

The surface is a shop shutting for the night, and 'is over' does double duty: closing time above, and turn the letters round underneath.

26 across PERMAFROST

A couple of hairstyles, extremely smart feature of the Arctic (10)

Definition: feature of the Arctic · charade + outer letters

  • A couple of hairstyles → PERM AFRO two hairdos
  • extremely smart → ST the extremes (outer letters) of SmarT

Two hairdos and a smart appearance make a complete little scene at the salon, which is about as far from the Arctic as a surface can get.

27 across BOIL

In rage, black a source of energy (4)

Definition: In rage · charade

  • black → B b = black (pencil grades, chess)
  • a source of energy → OIL oil, the fossil fuel

The difficulty is placement: 'In rage' opens the clue looking like a scene-setter, when it is the definition. To boil is to seethe.

28 across PEANUTS

Lousy payment for cartoon (7)

Definition: Lousy payment · double definition

  • Lousy payment → PEANUTS 'they pay peanuts'
  • cartoon → PEANUTS Charles Schulz's comic strip, home of Snoopy

The surface is a cartoonist grumbling about his fee, and the two definitions sit either side of 'for', which is doing nothing at all but joining them.

29 across TRILOGY

Hear about one record in a set of three (7)

Definition: a set of three · container

  • Hear → TRY a court hears, or tries, a case
  • one → I I = one
  • record → LOG a log is a record

'Hear' as a court hears a case is the step to find, and the surface — a record, a hearing — keeps you in a recording studio rather than a courtroom.

Down

2 down ADAMANT

Unyielding squad a man triumphantly arrests (7)

Definition: Unyielding · hidden word

  • squad a man triumphantly → ADAMANT hidden: squAD A MAN Triumphantly

'Arrests' is both the hiding instruction and part of the police story on the surface. Hidden words are easy to miss precisely because everything you need is already in plain sight.

3 down BOGUS

Fraudulent attempt to climb into conveyance (5)

Definition: Fraudulent · container + reversal

  • attempt → GO an attempt = a go; 'to climb' reverses it (down clue) to OG
  • conveyance → BUS a bus

'To climb' only means reverse because this is a down clue; in an across entry the same word would do nothing. Position words always depend on which way the answer runs.

4 down GLOVERS

Creators of digital protection are not just good friends? (7)

Definition: Creators of digital protection · charade

  • good → G g = good
  • not just good friends? → LOVERS more than 'just good friends' = lovers

'Creators of digital protection' is the joke, and it survives being explained: digits are fingers, so gloves protect them. Nothing to do with computers.

6 down HOT TUB

In base, extravagant to luxuriate in this? (3,3)

Definition: to luxuriate in this? · container

  • base → HUB a hub is a base (of operations)
  • extravagant → OTT OTT = over the top

The definition is the whole playful phrase, 'to luxuriate in this?', the question mark conceding that it describes the answer rather than defining it. OTT for over the top is worth having ready.

7 down PETRI DISH

Spirited, transforming hotel into a place of culture (5,4)

Definition: a place of culture · anagram

  • Spirited → SPIRITED anagram fodder
  • hotel → H hotel = H in the NATO alphabet
  • transforming → anagram indicator

'A place of culture' is the joke — bacterial, not artistic — and 'hotel' for H comes from the NATO alphabet, which setters will use for any of its twenty-six letters.

8 down LAMBAST

Roast meat: like a bit of turkey? (7)

Definition: Roast · charade + first letter

  • meat → LAMB lamb, the meat
  • like → AS as = like
  • a bit of turkey → T the first bit of Turkey

'Roast' works in both worlds, which is what holds the dinner-table surface together: to roast a person is to criticise them, and that sense is the definition.

9 down REINCARNATION

Being born again, robe outwardly in pink (13)

Definition: Being born again · charade + outer letters

  • robe outwardly → RE the outer letters of RobE
  • in → IN used literally
  • pink → CARNATION carnations are 'pinks', the flower

'Pink' for carnation is the piece to learn — a pink is a flower of that family, and setters treat the two words as interchangeable. The robe keeps you thinking about colour.

15 down TRIBESMAN

Fellow in community had injured brainstem (9)

Definition: Fellow in community · anagram

  • brainstem → BRAINSTEM anagram fodder, 9 letters
  • injured → anagram indicator

A single-word anagram under a grisly medical surface, and the only real difficulty is believing it is that simple.

18 down PRECEDE

Arrive before opening of park, and go back (7)

Definition: Arrive before · charade + first letter

  • opening of park → P first letter of Park
  • go back → RECEDE to recede = to go back

The surface is a note about opening hours, and the definition sits at the front, where two ordinary words — 'Arrive before' — disguise the fact that together they are one meaning.

20 down WOOSTER

Bertie develops from bad to worse (7)

Definition: Bertie · anagram

  • develops → anagram indicator
  • from bad → surface only — the familiar phrase 'from bad to worse' is split, and only the second half is fodder
  • to worse → TOWORSE anagram fodder, 7 letters

A familiar phrase split in half so that only 'to worse' is fodder. You read 'from bad to worse' as a single unit, which is exactly how the letters hide in the open.

21 down TICKING

About to go off perhaps, indicating right (7)

Definition: About to go off perhaps · double definition

  • About to go off perhaps → TICKING a ticking bomb is about to go off
  • indicating right → TICKING marking with a tick = marking as correct

Both halves sound like the same sentence about a bomb, and that is what stops you hearing the second one as a pen marking a box correct.

22 down MISFIT

Weirdo following one in poor visibility (6)

Definition: Weirdo · container

  • following → F f = following (as in 'p. 12f')
  • one → I I = one
  • poor visibility → MIST mist

The surface is someone being followed through fog, and it works because 'following' reads as a verb when it is really the abbreviation you meet in page references.

25 down LABEL

Brief description of timeless ballet dancing (5)

Definition: Brief description · anagram + deletion

  • timeless ballet → BALLE BALLET minus T ('time')
  • dancing → anagram indicator

'Timeless' for a word with its T removed is worth filing away. Setters lean on it constantly, alongside 'endless' and 'heartless' for the letters at the other ends.
