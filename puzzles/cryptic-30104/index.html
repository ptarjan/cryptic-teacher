Across

1 across FULL PITCH

Hit fine pull shot over Crawley’s head (poor delivery) (4,5)

Definition: poor delivery · anagram

  • Hit → HIT three letters of fodder, taken straight off the clue
  • fine → F F is fine on a pencil, and the crossword's usual short form for the word
  • pull → PULL four more letters, the cricket stroke lending its name to the fodder
  • over Crawley’s head → C Zak Crawley opens the batting for England, and a name's head is the letter it starts with

A batter middles a pull shot and the ball sails over a fielder's head.

Cricket vocabulary works both sides of the clue: 'shot' looks like the stroke being described and is really the shuffle instruction, while the definition sits quietly in the brackets.

6 across SNOG

What follows wine, women … turned on … ? (4)

Definition: What follows wine, women … turned on … ? · &lit + reversal

  • What follows wine, women → SONG the old trio is wine, women and song, so the word that comes next is SONG
  • on → no new letters: it names the two already sitting in the middle, the ones that get spun

The end of a familiar list of vices, with somebody hinting at how the evening finished.

Both readings of 'turned on' are live at once: the surface wants aroused, the wordplay wants a two-letter word spun round where it stands.

10 across CORGI

Old queen’s favourite brother, Joe (5)

Definition: Old queen’s favourite · charade

  • brother → COR 'oh brother!' and 'cor!' are the same noise of surprise, one British and dated
  • Joe → GI GI Joe is the American soldier, and GI is the crossword's standard one

Gossip about an old queen's favourite, and about the brother, Joe.

'Old queen' is the late Elizabeth II, whose dogs were a national fixture, while the wordplay needs 'brother' heard as an exclamation rather than a relative.

11 across FORGOTTEN

Neglected wetland in which ordinary river has become dry (9)

Definition: Neglected · container + charade

  • wetland → FEN a fen is the flat marshy ground of eastern England
  • ordinary → O O is the standard short form for ordinary
  • river → R R is the standard short form for river, as on a map
  • has become → GO milk goes sour and bread goes stale: to go a thing is to become it
  • dry → TT TT is teetotal, the crossword's usual dry

A marsh that has dried out, its ordinary little river gone.

'Dry' as teetotal and 'go' as become are the two everyday senses hiding behind the drought, and neither announces itself.

12 across PRO RATA

Commensurately paid singer left for auditorium (3,4)

Definition: Commensurately · charade + homophone

  • paid → PRO a pro is the paid one, as against the amateur
  • singer left → RATA a rat is both the one who sings to the police and the one who deserts, so someone who has done it is a ratter

A singer on proper wages setting off for the concert hall.

'Singer' is the crook's word here — the one who has informed on everybody else — and the auditorium is asking for the sound of that person, not the spelling.

13 across THOREAU

Formally, you are, curiously, probing social critic (7)

Definition: probing social critic · anagram

  • Formally, you are → THOUARE 'thou' is the archaic you that now sounds formal, with 'are' following it unchanged

An old-fashioned way of saying you are, dropped into a remark about a critic of society.

'Probing' looks like an insertion instruction and belongs to the definition, while the fodder hides in plain sight as a Sunday-best version of 'you are'.

14 across COUNTRY COUSIN

To acquire Amtrak, possibly involve US clodhopper (7,6)

Definition: clodhopper · container + charade

  • involve → COUNTIN to involve someone is to count them in, and the phrase splits apart around what follows
  • Amtrak → RYCO America's passenger rail operator: RY for railway, CO for company
  • US → US the two letters stand exactly as written

Buying Amtrak would probably mean bringing the American government in on the deal.

Amtrak is the United States' national passenger railway, and 'possibly' is the warning that the letters wanted are the generic operator rather than the name itself.

17 across BAREBACK RIDER

Barrels were returned empty? I’m not sure I can forgo tack (8,5)

Definition: I can forgo tack · charade

  • empty → BARE an empty shelf is a bare one
  • returned → BACK goods returned have come back
  • not sure → RIDER a rider is the reservation attached to an agreement, which is what stopping short of sure adds
  • Barrels were → surface only: it is the barrels that came home empty, and the words hand over no letters

A carter checking whether the barrels came back empty, and doubting he can manage without his gear.

A horse's barrel is the part of its body the saddle sits on, which is why the first half reads as trade talk while the answer is equestrian.

21 across SCALPEL

Essential for operator training to cut speaker’s initial connection (7)

Definition: Essential for operator · container + charade + first letter

  • speaker’s initial → S the initial of speaker is the letter it begins with
  • connection → CALL a call is the connection you make down a telephone line
  • training → PE PE is the school timetable's physical training

A trainee switchboard operator cutting off the first caller of the day.

'Operator' is a switchboard on the surface and a surgeon underneath, and 'cut' belongs to neither: it is the instruction that opens the telephone word up.

22 across PLAYBOY

Libertine tactic used to find car park space for Spooner? (7)

Definition: Libertine · spoonerism

  • car park space → PLAY a bay is a marked-out parking space, and it keeps its AY while borrowing the other word's opening
  • tactic → BOY a ploy is a tactic, and it keeps its OY under the B that has swapped in

A driver's trick for landing a parking space, told in Spooner's usual muddle.

The way in is to solve the plain phrase first — the scheme for getting a parking space — and only then trade the two opening sounds.

24 across MIDI-SKIRT

On counter, neatly sews single storage unit in garment (4-5)

Definition: garment · container + reversal + charade

  • neatly sews → MIRT to trim a garment is to sew edging neatly onto it, and those four letters run backwards here
  • single → I I is one, the Roman numeral
  • storage unit → DISK a disk is where a computer keeps its files

A tailor at the shop counter, sewing neatly and fitting a storage unit into a piece of clothing.

Almost everything reads as tailoring — the counter, the neat sewing, the garment — and the storage unit belongs to another century entirely, which is what gives the game away.

25 across INDIA

International relief supplies finally flown around land (5)

Definition: land · charade + reversal + last letter

  • International → I I is the standard short form for international
  • flown → N the last letter of flown, as asked for
  • relief supplies → DIA relief supplies are aid, and the word is turned about

Emergency aid flown out to a disaster and dropped over the country that needed it.

'Finally flown' is the smallest piece here and the easiest to skate past: it is one letter, the last of the word standing next to it.

26 across AIRE

Which current runs through area by centre of Leeds? (4)

Definition: Which current runs through area by centre of Leeds? · &lit + container

  • area → ARE an are is the metric unit of area, a hundred square metres
  • current → I I is the physicist's symbol for electrical current

A quiz question about which river flows through the middle of Leeds.

Leeds stands on this river, so the sentence is a straight question as well as a set of instructions; 'area' wants the metric unit, not the general word.

27 across NANOMETRE

Small unit moving near to Bury not including this person (9)

Definition: Small unit · anagram + deletion

  • near to → NEARTO six letters lifted straight out of the clue
  • Bury → MINE to mine a road is to bury explosives under it
  • this person → I the setter speaking of himself is I, and that is the letter left out

Moving house to somewhere near Bury, with one person left off the list.

Bury is a town in Greater Manchester, which is what the capital letter wants you to see; the verb hiding under it is the one about laying explosives.

Down

1 down FACE PACK

Unable to outrun wolves, must one turn to this beauty treatment? (4,4)

Definition: beauty treatment · charade

  • must one turn to → FACE to face something is to turn and meet it head on
  • Unable to outrun wolves → PACK wolves run in a pack, and outrunning one is not on offer

Someone caught in the open with wolves closing in and no chance of outrunning them.

The question mark carries the whole joke: the clue never claims the phrase is a beauty treatment, it asks whether the words the wolves force on you happen to name one.

2 down LARGO

Antagonist of Bond film topped by M’s predecessor (5)

Definition: Antagonist of Bond · charade

  • M’s predecessor → L the letter that comes before M in the alphabet
  • film → ARGO Ben Affleck's 2012 Tehran thriller, four letters of title

A rundown of a Bond film's cast, the villain listed under whoever ran the service before M.

'M's predecessor' looks like spy history — whoever had the job before M — and it is only the letter standing before M in the alphabet.

3 down PHILANTHROPIST

Man’s lover arranged NHS hospital trip to discharge son (14)

Definition: Man’s lover · anagram + deletion

  • NHS hospital trip → NHSHOSPITALTRIP fifteen letters offered up, one more than the grid has room for
  • son → S S is the standard short form for son, and it is the letter sent out

A partner fixes up a hospital visit so that a son can be sent home.

The NHS is Britain's health service, and 'discharge' does the setter's work twice: hospital word on the surface, deletion instruction underneath.

4 down TIFFANY

Jewel merchant and his glassmaker son, did they quarrel at all? (7)

Definition: Jewel merchant and his glassmaker son · charade

  • did they quarrel → TIFF a tiff is a small quarrel, the kind that blows over by teatime
  • at all → ANY 'did they eat at all' and 'did they eat any' ask the same question

A father-and-son business in jewels and glass, and the question of whether the two ever fell out.

The question mark at the end is the tell that the last four words are wordplay rather than biography: nothing is being claimed about a family row.

5 down HORATIO

Hamlet’s intimate means to compare Santa Clauses? (7)

Definition: Hamlet’s intimate · charade

  • Santa Clauses → HO Santa's laugh is ho ho ho, so HO is the syllable he is made of
  • means to compare → RATIO a ratio is how you set two quantities against each other

Somebody wondering how you would measure one Father Christmas against another.

Split the answer after two letters and it becomes a unit of measurement for Father Christmases, which is what the question mark is apologising for.

7 down NET-VEINED

Like some leaves I needn’t’ve braised (3-6)

Definition: Like some leaves · anagram

  • I needn’t’ve → INEEDNTVE nine letters once the apostrophes are dropped, exactly what the grid needs

A cook regretting the leaves they put in the pan.

The contraction is the whole difficulty: 'needn't've' is barely written English, which is why what it hands over does not look like fodder at all.

8 down GENIUS

One in a special class (6)

Definition: One in a special class · &lit + container

  • a special class → GENUS a genus is the rank a biologist sorts creatures into, one step above species
  • One → I the Roman numeral for one

A pupil taken out of the ordinary form and put into a class of their own.

Taxonomy is both trap and key: 'class' is a rank in the same system as genus, so the clue points at the wrong rung of that ladder on purpose.

9 down ZOROASTRIANISM

Faith right to stop Cross and Cook climbing peak of Mount Sinai (14)

Definition: Faith · container + charade + reversal + first letter

  • Cross → ZO a zo is the Tibetan cross between a yak and a cow
  • Cook → ROAST to roast is one way to cook
  • right → R R is the standard short form for right
  • Mount Sinai → IANISM the top letter of Mount with SINAI running on after it, the six of them read from the bottom up

Two climbers named Cross and Cook are stopped just short of the summit of Mount Sinai.

Cross and Cook read as climbers' surnames, capitals and all, and both are ordinary words here — one of them a Himalayan hybrid animal.

15 down UPBRAIDER

Who’s reprimanding United attacker for preserving lead? (9)

Definition: Who’s reprimanding · container + charade

  • United → U U is how football's United is written on a fixture list
  • lead → PB Pb is lead's chemical symbol, from the Latin plumbum
  • attacker → RAIDER a raider attacks and gets out again, which covers the soldier and the forward alike

A manager tearing into a United forward who is only trying to protect a one-goal lead.

Football and chemistry collide on one word: 'lead' looks like the scoreline the attacker is guarding and is the metal's symbol instead.

16 down GREY MARE

Mr Pearce unwisely let a lot of folk use his dominant partner (4,4)

Definition: dominant partner · double definition

  • Mr Pearce unwisely let a lot of folk use his → GREYMARE the Devon folk song Widecombe Fair begins 'Tom Pearce, Tom Pearce, lend me your...', and seven men then ride the borrowed animal to death
  • dominant partner → GREYMARE Chambers files this sense under the second word: the proverbial wife who is the better horse of the pair

A man who foolishly let half the village borrow the one who really rules him.

Two pieces of very English general knowledge meet on one phrase: a Dartmoor folk song about a lent-out horse, and the old proverb about which half of a marriage is in charge.

18 down BILLION

Performers in show withdrawing favourite number (7)

Definition: number · charade + reversal

  • Performers in show → BILL the bill is the printed list of who is on, so it stands in for the performers
  • favourite → ION the favourite is the No 1, and No with the Roman one drawn back gives these three

A theatre pulling its star turn out of the programme.

'No 1' is the favourite written as a racecard writes it, Roman numeral and all, and it is that scrap of shorthand that turns round.

19 down CAPSTAN

Turner makes function appear larger on screen? (7)

Definition: Turner · charade

  • appear larger on screen → CAPS typing in caps is how letters are made to look bigger on a screen
  • function → TAN tan is a trigonometric function, alongside sin and cos

Somebody enlarging a function on a monitor so it can be read from further off.

'Turner' wants to be the painter and is a piece of deck machinery instead, while the rest of the clue is a maths function shouted in capitals.

20 down TSAMBA

Wanting a taste of 25, cook basmati dish (6)

Definition: dish · anagram + deletion + first letter

  • basmati → BASMATI the long-grain rice, seven letters of fodder before anything is taken away
  • 25 → I 25 across is a country, and the letter it opens with is the one taken

A cook told to leave one flavour out of the rice and make something else of it.

The '25' points at another entry in the grid, so that one has to fall first; the dish itself is Himalayan, roasted barley eaten across Tibet.

23 down BIDET

Bathroom ‘fixture’? It’d be ‘fitting’ (5)

Definition: Bathroom ‘fixture’ · anagram

  • It’d be → ITDBE five letters once the apostrophe is dropped, which is all the grid asks for

Someone joking that a certain bathroom fixture would be just the right thing.

Both quoted words do sly duty: 'fixture' is plumbing vocabulary borrowed for the definition, and 'fitting' is the plumber's noun used as a shuffle instruction.
