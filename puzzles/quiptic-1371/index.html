Across

1 across LET-DOWN

Rental property close to ground now in a mess? That’s disappointment (3-4)

Definition: disappointment · charade + last letter + anagram

A LET-DOWN is the flat feeling left when something promised turns out worse than expected, which is a disappointment.

  • Rental property → LET a property on the rental market is a let
  • ground → D the last letter of the word, taken on its own
  • now → OWN the three letters of the word resettled into another order
  • close to — Being close to the end of a word puts you at its final letter, so that one letter is all that is taken.
  • in a mess — A thing in a mess has been tipped out of the arrangement it was given, so the letters beside it will not stay in their printed order.

A rented flat right beside the ground has been left in a complete state.

'Close to ground' looks like a description of where the flat is, and it is an instruction to take the last letter of a single word.

5 across FUCHSIA

Plant if such to be cultivated by area (7)

Definition: Plant · anagram + charade

A FUCHSIA is the garden shrub with hanging bell-shaped flowers, so 'Plant' defines it by naming the class it belongs to.

  • if such → FUCHSI the six letters of these two little words, put down in another order
  • area → A a is the standard abbreviation for area
  • to be cultivated — Cultivating ground means turning it over and breaking it up, so the letters handed to you get broken up and reset.

A gardener being told to grow something of that kind in a particular patch.

'If such' is a phrase doing no work in the sentence at all, which is the tell — setters write filler that awkward only when they need its letters.

10 across URGE

Impulse in abrupt removal of people after loss of power (4)

Definition: Impulse · deletion

An URGE is a sudden strong wish to do something, the same inner push that 'impulse' names.

  • abrupt removal of people → PURGE a purge is a sudden clearing-out of unwanted people from a party or an office
  • power → P P is the standard abbreviation for power, and it is the letter struck out
  • after loss of — Losing a thing means it is gone, so the letter named next comes out of the word rather than being added to it.

A political clear-out happening the moment the leadership loses its grip.

'Loss of power' reads as a government falling from office, which is the very same political story the first half of the clue is telling.

11 across AT VARIANCE

Vacate Iran suffering turmoil in conflict (2,8)

Definition: in conflict · anagram

Two people AT VARIANCE openly disagree with each other, which is what being in conflict describes.

  • Vacate Iran → VACATEIRAN ten letters of fodder, the verb and the country together
  • suffering turmoil — Turmoil is violent disorder, and whatever is suffering it does not keep its parts where they were.

People being told to leave Iran while the country is in upheaval.

The surface is a news bulletin, and the two words carrying the story — the order to leave and the country it applies to — are the letters themselves.

12 across FIASCO

Debacle taking place in Sofia’s condemned (6)

Definition: Debacle · hidden word

A FIASCO is a complete and humiliating failure, which is exactly what a debacle is.

  • Sofia’s condemned → FIASCO six letters in a row, starting inside the first word and running on through the apostrophe into the second
  • taking place in — Something taking place in a spot is sitting right there inside it, so the letters wanted are already present in the words that follow.

A disaster unfolding in Sofia, in a building that had already been condemned.

Sofia is the Bulgarian capital, so the sentence reads as a news report from abroad, and the possessive apostrophe does nothing but disguise the seam.

13 across CHATLINE

Repeated word, not new, linked to policy for conversational service (8)

Definition: conversational service · deletion + charade

A CHATLINE is a phone or online service people ring up simply to talk to strangers, which makes it a conversational service.

  • Repeated word → CHANT a chant is a phrase a crowd says over and over
  • new → N N is the standard abbreviation for new, and it is the letter cancelled
  • policy → LINE a party's line is the policy it expects its members to take
  • not — Putting this in front of a word cancels it, so the letter that word stands for comes out of the assembly instead of joining it.

Somebody dropping a stale slogan and sticking to the official position instead.

The party line is worth filing away as an equation: 'policy', 'stance' or 'official position' will all turn into the same four letters.

14 across TRADE FAIR

Commercial exhibition somehow rated reasonable (5,4)

Definition: Commercial exhibition · anagram + charade

A TRADE FAIR is the exhibition where firms in one industry show their products to buyers, which is a commercial exhibition.

  • rated → TRADE the five letters of the word, resettled into another order
  • reasonable → FAIR a fair price or a fair hearing is a reasonable one
  • somehow — The word admits the letters arrived by a route it will not name, and an unnamed route is a reordering.

An exhibition that somebody has judged to be decent enough.

'Rated reasonable' reads as one verdict on the show, but the two words are doing different jobs: one supplies letters, the other a synonym.

16 across AGREE

Correspond with a person keen on recycling, shortly (5)

Definition: Correspond · charade + deletion

Two sets of figures that AGREE correspond with each other — the tallying sense of the word rather than the sense of getting on.

  • a → A the indefinite article supplies its own single letter
  • person keen on recycling → GREEN someone who champions recycling and conservation goes by this name, after the colour the environmental movement adopted
  • shortly — Saying a thing shortly is saying it cut short, so the word it follows gives up its final letter.

Exchanging letters with an environmentally minded person before long.

'Correspond' looks like writing letters, which is the picture the surface wants; the definition needs the other sense, of two accounts matching.

17 across FLOSS

Perform dental hygiene following feeling of deprivation (5)

Definition: Perform dental hygiene · charade

To FLOSS is to work waxed thread between the teeth, which is performing dental hygiene.

  • following → F f is the abbreviation printed in references to mean the page following
  • feeling of deprivation → LOSS the ache of being deprived of somebody is a sense of loss

Cleaning your teeth after a spell of feeling bereft.

'Following' looks like a joining word telling you which piece comes after which, and it is a piece in its own right.

19 across ENTERTAIN

Consider register by old military reserve at home (9)

Definition: Consider · charade

To ENTERTAIN a proposal is to hold it in mind and give it a hearing without committing, which is exactly to consider it.

  • register → ENTER to enter a name in a book is to register it
  • old military reserve → TA the TA was the Territorial Army, Britain's part-time volunteer reserve force, renamed in 2014
  • at home → IN somebody at home is in

Signing up with a former volunteer force without leaving the house.

The TA is worth filing away: 'volunteers', 'reserves' or 'territorials' in a British clue almost always want those two letters.

23 across NO WONDER

Absence of singer Stevie? Hardly a surprise! (2,6)

Definition: Hardly a surprise! · charade

'NO WONDER' is what you say when an outcome was entirely predictable, which is what calling something hardly a surprise means.

  • Absence of → NO having an absence of something is having none of it
  • singer Stevie → WONDER Stevie is only the forename; the wordplay wants the surname of the Motown singer and pianist

Noticing that a famous soul singer has not turned up after all.

Reading 'Absence of' as an instruction rather than as part of the picture is the whole step, and the question mark is the setter owning up to the join.

24 across ASSUME

Take on a case for some about particular amount of money (6)

Definition: Take on · charade + outer letters + container

To ASSUME a duty is to take it on yourself, the sense of shouldering a role rather than the sense of supposing something.

  • a → A the article is taken at face value
  • some → SE the first and last letters of the word, which are its outside
  • particular amount of money → SUM a sum is a specified quantity of money
  • case for — A case is the shell round a thing rather than the thing itself, so only the outside letters of the word named are taken.
  • about — Something placed about a thing is wrapped round the outside of it, so these letters close over the piece that follows.

A lawyer agreeing to act for a group of people in a dispute over money.

'Case' is one of the setter's favourite words for the outside of something — a shell, a cover, a suitcase — and it never means the contents.

26 across ASSORTMENT

Variety of things scattered on mattress (10)

Definition: Variety of things · anagram

An ASSORTMENT is a mixed collection of different sorts of thing, which is what a variety of things amounts to.

  • on mattress → ONMATTRESS ten letters of fodder, the preposition counting as letters like any other word
  • scattered — Things scattered have been thrown about and no longer lie where they were put, so the letters land in a fresh order.

Odds and ends thrown down all over somebody's bed.

'On' is the word to notice: it looks like the preposition holding the picture together, and it is two of the answer's letters.

27 across INNS

Hostelries favoured by two Poles (4)

Definition: Hostelries · charade

An inn is a house offering travellers lodging and drink, so 'hostelries' is the same old-fashioned idea in the plural.

  • favoured → IN whatever is in is currently favoured or fashionable
  • two Poles → NS the north and south poles, each written as the initial it goes by on a compass or a magnet

Pubs that a couple of Polish visitors are fond of.

The capital P on 'Poles' points at Poland, and the clue means the ends of the earth's axis instead.

28 across DEGRADE

Cheapen degree, dear when reviewed (7)

Definition: Cheapen · charade + anagram

To DEGRADE a thing is to lower it in worth or standing, which is what cheapening does to its value.

  • degree → DEG deg is the standard written abbreviation for degree
  • dear → RADE the four letters of the word, resettled into another order
  • when reviewed — To review is to look at a thing again from a fresh angle, and letters looked at afresh come out in a different sequence.

Talking down the worth of a qualification that cost a lot to get.

'Reviewed' is the trap: it usually tells you to turn a word backwards, and backwards those four letters give a run that fits nothing.

29 across LYCHEES

Some bubbly, cheese and tropical fruits (7)

Definition: tropical fruits · hidden word

LYCHEES are the small sweet white-fleshed fruits, grown across the tropics, that come in a rough pink shell.

  • bubbly, cheese → LYCHEES seven letters in a row, beginning inside the first word and running straight across the comma
  • Some — Asking for some of a thing asks for part of it, and the part wanted is a run of letters lying inside the words that follow.

Champagne, cheese and fruit laid out for guests at a party.

A comma reads to the eye as a stop, which is exactly why a setter puts the join of a hidden answer on top of one.

Down

2 down EARLIER

Titled figure, one with old queen previously (7)

Definition: previously · charade

EARLIER and 'previously' both mean at a time before the one being talked about, and either can replace the other unchanged.

  • Titled figure → EARL an earl holds a rank in the peerage, so he is a figure with a title
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one
  • old queen → ER ER is the royal cipher Elizabeth Regina, and 'old' marks the reign as the last one rather than the present

A nobleman standing beside somebody who used to be a queen.

Two letters of royal shorthand carry the whole back end of this clue, and 'old' is not padding: it is what tells you which monarch's cipher is wanted.

3 down DRESS

Prepare article of clothing (5)

Definition: Prepare · double definition

As a verb DRESS means to make ready — a wound, a salad, a window; as a noun it is the frock itself.

  • Prepare → the verb used of getting a wound, a salad or a shop window ready
  • article of clothing → the one-piece garment a woman puts on over her head

An instruction to get a garment ready to wear.

Both halves define, so the clue asserts nothing at all, and the join is invisible because 'Prepare article of clothing' reads as one sensible order.

4 down WEAR OFF

Lose intensity with sport cancelled (4,3)

Definition: Lose intensity · charade

When a drug or an ache WEARS OFF its force fades away gradually, which is losing intensity.

  • sport → WEAR to sport a hat or a badge is to wear it where everyone can see
  • cancelled → OFF a match called off has been cancelled

A fixture called off, and the excitement draining away with it.

'Sport' reads as a noun in the surface, a game, and it is a verb in the wordplay, the one used of wearing something conspicuously.

6 down UPROAR

Commotion from posh pair over thing for rowing (6)

Definition: Commotion · charade

An UPROAR is a burst of loud confused noise and protest, which is what a commotion is.

  • posh → U U is the crossword's letter for upper class, from the U and non-U labels once given to posh and common usage
  • pair → PR pr is the catalogue abbreviation for a pair
  • thing for rowing → OAR the blade you pull a boat along with
  • over — One thing sitting over another is written above it, and in a down entry above means earlier, so these letters go on top.

Two well-bred people kicking up a din above somebody's boating gear.

'Rowing' can mean quarrelling, which is where the surface points, and the wordplay wants the boating sense instead.

7 down HEADLIGHT

Eighth lad repaired feature of a car (9)

Definition: feature of a car · anagram

A HEADLIGHT is one of the lamps at the front of a car, so the definition names it by the machine it belongs to.

  • Eighth lad → EIGHTHLAD nine letters of fodder, the two H's easy to lose count of
  • repaired — Repairing something means taking its broken pieces and fitting them back together differently, which is what happens to these letters.

The eighth boy in the line got a bit of somebody's car working again.

'Repaired' sits exactly where a solver expects a plain past tense, so the sentence reads as somebody fixing a car rather than as an instruction about letters.

8 down INCENSE

Source of sweet smell in faculty, reportedly (7)

Definition: Source of sweet smell · charade + homophone

INCENSE is the resin burned in churches and temples for its fragrance, so it literally is a source of sweet smell.

  • faculty → SENSE sight, hearing and smell are the senses, and a faculty is exactly one of those powers
  • in faculty → INCENSE run the little word and that five-letter word together, say them aloud, and they come out as one word spelled differently
  • reportedly — What is reported is spoken rather than written, so the letters have to be spelled out of the sound and not out of the words.

A pleasant scent said to be drifting through a university department.

A faculty is a university department in the surface and a bodily power in the wordplay, and it is the second sense the sound gets built on.

9 down EVICTION ORDER

A court could issue this direction over criminal (8,5)

Definition: A court could issue this · anagram

An EVICTION ORDER is the instruction a court issues requiring an occupier to leave a property, so the opening phrase points straight at it.

  • direction over → DIRECTIONOVER thirteen letters of fodder, the preposition counting as letters like any other word
  • criminal — A criminal is someone who will not keep to the rules, and letters that will not keep to the rules of their spelling come out rearranged.

A judge weighing up what instruction to hand down about an offender.

'Over criminal' reads as 'concerning a wrongdoer', which hides the fact that both words are letters to be shuffled rather than sense to be followed.

15 down DISHONOUR

Scandal is repressed by Dutch queen, perhaps (9)

Definition: Scandal · charade + container

DISHONOUR is the public disgrace attaching to someone whose wrongdoing comes out, which is what a scandal leaves behind.

  • Dutch → D D is the standard abbreviation for Dutch
  • is → IS the little word is taken straight off the page, letters and all
  • queen, perhaps → HONOUR in bridge and whist the honours are the top cards — ace, king, queen, jack, ten — so a queen is one instance of one
  • repressed by — Something repressed is held down and kept out of sight, so this word ends up buried among the letters on either side of it.

A scandal being quietly hushed up by a Dutch monarch.

'Perhaps' is the standard marker for definition by example: the queen is not the word wanted, only one of the things it covers.

18 down LOOK-SEE

Gents, maybe, with king appear largely for brief inspection (4-3)

Definition: brief inspection · charade + deletion

A LOOK-SEE is an informal quick glance at something to check it over, which is a brief inspection.

  • Gents, maybe → LOO the Gents is a British public lavatory, and loo is the everyday British word for one
  • king → K K is the standard abbreviation for king, as on a playing card or a chess score sheet
  • appear → SEEM to seem and to appear are the same verb, and the last letter of it goes
  • largely — Taking most of a thing but not all of it is what this asks for, so the word it follows keeps everything except its final letter.

The men's toilets and a king, by and large, turning up for a quick once-over.

The enumeration is the biggest hint here: a hyphenated four-and-three in a clue about a quick glance narrows the field hard before any parsing starts.

20 down EXACTLY

Times shown by a court in Fenland place in a precise way (7)

Definition: in a precise way · charade + container

EXACTLY means with complete precision, so the adverb stands in for the phrase 'in a precise way' word for word.

  • Fenland place → ELY Ely is the small cathedral city out in the Cambridgeshire fens
  • Times → X the multiplication sign, read aloud as 'times'
  • a → A the article is taken at face value
  • court → CT ct is the abbreviation for Court used in street addresses
  • in — The small word is doing positional work: whatever is in a place sits inside it, so the pieces before it get planted within the letters that follow.

The sitting times displayed by a courthouse somewhere out in the fens.

The Fens are the flat drained marshland of eastern England, and the cathedral city buried in this clue stands on what was once an island in them.

21 down IMMENSE

Small measure seen surprisingly to be huge (7)

Definition: huge · charade + anagram

IMMENSE means vast in extent or degree, an everyday synonym for huge that carries a hint of being past measuring.

  • Small measure → IMM one millimetre written out in symbols: the Roman numeral for one, then the two letters for millimetre
  • seen → ENSE the four letters of the word, resettled into another order
  • surprisingly — A surprise is a thing turning out other than expected, and letters turning out other than expected have swapped places.

Something tiny that turns out, against all expectation, to be enormous.

'Small' and 'huge' sit at opposite ends of one short sentence, and the setter is banking on you reading the first as part of the definition rather than as a unit of length.

22 down EDITED

Like a checked report with appeal kept by two journalists (6)

Definition: Like a checked report · charade + container

A report that has been EDITED has been gone over and corrected, so 'like a checked report' describes the state it is in.

  • two journalists → EDED ed is short for editor, and the clue asks for a pair of them side by side
  • appeal → IT IT is the crossword's shorthand for sex appeal
  • kept by — Something kept by somebody is held in their keeping, so this piece ends up shut inside the letters around it.

Two newspapermen hanging on to the charm in a piece of writing.

IT for sex appeal comes from Elinor Glyn's 1927 novel and the It girl who followed it, and crosswords have kept the sense long after ordinary speech dropped it.

25 down SWISH

Soprano has desire to be impressively smart (5)

Definition: impressively smart · charade

SWISH is British informal for smart in an expensive, eye-catching way: a swish hotel, a swish car.

  • Soprano → S S is the standard abbreviation for soprano on a choral score
  • desire → WISH a wish is a thing desired

An opera singer who wants to look glamorous.

The four choral voices all shorten to single letters on a score — S, A, T and B — so a clue naming any of them is usually after just the letter.
