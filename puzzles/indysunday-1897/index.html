Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,897

Across

1 across PISTON

Wedge hammered in restricts small moving part (6)

Definition: moving part · container

  • Wedge hammered in → PITON a piton is the metal wedge a climber hammers into a rock face
  • small → S S is the clothes-label abbreviation for small

The surface sounds like carpentry. The wedge is a climber's peg, and once that is seen the clue moves from the workshop to the engine.

4 across AFFIRM

Worried RAF leaders for instance make fighters stand by (6)

Definition: stand by · anagram + first letters

  • RAF → RAF the air force's initials go into the mix exactly as printed
  • instance make fighters → IMF the letter at the head of each of these three words joins the pile

Read straight it is a news bulletin about jittery commanders. In the wordplay only initials survive, and the three they give spell a famous monetary fund.

9 across ALTOGETHER

Quite good number by singer (10)

Definition: Quite · charade

  • singer → ALTO an alto is a singer whose voice sits between soprano and tenor
  • good → G G is the abbreviation for good, as on a school report
  • number → ETHER read 'number' as one that numbs, with a hard b, and the old anaesthetic answers to it

'Number' is the trap, and setters use it constantly: it is never a figure when the surface most wants it to be one.

10 across FACE

F1's brand representative? (4)

Definition: brand representative · charade

  • F → F the motor-racing formula is written with this letter, and it is used just as it stands
  • 1 → ACE the one of any suit is the ace

The question mark is doing real work. The definition is advertising jargon, and the clue only pretends to be about motorsport.

11 across LIMPET

Floppy alien creature adapted to Klingon, you hear? (6)

Definition: creature adapted to Klingon, you hear? · charade

  • Floppy → LIMP something floppy hangs limp
  • alien → ET the film alien everyone can name goes by two initials

The joke is in the definition rather than the wordplay: the Star Trek warrior, heard rather than read, turns into a description of what the creature does to a rock.

12 across TASTE BUD

Managing debts at university, might one signal bitterness? (5,3)

Definition: might one signal bitterness? · anagram

  • debts at → DEBTSAT seven of the eight letters, taken as these two words stand
  • university → U U is the standard abbreviation for university

The surface is a student in the red. 'At university' is not scene-setting but two more pieces of the mix, one of them abbreviated to a letter.

13 across ANTIQUE

E.g. Georgian opposed to what Spaniard says (7)

Definition: E.g. Georgian · charade

  • opposed to → ANTI someone opposed to a thing is anti it
  • what Spaniard says → QUE the Spanish for 'what' is que, so that is the word a Spaniard would use

Nothing is being sounded out here. The Spaniard is not mispronouncing English, he is simply speaking his own language, and 'says' means no more than that.

15 across ANDREW

Unlikely name for new baby aunt oddly picked at random (6)

Definition: Unlikely name for new baby · charade + alternate letters

  • aunt → AN first and third letters of the relative, the even ones discarded
  • picked at random → DREW to draw a name out of a hat is to pick it at random

The definition is topical rather than lexical. It is a perfectly ordinary boy's name, and only a royal scandal has made it an unlikely choice.

17 across GENERA

Article on green bananas named botanical types, say (6)

Definition: botanical types, say · anagram

  • Article → A the indefinite article is the single letter A
  • green → GREEN the colour joins the mix whole, all five of its letters

'Green bananas' is one image on the surface and two separate jobs underneath, the colour being the material and the fruit the instruction.

19 across ECLIPSE

Cover fastens in case of effervescence (7)

Definition: Cover · container + outer letters

  • effervescence → EE the first and last letters of the fizzing word, which happen to be the same letter twice
  • fastens → CLIPS to clip papers together is to fasten them

'Case of' is the giveaway once you know it: in a cryptic it never means a box, only the two ends of the word beside it.

21 across FLOURISH

Like meal to be successful? (8)

Definition: to be successful · charade

  • meal → FLOUR meal is ground grain, as in oatmeal and cornmeal, and so is what a mill turns out
  • Like → ISH the suffix -ish on the end of a word means rather like that thing: greenish, childish

'Like meal' is not a simile but a suffix instruction, which is how a four-word clue manages to be entirely fair.

23 across SPRAYS

Son asks earnestly for paints straight out of the can (6)

Definition: paints straight out of the can · charade

  • Son → S S is the genealogist's abbreviation for son
  • asks earnestly → PRAYS to pray is to ask earnestly, and the third-person -s comes along with it

The surface has a boy begging for art supplies. The definition is graffiti, and 'straight out of the can' is what makes it aerosol rather than brushwork.

24 across WADI

A little kid awoke, coming round in bed that's dried out (4)

Definition: bed that's dried out · hidden word + reversal

  • kid awoke → WADI four letters run consecutively across the gap between these two words, and an across entry reversed reads right to left

'That's dried out' sounds like a joke about someone giving up drink. It is geography, and the bed in question is a riverbed.

25 across IAN FLEMING

Dodgy file naming intelligence operative published in 1950s (3,7)

Definition: intelligence operative published in 1950s · anagram

  • file naming → FILENAMING the ten letters of these two words are the whole of the raw material

The surface is computer housekeeping. The definition is a biography squeezed into five words, and the date belongs to the novels rather than to the man.

26 across TRENDY

Shot, taking aim with it (6)

Definition: with it · container

  • Shot → TRY a shot at something is a try, in the have-a-go sense
  • aim → END an end is a purpose or an aim, as in 'to what end?'

'Shot' and 'aim' both point at a rifle range and neither is used that way: one is an attempt, the other a purpose.

27 across HOWDAH

Seat on jumbo had to be turned on using what? (6)

Definition: Seat on jumbo · charade + reversal

  • using what? → HOW asking by what means is asking how
  • had → DAH the past tense of 'have', its three letters set down from the back

'Turned on' looks like one phrase about switches. Splitting it is the step that unlocks the clue, since the closing question is a word in its own right.

Down

1 down PALPITATE

Receiving contrary tip, friend had flutter (9)

Definition: flutter · container + reversal

  • friend → PAL a friend is a pal
  • tip → PIT the word for a gratuity, its three letters written the other way round
  • had → ATE someone who had lunch ate it

'Receiving contrary tip' reads as a bribe that went the wrong way, which is the whole disguise: no money is involved anywhere in the wordplay.

2 down SHOT PUT

Group given lift in Transit up to HS2's trackside event (4,3)

Definition: trackside event · hidden word + reversal

  • Transit up to HS2's → SHOTPUT seven consecutive letters straddle these words, ignoring every space between them, and a down entry reads them from the bottom up

HS2 is the high-speed rail line, so every word of the surface is about trains. The letters pay no attention to where the words start and stop.

3 down OVERT

Exercise too much without shower in public (5)

Definition: in public · deletion

  • Exercise too much → OVERTRAIN an athlete who exercises too much overtrains
  • shower → RAIN a shower is a spell of rain, and those four letters are the ones that go

The surface is a gym joke about skipping the wash. Only the gym half is real: this shower is weather, not plumbing.

5 down FORESTALL

Anticipate logging area completely (9)

Definition: Anticipate · charade

  • logging area → FOREST trees are felled in a forest, so that is the area where logging happens
  • completely → ALL 'completely' and 'all' swap freely: all in, completely in

There is no indicator anywhere: the two halves simply sit end to end, which is how the clue gets away with reading as a plain sentence about forestry.

6 down INFIELD

Heathen has daughter dropped in marked area around bases (7)

Definition: marked area around bases · deletion + charade

  • Heathen → INFIEL a heathen is an infidel, and this is what is left of that word once the letter below has moved out
  • daughter → D D is the standard abbreviation for daughter, and it is the letter that shifts

The surface is a christening gone wrong. The answer is baseball, where the marked square holding all four bases is the diamond.

7 down MACAU

Lay off child who was Home Alone in busy city (5)

Definition: busy city · deletion

  • child who was Home Alone → MACAULAY the boy left behind in that film was played by Macaulay Culkin, and it is his first name that is wanted
  • Lay → LAY these three letters are the ones dismissed, stripped from the end of the name

'Lay off' reads as an instruction to leave the child in peace, which is exactly the film's plot and exactly the wrong sense of the phrase.

8 down WHITE ELEPHANT

Arranging new pet health insurance, primarily big waste of money (5,8)

Definition: big waste of money · anagram + first letter

  • new pet health → NEWPETHEALTH twelve of the thirteen letters, taken exactly as these three words stand
  • insurance → I the opening letter of the policy word supplies the thirteenth

The surface is household admin, and the join is hidden by 'primarily big': the adverb belongs to the letters before it, not to the size of the waste.

14 down QUALIFIED

Sentence of many years cut by one in court, subject to conditions (9)

Definition: subject to conditions · container

  • court → QUAD a quad is the enclosed courtyard of a college, and it opens up to take the rest
  • Sentence of many years → LIFE the sentence measured in decades is a life one
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one is I

'Court' is the trap. The legal one is all over the surface, and the word wanted is the college quadrangle instead.

16 down EVERY INCH

9 in complete list of Scottish islands (5,4)

Definition: 9 · charade

  • complete list of → EVERY a complete list of things leaves out none of them
  • Scottish islands → INCH inch is the Scots word for a small island: Inchcolm, Inchkeith, Inchmarnock

The definition is a cross-reference, so 9 across has to be solved first. The wordplay is a single pun: list all those islands and you have listed every Inch.

18 down ERUDITE

Doctor reunited with Newton leaving full of knowledge (7)

Definition: full of knowledge · anagram + deletion

  • reunited → REUNITED the eight letters of this word are the raw material
  • Newton → N N is the symbol for the newton, the SI unit of force, and that is the letter dropped

'Doctor' looks like a person in a sentence about scientists. It is the instruction, and the scientist is present only to donate a unit of force.

20 down PYRAMID

Wood for cremation almost in centre of mausoleum (7)

Definition: mausoleum · charade + deletion

  • Wood for cremation → PYR the stack of wood built for a cremation is a pyre, here docked of its final letter
  • in centre of → AMID 'amid' is a one-word substitute for the phrase, meaning in the middle of

The whole surface is a funeral, and every word of it belongs to the wordplay except the last, which is the tomb the clue is really describing.

22 down LEAST

Smallest portion left for banquet's starter (5)

Definition: Smallest portion · substitution

  • left → L L is the standard abbreviation for left
  • banquet → FEAST a banquet is a feast, and it is that word's opening letter which makes way

The whole clue is one swap. Read 'banquet' as its five-letter synonym first, then stand the abbreviation for left where the F was.

23 down SALVO

Five in comfortable bar save on round of shots (5)

Definition: round of shots · container + deletion

  • comfortable bar → SALOON the saloon bar of a pub is the comfortable one, with the upholstery and the carpet
  • on → ON these two letters are the ones excepted, and they come off the end
  • Five → V V is the Roman numeral for five

'Save on' reads as thrift and is really a subtraction, which is the disguise the whole clue rests on.
