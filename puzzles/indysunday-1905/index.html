Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,905

Across

1 across SPACE BAR

Box opened by wizard with bishop's long key (5,3)

Definition: long key · container + charade

  • Box → SPAR to box is to spar, trading punches in a gym
  • wizard → ACE a wizard at something is an ace at it, an expert
  • bishop → B B is the bishop in chess notation

The trap is reading 'Box' as a container a wizard opens; it is the verb, to fight. Chess supplies the B and the expert supplies the ACE, both worth banking.

6 across MIRAGE

Lip turned grey - effect of heat, you see (6)

Definition: effect of heat, you see · reversal + charade

  • Lip → MIR the lip of a cup is its rim, spun round here
  • grey → AGE to grey is to age, hair being the classic sign

The surface is a face losing its colour, which is why 'grey' reads as an adjective. It is the verb, and nothing before the dash is about heat at all.

9 across LIBRETTIST

Half of Stilgoe's Bitter ordered for Tim Rice? (10)

Definition: Tim Rice · anagram + deletion

  • Stilgoe's → STIL Richard Stilgoe is the British lyricist, and only his front half is wanted
  • Bitter → BITTER bitter is the English ale, and all six of its letters go into the mix

The surface is a drinks round at the theatre bar, and the two lyricists in it are doing different jobs: one supplies letters, the other is the definition.

10 across NONE

Sister reported an absence (4)

Definition: an absence · homophone

  • Sister → NONE a sister in a convent is a nun, and the two words are said the same way

'Sister' reads as a sibling until you say it out loud; the religious one is the sister the clue means, and the whole mechanism is in the sound.

11 across AT ONE FELL SWOOP

Leaflets now distributed round work all together (2,3,4,5)

Definition: all together · anagram

  • Leaflets now → LEAFLETSNOW eleven letters of fodder from the pamphlets and the moment
  • round → O a round shape is written as the letter O
  • work → OP op is short for opus, a composer's work

The trap is reading 'round' as an instruction to enclose something; here it is simply the round letter joining the fodder. Macduff gave the language the phrase, over his murdered family.

13 across CANDID

Frank's a hundred, with evidence to prove it (6)

Definition: Frank · charade

  • a hundred → C C is the Roman numeral for a hundred
  • with → AND the joining word is spelled out as the conjunction itself
  • evidence to prove it → ID the card you produce to prove who you are is your ID

The surface is a man named Frank turning a hundred with the birth certificate to show for it. Both scraps of wordplay are conventions worth banking.

14 across BAG LADY

Woman on the street delighted to fit into parking space (3,4)

Definition: Woman on the street · container

  • parking space → BAY a marked space for one car is a bay
  • delighted → GLAD glad is plain delighted

A parking bay is the painted rectangle you reverse into, and the surface is somebody thrilled to squeeze the car in.

15 across ASSUAGE

Temper made Gauss add brackets the wrong way (7)

Definition: Temper · hidden word + reversal

  • made Gauss add → EGAUSSA seven letters in a row, running across the joins of these three words

The mathematician is there to make 'add' look like arithmetic. Nothing is being calculated: the letters are simply lying in the sentence, facing the wrong way.

17 across OSTLER

Groom heartlessly pinched gold rings (6)

Definition: Groom · container + deletion

  • gold → OR or is gold in heraldry, the tincture on a coat of arms
  • pinched → STLE to pinch is to steal, and STOLE has lost the letter at its heart

'Groom' points at the stable rather than a wedding, and heraldic OR for gold is one of the conventions crosswords lean on hardest.

20 across HIPPOPOTAMUSES

Weed in cool river tickles bathers on the bottom (14)

Definition: bathers on the bottom · container + charade

  • cool → HIP hip is cool in the slang sense, fashionable and in the know
  • river → PO the Po is the long river across northern Italy, a crossword regular
  • Weed → POT pot is the common slang for cannabis
  • tickles → AMUSES to tickle somebody is to amuse them, as in tickled pink

Four separate pieces hide inside one smooth riverbank picture, and the definition is the whimsical half: the setter is describing what is standing on the riverbed.

22 across CAGE

Composer who wanted quiet shut up (4)

Definition: Composer who wanted quiet · double definition

  • Composer who wanted quiet → CAGE the American whose 4'33" is four and a half minutes of players not playing
  • shut up → CAGE to shut an animal up is to put it behind bars

The definition by example is the harder half: you need the composer whose most famous piece is silence, and then the everyday verb sitting beside him.

23 across BRAIN DRAIN

Loss of brightness, with black day between spells of bad weather (5,5)

Definition: Loss of brightness · charade

  • black → B B is black on a pencil and in chess
  • spells of bad weather → RAIN a spell of bad weather is a spell of rain
  • day → D d is the standard short form of day in a date
  • spells of bad weather → RAIN the plural says there are two such spells, one on each side

The surface is a weather forecast and the answer is about emigration. 'Spells' is the word doing the quiet work, since it has to be read as two of them.

24 across PEANUT

One contributing to very little? (6)

Definition: One contributing to very little? · cryptic definition

  • One contributing to → the surface offers a person chipping in their small share of something
  • very little → a derisory wage is always talked about in the plural, and the clue asks for a single one of them

There is no wordplay to unpick: the whole sentence is the definition. The move is spotting that the plural slang phrase for a trivial sum can be cut down to one of them.

25 across SOYA MILK

Vegan option really kind hosts left for Yolanda in the morning (4,4)

Definition: Vegan option · container + charade + substitution

  • really → SO so really is so tired, in the intensifying sense
  • left for Yolanda → Y left is L, and the clue trades it in for the letter Yolanda begins with
  • in the morning → AM am marks the hours before noon
  • kind → ILK an ilk is a kind or sort, as in others of that ilk

'Kind' is the noun, not the adjective, and the smooth breakfast surface about generous hosts is built to keep you reading it the other way.

2 down PRIMA

Italian's first proper answer (5)

Definition: Italian's first · charade

  • proper → PRIM prim means proper in the starchy, disapproving sense
  • answer → A A is the standard short form of answer, as on a quiz sheet

'Italian's first' looks like an instruction to take the opening letter of Italian; it is asking for the Italian word for first instead.

3 down CARTOON

E.g. Calvin and Hobbes love getting into wagon and going (7)

Definition: E.g. Calvin and Hobbes · container + charade

  • wagon → CART a cart is the horse-drawn wagon
  • love → O love is nil in tennis, written as the round nought
  • and going → ON a machine that is going is on

Love for O comes from tennis scoring and turns up everywhere. The surface reads as a child's day out, which is what hides the fact that two of its words are a title.

4 down BUTTERING UP

Charming parent stuck with Aunt Ethel every so often (9,2)

Definition: Charming · container + alternate letters

  • parent → BRING UP to bring up a child is to parent them
  • Aunt Ethel → UTTE counting through the two names and keeping the second letter of each pair yields four

'Parent' is the verb here, the thing you do to a child rather than the person doing it, and that shift is what keeps the long phrase out of sight.

5 down RAIDER

One could be cavalryman crossing area's frontier (6)

Definition: One could be cavalryman crossing area's frontier · container + first letter + &lit

  • cavalryman → RIDER a cavalryman fights on horseback, so he is a rider
  • area's frontier → A the leading letter of area, its border on the page

Every word does double duty: the same sentence that pictures a horseman slipping over a border is also the instruction that builds him.

6 down MET

Police came to rally (3)

Definition: Police · double definition

  • Police → MET London's Metropolitan force, known everywhere by its short name
  • came to rally → MET people who came together for a gathering assembled there

Three words, no joinery, and the seam falls after the first one. The surface pretends to report a demonstration being policed.

7 down RENEWAL

We learn in a forced way, repetition (7)

Definition: repetition · anagram

  • We learn → WELEARN seven letters of fodder, straight from the opening two words

The surface sounds like a grumble about rote schooling, so the last word reads as part of that complaint rather than as the definition sitting at the end.

8 down GUNPOWDER

Prisoner charges grand less than original charge for weapons (9)

Definition: original charge for weapons · container + charade

  • grand → G G is the short form of a grand, a thousand pounds
  • less than → UNDER less than a figure is under it
  • Prisoner → POW a prisoner of war is a POW

'Charges' looks like part of the definition, since a charge is what a weapon takes, and it is the instruction instead.

12 down LOADSAMONEY

Big spender delays start of ambitious moon shot (11)

Definition: Big spender · anagram + first letter

  • delays → DELAYS six letters of fodder, from the hold-ups on the launch pad
  • start of ambitious → A the opening letter of ambitious joins the mix
  • moon → MOON four more letters, straight from the night sky

Comedian Harry Enfield's plasterer waved his wad at the camera in 1988 and handed British English a word for a flashy spender. The rocket surface hides him completely.

13 down CASTIGATE

Exit from garden behind shed I slam (9)

Definition: slam · charade

  • shed → CAST a snake sheds its skin by casting it off
  • I → I the letter is taken at face value
  • Exit from garden → GATE the way out of a walled garden is its gate

'Shed' is the verb, to cast off, and not the hut at the bottom of the garden, and that swap is the whole trick of the surface.

16 down UMPTEEN

Many men up there in trouble without her (7)

Definition: Many · anagram + deletion

  • men up → MENUP five letters of fodder, taken as they stand
  • there → TE HER comes out of THERE and two letters survive

This entry runs down the grid, so 'up' looks like an instruction to reverse something. It lifts nothing here; it is just two more letters to shuffle.

18 down LUSTRUM

Liberal America curtailed president's five year term (7)

Definition: five year term · charade + deletion

  • Liberal → L L is the ballot-paper abbreviation for Liberal
  • America → US the United States, shortened as ever
  • president → TRUM Trump is the president, with his final letter docked

The surface is all politics, and the definition is Roman: an American president's term runs four years, not five, which is the wrong scent the clue lays.

19 down STRAYS

Queen wearing corset gets out of control (6)

Definition: gets out of control · container

  • corset → STAYS stays is the old word for a corset, stiffened with whalebone
  • Queen → R R is Regina, the Latin queen a monarch signs herself

R for Regina is the crossword's standard queen, and stays for a corset is worth banking too, since Victorian underwear turns up in grids far more than in shops.

21 down STILL

Boiling apparatus not bubbling yet (5)

Definition: Boiling apparatus · double definition

  • Boiling apparatus → STILL the copper vessel in which a wash is boiled to make whisky
  • not bubbling yet → STILL water sold without gas is flat, and the same word also means even now

Three senses, one word, and the whole clue reads as a single picture of a pot that has not come to the boil, which is what does the hiding.

23 down BAT

Club spa where Henry has gone (3)

Definition: Club · deletion

  • spa → BATH the Somerset city of Bath, built on Roman hot springs, is England's spa town
  • Henry → the henry is the unit of inductance and its symbol is the single letter H

Two facts have to be held at once: the West Country spa city the Romans built, and the electrical unit named after Joseph Henry.
