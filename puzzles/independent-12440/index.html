Across

1 across COMPASS

Limit kindness? No one turned away (7)

Definition: Limit · deletion + reversal

  • kindness → COMPASSION kindness shown to someone in trouble is compassion
  • No one → 'No' plus I for one spells NOI, and read the other way that is the ION which goes

The surface is a grumble about rationed kindness, so 'No one' looks like a phrase rather than letters to be turned round and thrown out.

5 across MODICUM

Little doctor’s pronouncement, taking temperature (7)

Definition: Little · charade + deletion

  • doctor’s → MO a medical officer is an MO, and the apostrophe-s carries on into the next word
  • pronouncement → DICTUM a formal pronouncement is a dictum
  • temperature → temperature is T, and that is the letter lifted out of DICTUM

'Little doctor' reads as one phrase and is really the seam of the clue: the definition stops after a single word.

9 across NIX

Cancel nine to begin with? Nine (3)

Definition: Cancel · charade + first letter

  • nine → N the letter nine begins with
  • Nine → IX nine in Roman numerals is IX

The same word twice, read two ways — English initial, then Roman numeral — which is why the second 'Nine' looks like a definition.

10 across DOUBLE-CROSS

Cheat, repeating final letter of last answer? (6-5)

Definition: Cheat · charade + last letter

  • repeating → DOUBLE to repeat a thing is to double it
  • last answer → CROSS the entry numbered before this one is 9 across; it stops on X, and X is a cross

This one points out of itself at the grid, so it cannot be started until the entry numbered before it has already gone in.

11 across UNDERWRITE

What insurer may do, governed by legal document (European) (10)

Definition: What insurer may do · charade

  • governed by → UNDER a country governed by a ruler is under them
  • legal document → WRIT a writ is the court's written order
  • European → E E is the usual abbreviation for European

There is no indicator at all here — the pieces simply sit in order — so the only thing to spot is where the definition stops.

12 across SKUA

Seabird a European country’s repulsed (4)

Definition: Seabird · reversal + charade

  • a → A the indefinite article taken as itself
  • European country’s → UKS the UK is a European country, and the apostrophe-s adds an S

Read forwards the wordplay gives a different seabird entirely, which is the joke: the auks are in plain sight and facing the wrong way.

14 across RAMROD

Staff concealing a title for man, a strict disciplinarian (6)

Definition: a strict disciplinarian · container + charade

  • Staff → ROD a staff carried as a stick is a rod
  • a → A the article taken as itself
  • title for man → MR Mr is the everyday title for a man

The split falls after the first letter of the staff word, and a container that opens one letter in is very nearly invisible.

16 across FREAK OUT

Arrangement of Take Four? How Brubeck might respond (5,3)

Definition: How Brubeck might respond · anagram

  • Take Four → FREAK OUT the eight letters of TAKE FOUR, rearranged

Brubeck's quartet recorded 'Take Five', and the italics dress the two words up as a real title — being one digit out is the whole joke.

18 across MESSAGES

More than one report gets old after confusion (8)

Definition: More than one report · charade

  • confusion → MESS a mess is confusion
  • gets old → AGES whatever gets old ages

The definition spells the plural out in words instead of adding an S, which is the setter being scrupulous — 'report' alone would not be fair.

19 across HOOKED

Under compulsion, Home Office initially agreed (6)

Definition: Under compulsion · charade + first letters

  • Home Office → HO the openings of Home and Office, which is also the department's real abbreviation
  • agreed → OKED to have agreed to something is to have OK'd it

The letters produce the very abbreviation the words already have, so the wordplay and the surface arrive in the same place and nothing jars.

22 across MACE

Spicy stuff: hint of masochism excellent (4)

Definition: Spicy stuff · charade + first letter

  • masochism → M the opening letter of masochism
  • excellent → ACE something excellent is ace

Spice and a hint of masochism make one unpleasant picture, and that picture is all that holds the two halves of the clue together.

23 across BATTLESHIP

Tablet’s revamped trendy element of pen-and-paper game (10)

Definition: element of pen-and-paper game · charade + anagram

  • Tablet’s → BATTLES the seven letters of TABLETS made over
  • trendy → HIP something trendy is hip

'Tablet' and 'trendy' both belong to the gadget surface, which is why neither reads as raw material for a game played on squared paper.

26 across STORYTELLER

Fabulist, believer in markets, perhaps, keeping right-winger tense (11)

Definition: Fabulist · container + charade

  • believer in markets, perhaps → SELLER 'perhaps' offers an example: one sort of believer in markets is a seller
  • right-winger → TORY a Tory sits on the right wing of British politics
  • tense → T T is the grammarian's abbreviation for tense

The politics are a decoy — a market believer and a right-winger belong in one sentence, and neither has anything to do with fables.

27 across RUN

One avoiding loss in delivery round? (3)

Definition: delivery round · deletion

  • loss → RUIN a loss that finishes you is your ruin
  • One → one is the Roman numeral I, and it is the letter keeping clear

Cricket owns every word of the surface — a delivery, a round, avoiding a loss — and not one of them is doing that job in the parse.

28 across NOMINEE

Candidate: a lot of negative votes about my selection? (7)

Definition: Candidate · container + deletion

  • negative votes → NOES the noes are the votes cast against
  • a lot of → the final S of NOES comes off
  • my selection → MINE whatever I pick out is mine

'my selection' is the sly one: not a choice and not a coal seam, but the possessive — the thing that is mine.

29 across SECRETS

Religious groups gathering about things to be revealed? (7)

Definition: things to be revealed · container

  • Religious groups → SECTS sects are religious groups
  • about → RE RE is the business letter's 'about', as in 'Re: your enquiry'

A gathering of sects is a perfectly good picture in its own right, which is why 'gathering' never announces itself as an instruction.

Down

1 down CONQUER

Do well to study question, eliminating unknown component (7)

Definition: Do well · charade + deletion

  • study → CON to con something is to study it closely, an old sense kept alive by crosswords
  • question → QUERY a question put to someone is a query
  • unknown component → algebra's unknown quantity is Y, and Y is the letter taken out of QUERY

The surface is sound exam advice, so nothing in it reads as an instruction; even 'eliminating' passes for revision strategy.

2 down MIXED

The writer’s upset Times editor in confusion (5)

Definition: in confusion · charade + reversal

  • The writer’s → MI the writer of a clue is I, so 'the writer's' is I'M, tipped over
  • Times → X the times sign in arithmetic is a cross, X
  • editor → ED Ed is the standard short form for an editor

The italics make 'Times' look like the newspaper, which is why the arithmetic reading takes so long to arrive.

3 down ALDERWOMAN

Civic dignitary possibly now alarmed (10)

Definition: Civic dignitary · anagram

  • now alarmed → ALDERWOMAN the ten letters of NOW ALARMED, put in a different order

'possibly' looks like the hedge a setter adds to an approximate definition, and it is really the instruction — a maybe and an order in one word.

4 down SQUIRM

Knight embracing Queen and maiden, making you feel uncomfortable (6)

Definition: feel uncomfortable · container + charade

  • Knight → SIR a knight is addressed as Sir
  • Queen → QU Qu. is the recognised short form of Queen
  • maiden → M M is the cricket scorer's mark for a maiden over

Chess owns both a knight and a queen, so the surface holds together as a board position right up to the maiden — which belongs to cricket instead.

5 down MILITARY

Landed area in muddy surroundings for the army (8)

Definition: for the army · container + charade

  • muddy → MIRY ground thick with mud is miry
  • Landed → LIT a bird that has landed has lit on its perch
  • area → A A is the standard abbreviation for area

'Landed area' reads as a plot of ground, which is exactly the wrong reading: the two words are separate pieces from quite different senses.

6 down DUCK

Avoid coarse cotton, dear (4)

Definition: Avoid · double definition

  • dear → DUCK a third definition rather than wordplay: a Midlands and northern term of endearment

Three unrelated senses in four words, and only the first is the one everybody knows; the bird never appears at all.

7 down CLOCKWORK

Face elevated argument over last of steampunk machinery (9)

Definition: machinery · charade + reversal + last letter

  • Face → CLOCK a clock has a face, and 'clock' is also slang for the human one
  • argument → WOR an argument is a row, raised up the entry so it reads WOR
  • last of steampunk → K steampunK finishes on K

Unusually the surface and the answer agree — steampunk really is brass gears — so the misdirection has to live in the small word 'over'.

8 down MISCAST

One starts to seem crazy, drilling section of yacht with the wrong part (7)

Definition: with the wrong part · container + charade + first letters

  • section of yacht → MAST the mast is the section of a yacht that carries the sail
  • One → I I is one in Roman numerals
  • seem crazy → SC the openings of Seem and Crazy

The surface is a boatyard job — drilling a yacht section, fitting the wrong part — and every word of it is really about the theatre.

13 down BAROMETRIC

Describing pressure arising from wandering of erratic mob (10)

Definition: Describing pressure · anagram

  • erratic mob → BAROMETRIC the ten letters of ERRATIC MOB in a new order

'Erratic' describes a mob perfectly and reads like a shuffling signal too — but it is the material, and 'wandering' is the instruction.

15 down MUSIC ROOM

American mother keeping various curios in playing area (5,4)

Definition: playing area · container + anagram

  • American mother → MOM an American calls his mother Mom
  • curios → USICRO the six letters of CURIOS in a different order

The surface is a house full of knick-knacks, and 'various curios' is exactly the phrase you would use about them, so it never reads as an order.

17 down LEGALESE

Storms penetrating sheltered zone? Here’s the small print on policy (8)

Definition: small print on policy · container

  • sheltered zone → LEE the lee is the side out of the wind
  • Storms → GALES gales are storms of wind

Shelter and gales are opposites, so the surface reads as weather and the container hides inside the contradiction.

18 down MU MESON

Particle’s energy absorbed by quiet child (2,5)

Definition: Particle · container + charade

  • quiet → MUM to keep mum is to stay quiet
  • energy → E E is the physicist's symbol for energy
  • child → SON a son is a child

'quiet child' reads as one description of a well-behaved infant, and the clue needs it as two unrelated words with a gap between them.

20 down DAPHNIS

Famous lover's lust for one with husband - apartment overturned (7)

Definition: Famous lover · charade + reversal

  • lust → SIN lust is one of the seven deadly sins
  • husband → H H is the standard abbreviation for husband
  • apartment → PAD somebody's pad is their apartment

Greek pastoral romance is a long way from a lustful husband in a flat, and that distance is the whole of the misdirection.

21 down STYLUS

Pen, mostly attractive - digital pen? (6)

Definition: digital pen? · charade + deletion

  • Pen → STY a pig's pen is a sty
  • attractive → LUSH something attractive can be called lush
  • mostly → the H at the end of LUSH is dropped

The clue prints 'pen' twice in unrelated senses — an enclosure for animals, then something you write with — and the dash is all that separates them.

24 down HORDE

Lots of people taking road through garden (5)

Definition: Lots of people · container

  • garden → HOE to garden is to hoe
  • road → RD Rd is the abbreviation on any street sign

'Garden' is a verb here and not a place, and in a clue of seven words that single reassignment is the whole difficulty.

25 down HYMN

Song - again my heart will soar? Not entirely (4)

Definition: Song · hidden word + reversal

  • again my heart → HYMN agai(n my h)eart holds N MY H, and climbing the entry those letters read the other way

The romantic surface does all the work: nothing in 'again my heart' looks like material, and 'Not entirely' reads as a rueful afterthought.
