Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,085

Across

1 across INCLUSIVENESS

Senses uncivil rioting – the participation of everybody? (13)

Definition: the participation of everybody · anagram

  • Senses uncivil → SENSESUNCIVIL anagram fodder, 13 letters — exactly the enumeration
  • rioting → anagram indicator

A long anagram hides best when its fodder reads as an opinion rather than a bag of letters, and 'Senses uncivil' does. Counting the fodder against the (13) is what breaks it open.

10 across NATASHA

Exclamation of success after recalling devil woman? (7)

Definition: woman · charade + reversal

  • devil → NATAS Satan is the devil, written backwards by 'recalling'
  • Exclamation of success → HA 'Ha!' is the cry of triumph

The question mark is doing real work: a name can only ever be an example of 'woman', so the setter has to flag it. Satan backwards is a device you will meet again.

11 across CAMBIUM

Tramp beside Cambridge river taking in one feature of arboreal growth (7)

Definition: feature of arboreal growth · charade + container

  • Cambridge river → CAM the Cam runs through Cambridge
  • Tramp → BUM a bum is a tramp, and it opens to take the next piece
  • one → I the Roman numeral one

The Cam is the crossword's default river, turning up whenever Cambridge or its university is mentioned, and 'bum' for tramp is the American register the surface quietly borrows.

12 across GET AT

Mean for one to return rubbish (3,2)

Definition: Mean · charade + reversal

  • for one → GE 'for one' is e.g., turned round by 'to return'
  • rubbish → TAT tat is cheap rubbish

'For one' is the everyday way of saying e.g., and an abbreviation is fair game to reverse — two letters are as reversible as ten.

13 across HARD CIDER

Woman’s concealing a way with detectives and juice (4,5)

Definition: juice · container + charade

  • Woman’s → HER the woman's = her, and it splits to hide the rest
  • a → A the clue's own 'a', taken at face value
  • way → RD rd = road, the crossword's usual way
  • detectives → CID the CID is the Criminal Investigation Department

'Woman's' is asking for the possessive HER rather than a noun, a small grammatical step that is easy to miss while the surface reads as somebody hiding something.

14 across TORCH

Light bit of rest in hospital bed rejected (5)

Definition: Light · charade + reversal + container + first letter

  • hospital → H H is the map-and-crossword abbreviation for hospital
  • bed → COT a cot is a bed; behind the H it makes HCOT, which 'rejected' turns round
  • bit of rest → R 'bit of' takes the first letter of rest

Note the scope of 'rejected': it turns the whole hospital bed round, not just the bed. An instruction at the end of a clue reaches back over everything the phrase covers.

16 across GOOSE STEP

Fascist movement? Sees to changes among Republicans (5,4)

Definition: Fascist movement · anagram + container

  • Republicans → GOP the GOP is the Grand Old Party, and it splits to hold the anagram
  • Sees to → OSESTE the letters of SEES TO, rearranged by 'changes'

GOP for the Republicans is American shorthand a British puzzle assumes you know, and the surface — Republicans making changes — is where all the misdirection sits.

18 across E-PAYMENTS

Mean types ruined online transactions (1-8)

Definition: online transactions · anagram

  • Mean types → MEANTYPES anagram fodder, nine letters
  • ruined → anagram indicator

The enumeration is the tell: (1-8) means one letter, a hyphen and an eight-letter word, and in a modern crossword that opening letter is nearly always the e- of email or e-commerce.

19 across KHAKI

Book launch finally having a lot of similar military material (5)

Definition: military material · charade + last letters + deletion

  • Book launch finally → KH the last letters of Book and launch
  • a lot of similar → AKI akin means similar, and 'a lot of' keeps all but its last letter

'Finally' can harvest the last letter of every word in front of it, not just one. Reading it as a plural instruction is what makes the opening pair available.

20 across REASSURED

Relieved apprehension in various readers about The Guardians volte-face (9)

Definition: Relieved apprehension · anagram + container + reversal

  • readers → REASRED the letters of readers, shuffled by 'various'
  • The Guardian → SU the paper printing the puzzle is US, and a volte-face turns it about

The Guardian calling itself 'us' is the setter winking at the paper the puzzle appears in — a trick every publication's crossword allows itself, and one to watch for in the Times too.

23 across INSET

Where many maps show Alaska and Hawaii? This month around Spain (5)

Definition: Where many maps show Alaska and Hawaii? · container

  • This month → INST inst. is the old commercial abbreviation for the current month
  • Spain → E E is Spain's international vehicle code, from España

Two abbreviations to bank here: inst for the present month, still lurking in old business letters, and E for Spain from the plate on the back of the car.

24 across NAIVIST

Note excellent view, largely ingenuous (7)

Definition: ingenuous · charade + deletion

  • Note → N n. is the standard written abbreviation for note
  • excellent → AI A1 means first-rate, and the crossword spells its numeral as the letter I
  • view, largely → VIST a VISTA is a view, and 'largely' keeps all but its last letter

A1 written as AI is the convention worth banking: whenever a clue says excellent or top-notch, expect the numeral one to be doing duty as the letter I.

25 across NOISOME

Offensive is torpedoing rotating satellite close to home (7)

Definition: Offensive · anagram + last letter

  • is → IS the clue's own 'is' joins the fodder
  • satellite → MOON a moon is a natural satellite, and it supplies the bulk of the letters
  • close to home → E 'close to' takes the last letter of home

Noisome has nothing to do with noise: it means foul or offensive, and comes from the same root as annoy. That false friend is the whole difficulty of the clue.

26 across PINE TREE STATE

Maine – Eastern and petite, possibly (4,4,5)

Definition: Maine · anagram

  • Eastern and petite → EASTERNPETITE anagram fodder, 13 letters
  • possibly → anagram indicator

With thirteen letters to place, working out what sort of phrase you are building matters more than the letters themselves — and American state nicknames are fair game in a British cryptic.

Down

2 down NATATORIA

North American cheers most of experiment involving old swimming pools (9)

Definition: swimming pools · charade + deletion + container

  • North American → NA NA is the standard abbreviation for North American
  • cheers → TA 'cheers' in its thank-you sense is TA
  • most of experiment → TRIA a TRIAL is an experiment, and 'most of' drops its last letter
  • old → O o = old, one of the workhorse crossword abbreviations

A natatorium is a building put up around an indoor pool, and its plural swaps the Latin -um for -a, the same way stadium goes to stadia.

3 down LISZT

Composer’s inclination for radio broadcast? (5)

Definition: Composer · homophone

  • inclination → LISZT a ship's list is its inclination

Franz Liszt's surname is pronounced 'list' in English, so a foreign name is being reached through an ordinary English word. The nautical sense of list, a lean to one side, is the one to bank.

4 down STASH

Hidden material – timber under stone (5)

Definition: Hidden material · charade

  • stone → ST st is the abbreviation for stone, the imperial weight
  • timber → ASH ash is a hardwood, so it answers to timber

'Under' places a piece further down the grid, so in a down entry the word named first ends up second. Expect that inversion whenever a down clue says under, below or supporting.

5 down VICARIOUS

Malevolent, grabbing a lot of power at second hand (9)

Definition: at second hand · container + deletion

  • Malevolent → VICIOUS vicious = malevolent, and it opens up to take the other piece
  • a lot of power → AR arm carries the sense of might — the arm of the law — and 'a lot of' keeps all but its last letter

'Power' for ARM is the muscular sense: the arm of the law, force of arms. That equation is the piece of vocabulary the clue is really testing.

6 down NAMECHECK

Mention Australian mechanics – something audacious about that (9)

Definition: Mention · container + charade

  • something audacious → NECK neck is brass nerve — 'you've got some neck' — and it splits to wrap the rest
  • Australian → A A is the abbreviation for Australian
  • mechanics → MECH mech is the short form of mechanics

Neck for nerve is slang worth keeping. The 'that' is the setter pointing back at words already used rather than introducing anything new, which is how a short clue can afford two pieces of wordplay.

7 down SKIED

Travelled in winter, perhaps raised aloft (5)

Definition: Travelled in winter, perhaps · double definition

  • Travelled in winter, perhaps → SKIED the winter-sports verb: to ski is to travel on skis
  • raised aloft → SKIED in cricket, to sky a ball is to hit it steeply into the air

The 'perhaps' is honest labelling: skiing is only one way of travelling in winter. Both halves are past tenses of verbs, which is the join a double definition always needs.

8 down KNIGHTS ERRANT

Adventurous types in middle of Hawkins – broadcast of Stranger Things two-thirds complete (7,6)

Definition: Adventurous types · charade + anagram + deletion + middle letter

  • in middle of Hawkins → K HAWKINS has seven letters, so its middle one is K
  • Stranger Things two-thirds complete → STRANGERTHIN STRANGER in full, then two-thirds of THINGS — four of its six letters

Hawkins is the town Stranger Things is set in, so the surface is one thought while the wordplay treats the two halves separately. A fraction of a word is rare enough to be worth counting twice.

9 down IMPROPRIETIES

The writer’s supporting Minister (mostly) about relationship and scandalous behaviours (13)

Definition: scandalous behaviours · charade + container + deletion

  • The writer’s → IM the setter writing as himself: the writer's = I'M
  • supporting → PRO pro means in favour of, so supporting
  • Minister (mostly) → PRIES a priest is a minister, and 'mostly' drops the last letter
  • relationship → TIE a tie is a bond or relationship, and it goes inside

'The writer' is the setter talking about himself, one of a small family of first-person conventions: it yields I, I'M or ME depending on what the apostrophe is doing.

15 down HAMPSHIRE

Source of heavy band equipment on charter in southern county (9)

Definition: southern county · charade + first letter

  • Source of heavy → H the first letter of heavy
  • band equipment → AMPS amps are what a band plugs into
  • charter → HIRE to charter a boat or coach is to hire it

'Source of' is one of the quiet indicators — no shuffling, no hiding, just the head of the word — and 'charter' is wanted as a verb here, not as a document.

16 down GENERATOR

Something that makes classes rushed? Not entirely (9)

Definition: Something that makes · charade + deletion

  • classes → GENERA genera are biological classes, the plural of genus
  • rushed → TOR tore is rushed, and 'Not entirely' leaves the last letter off

'Not entirely' sits at the end but only reaches back to 'rushed': a trimming instruction takes the word beside it, never the whole clue.

17 down TRANSPORT

Coaches? Coaches left after I disembarked (9)

Definition: Coaches? · charade + deletion

  • Coaches → TRANS the second Coaches is railway carriages, TRAINS, with the I stepping off
  • left → PORT port is the left-hand side of a ship
  • I disembarked → the letter taken out: I leaves TRAINS

The joke is one word doing opposite jobs: the first Coaches defines, the second is rolling stock. Repeating a word like that is a legitimate device, not a slip.

21 down ALIBI

Excuse a sexual impulse, but not act (5)

Definition: Excuse · charade + deletion

  • a → A the clue's own 'a', taken at face value
  • sexual impulse → LIBI LIBIDO is the sexual impulse, with two letters removed
  • act → act = DO, and DO is the pair of letters coming off LIBIDO

The deletion works on a word the clue never spells out and removes another it only hints at, so you have to reach LIBIDO and read 'act' as DO before anything can be trimmed.

22 down DANTE

Italian writer not ultimately hosted by another European (5)

Definition: Italian writer · container + last letter

  • another European → DANE a Dane is another European, and here the host word
  • not ultimately → T 'ultimately' takes the last letter of not

'Not' is raw material here rather than a negative, which is the trap. 'Ultimately' always means the final letter of the word beside it and never the word itself.

23 down IDIOT

One heartless sod is stupid (5)

Definition: stupid · charade + deletion

  • One → I the Roman numeral one
  • sod → DIOT a divot is a sod of turf, minus the letter at its centre
  • heartless → the heart of DIVOT is its middle letter V, and that is what goes

'Sod' is turf here rather than an insult — a divot is the lump a golfer digs out of the grass — and 'heartless' always means the middle letter leaves.
