Across

1 across LAPLANDER

Finn’s plot dividing the French and the German (9)

Definition: Finn · container

  • plot → PLAN a plot is a plan or scheme
  • the French → LA 'the' in French
  • the German → DER 'the' in German

Borrowed definite articles are a setter's favourite trick: 'the French' and 'the German' ask for the foreign word for 'the', not for a nationality. This clue uses two of them at once.

6 across JOKER

Comedian fine being embraced by Jeremy, half-cut (5)

Definition: Comedian · container + deletion

  • fine → OK fine = OK
  • Jeremy → JER 'half-cut' keeps only the first half of JEREMY — the wrapper

'Half-cut' reads as drunk but means exactly what it says: only half of Jeremy survives. One of several playing cards dealt through this puzzle.

10 across BUSHCRAFT

Topiary techniques essential in rural Australia (9)

Definition: Topiary techniques · double definition

  • Topiary techniques → BUSHCRAFT read literally: craft practised on bushes — which is what topiary is
  • essential in rural Australia → BUSHCRAFT the wilderness survival skill, vital in the Australian bush

The clue splits after 'techniques'. Read one way, shaping bushes is literally bush craft; read the other, bushcraft is the survival skill of the outback.

11 across TRUMP CARDS

President’s concealing scoundrel entertaining Royal (originally!) – they have an advantage (5,5)

Definition: they have an advantage · container + container + first letter

  • President’s → TRUMPS President Trump plus the possessive S — the outer wrapper
  • scoundrel → CAD a cad is a scoundrel
  • Royal → R 'originally' takes its first letter

Two containers nested one inside the other, which is rare enough to be worth taking slowly. 'Originally' is the first-letter signal, and the card theme runs through the surface and the answer alike.

12 across DEAL

Lay out banquet, perhaps, discounting a thousand – five hundred instead (4)

Definition: Lay out · deletion + charade

  • banquet, perhaps → MEAL a banquet is an example of a meal
  • a thousand → M Roman numeral M = 1,000, taken away
  • five hundred → D Roman numeral D = 500, put in M's place

A banquet is a meal 'perhaps'; then one Roman numeral is discounted and another put in its place — letter swaps like this are worth learning to spot.

14 across HAGGLED

'Finally' harvests the last letter of each of the seven preceding words — the designer-handbag bargain is pure window dressing.

15 across SANGRIA

  • inevitably abuse → IA their starting letters

Three mechanisms in a row: an abbreviation, ANGRY docked of its tail, then two initials. The soprano's tantrum is only surface noise.

22 across WATCHSTRAP

Might this keep you to time? (10)

Definition: Might this keep you to time? · cryptic definition

  • Might this → 'this' points at the thing itself — the answer is the object being described, not something it does
  • keep you to time → not 'make you punctual', which is how the surface wants you to read it: take it literally as holding something against you, and 'time' as the object that tells it

A pure cryptic definition: the strap keeps your watch — the timekeeper — attached to you, so it keeps you 'to time'. The question mark flags the pun.

'Anaesthetist' is read whimsically as NUMB-ER — something that numbs — and being 'on E' is being under ecstasy's influence.
