Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,887

Across

1 across CLEMATIS

Climber melted last ice with bit of muesli (8)

Definition: Climber · anagram + first letter

  • last ice → LASTICE seven letters handed over whole, in an order the solver has to abandon
  • muesli → M the opening letter of the cereal supplies the eighth

Everything in the surface is a mountain: a climber, melting ice, breakfast before the ascent. The climber wanted is a plant on a garden wall.

6 across CHICHI

That woman sounded doubly pretentious (6)

Definition: pretentious · homophone

  • That woman → SHE the pronoun that stands in for a female person
  • sounded doubly → CHICHI say that pronoun twice and the sound is spelt this way in the word English took from French

The pronoun does not contribute a single letter of its own spelling; only the noise it makes survives, and French spelling supplies the rest.

9 across SKIDDY

Small child having very weak grip (6)

Definition: having very weak grip · charade

  • Small → S S is small on any clothing label
  • child → KIDDY kiddy is the nursery word for a young child

The surface is a toddler with a feeble grasp. The definition belongs to roads and tyres, where grip is the friction that keeps a car pointing straight.

10 across EXACTING

Difficult pretending to be someone else on X (8)

Definition: Difficult · charade

  • X → EX the letter written out the way it is said
  • pretending to be someone else → ACTING playing a part you are not is acting

X is the social network, and the impostor on it is what holds the sentence together. The wordplay only wants the letter's name, said aloud as two.

11 across BLANDISHMENT

Guys that are fairly dull last to expect flattery (12)

Definition: flattery · charade + last letter

  • that are fairly dull → BLANDISH bland is dull, and the -ish on the end is what makes it only fairly so
  • Guys → MEN guys are men in everyday speech
  • expect → T the final letter of the verb

The -ish suffix is the piece worth keeping: setters attach it to an adjective to mean 'roughly', and it buys them eight letters that look like a description.

13 across PARAFFIN

Fuel with nasty smell brought round by soldier (8)

Definition: Fuel · charade + reversal

  • soldier → PARA a para is a paratrooper, the soldier who arrives by parachute
  • nasty smell → FFIN a niff is British slang for a bad smell, and it arrives back to front

The fuel really does smell, so the opening words read as one complaint and nothing marks where the definition stops. Only the first word is defining anything.

15 across ELEVEN

Team with Pele but no wingers, well-balanced? (6)

Definition: Team · deletion + charade

  • Pele → EL strip a letter off each end of the footballer's name and this pair is what is left standing
  • well-balanced → EVEN an even spread is a well-balanced one

Pele supplies both halves of the joke: the greatest of footballers hands over two letters, and his own game is where wingers stand on the edges.

16 across LE MANS

Mike wears heels for endurance race (2,4)

Definition: endurance race · container

  • heels → LEANS a boat heels over, which is to say it leans
  • Mike → M Mike is M in the radio alphabet, alongside Alpha and Bravo

The surface is a man in high heels, and the heels wanted are a yacht's. Mike is nobody: it is the radio operator's way of saying a single letter.

18 across UPSTAIRS

One entertained by revolting celebrities on flight (8)

Definition: on flight · charade + container

  • revolting → UP a population in revolt is said to be up, as in up in arms
  • One → I the Roman numeral for one, the crossword's usual single-letter one
  • celebrities → STARS celebrities are stars, the film-and-television sense, and they open up to take the guest

'Revolting' looks like the setter calling the celebrities disgusting, which is the wrong sense: a people in revolt are up, and that gives the first two letters.

20 across CONFIDENTIAL

Move lad with infection that mustn't be passed on (12)

Definition: that mustn't be passed on · anagram

  • lad → LAD three letters, handed over as they stand
  • infection → INFECTION the other nine, and twelve is the count the clue wants

The surface and the definition tell the same story from different ends: a boy is shifted because he is catching, and the answer is the thing that must not be caught by anyone else.

23 across MALINGER

Most of Mean Girls cast act poorly (8)

Definition: act poorly · anagram + deletion

  • Mean Girls → MEANGIRL the film's title less its final letter leaves eight, the length wanted

'Act poorly' reads as a review of the cast, and poorly is British for unwell: the definition is putting on an illness, not putting on a bad performance.

24 across CUTTER

Conservative, say, one reducing spending? (6)

Definition: one reducing spending · charade

  • Conservative → C C is how the Conservative party is printed on a British ballot paper
  • say → UTTER to say something is to utter it

The comma after the party invites you to read 'say' as 'for example'. It is a verb here, the plain word for speak, and the commas are surface punctuation.

25 across WEIGHT

Force directed towards Earth pause when told (6)

Definition: Force directed towards Earth · homophone

  • pause → WEIGHT a pause is a wait, and said aloud that word is spelt the heavier way

The physics is exact rather than decorative: what a scale reads is the pull of gravity on a mass, which is why the same body records less on the Moon.

26 across OVERRIDE

Emergency control on the other side of bike? (8)

Definition: Emergency control · charade

  • on the other side of → OVER over the road means on the far side of it
  • bike → RIDE a bike is a thing you ride, and a ride is a machine you get on

The question mark is doing real work: a bike is one ride among many, so the setter is offering an example where a synonym would not quite hold.

Down

2 down LIKE BLAZES

Fancy bishop does nothing with much passion (4,6)

Definition: with much passion · charade

  • Fancy → LIKE to fancy something is to like it, the mild sense of the word
  • bishop → B B is the bishop on a chess scoresheet, the one place the piece is routinely a letter
  • does nothing → LAZES someone doing nothing lazes about

Three separate pieces sit end to end while the surface reads as one idle churchman, and the phrase they build is nothing to do with the church.

3 down MADRASA

Friar spat regularly beneath cross in seminary (7)

Definition: seminary · charade + alternate letters

  • cross → MAD cross in the sense of angry, not the Christian symbol the surface wants
  • Friar spat → RASA the even-numbered letters of the two words, counting from the second

Friar, cross and seminary all point at a Christian house of study, and the school being defined is an Islamic one, where the Koran and religious law are taught.

4 down TOYED

Did flirt ruffle Daisy's skirts with toe? (5)

Definition: Did flirt · anagram + outer letters

  • Daisy's → DY the first and last letters of the name, the pair that edge it
  • toe → TOE three letters handed over intact, to be jumbled with the other two

The surface is a saucy scene at a garden party, complete with a ruffled skirt. Daisy is a name to be dismantled rather than a woman being bothered.

5 down STEPSON

Relative keeps walking (7)

Definition: Relative · charade

  • walking → STEPS walking is a business of steps, one to a pace
  • keeps → ON to keep doing a thing is to go on doing it, and this little word carries that continuing

Two words that describe someone still trudging along the road, run together, name a member of the family instead. The whole trick is where you put the gap.

6 down CHARMLESS

Cold tea left in canteen (9)

Definition: Cold · charade + container

  • tea → CHAR char is the British word for a cup of tea
  • left → L L is the standard abbreviation for left
  • canteen → MESS the mess is the canteen soldiers eat in, and it splits to take the letter above

Cold tea left in a canteen is one miserable picture and none of it is the definition: cold here is said of a person, and the tea is a separate piece of the build.

7 down INTENSE

Profound how decimal system counts irrational number (7)

Definition: Profound · charade

  • how decimal system counts → INTENS a decimal system counts in tens, and the phrase is taken at its word
  • irrational number → E e is the mathematician's constant, 2.718..., irrational like pi and written as one letter

The irrational number a solver reaches for first is pi. The one that fits is its neighbour from the same maths, and it is only ever one letter long.

8 down HAND

Worker delivered new parts (4)

Definition: Worker · container

  • delivered → HAD a mother who delivered a baby had it, the everyday way of saying she gave birth
  • new → N N is the standard abbreviation for new

Read straight, this is a mechanic's invoice. Each of the last three words is doing a different job: one is a word to be split, one a letter, one an instruction.

12 down PETROLHEAD

Driving fan parking here at old ground (10)

Definition: Driving fan · charade + anagram

  • parking → P P is the letter on every parking sign
  • here at old → HEREATOLD nine letters, which with the one above them is the full count

'Ground' wants to be the field the old cars are parked on, and it is the past tense of grind instead. Nothing in the clue is a place.

14 down FISTFIGHT

Throwing couple of rights, initially fear punch-up (9)

Definition: punch-up · charade + deletion

  • initially → FIST initially is FIRST, and that word minus its R leaves these four letters
  • fear → FIGHT fear is FRIGHT, and that word minus its R leaves these five
  • couple of rights → RR right is R in the usual abbreviation, and two of them are what gets thrown out

The boxing surface earns its keep: rights are punches thrown in the ring, and here they are two letter Rs thrown out of two perfectly ordinary words.

17 down ADORING

Very keen on Gordian knots (7)

Definition: Very keen on · anagram

  • Gordian → GORDIAN seven letters, exactly the count the clue asks for

The Gordian knot is the one Alexander the Great gave up untying and cut through instead, which is fair warning that these letters will not come apart tidily.

18 down UNDERGO

Bear short of energy (7)

Definition: Bear · charade

  • short of → UNDER under ten is short of ten, the plain arithmetic sense
  • energy → GO go means energy or drive, as in someone full of go

Go meaning energy is the piece worth pocketing: it turns up constantly, and here it hides behind a phrase that reads as one idea, running out of steam.

19 down AVIATOR

Traveller first to arrive by way of height? (7)

Definition: Traveller · charade + first letter

  • arrive → A the letter the verb starts with
  • by way of → VIA via is the Latin for by way of, and English uses it unchanged
  • height → TOR a tor is a rocky hilltop, the word used for the granite outcrops on Dartmoor

The question mark covers two loose joints at once: a tor is only one kind of height, and a flier only one kind of traveller.

21 down NICHE

Hospital in south coast resort attracting particular clientele (5)

Definition: attracting particular clientele · container

  • Hospital → H H is hospital on a road sign or a map
  • south coast resort → NICE Nice sits on the Mediterranean coast of France, the resort town of the Riviera

A south coast resort sounds like Brighton or Bognor, and the coast in question is French. The word for it is spelt like an English adjective and said nothing like one.

22 down GALE

Heavy blow toppled English prisoner (4)

Definition: Heavy blow · reversal + charade

  • prisoner → GAL an old lag is British slang for a convict, and it arrives here reversed
  • English → E E is the standard abbreviation for English

A heavy blow sounds like a punch, and the blow here is the weather's: sailors call a spell of hard wind a blow.
