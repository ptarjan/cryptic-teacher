Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,083

Across

8 across CARD GAME

Clown prepared for war, perhaps (4,4)

Definition: war, perhaps · charade

  • Clown → CARD A card is a joker or a wag — someone who plays the clown.
  • prepared → GAME Game means ready and willing: 'I'm game'.

This is the puzzle's keystone: half a dozen other clues point back at 8 across, and each of them wants a different one.

9 across PLAICE

Spot in which I will go fish? (6)

Definition: fish? · container

  • Spot → PLACE A place is a spot.
  • I → I The clue's I, which goes inside.

Even the surface stays on theme, since Go Fish is a children's card game. The question mark admits that plaice is one fish rather than a synonym for fish.

10 across SORE

Red four from sevens or eights (4)

Definition: Red · hidden word

  • sevens or eights → SORE Hidden across the join: sevenS OR Eights.

'Four from' is the extraction flag — take four letters from what follows — and the card numbers around it are camouflage, not arithmetic.

11 across FALSE TEETH

London school probing death on the wobbly bridge (5,5)

Definition: bridge · container + anagram

  • death → FATE Fate is one's doom — death.
  • London school → LSE The London School of Economics.
  • on the wobbly → ETH THE, made wobbly, comes out as ETH.

'The wobbly bridge' is the Millennium Bridge, and it is pure misdirection: the bridge wanted here is the dental one, and death is FATE rather than END.

12 across SEE RED

Pick up only hearts and diamonds then in snap? (3,3)

Definition: snap? · charade

  • Pick up → SEE To pick something up is to notice or see it.
  • only hearts and diamonds → RED Hearts and diamonds are the two red suits.

Snap is a card game and also what a person does on losing their temper; the second sense is the one the answer needs.

14 across PASSOVER

Bridge holiday (8)

Definition: Bridge · double definition

  • Bridge → PASSOVER To bridge a gap means crossing it entirely, skipping whatever lies between.
  • holiday → PASSOVER The Jewish festival of the exodus.

Two words in the clue, two definitions, no wordplay at all. The theme pushes you towards the card game, which is precisely what the setter is counting on.

15 across ANAGRAM

Hopeless at whist, I could get what it is! (7)

Definition: Hopeless at whist, I could get what it is! · anagram + &lit

  • at whist, I → Eight letters, and 'Hopeless' is the instruction to jumble them.
  • what it is → Jumbled, they come out as exactly this phrase — so the clue does the thing it is talking about.

The clue is its own example. Nothing spells the answer out; you have to notice that one phrase in it rearranges into another, and then name what that trick is called.

17 across TICKERS

Hearts, the first discarded by those not twisting? (7)

Definition: Hearts · deletion

  • those not twisting? → STICKERS At pontoon you either twist or stick, so those not twisting are stickers.
  • the first → S The leading S is the letter that goes.

Twist and stick are pontoon's two calls, so a card-table phrase turns out to be a plain beheading dressed up as gambling nerve.

20 across SPINSTER

Old maid and spider? (8)

Definition: Old maid · double definition

  • Old maid → SPINSTER The dated term for a woman who never married.
  • spider? → SPINSTER One who spins — which is a spider's whole trade.

Old Maid and Spider are both card games, so the surface never leaves the theme while both halves of the clue are quietly literal.

22 across FAR-OFF

8 Across blasted, inaccessible? (3-3)

Definition: inaccessible? · charade

  • 8 Across → FARO Faro, the old gambling-house card game.
  • blasted → FF FF is fortissimo — as loud as it will go.

Every '8 Across' in this puzzle wants a different card game. Note that the wordplay splits four and two while the answer is written three and three.

23 across BEER GARDEN

Brag about drinking always, with office in location adjoining pub (4,6)

Definition: location adjoining pub · reversal + container + charade

  • Brag about → BGAR BRAG keeps its B where it is and turns its RAG about.
  • always → EER E'er is the poets' always.
  • office → DEN A den is the room you work in at home.

Only part of the word turns: the B stays put while its RAG comes back. 'Drinking' does the swallowing and keeps the whole surface in the pub.

24 across EELS

A little patience, else fish (4)

Definition: fish · hidden word

  • patience, else → EELS Hidden across the comma: patiencE ELSe.

Patience is the card game the surface wants you to see, and 'a little' is the standard flag for taking a small piece of what follows.

25 across HUSTLE

Cheat thus toyed with love, heart surrendered (6)

Definition: Cheat · anagram + deletion

  • thus → THUS Four of the six letters, straight from the clue.
  • love → LOVE The word that is about to be trimmed.
  • heart → OV The heart of LOVE, its middle pair, is surrendered — leaving LE.

Two operations feed one shuffle: LOVE gives up its middle before anything is rearranged. 'Toyed with' is the anagram flag and the surface's flirtation at once.

26 across ELEPHANT

Solo effort’s last seen by retiring footballer, giant (8)

Definition: giant · charade + reversal + last letter

  • retiring footballer → ELEP Pelé, written backwards.
  • Solo → HAN Han Solo, so 'Solo' is a surname here.
  • effort’s last → T The final letter of EFFORT.

The capital S at the head of the clue hides the Star Wars surname, and Pelé is the footballer every setter reaches for when four letters are wanted.

Down

1 down NAPOLEON

8 Across I’m excited to take on in general (8)

Definition: general · charade

  • 8 Across → NAP Nap is a card game, so it answers the cross-reference.
  • I’m excited → OLE Olé! — the shout you let out when excitement takes over.
  • to take on → ON What the build takes on is ON itself, tacked on the end.

Paul's joke is circular: the card game nap is named after the very man you are assembling, so the wordplay and the definition point at one person.

2 down IDLE

Reportedly, celebrity out of work (4)

Definition: out of work · homophone

  • celebrity → IDLE an idol is a celebrity

'Reportedly' is the soundalike flag. The trap is that the spoken word is a noun and the written one an adjective, so nothing about the surface prepares you for it.

3 down FAFFED

Female agent in support of players’ association was dithering (6)

Definition: was dithering · charade

  • players’ association → FA The Football Association, the players' governing body.
  • Female → F F is the standard abbreviation for female.
  • agent → FED A fed is a federal agent.

In a down clue 'in support of' means underneath: whatever supports the FA is written below it. Read across the answer and the three fragments come out in that order.

4 down DEWLAPS

Commentator’s anticipated error – those hanging on by a neck? (7)

Definition: those hanging on by a neck? · homophone

  • anticipated → DEW 'due' means anticipated
  • error → LAPS a lapse is an error

The surface is a racing commentator calling a photo finish, which is why 'by a neck' sits there so innocently.

5 down APRES-SKI

Partying, where piano found in skip? (5-3)

Definition: Partying · charade

  • where piano found → APRES Piano is P, and in SKIP the P comes after — après is French for after.
  • in skip? → SKI The three letters the P follows.

Piano = P is a music-page convention worth banking, and the question mark admits the rest: this clue reads a word as a map of where its letters sit.

6 down CASEWORKER

A stitch bandaged by beauty, community professional (10)

Definition: community professional · container + charade

  • beauty → CORKER A corker is a beauty — an absolute stunner.
  • A → A The clue's own A, taken at face value.
  • stitch → SEW To stitch is to sew.

'Bandaged' keeps the surface medical while quietly doing container duty, and CORKER for beauty is the sort of dated slang Paul keeps in circulation.

7 down SCYTHE

Tool with key finally locked in dilapidated chest (6)

Definition: Tool · anagram + last letter

  • chest → CHEST Five of the six letters, straight from the clue.
  • key finally → Y The last letter of KEY.

Nothing is really put inside anything: 'locked in' only tells you the Y joins the other letters before 'dilapidated' rearranges the lot.

13 down REGENERATE

Eat greener bananas – and heal (10)

Definition: heal · anagram

  • Eat greener → REGENERATE The ten letters of EAT GREENER, rearranged.

'Bananas' is the anagram flag and the surface's fruit at once, which is why a diet clue can sit in a puzzle about cards without looking odd.

16 down ANTEATER

Budget ultimately invested in a better groomed animal (8)

Definition: animal · container + last letter

  • a better groomed → A NEATER Better groomed is neater, with the clue's own a in front.
  • Budget ultimately → T The last letter of BUDGET.

The surface offers a smarter animal, and the trick is spotting that a two-word comparative is really one word: 'better groomed' equals NEATER.

18 down RIFFLING

Preparation for 8 Acrosses, solo and fish (8)

Definition: Preparation for 8 Acrosses · charade

  • solo → RIFF A riff is a short solo phrase on a guitar.
  • fish → LING The ling is a long-bodied food fish.

LING is the crossword's workhorse fish and worth memorising. Note the plural cross-reference: 8 across is a whole category, and this clue wants all of them.

19 down BRIDLED

Drained cups free to be checked (7)

Definition: checked · container

  • Drained → BLED Drained of blood — bled.
  • free → RID To rid is to free.

'Cups' is the container indicator, because to cup something is to hold it — a noun in the surface doing a verb's job in the parse.

21 down PLEXUS

Benefit introducing former network (6)

Definition: network · container

  • Benefit → PLUS A plus is a benefit.
  • former → EX An ex is a former partner.

EX for 'former' is a two-letter gift the solver banks for life, and 'introducing' is doing container duty rather than announcing anyone.

22 down FINGER

Poker face in game, better concealing that (6)

Definition: Poker · container + first letter

  • better → FINER Better as in finer, of higher quality.
  • face in game → G A face is a front, and the front of GAME is G.

'Poker face' is one phrase in the surface and two jobs in the parse: the definition takes the first word, the wordplay the second.

24 down ECHO

Bounce back in elimination contest, hammering opponent firstly (4)

Definition: Bounce back · first letters

  • elimination contest, hammering opponent → ECHO The first letter of each of the four words.

'Firstly' sits at the end of the clue rather than the front: an instruction may come after the words it operates on, and often does.
