Across

8 across ROUGH

Unpleasant smell leaves area of city (5)

Definition: Unpleasant · deletion

  • area of city → BOROUGH a borough is an administrative area of a city
  • smell → BO BO is body odour, and it is the smell that walks out of the front of the word

'Area of city' looks like the definition and is really the word being cut down; the definition is the lone adjective at the front.

10 across BOOMERANG

"I hate this dessert," said one coming back (9)

Definition: one coming back · charade + homophone

  • I hate this → BOO a boo is what you shout at something you hate
  • dessert → MERANG the dessert is a meringue, and a meringue said aloud comes out with none of its own spelling

The quotation marks sell a diner complaining about pudding, but 'said' is not reporting the speech — it is the flag that only the sound of the sweet matters.

11 across CLOUD-CUCKOO-LAND

Criminal could undo a lock without key in ideal world (5-6-4)

Definition: ideal world · anagram + charade

  • key → C a key in music is named by a letter, and C is the plainest of them
  • could undo a lock → LOUDCUCKOOLAND those fourteen letters come out in a completely new order

'Without' is the joint the surface hides: read as lacking it makes a burglary story, and read as outside it parks a single letter in front of everything else.

12 across ACTIVATES

Sparks when Romeo meets Juliet set back by sides in Verona (9)

Definition: Sparks · charade + reversal + outer letters

  • when Romeo meets Juliet → ACT I the two of them meet in Act I of the play
  • sides in Verona → VA Verona is left with the V and A at its two ends
  • set → TES the stage set, written the other way round

Romeo and Juliet are the NATO alphabet's R and J on any other day; here they are the lovers themselves, and what you need is the act they meet in.

14 across OVERS

I've finished speaking special spell (5)

Definition: spell · charade

  • I've finished speaking → OVER 'over' is what a radio operator says to hand the conversation back
  • special → S S is the abbreviation for special

Over as a radio sign-off and over as a cricketing unit are the two halves of the joke, and the clue plays one against the other.

16 across MIDDLE OF NOWHERE

Where you might find husband off the beaten track (6,2,7)

Definition: off the beaten track · middle letter

  • husband → H H is the crossword's standing abbreviation for husband
  • Where you might find → this asks a question, and the answer is the reply to it: it says whereabouts in a word that letter is sitting

NOWHERE has seven letters with the H dead centre, so the answer is its own address — it tells you where the husband is by describing itself.

19 across REVUE

Show regret overtaking Tesla, say (5)

Definition: Show · container

  • regret → RUE to rue something is to regret it, and RUE splits after its R
  • Tesla, say → EV a Tesla is one make of EV, the electric vehicle

The whole surface is a stretch of road — overtaking, a Tesla — and the only word not driving anywhere is the one-word definition at the front.

21 across SNAIL MAIL

Post from sketch show entertaining great man in audience (5,4)

Definition: Post · container + charade + homophone

  • sketch show → SNL Saturday Night Live, the sketch show known by its initials, and it opens up before its L
  • great → AI A1 means top quality, and its 1 is written as the Roman numeral
  • man → MAIL a man is a male, and a male said out loud is spelt the postal way

A1 for 'great' is the piece worth banking: the numeral turns into a Roman I, which is how two letters come out of a one-syllable word.

23 across ON THE RIGHT TRACK

Set for success from section of record following where Trump lies (2,3,5,5)

Definition: Set for success · charade

  • where Trump lies → ON THE RIGHT a politician of that stripe lies on the right of the spectrum
  • section of record → TRACK each separate song on a record is a track

'Lies' is the pivot: the surface offers a politician telling untruths, and the wordplay wants only the direction in which he is lying down.

25 across GLADIATOR

Fighter happy a riot erupted (9)

Definition: Fighter · charade + anagram

  • happy → GLAD glad is the plain synonym, and it goes in unshuffled
  • a riot → IATOR the five letters of A RIOT come out in a new order

'A riot' does double duty in the surface — the scene of chaos in the story, and five letters to be rearranged in the clue — while only the first word is definition.

26 across EXITS

Team appearing in French and Saunders finally retires (5)

Definition: retires · container + charade + last letter

  • French and → ET et is the French for and, and it splits to take the team
  • Team → XI a side of eleven is written XI in Roman numerals
  • Saunders finally → S the last letter of Saunders

French and Saunders are a comedy double act, and the clue splits them down the middle: one name supplies a French word, the other only its final letter.

Down

1 down PROCLAIMER

Herald for one hundred and one entering the sea in France (10)

Definition: Herald · charade + container

  • for → PRO pro means in favour of — a pro-hunting vote is a vote for hunting
  • the sea in France → LA MER la mer is how the French say the sea, and it opens up in two places
  • one hundred and one → CI a hundred is C and one is I in Roman numerals, and they go in either side of LA

'In France' is the standing signal to answer in French: the clue wants the French words themselves, not an English translation of them.

2 down SHE-DEVIL

Perhaps repent? Not this woman (3-5)

Definition: Not this woman · charade

  • Perhaps repent → SHED one thing repenting can perhaps mean is shedding — giving up what you have been
  • repent → EVIL and what the penitent sheds is the evil in them

The enumeration is the tell: 3-5 breaks the two shed words in a place they never break in ordinary use, which is where the second reading appears.

3 down ABDUCTIONS

Captures one abandoning bid during sales (10)

Definition: Captures · container + deletion

  • sales → AUCTIONS auctions are sales, and the word splits open after its A
  • bid → BD a bid that has abandoned its one — I, the Roman numeral — is left as BD

The saleroom surface does all the work: 'bid' looks like part of the auction and is really a word waiting to be docked of a letter.

4 down BONK

Hit button on the counter (4)

Definition: Hit · reversal

  • button → KNOB a knob is the button you push or turn, and it comes round the other way

'Counter' is the pivot the surface hides: it looks like the shop fitting the button sits on, and it is the word telling you which way to read.

5 down DE NOVO

Study heart in love with love once more (2,4)

Definition: once more · charade + middle letters

  • Study → DEN a den is the small room you shut yourself in to study
  • in love → OV lOVe keeps these two at its centre
  • with love → O love is the tennis score of nothing, written as a nought

The same word does two different jobs a breath apart: first it is a written word with a middle, then it is a tennis score.

6 down NAZARENE

Perhaps Jesus and Hazard sent new losing sides (8)

Definition: Perhaps Jesus · charade + middle letters

  • and → N aNd keeps only its middle letter
  • Hazard → AZAR hAZARd gives up the H and D at its edges
  • sent → EN sENt is what is left once the S and T have gone
  • new → E nEw keeps only its middle letter

Eden Hazard was a footballer, which is the transfer story the surface is selling — and even the little word 'and' is material here, not grammar.

7 down AGED

Upcoming artist mostly past his prime (4)

Definition: past his prime · reversal + deletion

  • artist → DEGA Degas is the crossword's stock painter, and mostly he turns up without his S

'Upcoming' is doing two jobs at once: it sounds like a career on the rise and it is the instruction that sends the letters up the grid.

9 down U-BOAT

One attacks occasionally dumb loyalty (1-4)

Definition: One attacks · alternate letters

  • dumb loyalty → UBOAT dUmB lOyAlTy hands over its second, fourth, sixth, eighth and tenth letters

The 1-4 enumeration is the way in: a single letter and then a word almost always means a letter-plus-noun name, and the hyphen falls inside the harvest.

13 down SUNBATHERS

He’s as burnt at sea as others in his position (10)

Definition: others in his position · anagram

  • He’s as burnt → SUNBATHERS the ten letters of HE'S AS BURNT come round again in a new order

'At sea' is not a place in this clue but a state of confusion — and the beach it conjures up is exactly where the surface wants your eye to stay.

15 down STEAL A KISS

How to make waxing glide and move smoothly (5,1,4)

Definition: How to make waxing glide and move smoothly · cryptic definition

  • How to make waxing glide → Read straight, this is a question about polish: floors and skis are waxed precisely so that things slip along them
  • and move smoothly → The setter means smooth in the other sense — the sly move of a charmer, made while the two of you are gliding round the floor

There is no wordplay to separate out here: the whole sentence is the joke, and 'waxing' is what does the misleading, being the beauty treatment rather than the polish.

17 down DUVET DAY

United go over stopping time to attack — it’s not going to work (5,3)

Definition: not going to work · container + charade

  • time to attack → D-DAY D-Day was the appointed hour for the invasion, and the word splits after its D
  • United → U U is the abbreviation United takes in a club's name
  • go over → VET to vet a plan is to go over it carefully

D-Day is the piece to spot: 'time to attack' is a date in 1944 before it is anything else, and the football club is there to keep you on a pitch.

18 down WELL THEN

Time to stick spring chicken in that case (4,4)

Definition: in that case · charade + container

  • spring → WELL a spring rising in the ground is a well
  • Time → T T is the standard abbreviation for time
  • chicken → HEN a hen is the chicken in the yard, and she opens up to take the T

'Spring chicken' is the trap: as a phrase it means a youngster, and the clue wants its two words prised apart and read one at a time.

20 down EMETIC

Attention-seeking academic's plea to retire — it makes you sick (6)

Definition: it makes you sick · reversal

  • Attention-seeking academic's plea → CITE ME an academic hungry for attention begs to be quoted in your footnotes: cite me

The plea is a real sentence spoken by a real academic, which is the whole joke — nothing has to be done to it except read it the other way.

22 down ABACI

I taxi over after heading to Ann Summers (5)

Definition: Summers · charade + reversal + first letter

  • Ann → A the first letter of Ann
  • taxi → CAB a taxi is a cab, and it turns round
  • I → I the clue's own I, taken at face value

Ann Summers is a high-street lingerie chain, and that is the whole of the misdirection: the name has to come apart into one letter and a plural noun.

23 down ORGY

Some sexy group needing erection (4)

Definition: Some sexy group needing erection · hidden word + reversal + &lit

  • sexy group → ORGY the run of letters in sexY GROup climbs the entry and hands them over the other way round

Every word is doing two jobs at once: as English the sentence describes the answer, as instructions it points at four letters lying face down in the middle of it.

24 down IOTA

Dirtbag occasionally pens love letter (4)

Definition: letter · alternate letters + container

  • Dirtbag → ITA dIrTbAg hands over its second, fourth and sixth letters
  • love → O love is the nothing of a tennis score, written as a nought

'Love letter' is the phrase the surface builds and the clue immediately splits: one of those words is a nought and the other is the definition.
