Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,093

Across

1 across PAMPAS

Plain starter of meal daddy’s eating (6)

Definition: Plain · container + first letter

  • daddy’s → PAPAS daddy is papa, and papa's loses its apostrophe to become five plain letters
  • meal → M the first letter of meal, which is what a starter of it comes to

The surface is a father hogging the first course. Pampas grass is named after the plain it grows on, not the other way round, so the definition is the older sense.

5 across AUTOCRAT

At court, a new sovereign (8)

Definition: sovereign · anagram

  • At court, a → ATCOURTA eight letters, and every one of them is used

A royal court and a new sovereign make one smooth sentence, and every word of it except the indicator is raw material.

9 across SUBPOENA

Having cooked bean soup, one requires attendance (8)

Definition: requires attendance · anagram

  • bean soup → BEANSOUP B, E, A, N, S, O, U, P — the eight letters, with nothing added

The Latin is worth knowing: sub poena, 'under penalty', which is what makes the summons an order rather than an invitation.

10 across REPOSE

Take another attitude to rest (6)

Definition: Take another attitude · double definition

  • Take another attitude → REPOSE an attitude is a pose, so taking a fresh one is to pose again
  • rest → REPOSE this half asks for a word meaning to rest or lie at ease, usable as noun or verb

The split is at 'to', and the hyphen a grid cannot show is the whole clue: two different words happen to be spelt the same way.

11 across QUEEN'S SPEECH

Once Standard English no longer heard at opening of Parliament (6,6)

Definition: Once Standard English no longer heard at opening of Parliament · cryptic definition

  • Once Standard English no longer heard → The surface is the familiar grumble that nobody at Westminster speaks properly any more, with 'Standard English' as the grammarian's term for the correct form.
  • at opening of Parliament → The setter means the State Opening, where the monarch reads out the government's programme — and what that address is called depends on who is on the throne.

Nothing separates into letters here. Both halves lean on the same possessive: the monarch's English was the yardstick, the monarch's address opens Parliament, and a king ended both phrases.

13 across MULE

Slippery equine? (4)

Definition: Slippery · double definition

  • Slippery → MULE this half names a backless slipper, the kind you step into rather than put on
  • equine → MULE the offspring of a donkey and a mare

Two words and a question mark, which is where the difficulty lives: with no wordplay to unpick, the only lever is that question mark.

14 across TEETOTAL

18 on a course that is dry (8)

Definition: dry · charade

  • on a course → TEE every hole on a golf course is driven off from a tee
  • 18 → TOTAL eighteen holes means eighteen of them, so the number is the tally

Split the answer into two words and the clue is a fact about golf: count the tees on a full round and eighteen is what you get.

17 across BLOKEISH

Typically male slob struggling with hike (8)

Definition: Typically male · anagram

  • slob → SLOB four of the eight letters
  • hike → HIKE and the other four

The surface shows a man wrestling a rucksack up a hill, and the two nouns either side of the indicator are the only material in the clue.

18 across SHED

Drop tools here (4)

Definition: Drop · double definition

  • Drop → SHED trees let their leaves fall away each year, and snakes do the same with their skin
  • tools here → SHED the hut at the end of the garden, named by what is kept in it

Three words and no wordplay at all. The surface is an instruction to down tools, and the split comes after the very first word.

20 across MONTENEGRINS

Tense morning working for Europeans (12)

Definition: Europeans · anagram

  • Tense morning → TENSEMORNING twelve letters, which is the length of the answer

Balkan nationalities are a setter's friend: long, and stocked with the vowels and common consonants an anagram needs to hide inside an ordinary phrase.

23 across LIBIDO

Try to stop celebrity endlessly showing sexual urge (6)

Definition: sexual urge · container + deletion

  • celebrity → LIO a celebrity is a lion — the lion of the season, the guest everyone wants — with its tail letter gone
  • Try → BID a bid is an attempt: a bid for freedom

Lion for a celebrity is Victorian drawing-room English, the literary lion of the season, and it is that sense the clue needs rather than the animal.

24 across ARCHIVES

Old records from accomplished country singer (8)

Definition: Old records · charade

  • accomplished → ARCH arch means chief of its kind, as in arch-enemy, so an arch practitioner is a thorough one
  • country singer → IVES Burl Ives, who sang Big Rock Candy Mountain

A surname alone, with no first name offered, is the setter's standing notice that you are looking for a well-known person rather than a word.

25 across HEIGHTEN

Raise the hinge that’s wonky (8)

Definition: Raise · anagram

  • the hinge → THEHINGE eight letters, including the article, which the setter needs for its E

The article is fodder, which is the thing to spot: 'the' looks like grammar and is carrying two of the answer's letters.

26 across MANGLE

Make a mess of motorway gradient (6)

Definition: Make a mess of · charade

  • motorway → M M is the letter Britain puts on its motorway numbers, the M1 and the M25
  • gradient → ANGLE the angle of a slope is its gradient

A mangle was the pair of rollers that wrung the water out of washing, and anything fed through one came out flat — which is where the modern sense comes from.

Down

2 down AGUE

No place for pandemic, just fever (4)

Definition: fever · deletion

  • pandemic → PLAGUE a plague is a disease sweeping a whole population
  • place → PL PL is the standing abbreviation for Place, as in a street name, and it is the pair that goes

The sentence reads as a hopeful bulletin — not a pandemic after all, only a temperature — and the reassurance is exactly the instruction.

3 down PIPSQUEAK

Whippersnapper very quietly suppressing single sound from mouse (9)

Definition: Whippersnapper · container + charade

  • very quietly → PP pp is pianissimo, the mark on a score telling a player to be very quiet
  • single → I one, written as the Roman numeral
  • sound from mouse → SQUEAK the noise a mouse makes

Two conventions worth banking do the front half: pp for pianissimo, and I for one. The mouse is the only ordinary English in the wordplay.

4 down SEEMED

Appeared to do this from Nice beach? (6)

Definition: Appeared · charade

  • do this → SEE what you do on a beach, gazing out at the water
  • Nice beach? → MED Nice sits on the French Riviera, and the water in front of it is the Med

Nice keeps its capital letter and its sand, which is the whole disguise: split the answer in two and it is what a holidaymaker there does all afternoon.

5 down AGAINST THE GRAIN

Contrary to inclination, objecting to Scotch? (7,3,5)

Definition: Contrary to inclination · charade

  • objecting to → AGAINST to object to a proposal is to be against it
  • Scotch? → THE GRAIN grain whisky is one of the two kinds of Scotch, the other being malt; the question mark owns the leap

The comma is the join. One half is a plain synonym and the other reads the drink as a raw ingredient, which is the sort of leap a question mark always flags.

6 down THRUSHES

Birds in most of the riverside plants (8)

Definition: Birds · charade + deletion

  • the → TH most of THE, with its last letter dropped
  • riverside plants → RUSHES rushes are the reeds that grow in the shallows at a river's edge

Song thrush, mistle thrush and blackbird are garden and woodland birds, never water birds, so the riverbank the clue paints is pure misdirection.

7 down COPSE

Manage to get round small group of trees (5)

Definition: group of trees · container

  • Manage → COPE to cope with a job is to manage it
  • small → S S is the label on the smallest shirt on the rail

S for small comes off the clothes rail and is worth banking: setters reach for it constantly when a container needs a single letter inside.

8 down ASSOCIATED

Move cat aside, keeping very connected (10)

Definition: connected · anagram

  • cat aside → CATASIDE eight of the ten letters come from here
  • very → SO so means very — 'not so bad' — and its two letters make the count up

'Move the cat aside' is a sentence anybody might say, and none of it survives: the animal is only letters, and 'very' is hiding a two-letter word.

12 down GUILLOTINE

Killer’s destiny in being caught by subterfuge (10)

Definition: Killer · container + charade

  • subterfuge → GUILE guile is cunning, the deceiver's stock in trade
  • destiny → LOT your lot is what fate has handed you
  • in → IN the preposition is taken at face value, for its own two letters

The word doing least work on the surface is doing most underneath: 'in' looks like grammar and is really two letters of the answer.

15 down OBSESSION

Alumni’s pub evening is a thing! (9)

Definition: a thing · charade

  • Alumni → OB a school's alumni are its old boys, and OB is how the school magazine writes it
  • pub evening → SESSION a session is a drinker's own word for an evening spent at it

The exclamation mark is the setter grinning: 'is a thing' only became a definition in the last twenty years, and the clue is written in that register throughout.

16 down SIGNPOST

Initial contribution to thread that gives clear directions (8)

Definition: gives clear directions · charade

  • Initial → SIGN to initial a form is to sign it — the verb, not the letter
  • contribution to thread → POST a message added to an online thread is a post

'Initial' looks exactly like an instruction to take a first letter, which is the one thing it is not doing here. Read it as a verb.

19 down SITCOM

Relax on firm topper on mattress for programme (6)

Definition: programme · charade + first letter

  • Relax → SIT to sit down is to take the weight off
  • firm → CO CO is company, the abbreviation on any letterhead
  • mattress → M the first letter of mattress, the end a topper covers

Sitcom is short for situation comedy, and the clue never mentions comedy at all — the definition works at the widest level it can get away with.

21 down TYING

Securing hearing without resistance (5)

Definition: Securing · deletion

  • hearing → TRYING a case being heard in court is being tried, so a hearing is a trying
  • resistance → R R is the physicist's symbol for resistance, and it is the letter that goes

Court and courtroom are never mentioned, so the step to find is that a hearing and a trying are the same event seen from two benches.

22 down ZEAL

Enthusiasm in the heart of Commonwealth country (4)

Definition: Enthusiasm · middle letters

  • Commonwealth country → ZEAL the country is New Zealand, and reading its two words as one continuous string, the wanted letters sit exactly in the middle, three trimmed from each end

The country is never named, only described, so the work is finding a Commonwealth member with a whole word parked at its middle.
