Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,028

Across

1 across FIVE A SIDE

Quins getting slaughtered reportedly in this game format (4,1,4)

Definition: this game format · charade + homophone

this game format → FIVE A SIDE: football with five players per team, a named format of the game rather than a description of one match.

  • Quins → FIVE quins are quintuplets, five babies born at one go
  • getting slaughtered → ASIDE the ending -cide means a killing, as in insecticide or regicide; said aloud, 'a-cide' is written out with an s
  • reportedly — What is reported reaches you by word of mouth, so you write down the sound of the words rather than their spelling.

Harlequins, the rugby club, are getting a hammering by all accounts.

'Quins' is Harlequins in the surface and quintuplets in the wordplay. What is being sounded out is not a word at all but the suffix -cide, which is why the spelling comes out so far from the noise.

6 across RIP UP

Take apart resistance with very little money raised (3,2)

Definition: Take apart · charade

Take apart → RIP UP: to rip something up is to tear it into pieces, the destructive sense of taking a thing apart rather than dismantling it neatly.

  • resistance → R R is resistance in physics, the R of Ohm's law
  • very little → I the least you can have of anything is one, and one is I
  • money → P p is pence, so the two letters together read as 1p
  • raised → UP anything raised is up, and here that is the word itself rather than an instruction

A resistance movement has raised barely any money and is being dismantled.

'Raised' looks like the reversal instruction this grid is full of, and it is simply the word up. Setters write very little money as 1p, which on the page is an I beside a p.

9 across CARBON EMISSIONS

Transport doctor secures undertaking to show damaging output (6,9)

Definition: damaging output · charade + container

damaging output → CARBON EMISSIONS: the standard term for the greenhouse gas released by burning fuel, which is exactly what the surface's car puts out.

  • Transport → CAR the everyday means of transport, three letters of it
  • doctor → BONES Bones is the standing nickname for a ship's doctor
  • undertaking → MISSION a mission is an undertaking somebody is charged with
  • secures — To secure something is to have it held safely inside you, so what follows is locked into what has been built already.

A doctor pins down a promise of a lift somewhere.

'Doctor' is so reliably an anagram signal that it hides in plain sight here as a plain noun, and the insertion goes into a word the charade has only just finished building.

10 across SCAT

Muck about in street (4)

Definition: Muck · container

Muck → SCAT: the field naturalist's term for animal droppings, so it is muck in the literal dung sense rather than dirt in general.

  • street → ST st is the postal short form of street
  • about → CA ca is circa, the Latin for about, the one you see before a date
  • in — 'In' names a destination: the letters just taken go inside the word that follows, splitting it.

Children messing about somewhere down the road.

'Muck about' wants to be read as one phrase, and the definition is 'Muck' on its own. Scat is the naturalist's word for the droppings an animal leaves behind.

11 across A BIT MUCH

Casting chum bait overboard? (1,3,4)

Definition: overboard · anagram

overboard → A BIT MUCH: to go overboard is to take a thing too far, and 'a bit much' is the understated British verdict on exactly that excess.

  • chum bait → CHUMBAIT eight letters of fodder — and chum really is a fishing bait, minced-up fish
  • Casting — To cast is to throw, and letters thrown land in whatever order they land in.

An angler is chucking chopped fish into the sea to bring the catch in.

Every word here does two jobs at once: chum and bait are a real fishing scene and the letters, while 'casting' is what an angler does and the instruction to shuffle them.

14 across TESTIMONY

Examine current wealth declaration that’s provided in court (9)

Definition: declaration that’s provided in court · charade + homophone

declaration that’s provided in court → TESTIMONY: the formal name for evidence given on oath by a witness, spoken from the box.

  • Examine → TEST to test something is to examine it
  • current → I I is the physicist's symbol for electric current
  • wealth → MONY wealth is money, and what the setter wants is the sound of that word, which comes out an e short

A court going through somebody's declaration of what they are currently worth.

'Declaration' works twice: it belongs to the definition, and it is also the signal to say 'money' out loud rather than spell it. Only the noise is borrowed, so the e never arrives.

15 across NOTES

Resolved to return short communications (5)

Definition: short communications · reversal

short communications → NOTES: a note is the briefest written message there is, a line on paper rather than a letter.

  • Resolved → SETON someone set on doing a thing is resolved to do it
  • return — To return is to come back the way you went, so the letters are read back along the row.

A decision to send back some brief messages.

'Resolved to return' reads as a single decision — to go home again — and the parse cuts it in two, with 'set on' standing for resolved and 'return' giving the instruction.

16 across ETHIC

Traditional name dropped – it’s a matter of principle (5)

Definition: a matter of principle · deletion

a matter of principle → ETHIC: an ethic is a governing principle somebody holds to, as in the work ethic, rather than the study of right and wrong.

  • Traditional → ETHNIC ethnic dress or ethnic music is traditional to a people
  • name → N n is the standard short form of name, and it is the letter that goes
  • dropped — A letter dropped falls out of the word it was in, and what is left closes up over the gap.

A long-standing name is being dropped, and somebody insists that was a point of principle.

'Name dropped' reads as name-dropping, the celebrity habit, when it is a plain instruction to lose a letter.

18 across DROVE HOME

Made clear: hero moved improperly (5,4)

Definition: Made clear · anagram

Made clear → DROVE HOME: to drive a point home is to make it land unmistakably, and both are past tense, so the swap works in a sentence.

  • hero moved → HEROMOVED nine letters of fodder, exactly the length of the light
  • improperly — Done improperly, a thing is done out of the correct order, and letters out of their correct order are the whole of an anagram.

A report that a hero was shifted somewhere in an underhand way.

The colon does the joining, so the definition is the two words in front of it. To drive a point home is to make it land, and both halves of the clue are in the past tense.

20 across TRIBUNAL

Enquiry identifies food included in sample (8)

Definition: Enquiry · container

Enquiry → TRIBUNAL: a panel appointed to hear evidence and rule on it, which is what a public enquiry into something is.

  • sample → TRIAL a trial of a new product is a sample run of it
  • food → BUN a bun is food, a small sweet bread roll
  • included in — What is included in something sits within it, so the food ends up inside the word beside it.

An investigation turns up food hidden inside a specimen.

A trial is a courtroom in the answer's world and a sample everywhere else, and the clue hands you the second sense while the answer belongs firmly to the first.

21 across ET AL

Retaliation involves the others (2,2)

Definition: the others · hidden word

the others → ET AL: the abbreviation of Latin et alii, tacked onto a list of names to stand for the rest of them.

  • Retaliation → ETAL four consecutive letters inside this single word, starting at its second
  • involves — What a thing involves is caught up inside it, so the letters wanted are already sitting in the word before.

A revenge attack that sweeps up everybody else as well.

The Latin et alii means 'and others' with no article, so 'the others' is a shade loose, and the hiding place is unusually short — one word rather than the customary three.

25 across THE BACK OF A LORRY

Terribly fat York bachelor the source of hot stuff (3,4,2,1,5)

Definition: the source of hot stuff · anagram

the source of hot stuff → THE BACK OF A LORRY: stolen goods are 'hot', and are said to have come off the back of a lorry.

  • fat York bachelor → FATYORKBACHELOR fifteen letters of fodder, the full width of the grid
  • Terribly — Done terribly, a thing comes out badly wrong, and letters that come out wrong are letters in the wrong order.

A stout unmarried Yorkshireman is said to be where the hot stuff comes from.

Goods that fell 'off the back of a lorry' are stolen, and 'hot' is the same underworld shorthand for stolen — two bits of British slang meeting in one definition.

26 across SUSIE

Defence is useless to hold back this woman (5)

Definition: this woman · hidden word + reversal

this woman → SUSIE: the definition points at nothing but a woman's given name, the familiar short form of Susan.

  • Defence is useless → EISUS five consecutive letters running across all three words, starting inside the first
  • hold back — To hold a thing is to have it inside you, and holding it back turns it round to face the other way.

A warning that nothing anyone can do will stop the woman who is coming.

Nothing in the clue looks like fodder, which is what makes a hidden reversal hard: the letters run backwards over two word joins, and 'hold back' is the only instruction there is.

27 across TROJAN WAR

Extended conflict has grafter almost completely apprehensive (6,3)

Definition: Extended conflict · charade + deletion

Extended conflict → TROJAN WAR: definition by example — the siege of Troy lasted ten years and is the archetype of a war that drags on.

  • grafter → TROJAN graft is British for hard work, and someone who works like a trojan never stops
  • apprehensive → WAR wary is apprehensive, and the y at its end is the letter that never arrives
  • almost completely — Almost completely is not quite all of it, so the word arrives one letter short of its full length.

A long feud has left a hard worker almost entirely on edge.

The definition is the ten-year siege of Troy, and neither half of the answer comes from anything Greek — one is hard work and the other is nerves.

Down

1 down FOCUS

Aim is to shrink Fort Custer by half (5)

Definition: Aim · charade + deletion

Aim → FOCUS: both name the point you direct effort at — 'our aim this year' and 'our focus this year' swap freely.

  • Fort → FO half of a four-letter word is two letters, taken from the front
  • Custer → CUS half of the general's six-letter name, again from the front
  • shrink — To shrink a thing is to leave it smaller than you found it, and a word left smaller here keeps only its opening letters.
  • by half — Cut by half, a word keeps half the letters it had: two of four, three of six.

A cost-cutting plan to halve the size of an army fort.

Custer is the US cavalry general of Little Bighorn, so 'Fort Custer' reads as one real garrison rather than as two words each waiting to be cut in two.

2 down VERBALS

Block clergyman over argument (7)

Definition: argument · charade + reversal

argument → VERBALS: British slang for the words traded in a row, the shouted half of a fight rather than the physical one.

  • Block → SLAB a slab is a thick flat block of stone or concrete
  • clergyman → REV Rev is the standard short form for a reverend
  • over — Turned over, a thing lands the other way up, so the letters collected so far are written out from the back.

A clergyman is being blocked over some quarrel.

'Over' turns both pieces at once, not just the one it sits beside. British slang keeps 'the verbals' for the words traded in a row, which is why the answer is plural and its definition is not.

3 down A TO B

Common direction of travel after they open border fronts (1,2,1)

Definition: Common direction of travel · first letters

Common direction of travel → A TO B: 'getting from A to B' is the everyday phrase for a journey, A and B standing for start and finish.

  • after → A the opening letter of the first word in the run
  • they → T the opening letter of the second
  • open → O the opening letter of the third
  • border → B the opening letter of the fourth
  • fronts — A word's front is the letter it starts with, so 'fronts' collects the opening letter of each word standing before it.

Once the border posts are opened, everybody heads the same way.

The enumeration is the way in: three words of one, two and one letters is a shape almost nothing but this phrase fits, and 'fronts' is the only instruction in the clue.

4 down ITEM

Thing that makes loving couple … (4)

Definition: Thing · double definition

Thing → ITEM: a single entry on a list; and 'loving couple' → ITEM in the gossip sense, two people said to be an item because they are together.

  • Thing → an entry on a list, an object named one at a time
  • loving couple → the gossip-column sense: two people who are going out together

Some object that brings two people together romantically, with the sentence running on into 5 down.

The ellipsis carries the sentence into 5 down, and it also hides the join between the two senses: nothing in the clue signals where one definition stops and the next starts.

5 down ENID BLYTON

… irritated by indolent author (4,6)

Definition: author · anagram

author → ENID BLYTON: definition by example — she is one particular writer, not a synonym for the word.

  • by indolent → BYINDOLENT ten letters of fodder, exactly the length wanted, with the little joining word part of the pile
  • irritated — Someone irritated has been rubbed the wrong way and put out of sorts, and letters put out of sorts come back in a different order.

Somebody is being annoyed by a lazy writer.

Enid Blyton wrote the Famous Five and Noddy for British children, and she was the opposite of indolent — six hundred-odd books. The clue opens mid-sentence because it runs on from 4 down.

6 down ROSE-TINTED

Got up coloured with optimism (4-6)

Definition: with optimism · charade

with optimism → ROSE-TINTED: from rose-tinted spectacles, so a rose-tinted account is one coloured by hope rather than seen plainly.

  • Got up → ROSE 'got up' is past tense, so it wants the past tense of rise
  • coloured → TINTED a tinted window is a coloured one, and both words are past participles

Someone got out of bed glowing with cheerfulness about the day.

Rose-tinted spectacles are the ones that make everything look better than it is, and the phrase has broken free of the spectacles to describe any determinedly cheerful view.

7 down PRODUCT

One paid on channel to provide content (7)

Definition: content · charade

content → PRODUCT: the trade's own word for what a studio or publisher makes and sells — a series is product before anyone calls it art.

  • One paid → PRO a pro is paid for playing, as against an amateur
  • channel → DUCT a duct is a channel carrying air, water or cable

A professional is paid by a television channel to make things for it to show.

Broadcasters really do call what they make 'product', so the definition belongs to the same industry the surface is painting, which is what makes the join invisible.

8 down POST-HASTE

Issue dispatch ASAP (4-5)

Definition: ASAP · charade

ASAP → POST-HASTE: from the old instruction written on urgent letters, 'haste, post, haste', and now the ordinary idiom for as soon as possible.

  • Issue → POST to post a notice is to issue it publicly
  • dispatch → HASTE dispatch as a noun is promptness — a job done 'with dispatch' is done quickly

An order to get a dispatch sent out as fast as possible.

Both wordplay words are quietly changing job: 'issue' from noun to verb, and 'dispatch' from the message the surface has you picturing to plain speed.

12 down DISCOURAGE

Put off recording what we’re living though (10)

Definition: Put off · charade

Put off → DISCOURAGE: to discourage someone is to put them off doing a thing — the same phrasal verb, not the postponing sense of 'put off'.

  • recording → DISC a disc is a recording, the crossword's usual one
  • what we’re living though → OURAGE the era you are alive in is your age, so the one we share is our age

Somebody keeps putting off the job of recording the times we are living through.

The printed clue says 'though' where it means through; read it that way and the phrase is our age, the era we are all alive in.

13 down BOND MARKET

British agent satisfied capturing vessel – something that intimidates governments! (4,6)

Definition: something that intimidates governments · charade + container

something that intimidates governments → BOND MARKET: states borrow there, so when traders demand higher yields a government's budget has to give way.

  • British agent → BOND James Bond is the British agent of the films, 007
  • satisfied → MET a demand that has been satisfied has been met
  • vessel → ARK the ark is the biblical vessel, three letters long
  • capturing — A captor keeps its prisoner inside, so the word before it closes round the word after it.

A contented secret agent seizes a ship, and governments are rattled.

Governments borrow by issuing bonds, so the traders who price them can force a chancellor's hand — the exclamation mark is the setter enjoying that fact.

14 down THE STATES

America sticks poor material in papers (3,6)

Definition: America · container

America → THE STATES: the everyday British name for the USA, short for the United States and always carrying the article.

  • papers → THESES theses are the long papers written for a doctorate
  • poor material → TAT tat is British for cheap shoddy goods, the junk-shop kind
  • sticks — To stick one thing in another is to shove it bodily inside, which is what happens to the three letters that follow.
  • in — 'In' names where they go: inside the word that comes after it, splitting it in two.

Someone is padding out their academic papers with rubbish.

'America' opens the clue looking like the subject of a sentence, a country doing something, rather than like the definition. Papers in the academic sense are theses.

17 down HEIFERS

They’ll come into estate carrying 50% of feed for livestock (7)

Definition: livestock · container

livestock → HEIFERS: definition by example — heifers are young cows that have not yet calved, one particular kind of farm animal.

  • They’ll come into estate → HEIRS heirs are the people who will come into an estate when it is left to them
  • feed → FE half of that word's four letters, the front two kept and the back two left behind
  • carrying — What carries something has it on board and inside, so the word before closes round what comes next.
  • 50% of — Half of a word is half its letters, and the half kept here is the front one.

The people in line to inherit are lugging half the cattle feed about.

An estate carrying something reads as a car with the boot loaded, which is the picture the clue is banking on; the estate here is the one you inherit.

19 down OUTDRAW

Beat in a gunfight with banned move? (7)

Definition: Beat in a gunfight · charade

Beat in a gunfight → OUTDRAW: to outdraw someone is to clear your gun from the holster first, which in a western settles the argument.

  • banned → OUT something banned is out — ruled out, not allowed
  • move → DRAW to draw is to move: a train draws in, a runner draws level

A duel decided by a move the rules do not allow.

The question mark is the setter admitting that 'move' for draw is loose, and the surface's illegal move in a shoot-out is where that looseness hides.

22 down LAYER

Sort of early film (5)

Definition: film · anagram

film → LAYER: a film of dust or oil is a thin layer of it, the covering sense of the word rather than the cinema one.

  • early → EARLY five letters of fodder, exactly the length wanted
  • Sort of — Sorting is putting things into a new order, so 'sort of' asks for that to be done to the letters that follow.

Some category of early cinema.

'Early film' reads as one phrase, silent pictures, and the film wanted is the other kind entirely — the thin covering on a pond or a lens.

23 down IF SO

Occasional infusion is required on that basis (2,2)

Definition: on that basis · alternate letters

on that basis → IF SO: 'if so' means 'if that is the case', which is what somebody means by 'on that basis' when they accept a premise.

  • infusion → IFSO every other letter of the word, starting with the first
  • Occasional — What is occasional happens every now and then rather than continuously, so you take letters at intervals instead of the lot.

A herbal tea that only gets made now and then.

One word carries the whole of the wordplay, which is why nothing in the clue looks like fodder until 'Occasional' turns out to be an instruction rather than a description of the tea.

24 down ALGA

Simple form of life spotted in spiral galaxy (4)

Definition: Simple form of life · hidden word

Simple form of life → ALGA: a single alga is one of the simplest plant-like organisms there is, with no roots, stems or leaves.

  • spiral galaxy → ALGA four letters running straight across the join between the two words
  • spotted in — To spot a thing is to catch sight of it where it already sits, so the letters are there in the words untouched.

Astronomers finding life somewhere out in a distant galaxy.

The join between two words is the classic hiding place, and 'spiral galaxy' is a real astronomical term, so nothing about the phrase looks assembled for the purpose.
