Independent Cryptic Crossword No 12,403

Across

1 across ABERRANT

Rogue charged seen in a dock endlessly (8)

Definition: Rogue · container + deletion

  • a dock endlessly → ABERT a berth is a dock for a ship, and with 'a' in front the phrase loses its last letter and splits after four
  • charged → RAN a bull that charged you ran at you, so the past tense of run stands in

The surface builds a courtroom — a rogue charged, standing in the dock — and both of those words belong somewhere else entirely: the dock is a mooring.

6 across TRAMPS

Weary Willies pee in streetcars (6)

Definition: Weary Willies · container

  • streetcars → TRAMS streetcars is the American word; the British one is five letters that open up before the last
  • pee → P the letter written the way its name is spelled

Weary Willie and Tired Tim were vagrants in an old British comic strip, which is why the definition arrives looking like two men's names.

9 across CHEMICAL WARFARE

Toxic campaign in Africa where calm disrupted (8,7)

Definition: Toxic campaign · anagram

  • Africa where calm → AFRICAWHERECALM fifteen letters of fodder, three words taken straight from the clue

'In Africa' reads as the place the campaign happened, so the continent looks like scene-setting; it is fodder, and 'in' is the only word here that carries nothing.

10 across TOWNIE

Urban resident to admit severing link (6)

Definition: Urban resident · container

  • link → TIE a tie is a link between two people, three letters cut after the first
  • admit → OWN to own up to something is to admit it

'To admit' looks like a plain infinitive holding the sentence upright; only the 'to' is doing that, and the verb after it is already fodder.

11 across ABERDARE

English road taken through a bleak Welsh town (8)

Definition: Welsh town · container

  • a bleak → ABARE a bleak hillside is a bare one, and with its 'a' in front the phrase splits after two letters
  • English → E E is the usual abbreviation for English, as on a school timetable
  • road → RD Rd is how a road is shortened in an address

Aber- opens a great many Welsh place names and means the mouth of a river; this one is a former coal town in the Cynon Valley, north-west of Cardiff.

12 across CORINTHIAN

Amateur player from insanely rich nation (10)

Definition: Amateur player · anagram

  • rich nation → RICHNATION ten letters of fodder, both words straight from the clue

Corinthian-Casuals still play in the English leagues, and the club's name is why the word came to stand for sport played for its own sake.

15 across VAIN

Ego in goods vehicle always looking in mirror (4)

Definition: always looking in mirror · container

  • goods vehicle → VAN the small lorry that carries goods, three letters that open up before the last
  • Ego → I the ego is the self, and ego is simply Latin for 'I'

The definition is a picture rather than a synonym, and it starts in the middle of what still reads as one sentence about a driver.

16 across ABLE

Clever message concealing its opening (4)

Definition: Clever · deletion

  • message → CABLE a cable is a message sent by telegraph, five letters with one to lose
  • its opening → C the letter that message word opens with, kept out of sight

'Concealing' tempts you to go hunting for letters buried in the clue; what it hides here is a single letter of the message, which simply goes.

17 across SALIVATION

Readiness to eat shown by one in safety (10)

Definition: Readiness to eat · container

  • safety → SALVATION being saved is safety in the religious sense, nine letters that part after three
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one

'Shown by' is pure joinery, asserting nothing except that the two halves of the clue are the same thing, and beginners lose time looking for a mechanism in it.

19 across SCOTLAND

Bed left in smooth state once (8)

Definition: state once · charade + container

  • smooth → SAND to sand a surface is to smooth it, and the four letters part after the first
  • Bed → COT a cot is a child's bed
  • left → L L is the standard abbreviation for left, as on a map or a boot

It was an independent kingdom until the Union with England in 1707, and that history is the whole job of 'once' at the end of the clue.

21 across TOW BAR

Support from AA where drinker heads without whiskey (3,3)

Definition: Support from AA · container

  • where drinker heads → TOBAR a drinker heads to the bar, and those five letters break apart at the join between the words
  • whiskey → W whiskey is W in the NATO alphabet that radio operators spell with

The AA is Britain's Automobile Association, whose patrols tow broken-down cars away — and the surface quietly lets you read it as Alcoholics Anonymous instead.

23 across ROCK OF GIBRALTAR

Surprise move for big wedding venue in European location (4,2,9)

Definition: European location · charade + anagram

  • Surprise → ROCK news that rocks you shocks you, so the verb can mean to surprise
  • for big → OFGIBR six letters out of two small words, rearranged
  • wedding venue → ALTAR the altar is where a couple stand to be married

The two words that look most like grammar are the fodder: 'for big' reads as filler holding the sentence together and it is the middle of the answer.

24 across SKATER

Fish by river: one to glide across frozen surface (6)

Definition: one to glide across frozen surface · charade

  • Fish → SKATE a flat cartilaginous sea fish, a close relative of the ray
  • river → R R is the map abbreviation for river

The colon is the setter marking his own seam: everything before it is wordplay, everything after it is the definition, and 'by' does the joining.

25 across MIND'S EYE

Object to view around yard in contemplation (5,3)

Definition: in contemplation · charade + container

  • Object to → MIND if you mind something you object to it, as in 'do you mind?'
  • view → SEE to view is to see, and the three letters part before the last
  • yard → Y y is the abbreviation for the imperial yard

The surface has someone peering over a fence at a garden, and not one word of it is scenery: every piece hands over letters and 'in' is the only join.

2 down BY HOOK OR BY CROOK

Whatever it takes from New York hobo and Cork boy (2,4,2,2,5)

Definition: Whatever it takes · anagram

  • York hobo → YORKHOBO eight letters of fodder, both words straight from the clue
  • Cork boy → CORKBOY seven more letters of fodder; Cork is a city and county in the south of Ireland

'New York' reads as one place and is really two jobs: the first word is the instruction, the second is raw material, and the fifteen letters come out as one flat idiom.

3 down RAMEN

Recipe the last word in noodles (5)

Definition: noodles · charade

  • Recipe → R R is the character a prescription opens with, from the Latin recipe, meaning 'take'
  • the last word → AMEN the word every prayer closes on, so having the last word is meant literally

Nothing in the surface is Japanese: it promises a cookery column having the final say, and the dish is assembled out of a prescription and a prayer.

4 down ANCIENT

Old canine somehow beginning to talk (7)

Definition: Old · anagram + first letter

  • canine → CANINE six letters of fodder, taken straight from the clue
  • beginning to talk → T the opening letter of talk

'Old canine' reads as one phrase about somebody's grey-muzzled dog; the parse cuts between the two words and keeps only the first for the definition.

5 down TULSA

Temperature lowered initially in America's city (5)

Definition: city · charade + container + first letter

  • Temperature → T T is the standard symbol for temperature, on a dial or in a formula
  • America's → USA the country in its three-letter form, which opens up after the first letter
  • lowered initially → L the opening letter of lowered

The apostrophe-s does two jobs at once: it makes the sentence read as a possessive while handing over a country whose short form is three letters.

6 down TRADE-IN

Drake's outside drawn into coach deal (5-2)

Definition: deal · container + outer letters

  • coach → TRAIN a coach is a railway carriage, and the whole five-letter word parts after three
  • Drake's outside → DE the first and last letters of the word for a male duck

Drake wants to be Sir Francis, or the rapper, or a first name at least; here it is only the male duck, and only its two ends.

7 down AFFIDAVIT

Declaration FIFA put out: Stateside lawyer against it (9)

Definition: Declaration · anagram + charade

  • FIFA put out → AFFI the four letters of football's world governing body, rearranged
  • Stateside lawyer → DA a District Attorney is the prosecuting lawyer in an American court, which is the work 'Stateside' does
  • against → V v for versus is how a case name sets one party against another
  • it → IT two letters lifted from the clue exactly as they stand

The surface is a legal row about football, and there is no padding anywhere in it: the governing body, the lawyer, the versus and even 'it' are all doing letter work.

8 down PORTRAIT OF A LADY

Mona Lisa in novel form? (8,2,1,4)

Definition: Mona Lisa · double definition

  • Mona Lisa → Leonardo painted one woman, half-length, and what hangs in the Louvre is exactly what these four words describe
  • novel → Henry James published a book under that same string of words in 1881

The italics are the tell that a title is being quoted, and the clue asks you to carry the same words off a gallery wall and onto a shelf of fiction.

13 down IDENTIKIT

Facial make-up used by the police? (9)

Definition: Facial make-up used by the police? · cryptic definition

  • Facial make-up → the surface offers cosmetics; read it the other way and make-up is what a thing is made up of, the parts a face is composed from
  • used by the police → detectives build a suspect's face to a witness's description, picking eyes, nose and chin out of a stock set

It began as a trade name and slipped into ordinary English the way Hoover did, which is why the answer looks like a common noun.

14 down ILL

Bad practice where doctor dismissed (3)

Definition: Bad · deletion

  • practice → DRILL a drill is a practice run, the sort a fire alarm or a squad does
  • doctor → DR Dr is the standard abbreviation, and this is the pair that leaves

'Bad practice' reads as one phrase, close to malpractice, which keeps the surface honest about doctors; the parse wants the second word on its own.

17 down STAFFER

Employee in post about to be recalled (7)

Definition: Employee · charade + reversal

  • post → STAFF a staff is a stout pole, which is what a gate post is
  • about → ER about is 're' at the head of a memo, and here it is turned back to front

'Post' offers a job, the mail and a piece of wood at once, and the surface leans on the first two; the parse wants the wooden one.

18 down VETERAN

Animal doctor worked near warhorse (7)

Definition: warhorse · charade + anagram

  • Animal doctor → VET the doctor animals are taken to
  • near → ERAN the four letters of near, rearranged

The surface puts a vet at work beside an old cavalry mount, which is exactly the wrong seam: the horse is the definition, not part of the scene.

20 down DEISM

Reasonable belief in beatitude is misplaced (5)

Definition: Reasonable belief · hidden word

  • beatitude is misplaced → DEISM five consecutive letters run out of the end of the first word, across the second and into the third

'Misplaced' looks like an instruction to shuffle everything in front of it; it is only the last word of the hiding place, and nothing moves at all.

22 down WELLS

Social realist in healthy society (5)

Definition: Social realist · charade

  • healthy → WELL someone healthy is in good health
  • society → S S is the abbreviation for society, the one a club puts in its initials

H.G. Wells is remembered for the time machine and the Martians, but he also wrote closely observed novels of shop assistants and clerks, which is the side the definition takes.
