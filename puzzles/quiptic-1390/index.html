Across

1 across RAMBLERS

Countryside walkers go on grass regularly (8)

Definition: Countryside walkers · charade + alternate letters

  • go on → RAMBLE to go on — to talk at length — is to ramble
  • grass → RS the even letters of gRaSs, taken 'regularly'

The surface pictures hikers misbehaving on the lawn, but 'go on' means chatter — the chatty sense of RAMBLE — before grass surrenders its alternate letters.

6 across PROMPT

Physical training on esplanade is timely (6)

Definition: timely · charade

  • esplanade → PROM the seaside promenade — an esplanade
  • Physical training → PT PT is the standard abbreviation for physical training

In an across entry 'on' means 'following', so the piece the clue names first ends up second in the answer. That reversal is a positional convention worth learning, and it points the other way in a down clue.

9 across SOURCE

Origin of flexible course (6)

Definition: Origin · anagram

  • course → COURSE the six letters to rearrange

'Flexible' bends COURSE into shape — a neat pairing, since a river's course begins at its source.

10 across COERCION

Force translation of Cicero? No! (8)

Definition: Force · anagram

  • Cicero? No! → CICERONO the Roman orator plus the exclamation supply the eight letters

'Translation' is a sly indicator for a Latin orator: it rearranges Cicero's letters, NO included, rather than his prose.

11 across LIMESTONE

Travelling miles to north-eastern rock (9)

Definition: rock · anagram + charade

  • miles to → MILESTO seven letters sent 'travelling'
  • north-eastern → NE the compass abbreviation, tacked on unshuffled

The setter splits the work: an anagram builds the front while NE simply follows it — the compass point stays out of the shuffle.

13 across VENUS

Seven ushers surrounding goddess (5)

Definition: goddess · hidden word

  • Seven ushers → VENUS hidden across the two words: seVEN UShers

'Surrounding' says the ushers have the goddess inside them — the answer runs straight across the gap between the words.

15 across TRAGIC

Very sad ending to Hamlet leads to riots against Gertrude in consequence (6)

Definition: Very sad · charade + last letter + first letters

  • Hamlet → T the play's final letter — its 'ending'
  • riots against Gertrude in consequence → RAGIC the leading letters of the five words

'Leads to' looks like joinery but is the instruction: take the leads — the front letters — of the last five words. Fitting, for a play that ends strewn with bodies.

17 across SARTRE

Paris art revolution absorbs philosopher (6)

Definition: philosopher · hidden word

  • Paris art revolution → SARTRE hidden across all three words: pariS ART REvolution

A lovely match of surface and answer: Sartre was the great philosopher of Paris cafe society, and he is literally sitting in 'Paris art revolution'.

18 across REPEAL

Withdraw from drive back across Australia (6)

Definition: Withdraw · container

  • drive back → REPEL to drive back an attack is to repel it — it opens around the A
  • Australia → A the one-letter abbreviation, as in A$ for the Australian dollar

'Drive back' is one unit meaning REPEL, not a reversal — the surface wants you picturing an outback road trip that never happens.

19 across PALATE

Dish overwhelms a sense of taste (6)

Definition: sense of taste · container

  • Dish → PLATE a plate is a dish — it swallows the A
  • a → A the letter taken inside

'Overwhelms' is the container signal: the dish engulfs the A, and the food surface steers you neatly past the anatomical answer.

21 across SCARF

Oscar frequently clutches muffler (5)

Definition: muffler · hidden word

  • Oscar frequently → SCARF hidden across the two words: oSCAR Frequently

Oscar clutches the answer literally: it runs from inside his name into the word after it.

22 across FREE GIFTS

Incentives to buy liberal Guardian at first, then Independent, FT and Sun (4,5)

Definition: Incentives to buy · charade + first letters

  • liberal → FREE liberal means free — generous, unrestricted
  • Guardian → G its first letter
  • Independent → I its first letter
  • FT → FT already initials, so it goes in whole
  • Sun → S its first letter

'At first' serves every paper in the queue — the FT being initials already — and a Guardian puzzle clueing its rivals is a nice in-joke.

25 across DISTANCE

Reserve placing first in tango? (8)

Definition: Reserve · container

  • first → IST 1st — 'first' written with figures, the 1 reading as I
  • tango? → DANCE a tango is a dance — the question mark flags the definition by example

Writing 'first' as 1st and then reading the figure 1 as the letter I is the leap here. The question mark concedes that a tango is only one dance among many.

26 across ELICIT

Produce unlawful broadcast roughly (6)

Definition: Produce · homophone

  • unlawful → ELICIT illicit means unlawful

ILLICIT and ELICIT are not quite identical aloud, and the setter admits it: 'roughly' licenses the loose soundalike.

28 across UNISON

Complete agreement from academic offspring? (6)

Definition: Complete agreement · charade

  • academic → UNI university, informally — the academic place
  • offspring? → SON a son is one kind of offspring — the question mark allows for daughters

An 'academic offspring' sounds like a professor's child; the wordplay just glues UNI to SON.

29 across DISMALLY

Sadly, not big in home-improvement (8)

Definition: Sadly · container

  • not big → SMALL not big = small
  • home-improvement → DIY do-it-yourself — it wraps around SMALL

The comma after 'Sadly' is the misdirection: it makes the definition read as a passing aside rather than the word being defined. Home-improvement for DIY is a standard dodge.

2 down AGO

Principles of Ancient Greece overthrown in the past (3)

Definition: in the past · first letters

  • Ancient Greece overthrown → AGO the first letters of the three words

'Principles' puns on 'principal': take the principal — first — letters. The toppled civilisation is pure surface drama.

3 down BARGE

Move clumsily in boat (5)

Definition: Move clumsily · double definition

  • Move clumsily → BARGE to shove gracelessly through a space, as when pushing past someone in a hurry
  • boat → BARGE a flat-bottomed vessel used on canals and rivers for heavy cargo

The surface has you rowing badly; the split comes at 'in', leaving a verb on one side and a noun on the other.

4 down ELECTRICAL

Celtic and Real played with a kind of energy (10)

Definition: a kind of energy · anagram

  • Celtic and Real → CELTICREAL two famous football clubs supply the ten letters — 'and' adds none

Celtic and Real Madrid keep the surface on the pitch, where 'played' looks innocent — but it is the anagram indicator rearranging the clubs' letters.

5 down SECOND

Back in a mo! (6)

Definition: Back · double definition

  • Back → SECOND to formally support or back a proposal put to a meeting
  • a mo → SECOND a mo is short for a moment — a very brief unit of time

The whole clue reads as 'I'll be right back', which is the misdirection: split it at 'in' and each half defines the answer on its own.

6 down PIER

Reportedly look for breakwater (4)

Definition: breakwater · homophone

  • look → PIER to peer is to look closely

Say 'peer' aloud and you hear the answer — a classic seaside soundalike pair.

7 down ORCHESTRA

Gold box right next to a group of musicians (9)

Definition: group of musicians · charade

  • Gold → OR OR is heraldry's word for gold — a crossword staple
  • box → CHEST a chest is a box
  • right → R R abbreviates right
  • a → A the little word counts itself into the answer

Even the 'a' is a working part here, gluing itself onto the end — not every small word is padding.

8 down PROCUREMENT

Acquisition of professional remedy meant amateur excluded (11)

Definition: Acquisition · charade + deletion

  • professional → PRO a pro is a professional
  • remedy → CURE a cure is a remedy
  • meant → MENT MEANT once its A has gone
  • amateur → A A for amateur, the sports abbreviation — this is the letter 'excluded' from MEANT

Amateur = A comes from sport, where A once distinguished unpaid players from professionals — apt in a clue that opens with PRO.

12 down INTERACTION

Ancient trio improvised joint performance (11)

Definition: joint performance · anagram

  • Ancient trio → ANCIENTTRIO eleven letters of fodder

'Improvised' — just what a trio might do on stage — is the anagram signal, and the musical surface hides a much drier word.

14 down PARAMETERS

Badly parse a term for boundaries (10)

Definition: boundaries · anagram

  • parse a term → PARSEATERM the ten letters of fodder

A self-aware clue — parsing it is exactly what you are doing — with 'badly' handling the mangling.

16 down APPARATUS

Astronauts initially strap up a complicated piece of equipment (9)

Definition: piece of equipment · anagram + first letter

  • Astronauts → A 'initially' keeps just its first letter
  • strap up a → STRAPUPA eight more letters for the mix

'Initially' trims Astronauts to a single A before 'complicated' shuffles everything — the spacewalk surface disguises a very general word.

20 down FRIEND

Mate cooked around noon (6)

Definition: Mate · container

  • cooked → FRIED cooked in hot fat — it wraps round the N
  • noon → N N is the abbreviation for noon, as on old timetables

'Cooked' looks like an anagram indicator but is fodder here: FRIED, with midday's N dropped in.

23 down IBIZA

Current business account’s opening in island (5)

Definition: island · charade + first letter

  • Current → I I is the physics symbol for electric current
  • business → BIZ the showbiz-style shortening
  • account’s → A its opening letter

Three abbreviations in a row — physics, slang and an initial — assemble the Balearic party island out of office paperwork.

24 down ANON

Member of clergy loses hat immediately according to Shakespeare (4)

Definition: immediately according to Shakespeare · deletion + first letter

  • Member of clergy → CANON a canon is a cathedral cleric — about to lose its top

In a down entry the 'hat' is the top letter, so the C comes off CANON. 'I come, anon!' is how Shakespeare's characters say they'll be right there.

27 down ILL

Unwell? Unwrap tablets (3)

Definition: Unwell · deletion + outer letters

  • tablets → PILLS tablets are pills — unwrapping strips off the outer P and S

'Unwrap' means exactly what it says: peel the outside letters off PILLS, the way you would the packaging.
