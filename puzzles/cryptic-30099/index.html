Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,099

Across

1 across GROWN-UP

Cultivate witticism about being adult (5-2)

Definition: adult · charade + reversal

  • Cultivate → GROW to cultivate a crop is to grow it, taken straight as four letters
  • witticism → NUP a witticism is a pun, written the other way round here

'Cultivate witticism' reads as one instruction — be funny — which is what hides the join between the two pieces, and the turn happens on the second of them.

5 across BITCOIN

Currency is part I contributed to swindle (7)

Definition: Currency · charade + container

  • part → BIT a bit is a part or fragment of something
  • swindle → CON a con is a swindle, and this one opens up after its first two letters
  • I → I the clue's own I, taken as a single letter

'Part I contributed to' reads as somebody owning up to a share in a fraud, and the pronoun in the middle of that confession is one letter of the answer.

9 across PETIT

Small piece of advice about way to reassure your dog? (5)

Definition: Small · double definition

  • Small → the French adjective for little, borrowed whole into English in phrases about cakes and the bourgeoisie
  • way to reassure your dog? → respace the five letters as two words and they stop being an adjective and become the advice itself: stroke the animal

Read the answer as PET IT and it is the tip the clue offers; read it as one word and it is French for small. The question mark marks the respacing.

10 across NAUSEATES

USA Senate carve-up turns one’s stomach (9)

Definition: turns one’s stomach · anagram

  • USA → USA three letters as they stand
  • Senate → SENATE six more, completing nine letters of raw material

The surface is a political headline about Washington horse-trading, and the word doing the work, 'carve-up', is exactly the word such a headline would use.

11 across E-PASSPORT

Earl, unable to answer question, left to get some identification (1-8)

Definition: some identification · charade

  • Earl → E E is the standard abbreviation for earl in the peerage
  • unable to answer question → PASS on a quiz show you say pass when the answer will not come
  • left → PORT port is the left-hand side of a vessel, looking forward

Three pieces in a row and not one instruction between them, so the whole job is spotting that the commas break the sentence exactly where the parse does.

12 across FEWER

Less often confused with it when counting (5)

Definition: Less often confused with it when counting · cryptic definition

  • Less often confused → the surface offers a comparison about how frequently a mix-up happens, as though the setter were being reassuring
  • with it when counting → 'it' is the word 'less' itself, and the answer is the one the pedants insist on as soon as the things can be counted

There is no wordplay to find here, only a grammar lesson: 'less' belongs with what you measure, FEWER with what you count, and swapping them is the mistake the clue is about.

13 across RUINS

Heads of redbrick universities institute new standards for dilapidated buildings (5)

Definition: dilapidated buildings · first letters

  • redbrick universities institute new standards → RUINS five words in a row, each contributing its opening letter in the order written

The surface is a plausible sentence about higher-education policy, and a redbrick university is a British one founded in the industrial cities around 1900 — a phrase chosen so the list reads as news.

15 across ARCH RIVAL

Chief competitor, if losing run, would be matter of record (4,5)

Definition: Chief competitor · deletion

  • would be matter of record → ARCHIVAL material of record is archival, the kind of thing kept in an archive
  • run → R r is the cricket scorer's abbreviation for a run

Anto works backwards here: rather than building the answer, the clue tells you what would be left of it after a subtraction, and you have to put the letter back.

18 across FEEL GREAT

Left eager to get transformed and be happy (4,5)

Definition: be happy · anagram

  • Left → LEFT four letters off the front of the clue, as they stand
  • eager → EAGER five more, to be jumbled together with the first four

'Left eager' reads as a report on somebody's mood — she was left keen — which is exactly the sort of ordinary phrase that can hide nine letters of fodder.

19 across SPROG

Issue dealt with in Pilgrim’s Progress (5)

Definition: Issue · hidden word

  • Pilgrim’s Progress → SPROG the run starts at the possessive S and carries straight on into the next word

Bunyan's title is capitalised and italicised, which makes it look like reference material rather than raw letters. SPROG is British slang for a small child.

21 across NIGHT

Close temperature when sun goes down (5)

Definition: when sun goes down · charade

  • Close → NIGH nigh is the old word for near, as in 'the end is nigh'
  • temperature → T T is the standard abbreviation for temperature

'Close' is the adjective meaning near, not the verb meaning shut and not the muggy weather sense either — that choice is the whole difficulty in five letters.

23 across SANG FROID

RAF’s doing badly, showing coolness under pressure (4,5)

Definition: coolness under pressure · anagram

  • RAF’s → RAFS the air force's initials plus the S of the contraction, four letters in all
  • doing → DOING five more, taken exactly as they stand

A nine-letter anagram from only two words of fodder, and the trick is accepting that an apostrophe-S counts as a letter like any other.

25 across GESTATION

Post on half of wage when things are developing (9)

Definition: when things are developing · charade + last letters

  • half of wage → GE the word split down the middle, with the back two letters kept
  • Post → STATION a sentry's post is a station, the place they are set to keep

The surface is payroll admin. 'Post' is the military sense rather than the mail, and that switch is what hides the longer of the two pieces.

26 across CRIME

Giving lip in church is a sin (5)

Definition: a sin · container

  • church → CE CE is the Church of England, the crossword's usual church
  • lip → RIM the lip of a cup or a crater is its rim

The trap is 'lip' meaning cheek, which is what the surface wants — a parishioner being rude in church. The wordplay means the other lip, the edge of a vessel.

27 across EGOISTS

Selfish people say Oscar is extremely tedious (7)

Definition: Selfish people · charade + outer letters

  • say → EG 'say' introduces an example, which is what e.g. stands for
  • Oscar → O Oscar is O in the phonetic alphabet used over the radio
  • is → IS the clue's own word, taken at face value as two letters
  • extremely tedious → TS the outer letters of the adjective, with its middle thrown away

Four small pieces in a row, and the trap is reading Oscar as somebody being gossiped about. Every word here supplies letters, including the innocuous 'is'.

28 across SPLAYED

Moved quickly to protect place when stretched (7)

Definition: stretched · container

  • Moved quickly → SPED to speed is to move quickly, and the word opens after two letters
  • place → LAY to place something is to lay it down

'Place' looks like a noun in the surface — something worth guarding — and it is the verb 'lay', which is what makes the inner piece so hard to see.

Down

1 down GAP YEAR

Constantly talk equipment with Spooner in period before university (3,4)

Definition: period before university · spoonerism

  • Constantly talk → GAP to yap is to talk constantly, and this word hands its Y over to the one behind it
  • equipment → YEAR equipment is gear, and it takes in exchange the Y that was just given up

Say the wordplay aloud before you write anything down: the swap happens in the mouth, not on the page, and the surface's talk of kit is pure decoration.

2 down OSTRACISE

CIA stores criminal blacklist (9)

Definition: blacklist · anagram

  • CIA → CIA the agency's initials, three letters of raw material
  • stores → STORES six more, making nine to be rearranged

The surface is a spy story about files being kept, and 'criminal' sits where an adjective belongs in that sentence, which is why it does not read as an instruction.

3 down NOTES

Records found when breaking up sofa? (5)

Definition: Records · double definition

  • Records → the everyday sense: what a secretary keeps of a meeting, or a doctor of a patient
  • breaking up sofa? → break the word SOFA in two and you are holding SO and FA, two steps of the sol-fa scale singers learn

'Breaking up' looks like an anagram instruction with four letters to shuffle. What is broken up is the word itself, into two syllables a musician reads, and the question mark owns the whimsy.

4 down PANTO DAME

Criticise book about father being camp mother at Christmas (5,4)

Definition: camp mother at Christmas · charade + container

  • Criticise → PAN to pan a play is to criticise it savagely in print
  • book → TOME a tome is a weighty book, and it parts after its first two letters
  • father → DA da is a familiar word for father in Ireland and the north of England

'Camp' is what sells the misdirection: in the surface it is a jibe at the father, and in the answer it is a plain description of how the part is played.

5 down BLUNT

Plain type of sandwich has a French filling (5)

Definition: Plain · container

  • type of sandwich → BLT a BLT is bacon, lettuce and tomato, ordered by its three initials
  • a French → UN 'a' in French is un, the masculine form of the article

The lunch counter is the joke: a BLT really does take its filling in the middle, and the French article slides in as though it were on the menu.

6 down TREE-FERNS

Sea birds circling coastal feature to find plants (4-5)

Definition: plants · container

  • Sea birds → TERNS terns are the sea birds, and the word opens after its first letter
  • coastal feature → REEF a reef is the ridge of rock or coral lying just off a shore

Every word of the surface is coastal — birds, a shoreline — while the answer belongs in a damp forest. That distance is what a good container clue buys.

7 down ON TOW

Regular cognition women get being dragged around (2,3)

Definition: being dragged around · charade + alternate letters

  • cognition → ONTO every second letter of the word, in the order they stand
  • women → W W is the standard abbreviation for women, as on a lavatory door

'Regular cognition' sounds like a phrase about ordinary thinking and is really a counting instruction — a pairing that hides a very short answer.

8 down NOSTRIL

Central parts of genome system purify cells forming air passage (7)

Definition: air passage · charade + middle letters

  • genome → NO six letters, and only the middle pair survives
  • system → ST the same trim again, taken from the heart of the word
  • purify → RI two more from the centre, in the order they stand
  • cells → L an odd-length word has a single letter at its middle

The real difficulty is scope: 'Central parts of' governs all four words of the scientific-sounding list, not just the one standing next to it.

14 down SIGHT GAGS

In one sense, mufflers funny things to look at (5,4)

Definition: funny things to look at · charade

  • In one sense → SIGHT one of the five senses, named rather than described
  • mufflers → GAGS a gag is tied over the mouth to muffle whoever wears it

'In one sense' looks like a concession — in one respect, scarves are amusing — and it is naming a faculty instead. That switch is the whole clue.

16 down CO-TENANTS

Business has ten workers who share accommodation (2-7)

Definition: who share accommodation · charade

  • Business → CO co. is how company is written after a firm's name
  • ten → TEN the number goes in as the three letters that spell it
  • workers → ANTS ants are the crossword's standard workers, the labourers of the colony

'Ten workers' reads as a headcount at a small firm. Both halves of it are letters, and 'worker' meaning ant is a convention worth owning for good.

17 down VERBOSITY

Talking too much, manager almost overwhelmed by truth (9)

Definition: Talking too much · container + deletion

  • truth → VERITY verity is truth, the old noun still heard in 'eternal verities'
  • manager almost → BOS the manager is the boss, stopped one letter short

The surface sets up an office scandal, a manager buried under the facts, and its two most ordinary-sounding words are both instructions.

18 down FINAGLE

Trick old horse to get into line (7)

Definition: Trick · container

  • line → FILE soldiers in a line stand in file, and the word parts after two letters
  • old horse → NAG a nag is a worn-out horse past its working days

The surface is a stable-yard picture of a stubborn animal being led into line, and the definition is the single word standing at the front of it.

20 down GODSEND

Without AI, diagnosed ruptures – it’s an unexpected benefit (7)

Definition: an unexpected benefit · anagram + deletion

  • diagnosed → DGNOSED the nine letters of the word with the A and the I already removed, leaving seven to rearrange

A thoroughly modern surface — a diagnosis reached without artificial intelligence — and the topical abbreviation is doing plain letter-work.

22 down GUSTO

Some plot suggestions raised, showing enthusiasm (5)

Definition: enthusiasm · hidden word + reversal

  • plot suggestions → OTSUG the run begins inside the first word and finishes inside the second, and this column is read from the bottom

The surface is a writers' meeting where ideas go up on the board. Two instructions stack: take part of the text, then read what you took the other way.

23 down SKIPS

Dirty place in ship where dumped material stored (5)

Definition: where dumped material stored · container

  • ship → SS SS heads a steamship's name, as in SS Great Britain
  • Dirty place → KIP a kip is British and Irish slang for a filthy, run-down place

The surface paints a grubby corner of a boat. Kip is worth learning twice over: as well as a squalid place it means a sleep, which is how it usually turns up.

24 down FOCAL

Critical secondary characters in office come across major flaws (5)

Definition: Critical · second letters

  • office → F the letter standing second in the word
  • come → O second again, one word further along
  • across → C the same position in the third word of the run
  • major → A second letter once more
  • flaws → L and the last of the five, taken from the same place

'Secondary characters' reads as the minor players in an office drama, which is a fine sentence and a counting instruction at the same time.
