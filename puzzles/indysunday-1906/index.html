Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,906

Across

1 across ST JAMES'S

Judge kept to station canteen's clubby area (2,6)

Definition: clubby area · container + charade

  • station → STA STA is the abbreviation for station on maps and timetables
  • Judge → J J is the abbreviation for Judge or Justice in law reports
  • canteen → MESS the mess is where soldiers eat, the services' word for a canteen

A judge who has to take his lunch in the station canteen rather than anywhere grander.

The area is the patch of central London round Pall Mall where the old gentlemen's clubs sit, so 'clubby' is literal rather than a joke about atmosphere. The possessive on 'canteen's' belongs to the sentence, not to the letters.

5 across SPOT ON

From behind, all bottoms presumably perfect (4,2)

Definition: perfect · reversal

  • all bottoms presumably → NOTOPS if every last thing is a bottom then there are no tops, and those two words are what gets turned round

A rear view in which everything on show is a bottom.

'Presumably' is the setter's nudge that you are being asked to reason rather than to look up a synonym: work out what is left over once every last thing is a bottom, then read it the other way.

10 across TAXES

You shouldn't have relations over, they're a burden every year (5)

Definition: they're a burden every year · charade + reversal

  • You shouldn't have → TA 'you shouldn't have' is what you say on being handed a present, and ta is the British thank-you behind it
  • relations → XES relations in the carnal sense, written in backwards here

Advice against inviting the relatives round, since having them to stay is an annual chore.

Two everyday phrases are being read against their meaning at once: the relatives you have round become relations of quite another kind, and a modest protest over a gift becomes a two-letter thank-you.

11 across TRUMPED-UP

Invented politician accepted by genuine political party (7-2)

Definition: Invented · container + charade

  • genuine → TRUE a genuine article is a true one
  • politician → MP an MP is a Member of Parliament
  • political party → DUP the DUP is the Democratic Unionist Party, the main unionist party in Northern Ireland

A politician being accepted into a real political party.

The surname the surface plays on has nothing to do with the word's history: a trump was a trumpet blast, and to trump something up was to announce it loudly whether or not it was so.

12 across SPARTAN

Very plain and tough supermarket belt (7)

Definition: Very plain and tough · charade

  • supermarket → SPAR Spar is a chain of small grocery shops found on British high streets
  • belt → TAN to tan somebody's hide is to belt them

The plain, hard-wearing conveyor belt at a supermarket checkout.

'Belt' is the checkout conveyor on the surface and a beating underneath. The definition is the answer's own history: a city that made a virtue of doing without.

13 across SCOURGE

Whip's firm push on Reeves's back (7)

Definition: Whip · charade + last letter

  • Reeves's back → S the last letter of REEVES — Rachel Reeves, the Chancellor of the Exchequer
  • firm → CO CO is short for company, a firm
  • push → URGE to push somebody to do something is to urge them

A party whip giving the Chancellor a firm shove from behind.

The Whip of the surface is the MP who keeps a party voting the right way; the answer is the older, literal kind of whip. 'On' in an across clue puts a piece after what follows it, not above.

14 across DUO

Two performing circus's flying act wanting doctor (3)

Definition: Two · deletion

  • performing circus's flying act → DUMBO Dumbo is the circus elephant who flies with his ears in the Disney film
  • doctor → MB MB is Bachelor of Medicine, the degree letters a doctor carries

The circus's flying act going ahead with no doctor standing by.

The flying act is Disney's elephant rather than a trapeze artist, and 'wanting' is the older sense of lacking, not of desiring.

15 across POST-IT NOTE

Small leaf peeled off school clown's work passed round (4-2,4)

Definition: Small leaf peeled off · charade + reversal

  • school → ETON Eton is the English public school crosswords reach for first
  • clown's → TITS a tit is British slang for a fool or clown, and the possessive brings its own letter along
  • work → OP op is short for opus, a composer's work

The class clown's homework going round with a small leaf peeled off it.

Everything is written down the natural way and then read back to front, which is why the school comes out at the far end. 'Leaf' is a sheet of paper, the sense in leafing through a book.

18 across REAL TENNIS

Wimbledon champion on comeback nets key point in game (4,6)

Definition: game · reversal + container + charade

  • Wimbledon champion on comeback → RENNIS Jannik Sinner, the Italian who has won Wimbledon, with his surname read back to front
  • key → ALT Alt is the key at the bottom left of a computer keyboard
  • point → E E is east, one of the four points of the compass

A returning Wimbledon champion winning a crucial point.

The older indoor game, played off the walls of a covered court, is the one lawn tennis was named after — so putting a Wimbledon champion in the clue points you at the wrong sport on purpose.

21 across PEA

Some ripe avocado that could be eaten mushy (3)

Definition: that could be eaten mushy · hidden word

  • ripe avocado → PEA three letters in a row, starting inside the first word and finishing inside the second

An avocado ripe enough to be eaten mashed.

The avocado is there to have you thinking of the wrong green mash. Nothing in a shopping list asks to be read letter by letter, which is the cover a hidden word wants.

23 across FLAVOUR

Relish learner driver taking good turn on the outside (7)

Definition: Relish · container

  • good turn → FAVOUR doing somebody a good turn is doing them a favour
  • learner driver → L L is the plate a learner driver carries in Britain

A learner driver making a good turn from the outside lane.

'Relish' is the noun — the taste of a thing — rather than the verb, and 'good turn' is the kindness rather than anything the car is doing.

25 across AT A BLOW

Bill on last in Augusta down in one (2,1,4)

Definition: in one · charade + last letter

  • last in Augusta → A the final letter of AUGUSTA, taken on its own
  • Bill → TAB a tab is the bill you run up at a bar
  • down → LOW down and low are the same direction

A bill at Augusta and a drink knocked back in one go.

'Down in one' is a drinking phrase on the surface, so the definition looks as though it starts a word earlier than it does: 'down' has been kept back for the wordplay. Augusta is the American golf course.

26 across CRAPSHOOT

Posh actor played game of dice in US (9)

Definition: game of dice in US · anagram

  • Posh actor → POSHACTOR anagram fodder, nine letters
  • played → anagram indicator

A well-spoken actor taking a role.

Two words of ordinary description turn straight into an American gambling word, and in the States it covers any gamble at all rather than only the dice.

27 across MAGUS

King at Christmas starts to get upset during service, missing son (5)

Definition: King at Christmas · container + deletion + first letters

  • service → MASS Mass is the Catholic church service
  • son → S S is the standard abbreviation for son, and this one is taken out of the service
  • starts to get upset → GU the opening letters of GET and UPSET

A king growing upset during the Christmas service because his son is not there.

The kings at Christmas are the wise men of the nativity — three in the carol, one of them here — and 'starts to' is collecting letters rather than describing a mood.

28 across TEETER

Someone who posted on X dismissing Welsh rock (6)

Definition: rock · deletion

  • Someone who posted on X → TWEETER X was called Twitter until 2023, and somebody posting there was a tweeter
  • Welsh → W W is the abbreviation for Welsh

Somebody on X being rude about Welsh rock music.

'Rock' is the verb, to sway unsteadily, rather than the music the surface is talking about, and 'Welsh' shrinks to a single letter. The site's change of name does the rest.

29 across BRUNETTE

Dark web protected by monster (8)

Definition: Dark · container

  • monster → BRUTE a brute is a monster of a man
  • web → NET a spider's web is a net

The dark web being guarded by something monstrous.

'Dark web' reads as one thing and is doing two separate jobs: the first word is the definition, the second is three letters waiting to be hidden.

Down

1 down SET ASIDE

Reserve ultimate in opulent beach houses (3,5)

Definition: Reserve · container + last letter

  • beach → SEASIDE the seaside is where a beach is, the British word for the coast you go to on holiday
  • ultimate in opulent → T the last letter of OPULENT, taken on its own

Booking the very best of the luxury houses along the beach.

'Beach houses' wants to be one thing you rent for a fortnight. 'Houses' is really the verb, and the two words either side of it belong to different halves of the clue.

2 down JUXTAPOSE

Gravy with fresh bit of turkey and Paxo stuffing that European put together (9)

Definition: put together · container + anagram + first letter

  • Gravy → JUS jus is the thin gravy of meat juices on a restaurant menu
  • bit of turkey → T the first letter of TURKEY, taken on its own
  • Paxo → PAXO Paxo is the packet sage-and-onion stuffing mix on British Christmas tables
  • European → E E is the standard abbreviation for European

Christmas dinner on a plate: gravy, a fresh bit of turkey and some packet stuffing.

The surface is entirely Christmas dinner, and the answer is about setting two things side by side to compare them. 'Stuffing' is the word doing double duty, edible above and an instruction below.

3 down MISSTEP

Error from teacher upset special pupil (7)

Definition: Error · charade + reversal

  • teacher → MISS Miss is what British schoolchildren call a woman teacher
  • special pupil → TEP the teacher's pet is the child a teacher favours, arriving the other way up here

A teacher put out by the pupil she treats as special.

'Upset' reads as the teacher's mood and is only an instruction about direction. The phrase being leaned on is teacher's pet, with 'special' standing in for the possessive half of it.

4 down SET ONE'S HEART ON

Really fancy fleece secured by tenner bills on the counter? (3,4,5,2)

Definition: Really fancy · reversal + container

  • tenner → NOTE a tenner is a ten-pound note, so the money word wanted is NOTE
  • bills → NOTES bills is the American word for banknotes
  • fleece → SHEAR to fleece a sheep is to shear it

Falling for a fleece jacket while the ten-pound notes are counted out on the shop counter.

'Fleece' looks like the garment on the rail and is really the verb, what a farmer does to a sheep. The counter of the surface is a shop's; in the wordplay it is the direction the money is read.

6 down POPCORN

Hard-up network initially claims computer inflated fare (7)

Definition: inflated fare · charade + container + first letter

  • Hard-up → POOR someone hard-up has no money — they are poor
  • computer → PC a PC is a personal computer
  • network initially → N the first letter of NETWORK, taken on its own

A hard-up network blaming its computer for a fare that has been pushed up.

'Fare' is food here, the sense in bill of fare, not the price of a journey — and 'inflated' turns out to be literal rather than a complaint about prices.

7 down TUDOR

In France you impersonate king from 16C (5)

Definition: from 16C · charade

  • In France you → TU tu is the French word for you
  • impersonate → DO to do somebody is to impersonate them, as a comic does a politician
  • king → R R is rex, Latin for king, the letter that follows a monarch's name

In France you do an impression of a sixteenth-century king.

The definition is the tail of the clue and it is an adjective for a period, not a person. 'You' is the French pronoun rather than the solver being addressed.

8 down NEPHEW

Relative quiet with fellow entertained by novel (6)

Definition: Relative · charade + container

  • novel → NEW a novel idea is a new one
  • quiet → P p is piano in music, the marking for quiet
  • fellow → HE a fellow is a man, and the pronoun for a man is he

A quiet man and his companion being kept entertained by a book.

'Novel' is a book on the surface and the adjective underneath. 'Fellow' is how a two-letter pronoun gets into a clue without the word itself ever appearing.

9 down QUESTION MASTER

Examiner rearranged equation's terms (8,6)

Definition: Examiner · anagram

  • equation's terms → EQUATIONSTERMS anagram fodder, fourteen letters
  • rearranged → anagram indicator

A maths examiner moving the terms of an equation about.

In algebra you really do rearrange the terms of an equation, so the instruction is hiding inside a sentence that sounds like ordinary schoolwork.

16 down TOP-FLIGHT

Highest quality fine grass turned pale (3-6)

Definition: Highest quality · reversal + charade

  • fine → F F is fine, the grade stamped on a pencil
  • grass → POT pot is slang for cannabis, the other kind of grass
  • pale → LIGHT a pale colour is a light one

A patch of fine grass that has turned pale.

On the surface the grass is a lawn; in the wordplay it is the smoking kind. The instruction catches both words in front of it rather than only the one it stands next to.

17 down LAY WASTE

We stay outside for awful quakes destroy everything (3,5)

Definition: destroy everything · anagram + outer letters

  • We stay → WESTAY anagram fodder, six letters
  • outside for awful → AL the outer letters of AWFUL, its A and its L
  • quakes → anagram indicator

People staying out of doors while terrible earthquakes go on.

'Outside' looks like where the speaker is standing and is really an instruction about letters — the two ends of the word after it. The rest is what an earthquake does to anything loose.

19 down LOOK-SEE

Butcher's search for cycles behind outhouse (4-3)

Definition: Butcher's · charade + cycling

  • outhouse → LOO the loo is the British word for the lavatory, and an outhouse is one down the garden
  • search for → KSEE to search for something is to seek it, with the final letter brought round to the front

A butcher hunting for bicycles round the back of the outhouse.

'Butcher's' is rhyming slang — butcher's hook, hook rhyming with look, and the rhyming word dropped as it always is. 'Cycles' is not bicycles at all but the instruction next to it.

20 down SWAGMAN

Itinerant Aussie in business meeting held by trumpeter? (7)

Definition: Itinerant Aussie · container

  • trumpeter? → SWAN the trumpeter swan is a large North American bird named for its call
  • business meeting → AGM an AGM is a company's annual general meeting

A wandering Australian sitting in on a business meeting called by someone who plays the trumpet.

'Trumpeter' reads as a musician and is really a species of bird, which the question mark half admits. Waltzing Matilda is where most people outside Australia meet the answer.

22 down OFFCUT

Scrap cricketing tautology such as leg-pull? (6)

Definition: Scrap · charade

  • cricketing tautology → OFF the off is one side of a cricket field, the side the batter faces
  • such as leg-pull? → CUT a leg-pull pairs a side of the field with the stroke hit to it — a pull goes to leg, and the stroke played to the off is a cut

Somebody having a joke at a cricket match.

A leg-pull is a joke on the surface and a piece of cricket grammar underneath: pull and leg name the same side of the field twice over, and you are asked for the pair that does it on the other side.

24 down AMAZE

A former PM's caught hit for six (5)

Definition: hit for six · homophone

  • A former PM's → AMAZE Theresa May was Prime Minister from 2016 to 2019, and her name with a possessive on the end is what you say aloud

A former prime minister caught out at cricket.

'Caught' looks like the cricket dismissal and means heard. Being knocked for six is being hit clean out of the ground, which is where the idiom for astonishment comes from.
