Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,146

Across

1 across AS PER USUAL

Predictably, sadly, start to unclasp purse, alas! (2,3,5)

Definition: Predictably · anagram + first letter

  • start to unclasp → U one letter, the one 'unclasp' begins with
  • purse, alas! → PURSEALAS nine letters handed over whole, punctuation ignored, to be jumbled with that U

Two adverbs in a row at the front is the tell: one of them is the definition and the other is an instruction. 'Alas' looks like the setter sighing, and it is really fodder.

6 across USER

Computer owner regularly getting bum steer (4)

Definition: Computer owner · alternate letters

  • bum steer → USER count the second letter of each pair, straight through the gap, and four come out in order

A bum steer is a duff piece of advice, and the whole surface exists to make that phrase sound natural. Only one word here is an instruction.

9 across HAMSTRINGS

Bad actors smothering series - but to pull them would be painful (10)

Definition: to pull them would be painful · container

  • Bad actors → HAMS a ham is the theatre's word for someone who overacts, and the plural is four letters
  • series → STRING a string of victories is a series of them, one after another

The surface is a box-set binge, which is the trap: 'series' here is not television but a run of things, and 'bad actors' is the theatrical insult rather than villains.

10 across TEAM

Eleven, maybe, in swarm that's heard (4)

Definition: Eleven, maybe · homophone

  • swarm → TEAM to swarm is to teem, to be present in overwhelming numbers

An eleven is a football or cricket side, so the definition names the answer by the number it fields. 'Maybe' is the flag that a specific case is standing in for the general word.

11 across GOOD CLEAN FUN

Canoodle, most of fungi spreading - hardly this! (4,5,3)

Definition: hardly this! · anagram + deletion

  • Canoodle → CANOODLE eight letters handed over entire
  • most of fungi → FUNG 'fungi' drops its last letter, leaving four to join the shuffle

The joke is the definition: canoodling is precisely what the answer is not, so the clue defines by pointing at the opposite of the word it has just anagrammed.

15 across HILLARY

Adventurer going to extremes with many heights surrounding? (7)

Definition: Adventurer going to extremes with many heights surrounding? · &lit + container + outer letters

  • many heights → HILLY a height is an old word for a hill, and ground with many of them is hilly
  • Adventurer going to extremes → AR the first and last letters of 'Adventurer', in that order

The whole clue works twice: the words that build the letters also describe the man, since the first to stand on Everest really did go to extremes with heights all round him.

16 across CHARGER

It'll bring your phone back to life - or is that bull? (7)

Definition: It'll bring your phone back to life · double definition

  • It'll bring your phone back to life → the thing you plug in overnight to put the battery back
  • bull → an animal that charges is one, and a bull is the beast famous for doing it

Two definitions with a joke in the join: the modern gadget and the animal both earn the same word, one because it charges a battery and the other because it charges at you.

17 across TOURISM

The writer visiting French city with Frenchman - doing this? (7)

Definition: doing this? · container + charade

  • French city → TOURS Tours stands on the Loire and is the French city crosswords reach for most
  • The writer → I the person writing the clue refers to himself as I
  • Frenchman → M M is short for Monsieur, the French title

'The writer' means the setter, and in a crossword the setter is I or ME. The definition then points back at the surface: what he is described as doing is the answer.

19 across AT HEART

Basically a menace when a little rascal scoots over (2,5)

Definition: Basically · anagram

  • a menace → ATHREAT a menace is a threat, and the clue's own 'a' comes along in front of it
  • rascal → the R that 'rascal' begins with is the letter told to move; the word brings no letters of its own

Unusually generous fodder: the setter not only shuffles a phrase but names the single letter that shifts, so the rebuild is one small slide rather than a free-for-all.

20 across BAD RECEPTION

It has insufficient bars - or no bar at all (3,9)

Definition: It has insufficient bars · double definition

  • It has insufficient bars → the bars are the little signal-strength blocks on a phone screen
  • no bar at all → a wedding or a party is also a reception, and the bar there is the one serving drinks

One word, two worlds. Neither half is a synonym: each describes the same failure, once in a phone signal and once at a party, and the pun lives in 'bar'.

23 across ERRS

Everyman's introduction, meeting bishop, first of several blunders (4)

Definition: blunders · charade + first letter

  • Everyman's introduction → E the Observer's setter signs himself Everyman, and only his opening letter is taken
  • bishop → RR RR is Right Reverend, the style by which a bishop is addressed
  • first of several → S the word 'several' gives up the letter it starts with

Setters often write themselves into their own clues, and Everyman does it here for a single letter. RR for bishop is worth keeping: it is Right Reverend, not the chess piece.

24 across STURDINESS

Stud rinses off showing lusty quality (10)

Definition: lusty quality · anagram

  • Stud rinses → STURDINESS ten letters between the two words, all of them used

The surface is a stable hand at the tap, and 'stud' plus 'rinses' happens to hold exactly the ten letters wanted, which is what makes the picture worth building.

25 across DADA

Duchamp's / Arp's derisive avant-gardism, primarily? (4)

Definition: Duchamp's / Arp's derisive avant-gardism, primarily? · &lit + first letters

  • Duchamp's / Arp's derisive avant-gardism → DADA four words in a row, each handing over its opening letter

The clue defines itself: Marcel Duchamp and Jean Arp were the movement's founders, and mocking the art establishment was the point of it, so the initials describe what they spell.

26 across CONDIMENTS

After split second, mint for mint sauce, etc (10)

Definition: mint sauce, etc · anagram

  • second, mint → CONDIMENTS ten letters across the two words, every one of them used

'Split second' reads as an instant of time, and the setter prints 'mint' twice on purpose: the first is fodder to be broken up, the second belongs to the definition.

Down

1 down ACHE

A revolutionary's a persistent pain (4)

Definition: a persistent pain · charade

  • A → A the clue's first word, taken at face value
  • revolutionary → CHE Che Guevara is the crossword's standing revolutionary, and his first name is the three letters

The apostrophe-s does the joining, so 'A revolutionary's' means A plus CHE is. Guevara turns up so often that the word alone is enough to summon him.

2 down PUMA

Quiz mostly a beast (4)

Definition: beast · charade + deletion

  • Quiz mostly → PUM to pump somebody is to quiz them for information; three of its four letters are wanted
  • a → A the clue's own word, used as the letter it is

'Quiz' looks like the setter addressing you, and it is a synonym instead: pumping someone for facts is quizzing them.

3 down RATIONALISE

How to make realisation? (11)

Definition: How to make realisation? · &lit + anagram

  • realisation → RATIONALISE eleven letters handed over entire and given back in a new order; nothing added, nothing dropped

Four words doing two jobs at once. Read as an instruction the clue shuffles a word; read as a description it says what the answer means, which is to reorganise something into a tidier arrangement.

4 down SPIN-DRY

Activate a kind of cycle and long to swap hands (4-3)

Definition: Activate a kind of cycle · substitution

  • long → SPINDLY a spindly stem is long and thin, so the adjective covers 'long'
  • to swap hands → R the left hand's L gives way to the right hand's R

The washing machine in the surface is genuine: this is the button you press. What the clue hides is that seven of its letters arrive whole, with a single one exchanged.

5 down ANGELIC

Orange licorice, somewhat divine (7)

Definition: divine · hidden word

  • Orange licorice → ANGELIC seven consecutive letters that pay no attention to the gap between the two words

A sweetshop shelf is all the surface is doing. Orange and licorice are two flavours nobody would put together, and an odd pairing of words is usually a hiding place.

7 down STEFFI GRAF

She regularly attended court having broken giraffe's foot (6,4)

Definition: She regularly attended court · anagram

  • giraffe's → GIRAFFES eight letters, with the apostrophe counting for nothing
  • foot → FT ft is the standard short form of foot, as in six ft tall

'Regularly' looks like an instruction to take every other letter, and it is part of the definition instead: the German won 22 grand slam singles titles, so she was on court constantly.

8 down REMUNERATE

Pay rock band in Paris, one wanting speed (10)

Definition: Pay · charade

  • rock band → REM the Athens, Georgia group behind Losing My Religion, three letters of initials
  • in Paris, one → UNE 'one' spoken in Paris is French, and the feminine form runs to three letters
  • speed → RATE the rate something moves at is its speed

'Rock band in Paris' reads as one phrase, a group playing a gig abroad. The comma is doing the real work: Paris belongs to the 'one' that follows it, not to the band.

12 down ANACHRONISM

Bad timing? (11)

Definition: Bad timing? · cryptic definition

  • Bad timing? → the reading the surface pushes: a remark made at the worst possible moment, or a comedian who fluffs the pause
  • timing → the reading the setter means: timing in the historical sense, a thing placed in the wrong century altogether

Two words, no separable wordplay, one pun on 'timing'. A wristwatch on a Roman centurion is bad timing in the setter's sense: the object is right, the era is wrong.

13 down WHITE BREAD

Chess player getting money - that might be a bloomer (5,5)

Definition: a bloomer · charade

  • Chess player → WHITE the two sides in chess are White and Black, and White moves first
  • money → BREAD slang for money since the 1930s, alongside dough

A bloomer is also a blunder, which is what 'that might be a bloomer' wants you to hear. Both halves of the wordplay are slang, one from the chessboard and one from the wallet.

14 down FLOUNDERED

Stumbled when given flatfish that's one-third cooked (10)

Definition: Stumbled · charade + last letters

  • flatfish → FLOUNDER the flounder lies flat on the seabed with both eyes on one side
  • one-third cooked → ED two of the six letters of 'cooked', counted from the end

'Cooked' is the trap: everywhere else in a cryptic it would be telling you to shuffle letters, and here it is a word to be measured out in thirds.

18 down MAESTRO

Top musician, cellist, smeared roset (7)

Definition: Top musician · charade + anagram

  • cellist → MA Yo-Yo Ma is the cellist crosswords mean, and his surname is two letters
  • roset → ESTRO five letters coming back in a different order

Roset is the rosin a player rubs on a bow, so it sits beside the cellist looking like part of the picture rather than like fodder waiting to be shuffled.

19 down ALTERED

Modified wonky treadle (7)

Definition: Modified · anagram

  • treadle → ALTERED seven letters given whole, every one of them used

A treadle is the foot pedal that drives a sewing machine or a lathe, so a wonky one sounds like a repair job in the workshop rather than an instruction.

21 down VEIN

Blood vessel's useless, you said? (4)

Definition: Blood vessel · homophone

  • useless → VEIN an effort that comes to nothing is made in vain, and the two spellings are said identically

The apostrophe-s reads as 'is', joining definition to wordplay. 'Useless' is the sound to be caught, and the question mark is the setter checking you heard him.

22 down ASKS

Requests jobs that will take no time (4)

Definition: Requests · deletion

  • jobs → TASKS jobs to be done are tasks
  • time → T T is the standard abbreviation for time, as in a physics equation

'That will take no time' sounds like a promise the work is quick, which is the whole disguise: it is an instruction to remove the letter that stands for time.
