Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,088

Across

1 across MADAME

Crazy French soul, this woman (6)

Definition: this woman · charade

  • Crazy → MAD mad is crazy
  • soul → AME ame is the French for soul, its accent dropped as crosswords always drop them

One word of French is all the clue asks you to know, and the comma is the join: everything before it builds, everything after it defines.

4 across ADOPTS

Takes over present-day stop-over (6)

Definition: Takes over · charade + anagram

  • present-day → AD AD dates the present era - anno Domini, as against BC
  • stop → OPTS the four letters of STOP, done over

The hyphen is the hiding place: 'stop-over' is one real word on the surface and two separate instructions underneath it.

9 across SNAG

Tin and silver catch (4)

Definition: catch · charade

  • Tin → SN SN is tin in the periodic table, from the Latin stannum
  • silver → AG AG is silver, from argentum

Two chemical symbols stacked, and both are worth banking: the ones that look nothing like the English word are the ones setters reach for.

10 across KHMER ROUGE

Ill-advised Greek ‘humor’ showing Asian guerrilla movement (5,5)

Definition: Asian guerrilla movement · anagram

  • Greek ‘humor’ → GREEKHUMOR anagram fodder, ten letters

Those quotation marks are load-bearing: the British HUMOUR would leave a U spare, so the setter is flagging that he means the American spelling.

11 across ARETES

Fixed time around rugged ridges (6)

Definition: rugged ridges · charade + reversal

  • Fixed → SET a jelly that has set is fixed
  • time → ERA an era is a stretch of time

'Around' looks like a container instruction and is not: nothing goes inside anything here, the two chunks are stacked and then spun.

12 across RENDERED

Got plastered? (8)

Definition: Got plastered? · cryptic definition

  • Got plastered? → The surface is the morning after: to get plastered is to get thoroughly drunk.
  • plastered → The setter means the wall. Brickwork given its coat of mortar has been plastered, and the building trade has its own word for that coat.

There is nothing to take apart - one phrase, two trades. The question mark is the setter admitting that the whole clue is the joke.

13 across ALMSHOUSE

Saul’s home rebuilt as one for the poor (9)

Definition: one for the poor · anagram

  • Saul’s home → SAULSHOME anagram fodder, nine letters

'One' points back at 'home' in the surface and at nothing in the parse: the fodder's own word is being borrowed to make the definition read naturally.

15 across DUET

Verse omitted from cover song (4)

Definition: song · deletion

  • cover → DUVET the cover on a bed is a duvet
  • Verse → V V is the standard shortening of verse, the one printed in a hymn book

'Cover song' is a real thing - a band playing somebody else's hit - and the setter needs the two words pulled apart and pointing opposite ways.

16 across ET AL

It’s seen amongst beta, lambda and others (2,2)

Definition: and others · hidden word

  • beta, lambda → ETAL the answer runs straight across the join: b-ETA, L-ambda

Two genuine Greek letters make a surface that means exactly what it says, which is the best cover a hidden word can hope for.

17 across SHUTTERED

With windows sealed, say, in hut (9)

Definition: With windows sealed · container

  • hut → SHED a hut is a shed
  • say → UTTER to utter a word is to say it

'Say' is a verb here, not the crossword's usual 'for example', and the commas around it are there to make you read it the other way.

21 across PEERLESS

Unrivalled, as the House of Lords in recess (8)

Definition: Unrivalled · charade

  • the House of Lords → PEER the upper chamber is full of peers, and the wordplay wants one of them
  • in recess → LESS in recess the chamber stands empty, so there are fewer of them there - less, not more

The parse and the joke are the same thing: send the Lords home and the House is short of peers. 'As' only holds the sentence together.

22 across UPKEEP

Cost of maintenance all the way to fortification (6)

Definition: Cost of maintenance · charade

  • all the way → UP up in the sense of right to the end - eat it up, use it up
  • fortification → KEEP the keep is the strongest tower of a castle

Both halves of the wordplay wear castle clothes and the answer is a bill rather than a building; the definition is the three words at the front.

24 across ZABAGLIONE

Dessert Azed rejects, served in a globe (10)

Definition: Dessert · deletion + anagram

  • Azed → AZ the Observer setter's name, minus the ED it ends with
  • in a globe → INAGLOBE eight more letters of fodder, taken as they stand

Azed sets the hardest cryptic in Britain, so borrowing his name and then discarding half of it is a setter's private joke at his expense.

25 across ZEAL

Enthusiasm at heart of Maoris’ home (4)

Definition: Enthusiasm · middle letters

  • Maoris’ home → ZEAL the Maoris' home is NEW ZEALAND, and these four letters are its exact centre

The country is never named, so you have to supply New Zealand yourself before there is anything to take the heart of.

26 across LIEDER

Songs by Schubert suggested by violinist (6)

Definition: Songs by Schubert · homophone

  • violinist → LIEDER the leader of an orchestra is its principal violinist, and LEADER is what the answer is heard as

The German plural is what makes it work: one Lied, many Lieder, and only the plural lands on the orchestra's leader.

27 across EUROPE

Old England, Peru – new continent (6)

Definition: continent · anagram

  • Old → O O is the standard shortening of old, as in Old English
  • England → E E is England on a scoreboard or a car plate
  • Peru → PERU four more letters of fodder, taken as they stand

'Old England' reads as one patriotic phrase and is two separate abbreviations; the dash is where the wordplay stops and the definition starts.

Down

1 down MINERAL

Ore obtained by worker underground, a trainee (7)

Definition: Ore · charade

  • worker underground → MINER the worker underground is a miner
  • a → A the clue's own a, taken as the letter
  • trainee → L a trainee driver carries L-plates, so L is the crossword's learner

'A trainee' looks like the tail of the surface sentence and is really two more chunks of the build, the last of them an L-plate.

2 down DIGIT

Green finger, perhaps, and what the gardener will do in bed (5)

Definition: Green finger, perhaps · charade

  • what the gardener will do → DIG a gardener's work in a flowerbed is to dig
  • in bed → IT the bed is the thing being dug, so it supplies the 'it' of 'dig it'

'Green finger' is the definition wearing gardening clothes, which is why the whole clue reads as one unbroken thought about allotments.

3 down MAKES DO

Arranges party – with what’s available (5,2)

Definition: with what’s available · charade

  • Arranges → MAKES to make an arrangement is to arrange it
  • party → DO a do is a party - an office do, a birthday do

The wordplay says 'makes a do' and the surface says 'arranges a party', which are the same sentence; the dash is where the meaning switches to coping.

5 down DARING

Fearless Bobby that’ll open the Guardian (6)

Definition: Fearless · charade + first letter

  • Bobby → DARIN Bobby Darin, who sang Mack the Knife
  • the Guardian → G the opening letter of the paper's name

The italics are the tell: a newspaper title is being named, and a title is exactly the sort of thing a setter wants one letter of.

6 down PROCEDURE

Action completely adopting inverted interior design (9)

Definition: Action · container + reversal

  • completely → PURE pure as an intensifier - pure nonsense, pure luck - means utter
  • interior design → ROCED interior design is DECOR, stood on its head

Both instructions are doing surface duty in a sentence about home makeovers, which is why neither reads as an instruction at all.

7 down SUGGEST

Recommend guests get changed around midnight (7)

Definition: Recommend · anagram + container + middle letter

  • guests → SUGEST the six letters of GUESTS, rearranged and parted to let the other letter in
  • midnight → G mid-night: the middle letter of NIGHT

'Midnight' explains itself once you split it, and the party surface never lets on that it is an instruction rather than an hour.

8 down X MARKS THE SPOT

Frantically text Posh about test result of where’s it at (1,5,3,4)

Definition: where’s it at · anagram + container

  • text Posh → XTHESPOT the eight letters of TEXT POSH, shaken up and parted to admit the other piece
  • test result → MARKS a test result comes back as marks

Posh Spice and a frantic text make a tabloid surface out of a treasure map. Thirteen letters is a long anagram, so the enumeration is half the help.

14 down STAIRCASE

Domestic flight (9)

Definition: Domestic flight · cryptic definition

  • Domestic flight → The surface is an airline timetable - a flight that stays inside one country instead of crossing a border.
  • flight → The builder's sense is the one wanted: an unbroken run of steps from one floor to the next is a flight, and 'domestic' puts it in a house.

Two words, no letters to move, and the whole clue turns on which industry owns 'flight'. The nine-letter enumeration is what rules out the aeroplane.

16 down EYEBALL

Closely examine as I weep noisily (7)

Definition: Closely examine · charade + homophone

  • I → EYE the letter I, said out loud, is the organ you look with
  • weep → BALL to bawl is to weep hard, and BAWL taken by ear is spelt the other way here

A homophone in two pieces: the letter written I, then a word for crying hard. Neither is spelt the way it is said.

18 down TRUDEAU

Letter about impolite PM (7)

Definition: PM · container

  • Letter → TAU tau is the Greek letter, the one that looks like a T
  • impolite → RUDE rude is impolite

'Letter' in a cryptic is very often a Greek one, and there are only two dozen of them - the short ones repay learning.

19 down ELEVATE

Most of team gathering at lift (7)

Definition: lift · deletion + container

  • team → ELEVEN a football side is an eleven, and it loses its last letter here
  • at → AT the little word goes in letter for letter

'Lift' is the definition, not the lift everyone is gathering by, and that swap is the whole of the misdirection in a six-word clue.

20 down WELLIE

Great effort making boot (6)

Definition: Great effort · double definition

  • Great effort → WELLIE give it some wellie - put your back into it
  • boot → WELLIE a wellie is a wellington, the rubber one

One word, two lives, and the second explains the first: putting your foot into something hard is where the effort sense comes from.

23 down KAZOO

Tibetan priest leaves US city for instrument (5)

Definition: instrument · deletion

  • US city → KALAMAZOO Kalamazoo, Michigan, of the Glenn Miller song
  • Tibetan priest → LAMA the lama is the Tibetan priest, lodged in the middle of the city

Kalamazoo is the whole clue: a real Michigan city that happens to have a Tibetan priest sitting inside it.
