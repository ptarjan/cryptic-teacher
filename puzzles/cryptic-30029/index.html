Across

1 across WHAT FOR

Why it’s hard to open where a gap is unclosed (4,3)

Definition: Why · container + deletion

Why → WHAT FOR: 'what for' is the spoken form of why — what did you do that for? — asking the purpose behind an action.

  • where a gap is → WATFORD the Watford Gap is the low pass in Northamptonshire that road and rail have always taken between southern England and the Midlands
  • unclosed → D the D on the end of that place name is the letter it loses
  • hard → H H is the pencil grade for hard, and it slides in behind the first letter
  • to open — Whatever opens makes a space in the middle of itself, and that space is where the loose letter is dropped.
  • unclosed — Something unclosed has lost the piece that shuts it, so the word gives up the letter on its end.

A jammed door: why will it not open when a gap has been left unclosed?

'Where a gap is' reads as part of the stuck door and names a place on the M1 instead; 'unclosed' is an instruction to lose a letter, not a description of the gap.

5 across GARAGED

Kid pushed over, veteran shut up (7)

Definition: shut up · reversal + charade

shut up → GARAGED: a car that has been garaged is shut up out of the weather, and the definition matches the answer's past tense.

  • Kid → GAR to kid somebody is to rag them, and those three letters are then turned round
  • veteran → AGED a veteran is aged, long in service
  • pushed over — Something pushed over topples and comes to rest the other way round, so those letters read backwards.

A child is knocked over and an old soldier tells him to be quiet.

'Shut up' looks like an order for silence and means a car put away under cover for the night.

9 across ORGAN

For example, brain that’s stopped (5)

Definition: For example, brain · double definition

For example, brain → ORGAN: the brain is one organ among the body's many, and the second sense is the instrument whose pipes are opened by stops.

  • For example, brain → first definition: the brain is named as one instance of the wider word
  • that’s stopped → second definition: an instrument fitted with stops, which the surface reads as one that has ceased

Somebody's brain has packed up altogether.

A pipe organ's notes are chosen by pulling out its stops, so being 'stopped' here has nothing to do with stopping.

10 across BREAK EVEN

Neither profit nor gain from holiday flat (5,4)

Definition: Neither profit nor gain · charade

Neither profit nor gain → BREAK EVEN: to break even is to come out exactly level, taking back what you laid out and nothing more.

  • holiday → BREAK a break is a holiday, a week away
  • flat → EVEN a flat surface is an even one

A holiday flat that makes its owner no money at all.

'Holiday flat' is one thing in the surface — a rental by the sea — and two unrelated words in the wordplay.

11 across PRESENT-DAY

18 needs to be anything but neanderthal (7-3)

Definition: anything but neanderthal · anagram

anything but neanderthal → PRESENT-DAY: neanderthal stands for the hopelessly primitive, so its opposite is whatever belongs to now.

  • 18 → PARTY the second word of the answer at 18 across, handed over as five letters
  • needs → NEEDS the clue's own word joins the shuffle with all five of its letters
  • 18 — The answer at 18 across is an order as well as a noun — reform the party — so its first word is the instruction to remake the letters.

A political party being told to drag itself into the modern age.

The number does two jobs: 18's second word is the fodder and its first word is the shuffling instruction, which is why nothing else here looks like a signal.

12 across BAKU

Defended by Cuba, Kuwaiti capital (4)

Definition: capital · hidden word

capital → BAKU: Baku is the capital of Azerbaijan, so the bare word defines it as one particular city.

  • Cuba, Kuwaiti → BAKU four consecutive letters bridge the comma between those two words
  • Defended by — Whatever is defended is held safe inside its defenders, which is where these letters are sitting.

Two oil states holding a line together, one of them named for its capital.

'Kuwaiti capital' wants to be the definition and sends you to Kuwait City; only 'capital' is the definition, and the country is fodder. The capital wanted is Azerbaijan's, on the Caspian.

14 across MADEIRA CAKE

Sponge back in shower with a bar of soap, having created one earlier (7,4)

Definition: Sponge · charade + last letter

Sponge → MADEIRA CAKE: madeira cake is a plain lemony sponge loaf, so the general word for the crumb names one particular cake.

  • created → MADE created and made are the same past tense
  • one → I I is one in Roman numerals
  • shower → R the letter at the back of SHOWER, taken on its own
  • a → A the clue's own 'a' comes through as itself
  • bar of soap → CAKE soap is sold in cakes, which is what a bar of it is
  • back in — The back of a word is the letter it ends on, and that single letter is all that is taken.
  • earlier — 'Earlier' sends these pieces to the front, ahead of everything the clue has already handed over.

Someone in the shower with a bar of soap, and a sponge they prepared earlier.

'Having created one earlier' is the British children's-television catchphrase — here's one I made earlier — put to work as an instruction about where the pieces go.

18 across REFORM PARTY

Concerned with supporting Leave (my, that’s extreme) – and now this! (6,5)

Definition: and now this! · charade + container

and now this! → REFORM PARTY: the bulletin's handover to the next item, aimed at the party then supplying them, Nigel Farage's Leave-born Reform UK.

  • Concerned with → RE RE is the 'concerning' at the head of a memo
  • supporting → FOR to be for something is to support it
  • my → M the front letter of MY, pushed out to the edge
  • Leave → PART to part is to leave, and the capital L belongs to the Brexit campaign of the surface
  • my → Y the back letter of MY, closing the sandwich
  • that’s extreme — Calling the two letters extreme drives them to the outside, leaving the gap between them for another word.

A newsreader introducing yet another story about hardline Brexit campaigners.

The words that spell the answer also describe it — an outfit for Leave, and extreme with it — which leaves only the exclamation at the end to point you anywhere.

21 across SOUP

Ergo ready for liquid lunch? (4)

Definition: liquid lunch? · charade

liquid lunch? → SOUP: soup is the lunch you drink from a bowl, so the phrase is literal rather than the drinker's joke it usually is.

  • Ergo → SO ergo is Latin for therefore, and so is the English
  • ready → UP when the meal is up it is ready and on the table

Somebody deciding that lunch today will be taken in liquid form.

A liquid lunch normally means drinking through the middle of the day; this one is only the first course.

22 across WATER NYMPH

Flier with orbital course, a measure of speed and spirit (5,5)

Definition: spirit · container + charade

spirit → WATER NYMPH: in Greek myth a water nymph is the spirit that lives in a stream or spring, so 'spirit' names her class.

  • course → WAY a way is a course or route, and it opens up to take a passenger
  • Flier → TERN the tern is a seabird, the crossword's usual flier
  • a measure of speed → MPH miles per hour, the measure on every British road sign
  • orbital — Something orbital goes all the way round, so this word is bent about the letters it meets.

A bird circling overhead, clocked for speed and admired for its spirit.

'Flier' is a bird rather than an aviator or a leaflet, and 'orbital course' is two jobs in two words: a word meaning route, and the instruction bending it round.

25 across ON THE WING

Flying weight non-negotiable? (2,3,4)

Definition: Flying · anagram

Flying → ON THE WING: a bird on the wing is one in flight, the phrase standing in for 'flying' wherever wings are involved.

  • weight → WEIGHT six letters straight from the clue
  • non → NON the front half of the hyphenated word joins the fodder, leaving the back half to do the shuffling
  • negotiable — Whatever is negotiable can be altered by agreement, so these letters will not stand as printed.

An airline's baggage limit that nobody is going to argue their way past.

The hyphen is the trick: 'non-negotiable' is not one word but three letters of fodder followed by their own anagram signal.

26 across RHONE

European river runs where white castle initially located? (5)

Definition: European river · charade

European river → RHONE: the Rhône runs from the Swiss Alps through Lyon to the Mediterranean, so the plain phrase names one particular river.

  • runs → R R is runs on a cricket scorecard
  • where white castle initially located → HONE chess names every square by a letter and a number, and White's castle — the rook — begins the game on h1

A river running past the spot where a white castle first stood.

'Initially' looks like an instruction to take a first letter and means at the start of the game instead; the square's name is then written out, letter and number.

27 across FARADAY

Scientist’s spirit broken by school of players (7)

Definition: Scientist · container

Scientist → FARADAY: Michael Faraday, who discovered electromagnetic induction, is one particular scientist, so the word defines him by example.

  • spirit → FAY a fay is a fairy, the spirit of the old ballads
  • school of players → RADA RADA is the Royal Academy of Dramatic Art, the London school where British actors train
  • broken by — What is broken has been forced open, and whatever forced it ends up sitting in the break.

A scientist's morale broken by a troupe of actors.

'Players' are actors rather than sportsmen, which is what makes the school a drama school, and the definition names one man by his trade.

28 across THERAPY

18 and he gets counselling (7)

Definition: counselling · anagram

counselling → THERAPY: the talking treatment somebody is referred for, the general word covering the course rather than a single session.

  • 18 → PARTY the second word of the answer at 18 across, lending five letters
  • he → HE two more letters taken from the clue as they stand
  • 18 — The answer at 18 across doubles as an order — reform the party — and that order is what is carried out on these letters.

A man and his party both ending up in the counselling room.

18 supplies the letters and the order to disturb them at the same time, and 'he' quietly adds two more of its own.

Down

1 down WHOOPS

Doctor oversees surgical procedures that went a bit wrong (6)

Definition: that went a bit wrong · charade

that went a bit wrong → WHOOPS: the noise you make on dropping something, a spoken flinch at a small mishap rather than a word for the mishap.

  • Doctor → WHO the BBC's time-travelling Doctor answers to Who, the crossword's standard doctor
  • surgical procedures → OPS operations are ops on any hospital list
  • oversees — To oversee is to sit above, and in a down clue whatever sits above is entered first.

A consultant keeps an eye on the theatre list while the operations fail to go to plan.

'Oversees' is doing positional work while looking like supervision, and the doctor is a television character rather than a medic.

2 down ANGLER

Riverside waiter, earring perhaps, with shaved head? (6)

Definition: Riverside waiter · deletion

Riverside waiter → ANGLER: an angler stands at the water's edge doing nothing but waiting for a fish, so 'waiter' is literal rather than a job.

  • earring perhaps → DANGLER an earring is one thing that dangles, so a dangler
  • with shaved head → D the head of that seven-letter word is its first letter, and the shaving takes it off
  • perhaps — 'Perhaps' marks an example: the thing named is one particular case of the wider word wanted.
  • with shaved head — A shaved head loses what is on top, and the top of a word is the letter it starts with.

A waiter working by the river, wearing an earring, with his head shaved.

'Waiter' is not restaurant staff but somebody who waits, and what he is waiting for is a bite.

3 down FINGERMARK

Smear bit of fish with virus onto vessel (10)

Definition: Smear · charade

Smear → FINGERMARK: a smear is any greasy mark left on a surface, and the one a finger leaves on glass is exactly that.

  • bit of fish → FIN a fin is one part of a fish
  • virus → GERM germ is the everyday word for a virus or microbe
  • vessel → ARK Noah's ark is the crossword's stock vessel

Somebody wiping fish and germs onto the side of a boat.

'Smear' is a noun here, not the verb the surface wants, and three ordinary words simply stack up left to right.

5 down GRENADIER

Guard on visits reading novel (9)

Definition: Guard · anagram + container

Guard → GRENADIER: a grenadier is a guardsman of that regiment, so the plain word names him as one particular kind.

  • reading → GNADIER the seven letters rearranged, then opened up after the first to take the visitor
  • on → RE 'on' in the sense of about or concerning is RE, as at the head of a memo
  • visits — A visitor goes inside the house rather than standing beside it, so this piece lands within the letters it meets.
  • novel — Novel means newly made, and letters made new cannot be left in the order the clue printed them.

A guard on duty drops in on somebody who is reading a new book.

'Reading' is fodder rather than a pastime, and the Grenadier Guards are the senior regiment of the Household Division — the bearskins outside Buckingham Palace.

6 down RAKE

Romeo in drag? (4)

Definition: Romeo · double definition

Romeo → RAKE and drag → RAKE: a rake is a dissolute womaniser, and also the implement you drag over soil or fallen leaves.

  • Romeo → first definition: a man with a reputation, Shakespeare's lover standing in for the type
  • drag → second definition: the garden implement, named for the thing you do with it

Romeo turning up at the party in women's clothes.

'In drag' paints a fancy-dress picture, and the word wanted is the toothed tool you haul across soil — the same word as the man of loose morals.

8 down DENTURES

A victory proving elusive in daring feats – trap set? (8)

Definition: trap set · deletion

trap set → DENTURES: dentures are a full set of false teeth, and the trap is the mouth they are fitted into.

  • daring feats → ADVENTURES daring feats are adventures
  • A → A the clue's opening A is the first letter to disappear from it
  • victory → V V is victory, as in Churchill's two-finger sign
  • proving elusive in — Whatever proves elusive is not there to be found, so those letters go missing from the word that follows.

A daredevil's stunts keep failing to bring him a win, and something has been laid for him.

'Trap' is British slang for the mouth — shut your trap — so the set being clued is a set of teeth rather than a snare.

13 down BANYAN TREE

Nearby supply cut by carpenter, say, European and Asian wood (6,4)

Definition: Asian wood · anagram + container + charade

Asian wood → BANYAN TREE: the banyan is the huge aerial-rooted fig of India and south-east Asia, and 'wood' here is growing timber.

  • Nearby → BANYRE the six letters rearranged, then split four and two to make room
  • carpenter, say → ANT the carpenter ant is one kind of ant, and 'say' marks it as an example
  • European → E E is the standard abbreviation for European
  • supply — Supply is the adverb of supple here — done with a bend in it — so the letters beside it will not hold their shape.
  • cut by — Something cut by a blade is opened at the point the blade goes in, and the next piece goes into that gap.

A carpenter cutting up a local supply of European and Asian timber.

'Supply' is not the noun the surface wants but the adverb of supple, one of the sneakiest anagram signals going, and 'carpenter' is naming an insect.

15 down DEPRAVITY

Absence of moral rectitude with which 18 vied (9)

Definition: Absence of moral rectitude · anagram

Absence of moral rectitude → DEPRAVITY: depravity is settled moral corruption rather than a lapse, the state of having no rectitude left.

  • 18 → PARTY the second word of the answer at 18 across, lending its five letters
  • vied → VIED four more letters straight from the clue
  • 18 — Read as an instruction, the answer at 18 across tells you to reform a party, and reforming is what happens to these letters.

A political party in open competition with moral squalor.

'Vied' looks like the verb holding the sentence together and is really four more letters for the pot.

16 down BRUSH-OFF

Slight boo-boo at first, skedaddle (5-3)

Definition: Slight · charade + first letter

Slight → BRUSH-OFF: a brush-off is a curt dismissal, which is what a slight is when the word is used as a noun for a snub.

  • boo-boo at first → B the first letter of BOO-BOO and nothing more
  • skedaddle → RUSHOFF to skedaddle is to rush off in a hurry
  • at first — 'At first' keeps only the opening letter of the word beside it and throws the rest away.

Someone makes a small blunder and immediately bolts.

'Slight' is the noun — a snub — rather than the adjective the surface wants, and 'boo-boo' is in the clue for its B alone.

17 down AFLUTTER

Nervous say after a fall, stuffing knocked out (8)

Definition: Nervous · charade + deletion

Nervous → AFLUTTER: to be aflutter is to be in a state of nervous agitation, the heart going like a bird's wings.

  • a → A the clue's own 'a' is taken as it stands
  • fall → FL FALL loses the two letters in its middle and keeps its outer pair
  • say → UTTER to say something is to utter it
  • after — What comes after something is entered behind it, so this piece belongs at the end rather than the front.
  • stuffing knocked out — The stuffing of a word is the letters in its middle, and knocking them out leaves only the outside.

Somebody left jittery by a tumble that knocked the stuffing out of them.

'Stuffing knocked out' reads as what a hard fall does to a person, and it is an instruction to gut a word.

19 down I'M NOT A ROBOT

It really is me in motorboat at sea! (2,3,1,5)

Definition: It really is me · anagram

It really is me → I'M NOT A ROBOT: the CAPTCHA box asks you to declare your humanity, which is the claim 'it really is me' makes.

  • in motorboat → INMOTORBOAT eleven letters taken from the clue exactly as they stand, ready to be rearranged
  • at sea — Anything at sea is adrift with no fixed position, and neither have the letters beside it.

Somebody shouting from a motorboat that it really is them out there.

You are hunting for a person and the answer is a sentence — the wording beside the tickbox every website makes you confirm before it lets you in. It runs on into 4 down.

20 down CHEEKY

Forward – as one’s behind? (6)

Definition: Forward · double definition

Forward → CHEEKY and one's behind → CHEEKY: forward means impudent, and a backside is cheeky in the plainest anatomical sense.

  • Forward → first definition: forward in the sense of pushy, not of facing the front
  • one’s behind → second definition: the part of the body made of two cheeks

A pushy person, with the suggestion that the pushiest thing about them is at the back.

The bottom is the one part of us made of cheeks, so a word for impudence doubles as a description of it. The dash makes both halves read as a single grumble about somebody.

23 down EIGHT

Cube, elevation skimmed (5)

Definition: Cube · deletion

Cube → EIGHT: eight is a cube number, two cubed, so 'cube' names it the way 'square' names nine.

  • elevation → HEIGHT elevation is height, how far up something stands
  • skimmed → H the letter sitting on top of that word is the one taken off
  • skimmed — To skim is to take off whatever is riding on top, and in a down entry the top is the first letter.

A solid cube with a slice taken off the top of it.

'Cube' is the mathematician's noun, not the shape the surface draws: two multiplied by itself three times.

24 down DEAD GIVEAWAY

Easy clue: ‘Funeral director’s special offer?’ (4,8)

Definition: Easy clue · charade

Easy clue → DEAD GIVEAWAY: a dead giveaway hands over what it was meant to conceal, which is what a clue this easy does.

  • Funeral director’s → DEAD an undertaker's whole business is with the dead
  • special offer → GIVEAWAY a giveaway is a promotion that costs the customer nothing

An undertaker running a promotion, which is a queasy thought.

The quoted phrase is offered as a little clue inside the big one, and it solves to a phrase meaning something that betrays a secret at once. It runs on into 7 down.
