Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,052

Across

8 across DELICATE

Fine food store providing meals 50% off (8)

Definition: Fine · charade + deletion

  • food store → DELI a delicatessen, shortened the way every high street shortens it
  • providing meals → CATERING providing meals for an event is catering — eight letters, and only the front four survive
  • 50% off → the instruction, dressed as a discount

Fine, food and store read as one shop-window phrase, and only the first word of it is the definition. That join is the clue's real trick.

9 across PUNCH

Wordplay by church magazine in the past (5)

Definition: magazine in the past · charade

  • Wordplay → PUN a pun is wordplay at its plainest
  • church → CH CH is the standard crossword abbreviation for church

The surface is a joke told in a pew, and it gives no hint that the answer is a magazine — or, for that matter, a blow.

10 across FREE

Release knot after cycling (4)

Definition: Release · cycling

  • knot → REEF the reef knot, the everyday square knot in a scarf or a bandage

Cycling looks like an anagram until you notice the letters keep their order: only the starting point moves, one place round. The surface cyclist is decoration.

11 across DETENTIONS

50 leave prayers over 10 school punishments? (10)

Definition: school punishments · substitution

  • prayers → DEVOTIONS devotions are prayers, the ones said morning and evening
  • 50 → VO V is the Roman five and O a nought, so this pair reads as the figure — and it is sitting in the middle of that word
  • 10 → TEN the number spelled out in letters

Qaos writes a figure by setting a Roman numeral beside a nought, and pulls the same trick at 3 down. Watch out for the 10, too: six clues in this grid use a number, and only some of them are cross-references.

12 across BARREN

Lord caught being unproductive (6)

Definition: unproductive · homophone

  • Lord → BARREN the lowest rank in the peerage is a baron, and said out loud it comes out spelled the other way

There is cricket all through this puzzle, so 'caught' does a lot of work: it sounds like a dismissal and means an overheard word. BARON heard becomes BARREN.

14 across EMBODIED

Half of them plot to protect cricket match represented (8)

Definition: represented · charade + container + last letters

  • Half of them → EM the back two letters of thEM
  • plot → BED a plot of ground is a bed, as in a flower bed — and it opens after its first letter
  • cricket match → ODI an ODI is a One Day International, the fifty-over form of the game

ODI is worth banking for future grids. 'Plot' reads as conspiracy in a clue full of scheming and turns out to be gardening, which is the whole misdirection.

16 across SHUFFLE

Prepare to deal in Harlem, maybe (7)

Definition: Prepare to deal · double definition

  • Prepare to deal → SHUFFLE what a dealer does to the pack before the cards go out
  • in Harlem, maybe → SHUFFLE the dance named after that New York district, and the 1963 hit song about it

Two senses, no letters moved: the card table on one side, Bob and Earl's Harlem Shuffle on the other. 'Maybe' is the flag that an example is standing in for a definition.

18 across EXPRESS

Ruler deports 1,000 to win vote, say (7)

Definition: say · substitution

  • Ruler → EMPRESS an empress rules an empire in her own right
  • 1,000 → M M is the Roman thousand — the letter being sent away
  • vote → X a ballot paper is marked with a cross, and the cross is written X

M for a thousand and X for a vote are both worth banking. 'Ruler' invites you to picture the thing on a desk, and the comma before 'say' is the only signpost to the definition.

21 across OFF AND ON

Occasional cricket sides (3,3,2)

Definition: Occasional · double definition

  • Occasional → OFF AND ON happening now and then, with gaps in between
  • cricket sides → OFF AND ON the field is divided either side of the batter's stance, and each half carries its own name

Two words, both defining. On the surface it sounds like a village team that only turns out sometimes; underneath, one word is frequency and the other is a fielder's map.

23 across PADDLE

Wearing white, England finally get 500 with sweep (6)

Definition: sweep · container + last letter

  • white → PALE pale is white of complexion or colour, and it opens after its second letter
  • England finally → D the letter EnglanD finishes on
  • 500 → D D is the Roman five hundred

Whites are what cricketers wear, so 'Wearing white' reads as kit and works as an instruction. The two Ds arrive from opposite worlds: a country's last letter and a Roman numeral.

24 across TENNIS BALL

One in court sent in criminal to get shot (6,4)

Definition: One in court · anagram + charade

  • sent in → TENNIS the six letters of SENT IN, rearranged
  • shot → BALL shot is what a gun fires — lead balls, from cannon shot down to birdshot

The surface is a police story, and every word of it is doing another job: 'sent in' is fodder rather than a verb, and 'shot' comes from gunnery rather than sport.

26 across WING

Wide initially given following batting side (4)

Definition: side · charade + first letters

  • Wide → W the initial of Wide
  • batting → IN the batting team in cricket is the team that is in
  • given following → G the initial letter of the first of these two words, and the second says where it goes: behind what has just been built

Cricket runs right through the surface: a wide is given by the umpire, and the batting side is 'in'. The definition is the single word doing no cricket at all.

27 across DANCE

Perhaps Hamlet seizes Claudius’s crown in play (5)

Definition: play · container + first letter

  • Perhaps Hamlet → DANE Hamlet is the Prince of Denmark, so he is one example of a Dane — 'perhaps' is the flag that says by example
  • Claudius’s → C the letter Claudius begins with

Claudius really does seize the crown in the play, so the surface reads as plot summary. The crown the wordplay wants is one letter of a usurper's name.

28 across DRUM ROLL

‘Beats drinking alcohol’ – aristocrat on reflection with slice of lemon (4,4)

Definition: Beats · container + reversal + first letter

  • aristocrat → DROL an aristocrat is a lord, and this is that word mirrored
  • lemon → L the letter the fruit starts with
  • alcohol → RUM rum is the sailor's spirit, and it is what gets swallowed here

The quotation marks sell 'Beats drinking alcohol' as a temperance slogan, which is exactly the wrong reading — the alcohol has to be swallowed by the rest of the wordplay.

Down

1 down REPROACH

Scold or preach conversion (8)

Definition: Scold · anagram

  • or preach → ORPREACH anagram fodder, eight letters
  • conversion → anagram indicator

The surface offers 'or' as a choice between two ways of telling someone off. It is really the first two letters of the fodder — a joining word that turns out to be raw material.

2 down TIME

Magazine’s obligation to describe source of material (4)

Definition: Magazine · container + first letter

  • obligation → TIE a tie is a binding obligation — family ties, ties of duty
  • material → M the initial letter of Material

The surface is journalistic ethics — a reporter's duty to name a source — which is why nothing in it sounds like a set of letters being wrapped around one more.

3 down PARDON

What even number is divided by 5000? (6)

Definition: What · charade + container

  • even → PAR par is the golfer's level score — being on par is being even
  • 5000 → DO D is the Roman five hundred, O is a nought, so writing them together writes the figure out
  • number → N n is the mathematician's letter for any number you like

Qaos writes numbers with letters twice in this grid — here and at 11 across — by putting a Roman numeral in front of a nought. Read the digits, not the arithmetic.

4 down FEATHER

Iron Lady’s bored by acting Tory leader’s ‘soft touch’ (7)

Definition: soft touch · container + first letters

  • Iron → FE iron's symbol on the periodic table, from Latin ferrum
  • acting Tory → AT the letters at the head of each of these two words
  • Lady’s → HER what belongs to a lady is hers

The Iron Lady is Margaret Thatcher on the surface, and the wordplay splits her in half: iron is the chemical symbol, a lady's is simply the possessive pronoun.

5 down SPIN

Country rejects American PR (4)

Definition: PR · deletion

  • Country → SPAIN the country on the Iberian peninsula
  • American → A A is the standard abbreviation for American — this is what gets thrown out

The surface is a diplomatic snub. The definition is two capital letters at the end of the clue, which is exactly where a solver's eye slides past them.

6 down UNHINDERED

10 hide under end of divan, trembling (10)

Definition: 10 · anagram + last letter

  • hide under → HIDEUNDER anagram fodder, nine letters
  • divan → N the letter divaN stops on
  • trembling → anagram indicator

The definition is the number 10, pointing at the answer to 10 across. Cross-references look like wordplay and are not: go and solve the other clue first.

7 down CHANGE

Transfer money (6)

Definition: Transfer · double definition

  • Transfer → CHANGE moving from one train, account or job to another
  • money → CHANGE the loose coins in a pocket, the small stuff a till hands back

A two-word clue is nearly always two definitions: with no room for an indicator there is nowhere for wordplay to hide, so each word has to define on its own.

13 down REFRAINING

Stopping official education ignoring tense (10)

Definition: Stopping · charade + deletion

  • official → REF the referee, the official who runs a match
  • education → TRAINING training is education of the practical sort
  • tense → T T is the grammarian's abbreviation for tense — this is the letter left out

Cricket has umpires, football has the REF, and crosswords use REF for any official at all. The surface, a school losing a lesson, has nothing to do with grammar.

15 down BOX

Beauty product to go in container (3)

Definition: container · deletion

  • Beauty product → BOTOX the wrinkle-smoothing injection sold as a beauty treatment
  • to → TO these are the two letters that leave, and the clue names them by using the word

The instruction is hiding in plain sight: BOTOX loses the very word the clue used to tell it to go, and BOX is what is left standing.

17 down LED

Guided light (3)

Definition: Guided · double definition

  • Guided → LED the past tense of lead: showed the way, went in front
  • light → LED the light-emitting diode now in every torch, bulb and standby indicator

A three-letter clue with no indicator is nearly always two definitions. The trap is reading 'light' as an adjective when it is a noun, and an acronym pretending to be a word.

19 down SILENTLY

Foolish to steal ten pounds without a sound (8)

Definition: without a sound · container + anagram

  • Foolish → SILLY foolish behaviour is silly, and this word is about to be opened up after its third letter
  • ten → ENT the three letters of TEN, hammered into a new order

'Ten pounds' looks like money and is really two instructions in a row: fodder, then the hammer. The definition is the only part of the clue not about crime.

20 down EN GARDE

Take defensive position as grenade’s thrown (2,5)

Definition: Take defensive position · anagram

  • grenade → GRENADE anagram fodder, seven letters
  • thrown → anagram indicator

The surface is a soldier diving for cover and the answer is a fencer coming on guard. The enumeration (2,5) is the first hint that one word will not do.

22 down FIENDS

Demons? Companions, run away! (6)

Definition: Demons · deletion

  • Companions → FRIENDS the companions you choose are your friends
  • run → R R is cricket's abbreviation for a run — this is the letter that goes

The exclamation mark sells 'run away!' as panic, and it is really an instruction to a single letter. Companions and demons are about as far apart as a surface can stretch.

23 down PULL-UP

Stop going both ways? (4-2)

Definition: Stop · palindrome

  • Stop → the definition end of the clue: what a driver does at the kerb
  • going both ways → the wordplay end: six letters that read the same backwards, which is all the help the clue gives

PULL-UP reads the same in either direction, and that symmetry is the entire wordplay — the question mark is the setter admitting there is nothing else to find.

25 down STEP

Society backing favourite way to go up in the world (4)

Definition: way to go up in the world · charade + reversal

  • Society → S S is the standard abbreviation for society
  • favourite → TEP a favourite is a pet, and this is that word written the other way round

A pet is a favourite, and reversed it stops looking like a word at all, which is what makes the join hard to see. The surface is all social climbing.

26 down WORM

Creature of indeterminate gender? (4)

Definition: Creature · charade

  • gender → W W is the label for women, on a form or a changing-room door
  • indeterminate → OR when it is not settled which of two, the two are joined by this little word
  • gender? → M M is the matching label for men, so one word supplies both letters

Read the answer as three chunks and it says 'W or M', the two boxes on a form. Worms really are hermaphrodite, so the surface is true as well as funny.
