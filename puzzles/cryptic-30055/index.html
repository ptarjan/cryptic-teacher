Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,055

Across

1 across COFFEE

Drug supplier cut short proposal in church (6)

Definition: Drug supplier · container + deletion

  • church → CE the Church of England shortens to CE, and the two letters part company to let something sit between them
  • proposal → OFFE a proposal is an offer, docked of its final letter

The surface is a dealer collared in a pew, which is why the real definition arrives so quietly: the drug in question is caffeine.

4 across SCREAM

Comic character in yellow? (6)

Definition: Comic · charade

  • character → S 'character' asks for a single letter of type, and which one is left to the crossing letters
  • yellow? → CREAM cream is a pale yellow, and the question mark is the setter owning up to a shade standing in for the colour

Definition and wordplay both live in informal English: a hilarious person is 'a comic', and the same person is 'a scream'.

9 across CAKE

Greek actress partly redirected Hollywood production? (4)

Definition: Hollywood production? · hidden word + reversal

  • Greek actress → CAKE four letters in a row straddle the gap between the two words, and they are read from right to left

This is the clue the rest of the grid leans on, so it pays to spot the trick early: the question mark is doing a lot of work for one small joke.

10 across SUPERFOODS

Poor US Feds demolished Fruit & Nuts? (10)

Definition: Fruit & Nuts? · anagram

  • Poor US Feds → POORUSFEDS the ten letters of the three words are the whole of the material

'Demolished' works both ways round: the agents are wrecked on the surface and the plate of food is demolished at the table, while the letters quietly change places.

11 across RETYPE

Peter Kay at last playing key characters again (6)

Definition: key characters again · anagram + last letter

  • Peter → PETER the five letters of the first name are the bulk of the fodder
  • Kay at last → Y the last letter of the surname makes up the six

Peter Kay is a comedian and 'playing' is what comedians do, so the whole first half reads as showbusiness rather than as raw letters.

12 across LOOKED IN

Briefly visited butcher’s next to Scottish city (6,2)

Definition: Briefly visited · charade

  • butcher’s → LOOK Cockney rhyming slang, and the rhyming half is never spoken: a butcher's hook
  • Scottish city → EDIN Edinburgh in its dictionary short form

Both halves are abbreviations of a kind — one by slang, one by the dictionary — and neither is signalled, which is what makes an eight-letter answer feel like a guess.

13 across SULTANATE

American making a comeback with bronze in former territory (9)

Definition: territory · charade + reversal + container

  • American → SU the US, turned round
  • former → LATE 'late' is the word for former — the late chairman — and it opens after one letter
  • bronze → TAN a tan is the bronze a fortnight in the sun leaves you with

Three separate liberties with plain words — a country as two letters, a colour as a verb's worth of skin, and 'former' as an adjective for the dead — all inside one smooth sentence.

15 across BABA

9 sailors from the East (4)

Definition: 9 · charade + reversal

  • sailors → ABAB an AB is an able seaman, and the plural asks for a pair of them

'From the East' looks like scenery about foreign ports and is really a compass direction telling you which way along the row to read.

16 across COAT

9 having afternoon in bed (4)

Definition: 9 · container

  • bed → COT a cot is a bed, and it opens after two letters to take the insertion
  • afternoon → A 'afternoon' does loose work here, standing for a bare A — timetable shorthand rather than a dictionary abbreviation

The cross-reference is a verb here, not a noun: cake is what dried mud does to a boot, and that is the sense the definition wants.

17 across ENERGETIC

Great-niece unfortunately lacking the focus of Blake Lively (9)

Definition: Lively · anagram + deletion + middle letter

  • Great-niece → GRETNIECE the fodder, once the letter named next has gone
  • Blake → the centre of the five-letter name is A, and that is the letter left out

Blake Lively is one name doing two jobs: the surname defines the answer while the first name gives up its middle letter to the shuffle.

21 across SWEET PEA

Rock maybe cut mountain climber (5,3)

Definition: climber · charade + deletion

  • Rock → SWEET seaside rock is a stick of boiled sugar — one kind of confection, not a stone
  • mountain → PEA a mountain is a peak, docked of its last letter

Two plain words both need re-reading: the rock is edible and the climber is rooted. Nothing in the surface is what it first appears to be.

22 across FISHER

Rod Hull’s beginning to get into battered fries (6)

Definition: Rod · anagram + first letter

  • Hull’s beginning → H the first letter of the surname
  • fries → FRIES the five letters of the chips, which reorder around the letter joining them

Rod Hull and his emu carry the surface, and only the H of Hull is needed. 'Battered fries' is a chip-shop picture doing an anagram's job.

24 across BATTENBERG

Austrian barman hiding half of national treasure in 9 (10)

Definition: 9 · container + deletion

  • Austrian barman → BERG the Austrian is Alban Berg, the Viennese composer, and his name splits after one letter; 'barman' is the surface's own man behind the bar
  • national treasure → ATTENB the nation's treasure is David Attenborough, and his twelve letters are cut down the middle

Two famous names, one inside the other, which is how the surface gets to be about smuggling. Both are so well known that neither needs a first name.

25 across OATH

Attila the Hun originally behind old curse (4)

Definition: curse · charade + first letters

  • old → O O is the crossword's standing letter for old
  • Attila the Hun → ATH the opening letters of all three words, the little one included

Attila is a good villain for a bad word, and the trap is 'the': every word of the name is needed, including the one you would skip.

26 across SEEDED

Tennis player getting journalist stoned (6)

Definition: stoned · charade

  • Tennis player → SEED a ranked player at a tournament is a seed — the top seed, the third seed
  • journalist → ED ED is the editor, the crossword's standing journalist

Both pieces are conventions worth owning for good: the ranked player and the editor turn up in a grid most weeks.

27 across JEWELS

Joe Swash oddly hoarding article of Spanish treasure (6)

Definition: treasure · container + alternate letters

  • Joe Swash → JEWS the odd-numbered letters of the two names read straight through
  • article of Spanish → EL 'el' is the Spanish for 'the', and it slips inside

Counting through 'Joe Swash' letter by letter is the whole job, and the Spanish article hides in the sentence without disturbing the surface at all.

Down

1 down CHATEAU

Member of nobility beheaded 9 in castle (7)

Definition: castle · charade + deletion

  • Member of nobility → CH CH is the Companion of Honour, the crossword's two-letter grandee — an order in the sovereign's gift, which is the loose sense of rank the clue leans on
  • beheaded 9 → ATEAU 9 across is cake, and the French one — a gateau, to suit the French surface — gives up its first letter

A cross-reference usually hands over a word; this one hands over a category, and the letters only come out if you pick the French member of it.

2 down FAERY

Imagined being a queen in fancy clothing (5)

Definition: Imagined being · container + outer letters

  • fancy → FY only the outside of the word is kept — the F at the front, the Y at the back, and the middle three dropped
  • a → A the article is taken at face value
  • queen → ER ER is Elizabeth Regina, the crossword's standing queen

The joke is that nobody is wearing anything: 'clothing' is an instruction about the outside of a word, and the fancy dress is the surface's own.

3 down EASTERN

Oriental festival ending in confusion (7)

Definition: Oriental · charade + last letter

  • festival → EASTER the church's chief festival, the one the calendar is built around
  • confusion → N the final letter of the word is all that is wanted

A festival collapsing into confusion is a fine picture, and the picture is all the surface is for — the setter's festival is a perfectly orderly one.

5 down CARROT

Incentive rising in motor racing (6)

Definition: Incentive · hidden word + reversal

  • motor racing → CARROT six consecutive letters straddle the gap between these two words, and the entry climbs through them

Motor racing is a whole world of sponsorship and inducements, which is why the phrase reads as scenery instead of as the place the letters are lying.

6 down EXONERATE

Old joke by judge giving pardon (9)

Definition: pardon · charade

  • Old → EX ex- is the prefix meaning former, the crossword's standing 'old'
  • joke → ONE a joke is 'a good one' — have you heard the one about...
  • judge → RATE to rate something is to judge it, to set a value on it

Three everyday words in a row and not one instruction: the only real difficulty is seeing that the middle piece is a noun meaning a gag.

7 down MADEIRA

Mother has fixed idea about River Island (7)

Definition: Island · charade + anagram + container

  • Mother → MA MA is the nursery word for mother
  • idea about River → DEIRA the four letters of the notion are re-set around R, the map-maker's river

The surface's River Island is a high-street shop, so the capital letters mislead twice: the clue wants only R for river, and 'Island' is doing the defining.

8 down APPLETON LAYER

ELO apparently created a bit of atmosphere (8,5)

Definition: a bit of atmosphere · anagram

  • ELO apparently → ELOAPPARENTLY the band's three initials and the adverb behind them supply all thirteen letters

Edward Appleton took a Nobel for finding this region of the ionosphere, the one that bounces short-wave radio back down. 'Apparently' looks like a hedge and is really fodder.

14 down TEA KETTLE

Wooden bench with section removed for brewing accessory (3,6)

Definition: brewing accessory · charade + deletion

  • Wooden → TEAK 'wooden' points at a particular timber rather than at a quality
  • bench → ETTLE a settle is a long high-backed bench, here short of the letter named next
  • section → section abbreviates to S, and that is the letter that goes

The enumeration is the real disguise: (3,6) cuts through the middle of the timber, so neither half of the answer lines up with either half of the wordplay.

16 down COWPATS

More than one whoopsie from Business Secretary’s empty threats about women (7)

Definition: More than one whoopsie · charade + container + outer letters

  • Business → CO a business is a company, CO
  • women → W W is the standing abbreviation for women
  • Secretary’s → PA a secretary is a PA, a personal assistant
  • threats → TS the shell of the word, once the middle has been emptied out

Every piece here is a political abbreviation except the last, which is the same word made hollow — and the childish definition is the only plain English in the clue.

18 down REFUGEE

Heart of Hugh Bonneville ultimately captured by fancy-free Paddington Bear? (7)

Definition: Paddington Bear? · container + anagram + middle letters + last letter

  • free → REFE the four letters are re-set, and then they open up to take the rest
  • Hugh → UG the middle pair of a four-letter name
  • Bonneville → E the very last letter of the surname

Two actors' names and neither matters: only Hugh's middle and Bonneville's tail are used, and the bear from Darkest Peru does the defining.

19 down IDEATES

Imagines day in Rome includes scoff (7)

Definition: Imagines · container

  • day in Rome → IDES the Ides is a Roman day of the month — beware the Ides of March — and it splits after two letters
  • scoff → EAT to scoff is to eat, and greedily

Both halves need re-reading: 'day in Rome' is not a date but the Latin name for one, and 'scoff' is a meal rather than a sneer.

20 down SPONGE

Wipe bum (6)

Definition: Wipe · double definition

  • Wipe → SPONGE the first verb: to wipe a surface down with something soft and absorbent
  • bum → SPONGE the second verb: to cadge, as in bumming a cigarette off a stranger

Two words, two verbs, no wordplay at all — and the shortest clue in the grid is also the one most likely to be read as a rude joke.

23 down SCONE

Finally calls 999? (5)

Definition: 9 · charade + last letter

  • calls → S the last letter of the word, and nothing else from it
  • 99 → CONE a 99 is the ice cream served in a wafer with a flake pushed into it

Three nines do two jobs: the first two are the ice-cream 99, and the last one is the cross-reference at 9 across. The question mark covers the cheek.
