Guardian Quiptic Crossword No 1,386

Across

1 across SEASIDE

Start to survey papers inside facility in holiday area (7)

Definition: holiday area · charade + container + first letter

  • survey → S 'Start to' asks for the first letter of survey
  • facility → EASE facility means ease — the knack of doing something without effort
  • papers → ID your papers are your ID, and they go inside EASE

'Facility' is the trap: not a building but the noun ease. Papers for ID is a convention you will meet most weeks, and the surface is busy pretending to be a hotel inspection.

5 across MALAISE

Dejection sadly about island in Middle East (7)

Definition: Dejection · container

  • Middle East → ME ME is the standard abbreviation for the Middle East — the outer wrapper
  • sadly → ALAS alas is how you say sadly in one word, and it sits inside ME
  • island → I I = island, as printed on a chart, and it goes inside ALAS

Two containers nested one in the other, and both wrappers are abbreviations worth banking: I for island on any map, ME for the Middle East on any news desk.

10 across STEW

Some paste with dish cooked in liquid (4)

Definition: dish cooked in liquid · hidden word

  • paste with → STEW the answer runs across the gap: paSTE With

'Some' is the standing signal to take only part of what follows, and the answer straddles two words, which is exactly where the eye refuses to look.

11 across POLES APART

Eastern Europeans given a role with nothing in common? (5,5)

Definition: with nothing in common? · charade

  • Eastern Europeans → POLES Poles are the people of Poland
  • a role → APART an actor's role is a part, so 'a role' is A PART

The joke is that the Poles of the first half become the poles of the globe in the answer, and the space quietly moves one letter to the left.

12 across SNIPES

In retreat, secures edges of estuaries for wading birds (6)

Definition: wading birds · reversal + charade + outer letters

  • secures → SNIP secures = PINS, turned round by 'In retreat'
  • estuaries → ES the edges of the word, its first and last letters

'Secures' is the noun-verb trap: it means fastens with pins, and PINS is what gets turned round. An across entry can only retreat to the left.

13 across ON THE HOP

Unprepared husband? Hope not somehow when touring (2,3,3)

Definition: Unprepared · anagram + container

  • Hope not → ONTHEOP seven letters of HOPE NOT, rearranged
  • husband → H H is the register's abbreviation for husband, and it tours into the middle

The question mark after 'husband' is the setter smiling at his own surface, where a wife is doubting one. H for husband comes straight from the family tree.

14 across MANDATORY

Required money with a rightwinger (9)

Definition: Required · charade

  • money → M M is the standard shorthand for money, as in the money supply
  • with → AND with is being used as a word meaning and, not as joinery
  • a → A the clue's own a
  • rightwinger → TORY a Tory is the British right-winger of choice

Four pieces in five words, and the sting is 'with': everywhere else in the puzzle it would just be glue, and here it is three letters of the answer.

16 across PETTY

Attractive with no end of colour – and lacking in weight? (5)

Definition: lacking in weight? · deletion

  • Attractive → PRETTY attractive = pretty, about to lose a letter
  • colour → R the end of the word colour is R — that is the letter that goes

'No end of colour' is asking for the last letter of the word itself, not for a shade. The dash marks where the wordplay stops and the definition begins.

17 across SMASH

Break odd parts of some tree (5)

Definition: Break · charade + alternate letters

  • some → SM the odd letters of sOmE, first and third
  • tree → ASH the ash is a tree

ASH belongs with ELM and YEW in the small set of trees setters keep to hand. 'Some' is being taken apart letter by letter rather than meaning 'a few'.

19 across CLIENTELE

Tell niece to move customers (9)

Definition: customers · anagram

  • Tell niece → CLIENTELE the nine letters of TELL NIECE, rearranged

'To move' works both ways at once: on the surface it is persuading a relative to shift house, and underneath it is the instruction to rearrange letters.

23 across ASPIRANT

One with ambition managed to enter a second underground area (8)

Definition: One with ambition · charade + container

  • a → A the clue's own a
  • second → S s is the abbreviation for second, as on a stopwatch
  • underground area → PIT a pit is an underground working, and it takes the insertion
  • managed → RAN to manage a business is to run it — this goes inside the pit

'Managed' is past tense because RAN has to be, which is the kind of small agreement that confirms a parse. The surface is quietly about the Tube.

24 across CANDID

Plain-spoken person standing for election ignoring latest trio of characters (6)

Definition: Plain-spoken · deletion

  • person standing for election → CANDIDATE someone standing for election is a candidate
  • latest trio of characters → ATE the last three letters of CANDIDATE — the part that is ignored

'Characters' means letters here rather than people, which is the whole disguise: the surface is about a politician and the mechanism is about spelling.

26 across INCENTIVES

Invest in reforms to cover case for change as things to spur people (10)

Definition: things to spur people · anagram + container + outer letters

  • Invest in → INNTIVES eight letters of INVEST IN, rearranged by 'reforms'
  • change → CE the case of the word change is its outer letters, C and E

'Case' asks for the outside letters of the word beside it, the way a case encloses whatever it holds. The surface is a perfectly plausible line about company finance.

27 across DRUM

Daughter is given peculiar percussion instrument (4)

Definition: percussion instrument · charade

  • Daughter → D D is the family-tree abbreviation for daughter
  • peculiar → RUM rum meaning odd, as in 'a rum do'

Rum for peculiar is Victorian slang that now survives almost entirely inside crosswords, so it is worth learning the moment you meet it.

28 across SYSTEMS

Sally after vacation checks organised schemes (7)

Definition: organised schemes · charade + outer letters

  • Sally → SY vacated, the name keeps only its outer letters
  • checks → STEMS to stem something is to check it, as in stemming a flow

Two traps in five words: 'vacation' means a word emptied out rather than a holiday, and 'checks' wants the sense of halting rather than anything on a bank counter.

29 across ESCAPEE

Prisoner on the loose in cloak in eastern Kent area (7)

Definition: Prisoner on the loose · charade + container

  • eastern → E E = eastern, as on any compass
  • Kent area → SE Kent sits in the South East, SE on an address — and it is split open
  • cloak → CAPE a cape is a cloak, and it goes into the gap in SE

Both halves of the wrapper are map abbreviations, which is why the clue reads like a police bulletin rather than like wordplay.

Down

2 down ESTONIA

Russian neighbour shuffling in seat holding nothing (7)

Definition: Russian neighbour · anagram

  • in seat holding nothing → ESTONIA the letters of IN SEAT shuffled, with the O of 'nothing' held among them

Nothing is the letter O — the single most useful equivalence in the game. The surface sells you a restless passenger so that 'shuffling' reads as fidgeting.

3 down SEW UP

Conclude in this way, reportedly, ahead (3,2)

Definition: Conclude · charade + homophone

  • in this way → SEW 'in this way' is SO
  • ahead → UP to be up in a match is to be ahead in it

A homophone is the one device where the letters on the page are deliberately the wrong ones: you hear SO and you write SEW.

4 down DEPOSIT

Put down disbandment of top side (7)

Definition: Put down · anagram

  • top side → DEPOSIT the letters of TOP SIDE, broken up by 'disbandment of'

'Disbandment' does the shuffling while the surface talks football: a top side being broken up is exactly the sentence a sports page would print.

6 down ASSETS

Advantages when with groups (6)

Definition: Advantages · charade

  • when → AS as and when are interchangeable — 'as I arrived'
  • groups → SETS a set is a group of things collected together

Two very ordinary words doing wordplay duty, which is what makes the join hard to see: nothing in the surface announces that a seam runs through it.

7 down AT PRESENT

Parts teen misused currently (2,7)

Definition: currently · anagram

  • Parts teen → ATPRESENT the nine letters of PARTS TEEN, rearranged by 'misused'

The (2,7) enumeration is the tell: a nine-letter answer breaking after two letters is nearly always a short phrase, which narrows the guessing enormously.

8 down SORT OUT

Separate soldiers captivated by dark-coloured beer (4,3)

Definition: Separate · container

  • dark-coloured beer → STOUT stout is the black beer, and it is doing the captivating
  • soldiers → OR OR is Other Ranks, the army's non-officers

OR for Other Ranks is one of the few military abbreviations that earns its keep in crosswords. 'Captivated by' is a capture, not a compliment.

9 down BLOOD RELATIVE

Sister, say, in a bolder violet when dressed (5,8)

Definition: Sister, say · anagram

  • a bolder violet → BLOODRELATIVE thirteen letters of A BOLDER VIOLET, rearranged

'Say' is the little flag that says you are being given one example rather than a synonym. 'When dressed' shuffles the letters while the surface arranges flowers.

15 down DISSIDENT

Rebel is with team among Dutch conservationists (9)

Definition: Rebel · charade + container

  • Dutch → D D is the language code for Dutch
  • is → IS the clue's own is, taken at face value
  • team → SIDE a side is a team in every British sports report
  • conservationists → NT the National Trust, the crossword's usual conservationists

Both outer pieces are abbreviations rather than words, which is why the clue reads so smoothly: nothing in the surface admits that D and NT are letters.

18 down MASONRY

Stonework making mother and child really discontented (7)

Definition: Stonework · charade + outer letters

  • mother → MA ma is mother, informally
  • child → SON a son is one sort of child
  • really → RY the word loses its contents and keeps only its outside letters

'Discontented' is the elegant one here: a word emptied of its contents keeps only its outside letters. Its cousins do the same job — hollow, gutted, heartless, empty.

20 down EXCUSES

Overlooks poor grounds? (7)

Definition: Overlooks · double definition

  • Overlooks → EXCUSES to excuse a lapse is to overlook it — the verb reading
  • poor grounds? → EXCUSES the reasons people offer for a failure — the noun reading

Four words, no indicators, and the split falls after the first one. The question mark is doing the work of admitting that not every excuse is a poor one.

21 down LEISURE

Employee lately is taken in by enticement for free time (7)

Definition: free time · container + last letter

  • enticement → LURE an enticement is a lure, and it does the taking in
  • Employee → E 'lately' asks for the last letter of Employee
  • is → IS the clue's own is, following the E inside the lure

'Lately' is asking for a last letter, not telling you when — the sort of double life a short adverb leads. The whole surface reads like an HR memo.

22 down BANTAM

Hold up thing put on table to catch small chicken (6)

Definition: small chicken · reversal + charade

  • thing put on table → MAT a mat is what goes on the table under a hot dish
  • to catch → NAB to nab someone is to catch them

In a down clue 'hold up' can only mean stand the whole assembly on its head, since there is no left or right on a vertical entry.

25 down NADIA

Female has partly written a diary (5)

Definition: Female · hidden word

  • written a diary → NADIA the name spans three words: writteN A DIAry

'Partly' is the hidden-word flag. A name straddling three words is the hardest kind to spot, because the eye reads the words and not the letters.
