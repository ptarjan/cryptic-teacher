Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,082

Across

1 across FOOT FAULT

Splitting payslip results in bad service? (4,5)

Definition: bad service? · charade

  • payslip → FOOT split the word and the first half is 'pay' — to foot a bill is to pay it
  • payslip → FAULT the second half is 'slip', and a slip is a fault — a lapse, and to a geologist a crack in the rock

One word, two halves: the setter hands you 'payslip' whole and 'Splitting' is the instruction to break it open. The tennis court only appears once the answer does.

6 across BEES

Occasional brewers who will deliver honey? (4)

Definition: who will deliver honey? · alternate letters

  • brewers → BEES take every other letter: BrEwErS

'Occasional' means every other one rather than now and then — a favourite disguise for alternate letters. The brewery in the surface exists only to hide the insects.

10 across BLUFF

Deceive friendly bank (5)

Definition: Deceive · double definition

  • Deceive → BLUFF to bluff at cards is to deceive by pretending to hold what you do not
  • friendly → BLUFF a third sense the clue also uses: a bluff manner is hearty and good-natured, blunt without malice
  • bank → BLUFF a bluff is a steep bank or headland above water

Three senses in three words, not two: a verb, an adjective and a noun, and only the last of them has anything to do with rivers. English works this word hard.

11 across YOUNGSTER

Gutsy Nero wrestled child (9)

Definition: child · anagram

  • Gutsy Nero → YOUNGSTER 'wrestled' throws the nine letters of GUTSY NERO into a new order

An adjective in front of a name is the commonest disguise for anagram fodder, because it reads as description. Nero took the throne at sixteen, which makes the surface almost true.

12 across LEECHES

Suckers taking shelter before short game (7)

Definition: Suckers · charade + deletion

  • taking shelter → LEE the lee is the sheltered side, where you take shelter from the wind
  • game → CHES the game is CHESS, docked of its last letter by 'short'

'Short' is an instruction rather than a description: it takes the final letter off. The definition works twice over, since a sucker is also a dupe.

13 across INTROIT

Sacred music at heart of saintly shroud rite (7)

Definition: Sacred music · charade + middle letters

  • saintly → INT the heart of saINTly
  • shroud → RO the heart of shROud
  • rite → IT the heart of rITe

One instruction, three words: 'at heart of' works its way along all of them in turn. The funeral surface is pure decoy — shroud and rite are there for their middles.

14 across SERIOUS DAMAGE

Acute harm horrible images aroused (7,6)

Definition: Acute harm · anagram

  • images aroused → SERIOUS DAMAGE 'horrible' rearranges the thirteen letters of IMAGES AROUSED

Anagram indicators love to pose as adjectives: 'horrible' reads as part of the picture being described, and is really the instruction to break that picture up.

17 across FRUIT MACHINES

For example, Apple devices providing gambling opportunities (5,8)

Definition: gambling opportunities · charade

  • For example, Apple → FRUIT an apple is one example of a fruit; the capital letter sells you the computer company
  • devices → MACHINES a device is a machine

'For example' marks a definition by example: the clue gives you one member and asks for the class it belongs to. Apple's capital A is the setter's only sleight of hand.

21 across PARSNIP

Assigns blame when returning vegetarian food (7)

Definition: vegetarian food · charade + reversal

  • Assigns → PINS to pin something on somebody is to assign it to them
  • blame → RAP taking the rap is taking the blame

Two halves of one idiom — you pin something on a person and they take the rap — and the whole seven letters then turn round. In an across entry, 'returning' means read right to left.

22 across PRIAPIC

Chap so excited as couple make comeback in film (7)

Definition: Chap so excited · container + reversal

  • film → PIC a pic is a film, and it opens up to take the other piece
  • couple → PAIR a couple is a pair, turned round by 'make comeback'

Priapus, the Greek fertility god drawn with a permanent erection, is behind the word — which is why the excitement in the surface is not the ordinary kind.

24 across HANDS DOWN

Passes on instruction to tellers when bank heist’s over (5,4)

Definition: Passes on · charade

  • instruction to tellers → HANDS the raider's shout across the counter is 'Hands up!' — this is the first word of it
  • when bank heist’s over → DOWN the raid finished, the cashiers' hands come down again

The wordplay is a little scene rather than a string of abbreviations: an order shouted at the cashiers, then the moment it stops applying to them.

25 across TUTTI

All together now – express disappointment at what comes before do (5)

Definition: All together now · charade

  • express disappointment → TUT tut is the noise of disapproval
  • what comes before do → TI in sol-fa the scale climbs to ti and only then reaches doh again

Sol-fa is standing crossword vocabulary — do, re, mi, fa, so, la, ti — and 'before do' asks for the note just under it. The singalong surface hides a marking printed in scores.

26 across TWEE

Sentimental short message made even shorter (4)

Definition: Sentimental · deletion

  • short message → TWEET the short message is a tweet, before it loses its tail

The answer is a nursery word for cute, supposedly a child's attempt at 'sweet'. The messaging surface is thoroughly modern and the word it hides is Victorian.

27 across LISTENS IN

Catalogue standard no good bugs (7,2)

Definition: bugs · charade + deletion

  • Catalogue → LIST to catalogue is to list
  • standard → ENSIGN a standard is a flag — an ensign
  • no good → G good is G, and 'no' takes that letter out of ENSIGN

'Bugs' is a verb here, not a plague of insects, and it is the one word doing definition duty. Standard for flag is a crossword staple worth banking.

Down

1 down FABULIST

One telling stories featuring fantastic, very minor celebrities (8)

Definition: One telling stories · charade

  • fantastic → FAB fab is the sixties clipping of fabulous
  • very minor celebrities → ULIST fame is graded from the A-list downwards; keep going down the alphabet and the U-list is about as minor as it gets

The A-list, the B-list and the C-list are as far as anyone normally counts. The joke is that the alphabet carries on long after the fame has run out.

2 down OF USE

Beneficial to add oxygen to blend (2,3)

Definition: Beneficial · charade

  • oxygen → O O is the chemical symbol for oxygen
  • blend → FUSE to fuse is to blend two things into one

Chemistry supplies the shortest counters in the game: O for oxygen, H for hydrogen, N for nitrogen. The (2,3) enumeration warns you the answer is a phrase.

3 down FIFTH COLUMNIST

Internal enemy in appropriate position here? (5,9)

Definition: Internal enemy in appropriate position here? · cryptic definition

  • Internal enemy → the straight sense: someone inside your own camp working for the other side, a phrase that came out of the Spanish civil war
  • in appropriate position here? → look at where this light sits in the grid — it runs downwards, and it is the fifth of the columns, which is the setter putting the answer where its own name says it belongs

There is no separable wordplay here: the whole clue is the definition, with a wink at the grid. Count the columns from the left and the joke pays off.

4 down ULYSSES

Element of July’s session is something hard to understand? (7)

Definition: something hard to understand? · hidden word

  • July’s session → ULYSSES the answer straddles the two words: jULY'S SESsion

'Element of' is a quiet flag for a hidden answer — a part of what follows. The apostrophe counts as no letter at all, which is what makes the join so hard to see.

5 down TOURIGA

Travel around Wigan central to find Portuguese wine (7)

Definition: Portuguese wine · charade + middle letters

  • Travel around → TOUR to tour is to travel around
  • Wigan → IGA the central letters of wIGAn

'Central' wants the middle of the word, not the middle of the town — the geography is a decoy. Wines are routinely named after the grape they are pressed from.

7 down EAT NO LEAN

Mrs Sprat’s dietary requirement processed at lane one (3,2,4)

Definition: Mrs Sprat’s dietary requirement · anagram

  • at lane one → EAT NO LEAN 'processed' rearranges the nine letters of AT LANE ONE

Jack Sprat could eat no fat, and the rhyme gives his wife the opposite half of the bargain. The athletics meeting on the surface is there to make 'lane one' look like scenery.

8 down SCROTA

Gutted scientific programme providing containers for seed producers (6)

Definition: containers for seed producers · charade + outer letters

  • scientific → SC gutted, the word keeps only its shell — the outer letters
  • programme → ROTA a rota is the programme of who does what and when

'Gutted' empties a word of everything between its ends. The laboratory tone is cover for anatomy, and the plural in Latin form is what the crossing letters insist on.

9 down AGATHA CHRISTIE

She made crime novels airtight as each evolved (6,8)

Definition: She made crime novels · anagram

  • airtight as each → AGATHA CHRISTIE 'evolved' rearranges all fourteen letters of AIRTIGHT AS EACH

A fourteen-letter anagram hides in plain sight because the fodder reads as praise for a plot — an alibi is exactly what ought to be airtight.

15 down REFERENCE

Endorsement after president would indicate top choice (9)

Definition: Endorsement · deletion

  • top choice → PREFERENCE your top choice is your preference
  • president → P P is the standard abbreviation, and it is the letter the answer has to sit behind

Nothing here says 'remove'. The clue promises that the answer, parked behind a president's P, spells something else — so work backwards from that word and the P comes away.

16 down C-SECTION

Conservative faction working to gather information for delivery option (1-7)

Definition: delivery option · charade + container

  • Conservative → C C is the ballot-paper abbreviation for Conservative
  • faction → SECT a sect is a breakaway faction
  • information → I the i of a tourist information sign
  • working → ON a machine that is working is on

'Delivery' is obstetric here rather than postal, and the (1-7) enumeration tells you the answer opens with a single letter and a hyphen — two footholds before you parse a word.

18 down TOPSOIL

Labour adopts works creating best medium for growth (7)

Definition: best medium for growth · container

  • Labour → TOIL labour as plain hard work is toil; the capital L is party-political misdirection
  • works → OPS ops are operations — works, in the military or business sense

A capital letter at the head of a clue proves nothing, and here it does all the misleading: the party and its works turn out to be a gardener's spadework.

19 down ALPINES

In Scotland all large trees produce small flowers (7)

Definition: small flowers · charade

  • In Scotland all → A in Scots, all is written a'
  • large → L L is the clothing-label large
  • trees → PINES pines are the trees

Scots dialect earns its keep in crosswords — a' for all, wee for small, ken for know. Here the forest on the surface shrinks to a rockery in the answer.

20 down UPSHOT

Trophies stolen, clubs eliminated – it’s a result! (6)

Definition: a result · charade + deletion

  • Trophies → CUPS trophies are cups
  • clubs → C clubs is the card suit C — the letter that gets eliminated
  • stolen → HOT stolen goods are hot

The cards supply the abbreviation, C for clubs as on a bridge scoresheet, while the surface keeps you thinking of golf. Hot for stolen is thieves' slang a setter can rely on.

23 down PATES

Heads or livers chopped up? Depends how you say it (5)

Definition: Heads · double definition

  • Heads → PATES a pate is the crown of the head — the bald pate
  • livers chopped up? → PATES pâtés, with the accents a crossword grid cannot show

Five letters, two words, two quite different sounds — PAYTS for the skulls, pa-TAYS for the starter. A grid carries no accents, which is what lets the setter run them together.
