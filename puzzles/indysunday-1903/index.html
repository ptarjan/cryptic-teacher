Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,903

Across

1 across MARSHY

Very wet planet helpfully drained (6)

Definition: Very wet · charade + outer letters

  • planet → MARS Mars is the planet, the red one next out from us
  • helpfully → HY the two letters at the edges of helpfully, once the middle has gone

Drain a marsh and it stops being one, which is the joke sitting in plain sight. Mars is worth banking as the crossword's default planet.

4 across SMASHING

Wicked vandalism (8)

Definition: Wicked · double definition

  • Wicked → SMASHING both are schoolyard slang for terrific
  • vandalism → SMASHING breaking windows or property for the sake of it — violent, wanton destruction

Two words, no joinery, and the seam falls between them. Both halves are British to the bone, which is what makes the pair read as one phrase.

9 across DENIAL

Disclaimer not invoked initially when breaking contract (6)

Definition: Disclaimer · container + first letters

  • contract → DEAL a contract is a deal
  • not invoked → NI the initial letters of not and invoked

Every word belongs in a courtroom, so nothing announces itself as an instruction. The giveaway is 'initially', which no ordinary sentence about contracts needs.

10 across BULLETIN

Nearly all nonsense, admitted intelligence report (8)

Definition: intelligence report · charade + deletion

  • nonsense → BULL bull is nonsense, the printable half of the usual phrase
  • Nearly all → the last L never gets written
  • admitted → LETIN someone admitted at the door has been let in

'Admitted' looks like a confession and means the opposite kind of letting in. Bull for nonsense is worth banking — setters reach for it constantly.

12 across LOOK SMART

Can end of tack give sharp pain? Step on it (4,5)

Definition: Step on it · charade + last letter

  • Can → LOO the can and the loo are the same small room
  • end of tack → K the letter tack finishes on
  • sharp pain → SMART a smart is the stinging pain of a slap

'Can' for loo is standard crossword furniture, and 'smart' as a noun is the sense the question mark is apologising for.

13 across DIRGE

Rewind intro of extremely grating dismal song (5)

Definition: dismal song · charade + first letter + reversal

  • intro of extremely → E the letter extremely starts with
  • grating → GRID the grating over a drain is a grid

A grating is a grid, and seeing that is the whole clue — the surface is busy complaining about a song that grates.

14 across PADDLING POOL

Decomposing floater in place where kids play (8,4)

Definition: where kids play · container + charade

  • place → PL Pl. is the abbreviation for Place on any street sign
  • Decomposing → ADDLING an egg going rotten is addling
  • floater → POO a floater is the lavatory word for what will not flush away

Read straight, the clue is a warning about what is bobbing among the toddlers. 'Place' shrinking to its address abbreviation is the step that hides the join.

18 across RELATIONSHIP

Affair delights husband going through split (12)

Definition: Affair · container + charade

  • split → RIP a split in cloth is a rip
  • delights → ELATIONS elations are delights, and both are plural
  • husband → H h is the standard abbreviation for husband

H for husband is one to memorise — it also does hard, hot and hospital. The surface describes a marriage coming apart, which is the opposite of what the answer means.

21 across MEANS

Reluctant to spend son's money (5)

Definition: money · charade

  • Reluctant to spend → MEAN somebody reluctant to spend is mean
  • son → S s is the family-tree abbreviation for son

'Reluctant to spend' is a definition of mean dressed up as a character sketch, which is what keeps the surface reading as one complaint about a family.

23 across ENDEAVOUR

Labour's demise always on the radio (9)

Definition: Labour · charade + homophone

  • demise → END a demise is an end
  • always → EAVOUR always is ever, and run straight on after END that sound is spelt this way

Say the two pieces quickly and they slide into one word. The surface is a political obituary, whereas the definition wants labour as the plain noun for hard work.

24 across THIRTEEN

Figure suspected of depleting fortune moved her tent around island (8)

Definition: Figure suspected of depleting fortune · anagram + container

  • her tent → THRTEEN the seven letters of her tent, struck and pitched elsewhere
  • island → I I is the map-maker's abbreviation for island

Nothing in the clue says unlucky. 'Suspected of depleting fortune' is how a setter points at a superstition without naming it, and the camping surface hides the join.

25 across MISSUS

Dutch regret withdrawal from America (6)

Definition: Dutch · charade

  • regret → MISS to regret an absence is to miss it
  • withdrawal from America → US what pulls out when America withdraws is the US, its usual short form

'Dutch' is Cockney rhyming slang, from Duchess of Fife, and the talk of America is there so that you read it as a nationality instead.

26 across CREVASSE

Half agreed with Conservative minister's deep rift (8)

Definition: deep rift · charade + deletion

  • Conservative → C C is the abbreviation for Conservative on a ballot paper
  • minister → REV a minister of religion is a Rev
  • Half agreed → ASSE one who agreed assented, and the front half of that word is ASSE

The surface is Westminster and the answer is glaciology: 'minister' is a churchman here, and the rift is a hole in the ice rather than a quarrel in a party.

27 across ATKINS

Fat family's in need of fine dietician (6)

Definition: dietician · charade + deletion

  • Fat → FAT fat is taken at face value, letter for letter
  • family's → KINS kin is family, and the possessive adds the S
  • fine → F F is fine, the grade stamped on a pencil, and it is the letter lacking

The Atkins diet is steak and butter with the bread taken away, which is exactly what makes a fat family in want of one funny.

Down

1 down MODEL T

Might this be shown up in road safety test? (5,1)

Definition: Might this be shown up in road safety test? · container + reversal + &lit

  • road safety test → MOT the MOT is the annual roadworthiness test, named after the Ministry of Transport
  • shown → DEL shown round a house is led round it, and LED climbing the grid reads DEL

Ford's first mass-produced car left the factory in 1908, decades before any such test existed, so the straight reading is a joke about an antique meeting a modern inspection.

2 down RANDOM

Irregular shifts for doorman, with none repeated (6)

Definition: Irregular · anagram + deletion

  • doorman → DOORMAN the man on the door supplies the letters, before one of them goes
  • none repeated → one O drops out, since nothing here is allowed to appear twice

Nought for O is standing crossword shorthand, and the instruction hides inside a grumble about a rota — the surface is a doorman complaining, not a setter giving orders.

3 down HEAD START

Notice street in centre gets competitive advantage (4,5)

Definition: competitive advantage · container + charade

  • centre → HEART the centre of anything is its heart
  • Notice → AD a notice in a paper is an ad
  • street → ST St is the standard abbreviation on any address

'Notice' as a noun for a small advert is the step most solvers miss, and the shop-lined surface is there to keep you reading 'centre' as a town centre.

5 down MOUNTAINSIDE

Face with scars perhaps from rioting in one stadium (12)

Definition: Face with scars perhaps · anagram

  • in one stadium → INONESTADIUM twelve letters looking for a new arrangement

A scar is a bare rock face on a hillside — the north of England is full of them — so the definition is a geographer's word, and the crowd trouble is pure surface.

6 down SOLID

Concrete ran smoothly with nothing to stop it (5)

Definition: Concrete · container

  • ran smoothly → SLID what ran smoothly slid
  • nothing → O nothing is the nought, written O

'Concrete' is the trap: the surface is pouring the building material while the definition wants the adjective, meaning firm and definite.

7 down INTERPOL

Solvers worldwide let rip on cryptic (8)

Definition: Solvers worldwide · anagram

  • let rip on → LETRIPON eight letters, given in the wrong order

The surface is a roomful of crossword solvers enjoying themselves, and the answer is a police body — 'worldwide' is the word doing the quiet work of telling you which.

8 down GINGERLY

Cautiously leaving ahead of schedule, saving seconds (8)

Definition: Cautiously · charade + deletion

  • leaving → GOING leaving is going
  • ahead of schedule → EARLY ahead of schedule is early
  • saving seconds → the O of going and the A of early are held back, each being its word's second letter

'Seconds' keeps company with the schedule so that it reads as time on a clock. An early finish and a careful tread have nothing to do with each other, which is the misdirection.

11 down HANDSOMENESS

Personal appeal from Messi in the middle after pass: "Really, guys?" (12)

Definition: Personal appeal · charade + middle letters

  • pass → HAND to hand something over is to pass it
  • Really → SO 'is that so?' is 'really?'
  • guys → MEN guys are men
  • Messi → ESS the heart of the footballer's name, both ends covered

Four pieces and a football match: Messi lays the ball off while a team-mate complains about it, and not one word of that picture is about looks.

15 down GUITARIST

E.g. Harrison intuitive about India, playing sitar (9)

Definition: E.g. Harrison · container + anagram

  • intuitive → GUT an intuitive hunch is a gut feeling
  • India → I I is India in the radio alphabet
  • sitar → ARIST the five letters of sitar, rearranged

George Harrison played lead for the Beatles and then took up the sitar, so both instruments in the clue belong to the same man — which is why the surface reads so innocently.

16 down AROMATIC

One with amorous intentions dispatches note with perfume (8)

Definition: with perfume · charade + deletion

  • One → A one is a, the indefinite article
  • with amorous intentions → ROMANTIC somebody with amorous intentions is a romantic
  • note → N n is the abbreviation for note, and it is what gets sent away

N for note is a workhorse abbreviation. The surface — a suitor posting scented letters — does all it can to keep you reading 'note' as something written.

17 down FLEA BITE

Minor animal attack somewhat interrupting run (4,4)

Definition: Minor animal attack · container

  • run → FLEE to run from danger is to flee
  • somewhat → ABIT somewhat tired is a bit tired

The definition is literal and the answer is also an idiom, which is the pleasure here: the smallest possible bite has become the standard word for a trifle.

19 down BONSAI

Dwarf on BA is spoiled (6)

Definition: Dwarf · anagram

  • on BA is → ONBAIS six letters, the airline included, waiting to be reordered

BA is the crossword's usual airline. The surface puts a spoilt little person on a flight, which is a fairy tale with nothing to do with miniature trees.

20 down CRISIS

Problem written wrongly like that holding teacher up (6)

Definition: Problem · container + reversal

  • written wrongly like that → SIC sic is what an editor prints beside a word left exactly as it was wrongly written
  • teacher → SIR a schoolmaster is sir

Sic is Latin for 'thus', which is why a phrase meaning 'like that' spells it — the clue defines the editor's mark instead of using it.

22 down SUTRA

Eastern principles some start using the wrong way (5)

Definition: Eastern principles · hidden word + reversal

  • start using → SUTRA it lies across stART USing, and climbs the grid backwards

Only the Kama Sutra is famous in English, but the word covers any thread of teaching: sutra and 'suture' share a root meaning stitched together.
