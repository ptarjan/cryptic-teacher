Across

1 across SOCK

Biff's one that's odd when alone (4)

Definition: Biff · double definition

  • Biff → SOCK to biff someone is to land a blow on them, and there is a four-letter verb for that
  • one that's odd when alone → SOCK these come in pairs, so a single one left in the drawer has no partner

The joke is all in 'odd': one of a pair left by itself is an odd one, while the surface is offering you 'odd' in the sense of peculiar.

3 across BON APPETIT

Dig in pit - bone part fragments - run away (3,7)

Definition: Dig in · anagram + deletion

  • run → R R is the cricket scorer's mark for a run, and this is the letter that goes
  • pit - bone part → PITBONEPAT eleven letters with that R removed, leaving ten to rearrange

A pit, a bone and a run make a grim little archaeology scene, and none of it survives: the whole sentence after the first two words is raw material.

9 across RAND

Research - but seemingly no development - in S African money? (4)

Definition: S African money · deletion

  • Research → RANDD the everyday shorthand for a company's research-and-development arm sets two initials either side of the little joining word
  • development → D the final letter of that shorthand stands for development, and it is the one to drop

'Research' looks like a definition and is really an abbreviation written out in full. The dashes sell it as a complaint about a stalled project.

10 across ATKINS DIET

Date skin: it's cooked for restrictive regimen (6,4)

Definition: restrictive regimen · anagram

  • Date skin: it → DATESKINIT ten letters, taken exactly as they stand

'Date skin' offers you dried fruit or a beauty tip, and 'it's cooked' reads as slang for finished. Both are only letters and the instruction to move them.

12 across KLEPTOMANIA

Taking forever? (11)

Definition: Taking forever? · cryptic definition

  • Taking → not the time a job takes: this is taking in the sense of taking things that are not yours
  • forever → not a long wait either, but an urge that never lets up, however little is worth having

There is nothing to take apart here, only one phrase read twice: the impatient grumble about a slow queue turns into a clinical condition.

15 across STERNUM

Total including seabird's bone (7)

Definition: bone · container

  • Total → SUM the total of a column of figures is its sum
  • seabird → TERN a tern is the small fork-tailed sea bird, and it is the crossword's default one

The possessive in 'seabird's' is doing link duty rather than belonging to anyone: the bird is the wordplay and the word after it is the definition.

16 across NEEDLED

About 50 wanted to be annoyed (7)

Definition: annoyed · container

  • wanted → NEEDED wanted in the past tense, six letters
  • 50 → L L is the Roman numeral for fifty

'About' is most often C for circa in a cryptic, and here it is the wrapping instruction instead. The tense of 'wanted' is what makes the letters fit.

17 across UNCLASP

Relative mostly visible with snake - let go! (7)

Definition: let go · charade + deletion

  • Relative → UNCL a relative may be an uncle, and here the final E stays out of view
  • snake → ASP the asp is the small venomous snake of Egypt, and three letters make it a crossword regular

The clue reads as a warning shouted at somebody gripping a snake. That shout is the definition, and everything in front of it is construction.

19 across LATERAL

Idolater almost concealing cunning manner of thinking (7)

Definition: cunning manner of thinking · hidden word

  • Idolater almost → LATERAL seven letters in a single unbroken run across these two words, starting in the middle of the first

'Idolater almost' looks like an instruction to trim a word, which is the trap: it is the hiding place, and the join falls inside a word rather than at the space.

20 across DUDLEY MOORE

Comic actor Rod: you led me astray (6,5)

Definition: Comic actor · anagram

  • Rod: you led me → RODYOULEDME eleven letters, taken as they stand

The clue dangles a first name at you, so 'Rod' reads as the man being named. It is the first three letters of the shuffle instead.

23 across HEPTATHLON

No help - that's scuppered contest (10)

Definition: contest · anagram

  • No help - that → NOHELPTHAT ten letters, taken as they stand

The Greek hepta is seven and athlon a contest, so the answer says 'seven contests' on its own, which is why one word can define it.

24 across ASIA

Fantasias describing most of humanity (4)

Definition: most of humanity · hidden word

  • Fantasias → ASIA one word holds four consecutive letters, starting at its fifth

'Most of' is the standard signal for chopping a letter off a word, and here it belongs to the definition instead — a plain statement of population.

25 across INCEPTIONS

Starts off nine optics (10)

Definition: Starts · anagram

  • nine optics → NINEOPTICS ten letters, taken as they stand

'Starts off' looks like one phrase and splits down the middle: the noun in front is the definition, and the little word after it is the signal.

26 across BYTE

Slice of memory: snack picked up (4)

Definition: Slice of memory · homophone

  • snack → BYTE a small snack is a bite, and said aloud that word can be written another way

'Slice of memory' looks like an instruction to cut letters out of the word 'memory'. It is the definition, and the whole mechanism is in the ear.

Down

1 down STRIKES OUT

Deletes with a sweep of the pen - or leaves (7,3)

Definition: Deletes with a sweep of the pen · double definition

  • Deletes with a sweep of the pen → STRIKESOUT one line drawn through a word cancels it on the page
  • leaves → STRIKESOUT the sense of setting off somewhere new, as a pioneer does on their own

The dash does the misdirecting: 'or leaves' looks like more about the pen, when it is a whole second definition and it is about departure.

2 down CONFERENCE

Summit where you find a pear (10)

Definition: Summit · double definition

  • Summit → CONFERENCE a summit is a top-level meeting of leaders, talks rather than a mountain
  • a pear → CONFERENCE the long green variety that fills British greengrocers carries this name

'Where you find' is only joinery, and no letters are hidden anywhere: the two senses sit side by side, one from diplomacy and one from the fruit bowl.

4 down OPTIMUM

'Best'? 'Select'? Everyman is!? Um ... (7)

Definition: 'Best' · charade

  • 'Select' → OPT to select between things is to opt for one
  • Everyman is → I'M the setter refers to himself as I, so his name plus 'is' contracts to I'm
  • Um → UM the hesitation noise, taken down letter for letter

The surface is somebody groping for the right compliment and trailing off. That trailing off is not decoration: the noise at the end is two of the seven letters.

5 down AVIGNON

French city's answer: their refusal, German setter Victor rebuffed (7)

Definition: French city · charade + reversal

  • answer → A A is the standard short form of answer, as in a quiz's Q and A
  • their refusal → NON French for no, and 'their' points back at the country in the definition
  • German → G G is the usual single letter for German
  • setter → I the setter is the person writing the clue, and in a clue that person is I
  • Victor → V Victor is V in the phonetic alphabet, alongside Charlie and Romeo

'German' sits right beside 'refusal' and offers you NEIN. The refusal is French, like the city, and German is doing nothing but its single letter.

6 down POSTAGE STAMP

Stopgap team's assembled: it's stuck in the corner (7,5)

Definition: it's stuck in the corner · anagram

  • Stopgap team's → STOPGAPTEAMS twelve letters, taken exactly as they stand

'Stopgap' promises a makeshift arrangement and 'team's assembled' a squad turning up; neither survives into the answer, which is a thing on an envelope.

7 down TAIL

Dog story related (4)

Definition: Dog · homophone

  • story → TAIL a story is a tale, and said aloud that word can just as well be written the other way

'Dog story' reads as one phrase and is two: the first word is a verb, and only the second is being sounded out.

8 down TUTU

Lacking shred of compassion, twice cut up ballerina's clothing (4)

Definition: ballerina's clothing · reversal + deletion + first letter + charade

  • shred of compassion → C a shred is the smallest scrap of a thing, so this is the letter compassion opens on, and it is the one to lose
  • cut up → TU CUT climbing a down entry reads T-U-C, and the C just named comes off the end
  • twice → TU the same pair written out a second time, one straight after the other

The surface accuses somebody of heartlessness, and every word of it turns out to be machinery: even 'twice' is an instruction rather than a description.

11 down STANDARD LAMP

Classic song - hit - source of illumination (8,4)

Definition: source of illumination · charade

  • Classic song → STANDARD a song from the old repertoire that every band is expected to know goes by this name
  • hit → LAMP to lamp someone is British slang for hitting them hard

Both halves are decoys from the music business: one is a jazz musician's word, and 'hit' is not a chart-topper at all but a punch.

13 down GLORIOUSLY

Sir, you'll go off in magnificent style (10)

Definition: in magnificent style · anagram

  • Sir, you'll go → SIRYOULLGO ten letters, apostrophe ignored, taken as they stand

'Go off' looks like a single instruction, which costs you two letters: 'go' is material, and only the little word after it is the signal.

14 down ADULTERATE

With no hint of nervousness, taunt leader that's corrupt, corrupt (10)

Definition: , corrupt · anagram + deletion + first letter

  • hint of nervousness → N a hint of a word is the letter it opens on, and this is the one taken out
  • taunt leader → TAUTLEADER eleven letters with that N gone, leaving ten to rearrange

The setter writes the same word twice and gives it two jobs: the first tells you to disturb the letters, the second says what they add up to.

18 down PAD THAI

Unwisely, I had most of pâté stir-fry (3,4)

Definition: stir-fry · anagram + deletion

  • I had → IHAD four letters exactly as written
  • pâté → PAT the French spread, with its final letter left off

'Unwisely, I had...' sets up a confession about a bad meal, and the pâté is the giveaway: it is a French delicacy on the surface and three letters underneath.

19 down LIE DOWN

Act dishonestly, getting stuffing for duvet - then do this? (3,4)

Definition: then do this · charade

  • Act dishonestly → LIE to act dishonestly in speech is to tell an untruth
  • stuffing for duvet → DOWN the soft under-feathers a quilt is filled with

The definition arrives last and points backwards: having built the phrase, the clue simply tells you to go and do it. Both pieces are bedroom words already.

21 down CHAI

Primarily cinnamony, hot Asian infusion? (4)

Definition: Primarily cinnamony, hot Asian infusion? · &lit + first letters

  • cinnamony, hot Asian infusion → CHAI the opening letter of each of the four words, read in the order they stand

An all-in-one: the sentence is the recipe and the description at once, and the question mark admits this is not the only spiced hot drink going.

22 down EPIC

Huge eerie deathcap fungi: panic, ultimately (4)

Definition: Huge · last letters

  • eerie deathcap fungi: panic → EPIC the closing letter of each of these four words, read in the order they stand

The mushroom hunt is a decoy: deathcaps and panic build a poisoning scene, and every word in it is there only for the letter it ends on.
