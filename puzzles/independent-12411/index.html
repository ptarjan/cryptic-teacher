Across

1 across SHOT GLASS

Go to meet German girl somewhere for a little drink? (4,5)

Definition: somewhere for a little drink? · charade

  • Go → SHOT a go at something is a shot at it, one attempt
  • German → G G is the standard label for German, as on a dictionary entry
  • girl → LASS a lass is a girl, chiefly in northern British use

'German girl' asks to be a name — Greta, Heidi — but the two words are a one-letter label and a plain synonym, taken separately.

6 across FIVER

On retirement, provided with very limited amount of money (5)

Definition: amount of money · charade + reversal + deletion

  • provided → FI provided that means if, and those two letters are then turned round
  • very → VER the word docked of its last letter

The surface is a pension paying next to nothing, and both instructions hide as ordinary description: one word turns a piece, the other trims one.

9 across OFF-PEAK

Very loudly exercise in wood when it’s supposed to be quiet (3-4)

Definition: when it’s supposed to be quiet · container + charade

  • Very loudly → FF ff is fortissimo, the mark on a score for very loud
  • exercise → PE PE is physical education, the school lesson of exercise
  • wood → OAK oak is a hardwood, and it opens after its first letter

'In wood when it's supposed to be quiet' reads as one phrase about a peaceful forest; the definition actually starts at 'when'.

10 across AMNESIA

Recall failure of clumsy seaman to gather sails in the middle (7)

Definition: Recall failure · anagram + middle letter

  • seaman → SEAMAN six letters straight from the clue, waiting to be jumbled
  • sails → I the central letter of the five in sails

The surface is a hapless sailor failing to furl the canvas; 'sails' does no sailing in the parse, it is asked for one letter.

11 across CREME DE MENTHE

Eccentric cheered men and met for drink (5,2,6)

Definition: drink · anagram

  • cheered men and met → CHEEREDMENMET thirteen letters, matching the enumeration exactly

Three past-tense words that no sentence would need together are the giveaway: they are in the clue for their letters, not their meaning.

13 across MIRIN

Northern Ireland border rejected Japanese wine (5)

Definition: Japanese wine · charade + reversal

  • Northern Ireland → NI NI is the usual abbreviation for the province
  • border → RIM the rim of a plate is its border

Build the two pieces in clue order first and turn the whole five letters over: the reversal applies to the pair, not to either one alone.

15 across OVERTURES

Propositions concerning trade union resolution (9)

Definition: Propositions · charade

  • concerning → OVER a row over pay is a row concerning pay
  • trade union → TU TU is the abbreviation used for a trade union
  • resolution → RES res. is how a resolution is shortened on an agenda

Nothing but the definition sits in front of three chunks in clue order; the hard part is reading 'concerning' as a preposition rather than an adjective.

17 across RAINMAKER

Gardener employing Pole holding current degree – one earning a lot for the business (9)

Definition: one earning a lot for the business · container + charade

  • Gardener employing Pole → RAKER a rake is a toothed head on a long pole, so the gardener wielding one is a raker — a loose fit the setter asks you to allow
  • current → IN what is in is what is current, in fashion, up to date
  • degree → MA MA, Master of Arts, is the crossword's usual degree

'Current' wants to be the electrical kind and is the fashionable kind, which is what keeps this insertion hidden for so long.

19 across PEPSI

Caffeinated drink livens setter (5)

Definition: Caffeinated drink · charade

  • livens → PEPS to pep something up is to liven it, and the clue's third-person verb keeps the S
  • setter → I the setter is the person who wrote the clue, and he calls himself I

'Setter' is a dog or a compiler in cryptics; here it is the compiler, and he is worth just the one letter.

21 across CHAMPS ELYSEES

Church in the morning: on top of that cathedral city views showing famous avenue (6,7)

Definition: famous avenue · charade

  • Church → CH Ch is the abbreviation printed on a map for a church
  • in the morning → AM the hours before noon are a.m.
  • on top of that → PS a PS is the postscript added on top of a letter already finished
  • cathedral city → ELY Ely is a small cathedral city in the Cambridgeshire fens
  • views → SEES to view something is to see it, and the clue's verb form keeps the S

'On top of that' reads like an instruction about where a piece goes, and is two letters of its own — the afterthought at the foot of a letter.

25 across NITRATE

It fertilises egg at speed (7)

Definition: It fertilises · charade

  • egg → NIT a nit is the egg a head louse glues to a hair
  • speed → RATE the rate something travels at is its speed

The surface is about fertilising an egg, which is the wrong biology twice over: the egg is a louse's, and the fertilising is done to fields.

26 across OTHELLO

Leaving hotel, warm welcome for jealous husband (7)

Definition: jealous husband · deletion + charade

  • hotel → hotel is H in the radio alphabet, and that is the letter going
  • warm → OT warm is hot, now short of its aitch
  • welcome → HELLO the welcome you call out at the door

'Leaving hotel' looks like checking out, and it is where a letter goes missing; the rest is two ordinary words of greeting.

27 across STYLE

Kind steps taken in hearing (5)

Definition: Kind · homophone

  • steps → STYLE a stile is the set of steps built to carry a footpath over a wall, and it is said exactly this way

'Kind steps' reads as one gentle phrase, which is where the definition hides: the first word alone does that job.

28 across SPRUCED UP

Sick pups cured and made better (7,2)

Definition: made better · anagram

  • pups cured → PUPSCURED nine letters, matching the enumeration

'Cured' is the word that wants to be the instruction, since a cure fixes what is sick; it is fodder, and the first word does the shuffling.

1 down SLOW

Measured from top of seam down (4)

Definition: Measured · charade + first letter

  • seam → S the opening letter of seam
  • down → LOW someone feeling down is feeling low, so the two words swap

The surface is a tailor measuring a seam downwards, which makes 'Measured' look like a verb; it is an adjective about pace.

2 down OFFICER

Chip installed in present PC? (7)

Definition: PC? · container

  • present → OFFER to present something is to offer it, and the word opens up after three letters
  • Chip → IC a silicon chip is an integrated circuit, IC for short

The surface is a computer being upgraded; nothing electronic survives the parse, and 'present' is the verb offer rather than a gift.

3 down GREBE

Ogre beginning to eat diver (5)

Definition: diver · hidden word

  • Ogre beginning → GREBE five consecutive letters run across the join between these two words

'Ogre beginning' looks like an instruction to take an initial letter; nothing is being trimmed, and the two words hide a bird whole.

4 down ANKLE SOCK

Losing runs is irritating with stone in footwear (5,4)

Definition: footwear · deletion + charade

  • runs → runs is R on a cricket scorecard, and R is the letter that goes from both words
  • is irritating → ANKLES what rankles irritates; take the R away and six letters remain
  • stone → OCK a stone is a rock, less the same R

Two words each give up an R, and the break between the pieces is not where the enumeration puts it — the letters split after six, not five.

5 down SKATE

Ride on board Ford Model T, splitting southeast (5)

Definition: Ride on board · container + charade

  • southeast → SE SE is the compass abbreviation, and it is the pair being prised apart
  • Ford Model → KA the Ka is a small car Ford sold in Europe
  • T → T the letter is taken from the clue at face value

'Ford Model T' is one famous car in the surface and two separate scraps in the parse: the modern Ka, then the letter itself.

6 down FINGERTIP

Semi-finals leading to German upset – a nail-biting finish? (9)

Definition: a nail-biting finish? · charade

  • Semi-finals → FIN semi- means half, so finals hands over its first three letters
  • German → GER GER is the abbreviation for German or Germany, as on a scoreboard
  • upset → TIP to upset a bucket is to tip it over

The surface is a football tournament and a tense last minute; the definition turns anatomical, and 'nail-biting' goes from anxious to literal.

7 down VISITOR

Head guard smuggles note back for outsider (7)

Definition: outsider · container + reversal

  • Head guard → VISOR a visor is the guard hinged over the front of a helmet, and it opens after three letters
  • note → TI ti is the seventh note of the sol-fa scale, and it is turned round here

The surface is a prison guard passing contraband, so 'note' looks like a scrap of paper; it is the sung syllable instead.

8 down READERSHIP

Gibberish I rephrased for audience (10)

Definition: audience · anagram

  • I rephrased → IREPHRASED the ten letters of these two words, exactly the enumeration asked for

'Rephrased' is the word that looks like the instruction and is really the fodder; the shuffling is called for by the nonsense at the front.

12 down AMERICANOS

Diluted espressos since aroma is horrible (10)

Definition: Diluted espressos · anagram

  • since aroma → SINCEAROMA ten letters out of an everyday conjunction and noun

The whole surface is a complaint about coffee, which is also what the answer is — but only the two middle words carry any letters.

14 down NAMEPLATE

Badge identifying North American politician in Europe (deceased) (9)

Definition: Badge · charade

  • North American → NA NA is the standard abbreviation for North America
  • politician in Europe → MEP an MEP is a Member of the European Parliament, elected to sit in Strasbourg
  • deceased → LATE the late Mr Smith is the deceased one

'In Europe' looks like an instruction to put something inside something else; it is part of the politician's job title, and the chunks simply follow one another.

16 down EARLY DOOR

Yard party joined by other ranks after earl makes special entrance (5,4)

Definition: special entrance · charade

  • earl → EARL a rank of the British peerage, taken at face value
  • Yard → Y y is the abbreviation for a yard of length
  • party → DO a do is a party or celebration in British usage
  • other ranks → OR OR is the army's abbreviation for other ranks, the soldiers below officer level

The clue hands its pieces over back to front, which is what 'after' is for: everything named before it ends up behind the peer.

18 down INAPTLY

Home at apartment, put on lady’s clothing, in a way not fitting (7)

Definition: in a way not fitting · charade + outer letters

  • Home → IN someone at home is in
  • apartment → APT apt. is the short form of apartment on an address
  • lady’s → LY the two letters wrapped round the outside of lady

The surface dresses someone badly; in the parse 'in a way' belongs to the definition, supplying the adverb ending rather than an instruction.

20 down PESTLED

Ground water covering street beside lake beginning to drain (7)

Definition: Ground · container + charade + first letter

  • water → PEE pee is water in the crudest sense, and it splits after two letters
  • street → ST St is the abbreviation on a street sign
  • lake → L L is the map abbreviation for a lake
  • drain → D the opening letter of drain

The surface is a flooded street draining away; the answer belongs in a kitchen, and 'Ground' is the past tense of grind rather than the earth underfoot.

22 down EVENS

Paying equally, tucked into elevenses (5)

Definition: Paying equally · hidden word

  • elevenses → EVENS five consecutive letters sit inside the mid-morning snack, starting after its first two

Elevenses is the British mid-morning snack, so the surface is a picture of eating; the betting sense of the definition is the harder half.

23 down ETHIC

Release A and press middle of stick in complicated cheat code (5)

Definition: code · anagram + deletion + middle letter

  • cheat → CHET cheat with its A let go, leaving four letters
  • middle of stick → I the central letter of the five in stick

The order is unusual: one letter comes out and another goes in before anything is shuffled, and the count only reaches five once both have happened.

24 down HOOP

Form of tinned spaghetti that is rings covered in sauce (4)

Definition: Form of tinned spaghetti · container

  • sauce → HP HP Sauce is the brown sauce on British café tables
  • rings → OO two rings drawn on the page are a pair of Os

Both halves of the surface are British storecupboard food, which hides the seam: one half defines and the other spells.
