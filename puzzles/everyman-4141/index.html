Across

1 across WAYS

Approaches taking years - used to be (4)

Definition: Approaches · container

  • used to be → WAS the past tense of the verb to be, which is what a thing used to be
  • years → Y Y is the standard short form on any form asking for a span of time

The dash makes 'used to be' look like a definition trailing off at the end of the sentence. It is the wordplay, and the one word in front of it is what needs defining.

3 across ECOLOGICAL

Italian author sensible, prone to recycle (10)

Definition: prone to recycle · charade

  • Italian author → ECO Umberto Eco, the novelist who wrote The Name of the Rose
  • sensible → LOGICAL a sensible decision is a logical one, reasoned out rather than felt

Everyman keeps a small stable of short-named writers for the front of a word, and this Italian is the most useful of them all. Worth banking for next time.

10 across INDYREF

Ultimately, Kublai Khan granted country power because of plebiscite (7)

Definition: plebiscite · last letters

  • Kublai Khan granted country power because of → INDYREF the closing letters of seven words in a row, read in the order they stand

The emperor, the grant and the power are a complete decoy: even 'because of' is there for its two final letters. Britain's 2014 vote on Scottish independence went by this nickname in every headline.

11 across ADVANCE

Down payment? Proceed (7)

Definition: Down payment · double definition

  • Down payment → money handed over before the work is done, against what will be owed for it later
  • Proceed → to move forward, the way an army or a queue does

Two words, two senses, and the question mark is an apology: a deposit on a house is not quite the same as money paid ahead of earnings.

12 across SALTPETRE

Steel trap improved ... it's explosive (9)

Definition: explosive · anagram

  • Steel trap → SALTPETRE nine letters of fodder, the count the enumeration asks for

A mind like a steel trap is the phrase the surface leans on, and the ellipsis pretends the sentence is merely trailing off rather than handing its letters over.

13 across UTILE

Finally: bijou ceramic item of use (5)

Definition: of use · charade + last letter

  • bijou → U the letter that word ends on
  • ceramic item → TILE a glazed clay square for a wall or a floor

'Bijou' is estate-agent language for small and charming, which makes it read as part of the description of the ornament. It is only there to lend one letter.

14 across WINDSOR CASTLE

Wild ancestors dancing in royal residence (7,6)

Definition: royal residence · anagram

  • Wild ancestors → WINDSOR CASTLE thirteen letters of fodder, the count the enumeration asks for

'Wild' is the trap: it looks like the shuffling instruction and is really the first word of the material. The word doing the work is the one about the dance.

18 across WIDE-ANGLE LENS

It helps see big picture as well, needing tweaking (4-5,4)

Definition: It helps see big picture · anagram

  • as well, needing → WIDE-ANGLE LENS thirteen letters of fodder, the count the enumeration asks for

'needing tweaking' reads as one phrase about a photograph that wants work, and the split runs straight through it: the first word is material, the second the instruction.

20 across EGRET

Somewhat regrettably, I'm a wading bird (5)

Definition: wading bird · hidden word

  • regrettably → EGRET five consecutive letters beginning at the second, with the tail of the word left where it is

The comma invites you to read 'regrettably' as the setter apologising for the joke, and that is exactly why the letters sitting inside it go unread.

21 across BEER HALLS

Spooner's to try dancing in alehouses (4,5)

Definition: alehouses · spoonerism

  • to try → BEER to try a case in court is to hear it; this word gives up its H and takes a B in exchange
  • dancing → HALLS a ball is a formal dance, and it collects the H that the other word let go of

'Dancing' sits exactly where an anagram indicator would and shuffles nothing at all. Say the plain phrase first — hear, balls — and the swap does the rest.

23 across BRIGAND

Gang welcoming fit bandit (7)

Definition: bandit · container

  • Gang → BAND a band of outlaws is a gang of them
  • fit → RIG to rig a boat out is to fit it with what it needs

'Fit' looks like an adjective sizing up a new recruit, and the word wanted is the chandler's verb instead. Four words, and three of them are doing work.

24 across AT STAKE

In danger, like Joan of Arc (2,5)

Definition: In danger · double definition

  • In danger → the figurative sense, as in 'the whole firm's future is riding on this'
  • like Joan of Arc → she was burnt tied to a post, which is the literal reading of the same phrase

The second half is a picture rather than a synonym, which is the joke: the phrase is figurative in the first three words and horribly literal in the last four.

25 across ROSE-TINTED

Upbeat section of allegro set in Te Deum (4-6)

Definition: Upbeat · hidden word

  • allegro set in Te Deum → ROSE-TINTED ten consecutive letters starting near the end of the first word and running on through the next four

Everything in sight is musical — an Italian tempo marking, a Latin hymn of praise — and none of it is being defined. The italics dress the title up as the subject.

26 across VERT

In Yorkshire, the vicar's flipping green (4)

Definition: green · charade + reversal

  • In Yorkshire, the → T Yorkshire speech clips the definite article down to a single consonant, as in 'off t'moor'
  • vicar → REV a parish vicar is styled the Reverend, shortened to Rev in front of his name

'Flipping' is doing double duty as mild swearing about the clergy. The answer belongs to heraldry, where each colour on a shield has its own French-derived name.

Down

1 down WHIPSAWS

Now, at last, nasty waspish carpenters use these (8)

Definition: carpenters use these · anagram + last letter

  • Now → W the letter that word closes on
  • waspish → WASPISH seven letters of fodder, handed over whole

'Now, at last' reads as the setter's sigh of relief, which is exactly why the single letter buried in it walks past unnoticed. Eight letters of fodder is the confirmation.

2 down YODEL

Primarily yell, oscillating down Eiger lyrically? (5)

Definition: Primarily yell, oscillating down Eiger lyrically? · &lit + first letters

  • yell, oscillating down Eiger lyrically → YODEL the leading letters of five words in a row, read in the order they stand

Read straight it is a singer on an Alpine slope — the Eiger is one of the great Swiss peaks — and read again the same five words hand over their initials.

4 down CAFETERIA

Ice tea and farl cut and served up - here (9)

Definition: here · anagram + deletion

  • Ice tea → ICETEA six letters of fodder, taken exactly as they are written
  • farl → FAR a farl is a quarter of a round of Irish or Scottish soda bread, and it loses its final letter

A farl and a glass of iced tea make a plausible snack, and that is the whole disguise: the words are raw letters, not a menu.

5 down LEASE

I'm asking you - not quietly - for tenure (5)

Definition: tenure · deletion

  • I'm asking you → PLEASE the word you add to a request when you are asking rather than telling
  • quietly → P quietly is piano in music, marked P on the stave, and that is the letter dropped

The dashes stage a row over a flat, with somebody raising their voice. Bank the convention underneath it: musicians mark a soft passage with one letter.

6 down GIVE UP THE GHOST

Betray Marley and go west? (4,2,3,5)

Definition: go west · charade

  • Betray → GIVE UP to give up a fellow conspirator to the police is to betray them
  • Marley → THE GHOST Jacob Marley comes back to Scrooge in chains in A Christmas Carol, so he is the spectre of the story

Marley wants to be the reggae singer, and the question mark is there for the other one: Dickens's dead business partner, who returns as a haunting.

7 down CENTIPEDE

Leggy type baked decent pie (9)

Definition: Leggy type · anagram

  • decent pie → CENTIPEDE nine letters of fodder, the count the enumeration asks for

'Leggy' invites a model in heels and the pie invites a cook. Neither survives the count: nine letters of baking are sitting there waiting.

8 down LIEGES

Tells untruths about - to give an example - Lords (6)

Definition: Lords · container

  • Tells untruths → LIES somebody who tells untruths lies
  • to give an example → EG EG is the Latin exempli gratia, the standard short way of offering an instance

The dashes make the middle phrase read as an aside from a nervous witness, when it is two letters of the build. A liege lord is owed the loyalty; a liege man owes it.

9 down PROPOSED A TOAST

What the best man did, nervously: rose; 'To Dot's Papa!' (8,1,5)

Definition: What the best man did · anagram

  • rose → ROSE four letters of fodder, taken as they stand
  • 'To Dot's Papa!' → TODOTSPAPA ten more letters of fodder, apostrophe ignored, exactly as the little speech is printed

The colon sells the clue as a report of the speech itself, so the words of the toast look like meaning rather than material. Fourteen letters against (8,1,5) settles it.

15 down NO WORRIES

At this moment, top blown off transportation vehicles: not a problem (2,7)

Definition: not a problem · charade + deletion

  • At this moment → NOW the word for the present instant
  • transportation vehicles → LORRIES a lorry is what British English calls a truck, the vehicle that hauls goods by road, and these seven letters go on to lose their first

The colon sets up a report of an accident, roof torn off and nobody hurt. The answer is the Australian shrug that has spread everywhere since.

16 down AGGREGATE

Collect concrete material (9)

Definition: Collect · double definition

  • Collect → the verb for gathering separate things together into a single mass
  • concrete material → the sand, gravel and crushed stone a builder mixes with cement

Three words and no join between them: 'Collect concrete' reads as one instruction to a builder, and it is really two definitions standing side by side.

17 down AS I SEE IT

In my view, like, A-Ha has appeal (2,1,3,2)

Definition: In my view · charade

  • like → AS 'like a bird' and 'as a bird' do the same job in a sentence
  • A-Ha → I SEE aha is the noise you make the moment the penny drops, and so is the phrase it stands for here
  • appeal → IT IT is the old crossword word for sex appeal, as in the 1920s It girl

The Norwegian band is the decoy, hyphen and capitals and all. Say the exclamation aloud instead, and bank the convention: 'appeal' means those two letters most weeks.

19 down MEMBER

Maybe arm your representative (6)

Definition: arm · double definition

  • Maybe arm → a limb of the body, of which one is named and a leg would have done as well
  • your representative → the one you elect to sit in Parliament for your constituency

Four words, and the first is the signal that saves you: it flags a definition by example, so the body part is not the meaning but a specimen of it.

21 down BIDEN

In the auditorium, waitin' for former Pres. (5)

Definition: former Pres. · homophone

  • waitin' → BIDEN to bide your time is to wait, and the clue's dropped g asks for the same clipped form — say it aloud and this is what you write

Both words wear the same missing g, which is the setter pointing at sound rather than spelling. The full stop after 'Pres' is an abbreviation, not the end of the sentence.

22 down LEAVE

Idyllic spot? Very. Everyman's beginning to follow. Go away! (5)

Definition: Go away! · charade + first letter

  • Idyllic spot → LEA a lea is a meadow or stretch of open grassland, the word poets reach for
  • Very → V V is the standard short form of very, as in 'v. good'
  • Everyman's → E the setter signs himself Everyman, and only the letter that name begins on is wanted

The full stops chop the sentence into an estate agent's patter, so each instruction reads as praise. Everyman puts his own byline into a clue most weeks: expect it.
