Across

1 across POLITICIANS

Social pint I arranged for elected officials (11)

Definition: elected officials · anagram

  • Social pint I → SOCIALPINTI anagram fodder, eleven letters, and the lone I counts as one of them

The surface is a friendly evening in the pub, which is exactly why the fodder hides: three words that read as a sentence have to be seen as a bag of letters instead.

9 across OPERATE

Take page from religious leader facing class act (7)

Definition: act · deletion + charade

  • religious leader → POPE the Pope leads the Roman Catholic church, and his title is the word a letter comes out of
  • Take page → P is the standard abbreviation for page, and it is the letter removed from the front
  • class → RATE to rate a thing is to class it, to put it in a rank

'Facing class act' invites you to read a compliment about a performer. The class is a verb of ranking, and the act on its own is the definition.

10 across SUCTION

Row with son over right way to bond (7)

Definition: way to bond · substitution

  • son → S S is the usual abbreviation for son, the one a family tree uses
  • Row → RUCTION a ruction is a noisy quarrel or disturbance, which is what a row is here
  • right → R is the standard abbreviation for right, and it is the letter being covered up

'Row' is the quarrel, not the line of seats or the business with oars, and the swap is a neat piece of misdirection: a family word takes a direction's place.

11 across BOSSA NOVA

Brazilian moves head with a radiant body exuding energy (5,4)

Definition: Brazilian moves · charade

  • head → BOSS the head of a firm is its boss, the person in charge
  • a → A the article is lifted straight out of the clue as a single letter
  • radiant body exuding energy → NOVA a star that flares up and pours out light and energy is a nova

The astronomy does the disguising: 'radiant body exuding energy' sounds like a physics textbook describing the sun, and it is the name of a flaring star.

12 across SHOYU

Almost roar consuming last drop of spicy sauce (5)

Definition: sauce · deletion + container + last letter

  • Almost roar → SHOU to SHOUT is to roar, and the word is wanted with its final T lopped off
  • last drop of spicy → Y the final letter of spicy, which drops into the gap left before the U

The surface is a big cat prowling round the kitchen. Everything before the last word is instruction, and the last word alone is the definition.

13 across FOOL

Funny man and straight man, one overshadowed by other at first (4)

Definition: Funny man · substitution

  • straight man → FOIL a foil is the straight man of a double act, the one who feeds the jokes
  • one → I is the Roman numeral for one, and it is the letter being blotted out
  • other at first → O the opening letter of 'other', which moves into the vacated place

Both members of a double act are standing in the clue doing opposite jobs: the comic is the definition, his partner hands over the letters. That symmetry is the disguise.

14 across HOME TRUTHS

Harsh words hurt the most when blasted without hint of tenderness (4,6)

Definition: Harsh words · anagram + deletion + first letter

  • hurt the most → HURTTHEMOS anagram fodder, ten letters once one T has been taken out
  • hint of tenderness → T opens the word tenderness, and that is the letter withheld from the mixture

The whole sentence reads as a description of cruelty, which is why the anagram sits so quietly — 'hurt the most' looks like a phrase, not a bag of letters.

16 across REFOCUSSED

Sporting official swore over nothing and concentrated again (10)

Definition: concentrated again · charade + container

  • Sporting official → REF the referee who runs a match, shortened the way a commentator says it
  • nothing → O the round letter is the crossword's standard nothing, borrowed from the figure zero
  • swore → CUSSED to cuss is to swear, and the past tense supplies six letters

The definition is a double bluff. 'Concentrated again' reads as the chemist's word, the liquid boiled down a second time, and it is the everyday sense about paying attention.

19 across YETI

Still face of impassive mountain dweller (4)

Definition: mountain dweller · charade + first letter

  • Still → YET 'still waiting' and 'yet to arrive' say the same thing, so the two words swap freely
  • face of impassive → I the letter impassive begins with, added on the end

'Still face' looks like one phrase describing an expression giving nothing away, and the split falls between those two words rather than after them.

20 across TORSO

Body temperature, roughly (5)

Definition: Body · charade

  • temperature → T T is the scientific abbreviation for temperature, as on a weather chart
  • roughly → ORSO 'a mile or so' means roughly a mile, so those two little words stand in for it

Three words and no joinery: the comma is the only sign of where the definition stops. 'Roughly' is not an instruction here — it is a phrase to be written out as letters.

21 across EIDERDOWN

Spread or widened out (9)

Definition: Spread · anagram

  • or widened → ORWIDENED anagram fodder, nine letters, the conjunction included

Four words reading as a single idea about something being stretched, which is what makes a short anagram hard: nothing in the sentence looks like an instruction.

23 across MANHUNT

Student initially missing from crash joining staff in search (7)

Definition: search · deletion + charade

  • joining staff → MAN to man a desk is to staff it, the verb behind 'manned'
  • crash → HUNT a SHUNT is a minor crash between vehicles, and it arrives here with its front letter already gone
  • Student initially → S opens the word student, and that is the letter taken away

The surface is a newsroom sentence about a student who has disappeared, which is why 'crash' reads as an event rather than as a word with a letter knocked off it.

24 across MUTAGEN

Waving gun at me? That could be dangerous (7)

Definition: That could be dangerous · anagram

  • gun at me → GUNATME anagram fodder, seven letters, taken as they stand in the clue

The danger in the surface is a weapon pointed at the setter; the danger in the answer is chemical, a substance that damages DNA. The question mark warns you the definition only describes.

25 across EVANESCENCE

European vice-president takes in view when cycling for fleeting moment (11)

Definition: fleeting moment · charade + container + cycling

  • European → E E is the standard abbreviation for European
  • vice-president → VANCE JD Vance is the American vice-president, and his surname opens up before its last two letters
  • view → ESCEN a view is a SCENE, with the letter at its end rolled round to the front

The surface reads as a line of political news, and the clue expects you to know a serving vice-president by surname alone — the sort of name that dates a puzzle fast.

Down

1 down PRESS CONFERENCE

Where corps go for release hoping to break something? (5,10)

Definition: Where corps go for release hoping to break something? · cryptic definition

  • Where corps go for release → the surface suggests soldiers stood down for leave; a corps is also the body of reporters accredited to a government, and their release is the printed statement handed out
  • hoping to break something? → nothing is smashed here: a journalist breaks a story, which means getting it out before the rest of the room does

There is no separable wordplay to find — the whole sentence is one description in military dress. Both 'corps' and 'release' change trade between the two readings.

2 down LLAMA

Vietnam allegedly holding back source of wool (5)

Definition: source of wool · hidden word + reversal

  • Vietnam allegedly → LLAMA five consecutive letters straddle the join between these two words, and they only make sense read right to left

'Vietnam allegedly' does two jobs at once: it sounds like a line from a news bulletin, and it is the only stretch of the clue where five letters sit in a row.

3 down TREMOLO

Notes that repeat poolroom secrets regularly put up (7)

Definition: Notes that repeat · alternate letters + reversal

  • poolroom secrets regularly → OLOMERT every second letter of these two words, starting from the second, gives seven letters that still need turning round

The surface is a seedy tale of what gets said around a pool table, and the entire instruction is carried by two innocent-looking words at the end of the clue.

4 down CASCADE

Sudden surge by scoundrel breaking cover (7)

Definition: Sudden surge · container

  • cover → CASE a case is what covers and protects a thing, and this one opens up just before its final letter
  • scoundrel → CAD a cad is the old-fashioned word for a man who behaves badly, standard crossword currency

'Breaking cover' reads as a fugitive making a dash for it. In the parse the cover is a rigid box and the scoundrel is three letters crosswords keep permanently on hand.

5 down ANCESTRY

Historic family risks abandoning church before trial (8)

Definition: Historic family · deletion + charade

  • risks abandoning church → ANCES CHANCES are risks, and CH is the standard abbreviation for church, walking off the front
  • trial → TRY a try is an attempt, what you give a thing when you trial it

'Risks' has to be read as the plural noun and not the verb — that final S is what makes the first chunk long enough — and the church is only its two-letter abbreviation.

6 down SLIP OF THE TONGUE

Misstep that could make shoe feel uncomfortable? (4,2,3,6)

Definition: Misstep that could make shoe feel uncomfortable? · cryptic definition

  • Misstep → the straight sense, and a narrow one: a blunder made in speech rather than on a staircase
  • that could make shoe feel uncomfortable? → a shoe has a tongue, the flap that sits under the laces, and a tongue that slips out of place is what makes the shoe rub

Both halves of the phrase change trade in the second reading: the tongue stops being the one in your mouth, and the slip becomes a physical one.

7 down NOT BEFORE TIME

Fashion to benefit Rome at last (3,6,4)

Definition: at last · anagram

  • to benefit Rome → TOBENEFITROME anagram fodder, thirteen letters including the little word at the front

The surface promises an Italian charity gala, and the trap is 'Fashion': it looks like the noun about clothes at the head of the sentence, and it is a verb of making.

8 down UNQUESTIONING

Certain merger stopped by crusade against Dutch bank (13)

Definition: Certain · container + charade

  • merger → UNION a union is a merger of two bodies into one, and it splits open after its first two letters
  • crusade → QUEST a crusade is a long single-minded quest, the campaign thought of as a journey
  • Dutch bank → ING ING is the big Amsterdam-based bank, the orange lion of shirt sponsorship and cash machines

The surface is a page of corporate news — a merger held up by a protest campaign — and every one of those business words is really a separate scrap of letters.

15 down SCROFULA

The king’s evil son abused our calf (8)

Definition: The king’s evil · charade + anagram

  • son → S S is the standard abbreviation for son, the same one a family tree uses
  • our calf → CROFULA seven letters shaken out of their order, following straight on from the S

'Calf' is the trap: it looks like the young animal or the muscle in a leg, and it is doing neither job — it is seven letters and nothing else.

17 down SPECTRE

Outspoken music producer is transparent has-been? (7)

Definition: transparent has-been? · homophone

  • music producer → SPECTRE Phil Spector was the record producer behind the 1960s Wall of Sound, and his surname is said exactly like the seven letters wanted

The definition carries two jokes at once: a spectre really is see-through, and a has-been is here somebody who has been rather than a faded celebrity.

18 down ENDEMIC

Local experimental cinema snubbed over regressive editor (7)

Definition: Local · anagram + deletion + reversal

  • cinema snubbed → CINEM CINEMA loses the letter at its end, leaving five to go into the mix
  • regressive editor → DE ED is the standard abbreviation for editor, taken here the other way round

The surface is a review of an arthouse picture house, and 'Local' is not describing the cinema at all — it is the definition, sitting on its own at the front.

22 down RAT ON

Public official almost set up shop (3,2)

Definition: shop · deletion + reversal

  • Public official almost → NOTAR a notary is the public official who witnesses signatures, and 'almost' stops the word one letter short

'Set up shop' is a phrase begging to be read whole, and that is the trap: 'set up' is the instruction and 'shop' is the definition, in its slang sense.
