Across

7 across BARN OWLS

A better plan – finally – to stop game hunters (4,4)

Definition: hunters · container + last letters

  • game → BOWLS the game played on a green, which is then prised open
  • A better plan → ARN the closing letter of each of the three words, in the order they come

'game hunters' begs to be read as one phrase about safari. Only the last word is the definition; the game is the one played on grass.

9 across HYENAS

Bears controlling hunger – they eat carrion (6)

Definition: they eat carrion · container

  • Bears → HAS to bear a resemblance is to have one
  • hunger → YEN a yen is a craving, a hunger for something

'Bears' looks like the animals sharing the sentence with the answer. It is the verb, and the dash keeps the nature-note disguise going.

10 across GANG

Crew fitting angle brackets (4)

Definition: Crew · hidden word

  • fitting angle → GANG the letters run straight across the join between the two words

'angle brackets' is a real thing in typing and computing, so the phrase reads as one unit and hides the seam it is pointing at.

11 across REASONABLE

Just dread spending days working in dark (10)

Definition: Just · charade + deletion + container

  • dread spending days → REA D is the short form of day, and the plural means dread gives up both of its D's
  • dark → SABLE sable is the heraldic word for black
  • working → ON a machine that is working has been switched on

The surface is a night shift nobody wants. 'Just' does the definition's work in the very first word, which is where a solver least expects it.

12 across COWBOY

Rogue bully by boxing ring (6)

Definition: Rogue · charade + container

  • bully → COW to cow someone is to bully them into submission
  • by → BY the little word taken at face value, then split open
  • ring → O a ring is drawn as a circle, which is the letter O

'boxing ring' is one thing in the surface and two instructions in the parse — nothing here is about the fight game.

14 across ENTREATY

Two nurses ignoring half of my request (8)

Definition: request · charade + deletion

  • Two nurses → EN the first of the pair is EN, Britain's enrolled nurse, a standing crossword abbreviation
  • nurses → TREAT the second is the verb: to nurse a patient is to treat one
  • my → Y the M is the half that goes

'Two nurses' asks for two different words for the same job, one an abbreviation and one a verb — a common way to hide a short charade.

15 across BREAK A RECORD

Become the best by baking bread or a little rock cake (5,1,6)

Definition: Become the best · anagram + first letter

  • bread or → BREADOR seven letters straight into the mix
  • rock → R only the opening letter is wanted from this one
  • cake → CAKE four more letters for the mix

'rock cake' is a real bun, which is what makes the split so hard to see: those two words belong to different parts of the wordplay.

20 across CATS-EYES

Watches musical’s opening night – that’s when these things shine! (4-4)

Definition: night – that’s when these things shine! · charade

  • musical’s → CATS the Lloyd Webber show that ran for decades in the West End
  • Watches → EYES eyes watch, so the organ and the verb share a word

The possessive does the assembling: the musical's watches. In the surface, 'opening night' is a first performance, and the parse splits that phrase in two.

22 across BEETLE

Live in Le Mans – and the VW? (6)

Definition: VW? · charade

  • Live → BE to live is to be
  • and → ET the French for and
  • the → LE the masculine French article, the one standing in the town's own name

The instruction hides inside a place name, and the 24-hour race there keeps the whole surface pointing at cars.

23 across PLUM TOMATO

Cartoon dog bites old woman clutching cat food in tins? (4,6)

Definition: food in tins? · container

  • Cartoon dog → PLUTO Disney's dog, Mickey's pet, five letters that then get prised apart
  • old woman → MA the familiar word for mother
  • cat → TOM a tomcat is the male of the species

'cat food in tins' is a supermarket shelf and reads as one phrase; only the last three words are the definition, and the cat belongs to the wordplay.

24 across LACE

Large hole in one tie (4)

Definition: tie · charade

  • Large → L the clothing size, and the standard abbreviation for it
  • hole in one → ACE a hole in one at golf is an ace

'Large hole' looks like one idea and 'in one tie' like another; the break really falls between the size and the golfer's shot.

25 across ANTLER

Part of stag party in strip club getting wrecked in just seconds (6)

Definition: Part of stag · second letters

  • party in → AN the second letter of each of these two words, in order
  • strip club → TL the same again, two words further along
  • getting wrecked → ER and the second letters of the final two close it off

'strip club getting wrecked' reads like an anagram waiting to happen, and 'stag party' looks like one phrase — but the definition stops at 'stag'.

26 across DRAG RACE

Drive a good line in test of acceleration (4,4)

Definition: test of acceleration · charade

  • Drive → DR the street sign's abbreviation, as written on an envelope
  • a → A the clue's own letter, taken as it stands
  • good → G G is the standard short form, as in a report-card grade
  • line → RACE a race is a line of descent, a breed or a stock

'Drive' is not the verb the surface wants but the word on a street sign, shortened, and the clue is assembled one small piece at a time.

1 down CATACOMB

Combat manoeuvres – keeping watch regularly in caves? (8)

Definition: caves? · anagram + alternate letters

  • Combat → COMBAT six letters lifted straight off the clue, ready to be rearranged
  • watch → AC taken at every other letter: the second and the fourth

The surface is a military exercise, so 'Combat' reads as part of the picture rather than as the raw material. It is the raw material.

2 down SNUG

Tight biceps after lifting (4)

Definition: Tight · reversal

  • biceps → GUNS 'guns' is gym slang for a well-developed pair of biceps

The surface is a weights session, where lifting is what you do rather than what you do to a word.

3 down QWERTY KEYBOARD

Question weaker body? Try exercising. (One probably sits with a computer all day) (6,8)

Definition: One probably sits with a computer all day · charade + anagram

  • Question → Q the standard short form, as in Q and A
  • weaker body? Try → WERTYKEYBOARD thirteen letters that come back in a different arrangement

'One' pushes you towards a person — a coder, a gamer — and the definition turns out to be the object on the desk instead.

4 down THROW THE BOOK AT

This suggests he took bat for discipline (5,3,4,2)

Definition: discipline · charade + anagram

  • This suggests → THROW the opening word is the instruction as well as part of the phrase
  • he took bat → THEBOOKAT the same nine letters, hurled into a new order

A self-referential trick: the phrase carries the very verb that scrambles the rest of it, while 'he took bat' keeps the cricket surface going.

5 down SEA ANEMONE

Imagine taking group of drivers north on M1 to see Coral’s relative? (3,7)

Definition: Coral’s relative? · container + charade

  • Imagine → SEE to see something happening is to imagine it
  • group of drivers → AA the AA is Britain's Automobile Association, the motoring club that comes out to broken-down cars
  • north → N the compass point's usual single letter
  • M1 → MONE the motorway gives up its letter and then its number, spelled out

In a down clue 'on' means sitting on top of, so the motorway goes underneath. The surface is a drive up the M1 to the coast.

6 down WALLET

Keep everything in pathetic pocketbook (6)

Definition: pocketbook · container

  • pathetic → WET in British use a wet person is feeble and spineless
  • everything → ALL the plain three-letter word for the lot

The surface reads as a sad little purse. 'Pathetic' is not describing anything: it is supplying a schoolyard insult to build on.

8 down STATEN ISLAND

Tesla isn’t working with NYC borough (6,6)

Definition: NYC borough · anagram

  • Tesla isn’t → TESLAISNT nine letters as printed, the apostrophe counting for nothing
  • with → AND 'with' joins two things exactly as 'and' does, and those three letters go into the mix too

'isn't working' looks like the signal — a stalled electric car — and it is not: 'isn't' is material, and the joining word after it is material as well.

13 down BREAST MILK

Mother might express this clumsily – be smart and kind (6,4)

Definition: Mother might express this · anagram + charade

  • be smart → BREASTM seven letters that come back in a different order
  • kind → ILK your ilk is your kind, the sort of people you belong among

'be smart and kind' sounds like advice a mother would hand out, which is the whole disguise: the two halves do quite different jobs.
