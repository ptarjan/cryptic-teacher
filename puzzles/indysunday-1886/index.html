Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,886

Across

1 across STRAIGHT

Frank works in a gallery, retired in a sense (8)

Definition: Frank · container + reversal

  • in a sense → SIGHT one of the five senses, and this word parts after its first letter to take another piece in
  • works in a gallery → TRA the works hanging in a gallery are art, arriving back to front

'Frank' looks like the name of the man who works in the gallery, and it is the definition standing at the front of the clue all along.

6 across SIMPLY

Only son with acne wanting face wiped (6)

Definition: Only · charade + deletion

  • son → S s is the genealogist's abbreviation for son
  • with acne → IMPLY someone with acne is pimply, and that word turns up here with its front letter gone
  • face → P the face of a word is the letter at its front, and this is the one that goes

'Only son' reads as one phrase about a family, and its two words belong to opposite halves of the clue: one is the definition, the other a single letter.

9 across MIDGET

Wee half-way through good Spielberg film (6)

Definition: Wee · charade

  • half-way through → MID the point half-way through anything is its mid-point
  • good → G g is the standard abbreviation for good, as on a school report
  • Spielberg film → ET E.T. is the Spielberg film of 1982 about the stranded alien

'Wee' is Scots for tiny, the sense wanted rather than the nursery one. E.T. is worth remembering: the crossword's favourite two-letter film.

10 across RAMBLING

King on walkabout making little sense (8)

Definition: making little sense · charade

  • King → R R is rex, Latin for king, the letter a monarch signs after their name
  • on walkabout → AMBLING somebody on a walkabout is ambling about at an easy pace

A royal walkabout is a real fixture of British life, which is what makes 'King' look like part of the picture rather than a single letter of wordplay.

11 across AT ALL HOURS

Where we live, every hotel opens annoyingly late (2,3,5)

Definition: annoyingly late · container

  • Where we live → ATOURS 'at ours' is the everyday British way of saying at our place — come back to ours — and it parts in the middle
  • every → ALL every one of them is all of them
  • hotel → H H is hotel in the NATO alphabet

'Where we live' wants to be a country or a street name. It is the colloquial phrase instead, and 'hotel' is a letter rather than a building.

13 across MISS

Wish you were with younger woman? (4)

Definition: Wish you were · double definition

  • Wish you were → the verb: to feel somebody's absence and want them back
  • younger woman → the courtesy title put before an unmarried woman's name, and the noun for a girl

The first half is quoting the postcard — wish you were here — and the two halves run on as one sentence, with nothing at all marking the seam between them.

14 across WHITE CHRISTMAS

Noel made pretty clever electronics during joint postgrad courses (5,9)

Definition: Noel made pretty · charade + container

  • joint → WRIST the joint at the base of your hand, and it opens after its first letter
  • clever electronics → HITECH clever electronics are hi-tech, the two halves run together
  • postgrad courses → MAS MAs are Master of Arts degrees, courses taken after a first degree

The surface has electronics students on a joint degree. The joint wanted is the one in your arm, and what makes a Noel pretty is snow.

17 across PSYCHOANALYSIS

Doctor says no physical treatment for problems (14)

Definition: treatment for problems · anagram

  • says no physical → SAYSNOPHYSICAL fourteen letters handed over, the full length of the entry

'Doctor' at the head of a medical surface is doing the shuffling rather than attending anybody, and the treatment defined is the talking kind, with nothing physical about it.

19 across PAIR

Broadcast beginning with piano duet (4)

Definition: duet · charade

  • piano → P p is the mark in a score telling the player to play softly, from the Italian
  • Broadcast → AIR to broadcast a programme is to air it

'Broadcast' is exactly the sort of word that announces a homophone, and here it is a plain synonym. The piano is a marking on sheet music, not the instrument.

21 across ROUNDABOUT

With diversions, outdo urban one-way system (10)

Definition: one-way system · anagram

  • outdo urban → OUTDOURBAN ten letters handed over, exactly the length of the entry

The whole surface is traffic management, which hides the fact that two ordinary words are simply being taken to pieces. 'Outdo' is the last word you would think to shuffle.

23 across RACEGOER

Attendee at meeting developing career, full of pep (8)

Definition: Attendee at meeting · anagram + container

  • career → CAREER six letters handed over to be reordered
  • pep → GO go is pep or energy — there is plenty of go in him

'Meeting' is the racing sense, a day's card of races at one course, and 'developing career' is an appraisal-form phrase quietly doing letter work.

24 across CHASTE

Modest expedition led by Conservative (6)

Definition: Modest · charade

  • Conservative → C C is the standard abbreviation for the Conservative party on a ballot paper
  • expedition → HASTE expedition in its old sense of speed — doing a thing with expedition is doing it fast

'Expedition' looks like a journey and means promptness, the sense that survives in 'expedite'. The surface is a Tory-led trek and neither half of it is true.

25 across HEATED

Angry husband very happy when learner driver vanishes (6)

Definition: Angry · charade + deletion

  • husband → H H is the standard abbreviation for husband, as on a form
  • very happy → EATED very happy is elated, and that word arrives with its L missing
  • learner driver → L the L-plate a learner driver carries, and this is the letter that goes

The surface is a husband delighted to see the back of a learner on the road, which keeps the deletion out of sight; the definition is a single word at the front.

26 across LEMONADE

Article in French newspaper that's refreshingly sharp (8)

Definition: that's refreshingly sharp · container

  • French newspaper → LEMONDE Le Monde is the Paris daily, its title the French for 'the world'
  • Article → A the indefinite article, which in wordplay is simply the letter

'Article in French' wants to be LE, the French for 'the'. The article here is the English one, and it goes inside the newspaper's title.

2 down TWIN TOWNS

Fool admits going round new places sharing culture (4,5)

Definition: places sharing culture · charade + container

  • Fool → TWIT a twit is a fool, and these four letters open after the third
  • new → N n is the standard abbreviation for new, as on a product label
  • admits → OWNS to admit to a thing is to own up to it

'Admits' is placed to look like the instruction to put something inside, and it is a piece of the answer in its own right; a different phrase does the enclosing.

3 down ANGEL

Nurse fish with twisted tail (5)

Definition: Nurse · reversal

  • fish → ANG to fish is to angle, and the first three letters of that verb stay as they are
  • tail → EL the tail of that same verb is its last two letters, which arrive the other way round

'Fish' is a verb here, the sport of angling, not a creature — and the setter is enjoying the fact that the answer names a fish too.

4 down GUT

Get everything out of tower that's going up (3)

Definition: Get everything out of · reversal

  • tower → TUG a tug tows barges and liners, so it is a tower — nothing to do with buildings

'Tower' wants to be something tall with a spire on it. The small boat that does the towing is the reading that fits, and it is one of the oldest dodges in the crossword.

5 down THROUGH

Hard, taking hour from start to finish (7)

Definition: from start to finish · container

  • Hard → TOUGH a hard problem is a tough one, and this word splits after its first letter
  • hour → HR hr is the abbreviation printed on a timetable

The comma after 'Hard' makes it look like a remark aimed at the solver, so the definition seems to have started already. It is the wordplay, and the definition is the phrase at the end.

6 down SEMISKILLED

Somewhat inexpert heads of Spectre each botched assassinations? (11)

Definition: Somewhat inexpert · first letters + charade

  • Spectre each → SE the opening letter of each of these two words, taken in the order they stand
  • botched assassinations → MISKILLED killings carried out badly, using the prefix MIS- that English puts in front of anything done wrongly

Two traps in one clue: 'Somewhat' usually announces a hidden word and here belongs to the definition, and 'botched' shuffles nothing — it builds a MIS- word.

7 down MALEMUTES

Guy silences husky relatives (9)

Definition: husky relatives · charade

  • Guy → MALE a guy is a male, the man rather than the rope holding a tent up
  • silences → MUTES to silence a sound is to mute it

'Husky' sits there as an adjective describing a hoarse guy, and it is the dog. The breed is more often spelt malamute; both spellings are in the dictionaries.

8 down LANDS

Estate Volvo's unique character, alongside Sierra (5)

Definition: Estate · charade

  • Volvo's unique character → L the only letter appearing once in VOLVO, since the V and the O each turn up twice
  • alongside → AND putting one thing alongside another is putting this and that together
  • Sierra → S S in the NATO alphabet, where each letter has a name that carries over a radio

Volvo and Sierra are both cars and an estate is a body style, so the whole surface is a forecourt. Not one of those three words is doing what it appears to.

12 down HAEMORRHOID

Sorting out her hair and mood, a pain in the arse? (11)

Definition: a pain in the arse · anagram

  • her hair and mood → HERHAIRMOOD eleven letters handed over whole, in an order that will not survive

The surface is a hairdresser soothing a difficult client, and the definition is that idiom taken absolutely literally — which is what the question mark owns up to.

15 down INCORRECT

Wrong since exposed by cycling clergyman (9)

Definition: Wrong · charade + middle letters + cycling

  • since → INC the three letters in the middle of the word, once the S and the E at its ends have come off
  • clergyman → ORRECT a rector is the priest in charge of a parish, and his six letters roll round until the last two lead

Two unusual instructions in one clue and neither is an anagram: nothing here is jumbled at all, the letters simply start from a different place.

16 down ASSAULTED

Attacked when seconds at duel ordered (9)

Definition: Attacked · anagram

  • when → AS as means when — 'as I arrived' is 'when I arrived'
  • seconds → S s stands for second in any timing, and setters use it for the plural as readily as the singular
  • at duel → ATDUEL six more letters, the clue's own words taken exactly as they stand

A duel's seconds are the friends who attend the duellists and check the pistols, which is why the surface reads as a scene rather than a heap of letters.

18 down NEUTRAL

Nervous about Tesla's gearbox position breaking transmission (7)

Definition: gearbox position breaking transmission · container

  • Nervous → NEURAL nervous in the anatomical sense, of the nerves, and this word opens after its third letter
  • Tesla → T T is the symbol for the tesla, the unit of magnetic field strength

Everything in the surface is a car — Tesla, gearbox, transmission — and the one word doing wordplay is 'nervous', in its medical rather than its anxious sense.

20 down APACE

Secretary brought over card with dispatch (5)

Definition: with dispatch · reversal + charade

  • Secretary → AP a PA is a personal assistant, the office secretary, and the pair arrives the other way round
  • card → ACE the ace is the highest card in the pack

'With dispatch' looks like a link phrase — sent off with something — and it is the definition. Dispatch here means promptness, a sense that survives mainly in this phrase.

22 down BRAIN

Child in Scotland showing AI lowered mental ability (5)

Definition: mental ability · anagram

  • Child in Scotland → BAIRN a bairn is a child in Scots, the everyday word north of the border

Nothing is jumbled at random: the two letters named move down two places in the column and everything above them shuffles up to fill the gap.

24 down CAM

Turning and pushing piece starts to annoy Magnus Carlsen terribly (3)

Definition: Turning and pushing piece · first letters + anagram

  • annoy Magnus Carlsen → AMC the letter that begins each of these three words, taken in the order they stand

Magnus Carlsen is the world chess champion, which is what makes 'piece' read as a chessman. The piece defined is a piece of machinery.
