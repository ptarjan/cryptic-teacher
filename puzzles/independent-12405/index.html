Across

5 across TINAMOU

Can love tailless bird (7)

Definition: bird · charade + deletion

  • Can → TIN a tin is a can, the food container
  • love → AMOU amour is French for love, and its closing R is the tail that goes

Three short words and no obvious instruction: 'tailless' is the only signal, and it is pointing at a French word rather than an English one. The bird itself is a South American ground-dweller, a cousin of the rheas.

7 across MATTER

Count time mother's without (6)

Definition: Count · container

  • mother → MATER mater is Latin for mother and British public-school slang for one's mum; it opens up after two letters
  • time → T T is the standard abbreviation for time, as in the physics formula

Two traps in six words: 'Count' is the verb and not the nobleman, and 'without' is the archaic outside-of sense rather than an instruction to take something away.

9 across REAP

Gather agent's hiding 10% from accountant (4)

Definition: Gather · container + first letter

  • agent → REP a rep is an agent, the sales or theatrical kind, and it opens after two letters
  • 10% from accountant → A a tenth of a ten-letter word is one letter, and it is taken from the start

The surface is an agent taking commission, and ten per cent is exactly what an agent takes — the joke is that the percentage is arithmetic done on the word, not on the money.

10 across ECUMENICAL

Clue (1ac) solved - people involved promoting co-operation (10)

Definition: promoting co-operation · anagram

  • Clue (1ac) → CLUEIAC there is no 1 across in this grid, so the bracket is not a cross-reference: it is Roman one plus the abbreviation for across, tacked on to the four letters before it
  • people → MEN men are people, three more letters for the mix

Look at the numbering before you go hunting: this grid has no 1 across, which is the fair warning that the bracket is letters. Ecumenical began as a church word for unity between denominations.

11 across ASSIST

Dolly Parton's behind first benefit (6)

Definition: benefit · charade

  • Dolly Parton's behind → ASS the American word for a backside; naming a Tennessee country singer is how the clue asks for the American one rather than the British
  • first → IST first written as an ordinal is 1st, and Roman one plus that two-letter ending spells it

The surface is rude and the parse is not. Parton is in the clue for her nationality rather than her fame, and 1st is a standard three letters once the numeral is read as Roman.

12 across BASALT

Rock chick shortly gets back in club (6)

Definition: Rock · deletion + reversal + container

  • club → BAT a bat is a club you swing, and it splits after two letters
  • chick shortly → LAS a chick is informally a lass, here docked of its final letter and then turned round

'Rock chick' reads as one phrase and is two jobs: the definition stops after the first word, and the chick is fodder. The music-club surface is entirely a decoy.

15 across KNITTING NEEDLES

Garment manufacturers designed kit with tennis legend (8,7)

Definition: Garment manufacturers · anagram

  • kit → KIT three letters of fodder, straight from the clue
  • tennis legend → TENNISLEGEND twelve more letters — no particular champion is wanted, just the two words themselves

'Tennis legend' begs you to name a player, and Borg, Laver and Evert all suit the surface; the clue only ever wanted the twelve letters of the phrase.

19 across HACK IT

Cope with success that's netted Bill a thousand (4,2)

Definition: Cope · container + charade

  • success → HIT a hit is a success, a record or a show that lands, and it opens after its first letter
  • Bill → AC a bill is an account, and a/c is the standard abbreviation for one
  • a thousand → K K is a thousand, the kilo prefix used of salaries and file sizes

'Cope with success' reads as one phrase, and the definition stops at the first word. The capital letter on Bill is the other trap: it is a piece of paper, not a person.

22 across TETRAD

Turkey meal comes round with dinners originally for four (6)

Definition: four · container + charade + first letter

  • meal → TEA tea is a meal in Britain, the one eaten in the early evening, and it opens after two letters
  • Turkey → TR TR is Turkey's international vehicle registration code, the oval sticker on the back of a car
  • dinners originally → D the first letter of dinners

Turkey, meal and dinners in one sentence make a Christmas picture, and not one of them is food: a country code, a container and an initial.

24 across CANIS MAJOR

Wearing headphones, I stick around for late-starting Sky feature (5,5)

Definition: Sky feature · container + reversal + deletion + charade

  • headphones → CANS cans is studio slang for headphones, and the word opens before its last letter
  • I → I the pronoun is taken at face value, one letter lifted from the clue
  • stick → JAM to jam is to stick fast, and these three letters are then read backwards
  • for late-starting → OR the word for, without the F it starts with

'Sky feature' is all the clue says about the Great Dog, the constellation that carries Sirius. 'Late-starting' is the device worth keeping: a word that begins one letter in.

26 across HEAR

Pick up from the armourer's (4)

Definition: Pick up · hidden word

  • the armourer's → HEAR the letters run consecutively across the gap between these two words

Nothing announces the hiding place but 'from', which is why this one works: the span runs straight over a space, and the eye reads spaces as boundaries.

27 across EXTORT

To demand money with threats no longer wrong (6)

Definition: To demand money with threats · charade

  • no longer → EX an ex- anything is what it no longer is: ex-wife, ex-president
  • wrong → TORT a tort is a civil wrong in law, the sort you sue over rather than prosecute

The definition runs to five words and the wordplay to two, which is a shape worth recognising: when a clue reads like a dictionary entry, the parse is hiding at the end.

28 across CANVASS

May very stupid person attempt to influence voters? (7)

Definition: attempt to influence voters · charade

  • May → CAN may and can are the two words for being permitted
  • very → V v is the abbreviation for very, as in v good
  • stupid person → ASS an ass is a fool, by way of the donkey

The capital M wants to be a month or a prime minister, and it is only the plain verb 'may'. Canvassing is the doorstep work candidates do before polling day.

Down

1 down STRESSING

Lover in government leaving note - feeling the pressure (9)

Definition: feeling the pressure · charade + deletion

  • Lover → MISTRESS the word for a man's lover on the side, and it is about to lose its first two letters
  • note → MI is a note of the sol-fa scale, and those are the two letters that go
  • in → IN the little word is taken at face value, two letters straight from the clue
  • government → G G is the abbreviation for government, the G of G-men

The surface promises a Westminster affair, and the dash is where it stops: everything before it is letters. Sol-fa gives a setter do, re, mi, fa, so, la and te as ready-made two-letter scraps.

2 down END POINT

Terminus open in Telford deserted surprisingly (3,5)

Definition: Terminus · anagram + outer letters

  • open in → OPENIN six letters of fodder, taken straight from the clue
  • Telford → TD the outside letters of the Shropshire new town, its middle abandoned

The surface is a train leaving a Midlands town, which is exactly what the definition wants you to picture; Telford is only there for its first and last letters.

3 down GURU

Teacher's wig sent up by third of students (4)

Definition: Teacher · reversal + charade + third letter

  • wig → RUG a rug is slang for a hairpiece, and these three letters climb the entry
  • third of students → U counting three letters into students lands on this one

Wig, teacher and students all belong to the same schoolroom picture, and only one of the three words is the definition — the other two are hair and a single letter.

4 down IT GIRL

Charismatic female working with computers? (2,4)

Definition: working with computers? · charade

  • Charismatic → IT 'it' is the old word for sex appeal and star quality, the thing Clara Bow was said to have
  • female → GIRL the everyday word for a young female, four letters as they stand

The question mark is the setter owning up: the phrase really names a charismatic socialite, and only the literal reading sends her to a keyboard. IT for sex appeal dates from the silent films.

6 down MAESTRI

Working times are reduced for distinguished practitioners (7)

Definition: distinguished practitioners · anagram + deletion

  • times → TIMES five letters of fodder, straight from the clue
  • are → AR the word are, cut short, its final E left out of the mix

'Working times are reduced' is a whole sentence about shift patterns, which is why nothing in it looks like fodder. The answer is the Italian plural of maestro, which is why it ends in I where English would want an S.

7 down MEETS

Comes across English baseball team outside (5)

Definition: Comes across · container

  • baseball team → METS the New York Mets are a Major League club, and the name splits after its first letter
  • English → E E is the usual abbreviation for English

An American sport in a British paper is normal service: a four-letter ball club is as standard a piece of crossword furniture as ANT is for a worker.

8 down ELAN

Horny African shaking rear in style (4)

Definition: style · deletion

  • Horny African → ELAND a large spiral-horned antelope of southern Africa, five letters of it
  • shaking rear → the D that closes the antelope's name is what gets shaken off

'Horny' sets up a joke the clue never delivers: the horns belong to an antelope. French supplies the answer, as it usually does for words about dash and flair.

12 down BIGOT

One narrow-minded old priest, ultimately under grown-up (5)

Definition: One narrow-minded · charade + last letter

  • grown-up → BIG a grown-up is a big person, the way a small child puts it
  • old → O O is the standard abbreviation for old
  • priest, ultimately → T the closing letter of the word priest, taken on its own

The definition is 'One narrow-minded' — a person, not the quality — and the rest of the sentence is three separate scraps dressed up as a remark about the clergy.

13 down ACETIC

Mention about upsetting kind of acid (6)

Definition: kind of acid · charade + reversal

  • Mention → CITE to cite something is to mention it, four letters that will be read the other way
  • about → CA ca. is short for circa, Latin for about, the abbreviation put before an approximate date

The enumeration says six because the answer is split over two entries, 13 and 16 down. It is the acid that makes vinegar sour.

14 down METATARSI

Bones in state - one arm shattered (9)

Definition: Bones · anagram

  • state → STATE five letters of fodder, taken as they stand
  • one → I one is the Roman numeral I, the crossword's usual single letter for it
  • arm → ARM three more letters of fodder

The bones are in the foot, so 'arm' is pure misdirection, and 'state' looks as if it wants to be an abbreviation. The Latin plural of metatarsus ends in -i.

17 down ENTERON

Rene not swimming in canal (7)

Definition: canal · anagram

  • Rene → RENE a French given name, four letters of fodder
  • not → NOT three more letters, taken as they stand

'Canal' wants to be water, and Rene and swimming both push you towards a French waterway. It is the anatomical sense instead: the gut.

18 down DUTCHMAN

Wife and husband European (8)

Definition: European · charade

  • Wife → DUTCH in Cockney slang a man's wife is his old dutch, from the music-hall song
  • husband → MAN a woman's man is her husband, as in 'my man'

Cockney slang and plain English sit side by side with nothing to mark which is which — a fairness a British solver never notices and everyone else has to be told.

20 down A BIT OF

Aftershave at first was effective round female? Not much (1,3,2)

Definition: Not much · charade + first letter

  • Aftershave at first → A the opening letter of the word aftershave
  • was effective → BIT a remark that bit was one that took effect — the past tense of bite
  • round → O O is the round letter, the one drawn as a circle
  • female → F F is the standard abbreviation for female

'Round' looks like an instruction to put one piece outside another, and here it is only the shape of a letter. The rest of the clue is four scraps in a row.

21 down KEMPT

Tory leader drops one point - neat (5)

Definition: neat · deletion + charade

  • Tory leader → KEMI Kemi Badenoch leads the Conservative Party, whose members are the Tories, and her first name supplies four letters
  • one → one is the Roman numeral I, and that is the letter dropped
  • point → PT pt is the abbreviation for point, as on a compass or a scoreboard

'Tory leader' looks like an instruction to take the first letter of Tory; it is the politician herself. The answer survives in ordinary English mainly as the opposite of unkempt.

23 down FAUX

Nothing you said that's wrong or not genuine (4)

Definition: not genuine · charade + homophone

  • Nothing → FA sweet FA is British slang for nothing at all — Fanny Adams, put politely
  • you said → U the name of this letter is the word you, so saying it aloud is worth one character
  • that's wrong → X a cross in the margin is what marks an answer wrong

The surface is somebody conceding an argument — nothing you said was wrong — and the parse breaks it into three unrelated scraps, one of them a polite piece of slang.

25 down JACK

Lift up ball on green (4)

Definition: Lift up · double definition

  • Lift up → JACK the garage verb, what you do to a car with a hydraulic prop under the sill
  • ball on green → JACK in lawn bowls the small white target ball is rolled out first and the woods are aimed at it

Five words, no indicator, nothing to take apart — the tell is that no word in the clue asks you to do anything. Bowls is played on a green, which is what makes the second half fair.
