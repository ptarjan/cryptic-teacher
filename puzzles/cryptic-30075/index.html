Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,075

Across

1 across MASKED

Disguised male questioned (6)

Definition: Disguised · charade

  • male → M m = male, the abbreviation every form uses
  • questioned → ASKED to question someone is to ask them, and the past tense carries over

Nothing here announces itself as an instruction, which is what makes a bare two-part charade hard to spot: 'male' looks like part of the scene rather than a letter.

4 across RAREBIT

Dish of unusual grain (7)

Definition: Dish · charade

  • unusual → RARE a rare event is an unusual one
  • grain → BIT a grain of truth is a bit of it — the tiny-amount sense, not the cereal

The surface points at cereal crops, and 'grain' is the piece doing the misleading: it has to be read as a small quantity instead.

9 across SUCCULENT

Uncle cuts up plant (9)

Definition: plant · anagram

  • Uncle cuts → SUCCULENT the nine letters of UNCLE CUTS rearranged

The surface asks you to picture an uncle pruning, so the fodder reads as a sentence rather than as raw material. The definition is the bare last word.

10 across TERSE

Short tenor getting refreshed on and off (5)

Definition: Short · charade + alternate letters

  • tenor → T T = tenor, the voice part as marked on a score
  • refreshed → ERSE every second letter of rEfReShEd

'Short' is the definition, not an instruction to shorten anything — that is the trap. Count from the second letter, not the first.

11 across TASTE

Sample bit of pasta stealthily (5)

Definition: Sample · hidden word

  • pasta stealthily → TASTE lying across the join: pasTA STEalthily

'Sample' is a verb here, and it sits beside two food words that keep pulling it towards its noun sense. That is the whole disguise.

12 across ELABORATE

Make speech after European scientist’s workplace gets involved (9)

Definition: involved · charade

  • European → E E = European, the usual abbreviation
  • scientist’s workplace → LAB a laboratory, shortened as it always is
  • Make speech → ORATE to orate is to make a speech

'Involved' has to be read as the adjective meaning intricate, not as the verb it looks like at the end of that sentence.

13 across PLACARD

Bill quietly left a club? (7)

Definition: Bill · charade

  • quietly → P p for piano, the musical direction to play quietly
  • left → L L = left
  • a → A taken as itself
  • club? → CARD clubs are one of the four suits, so a club is a card — the question mark owns up to the example

The surface is a man slipping quietly out of a nightclub. Four pieces in a row with no indicator between them is what makes a charade this long hard to see.

15 across TURNIP

Root of change I proposed initially (6)

Definition: Root · charade + first letter

  • change → TURN the tide turns — a turn is a change
  • I → I taken as itself
  • proposed → P the first letter of 'proposed'

'Root of change' reads as an idiom about origins, which is the misdirection: 'root' is the definition all by itself, and 'of' merely joins.

17 across STATUE

Effigy of union’s leader embraced by nation (6)

Definition: Effigy · container + first letter

  • nation → STATE a state is a nation, and it opens up to take a letter in
  • union’s → U the leading letter of 'union'

The surface is trade-union politics, and 'union' is there only to lend its first letter. 'Effigy' is doing honest work as the definition.

19 across LACONIC

Brief note concerning one Conservative snorting cocaine (7)

Definition: Brief · charade + container

  • note → LA LA is the sixth note of the sol-fa scale
  • cocaine → C C is the standing abbreviation for cocaine — the piece that gets taken in
  • concerning → ON a talk on birds is a talk concerning birds
  • one → I I is the Roman numeral for one
  • Conservative → C C = Conservative, as printed on a ballot paper

Two different Cs, one a political party and one a drug, sit two letters apart. The definition is the very first word, meaning short of speech rather than a legal document.

22 across HERBAL TEA

That woman sorted out table with a hot drink (6,3)

Definition: hot drink · charade + anagram

  • That woman → HER that woman = HER, untouched by the shuffling
  • table with a → BAL TEA the letters of TABLE and A rearranged

The shuffled letters run straight across the gap between the two words, so the 6,3 in the clue tells you nothing about where the fodder ends.

24 across GLADE

Pleased with mouse finally finding space in forest (5)

Definition: space in forest · charade + last letter

  • Pleased → GLAD glad = pleased
  • mouse → E the last letter of 'mouse'

The definition is the last three words together, and 'finding' is pure joinery — no letters hide in it.

26 across IRONS

Smooths out chains (5)

Definition: Smooths out · double definition

  • Smooths out → IRONS to iron a shirt is to press the creases out of it
  • chains → IRONS a prisoner clapped in irons is in chains

Three words, two definitions, no link word and no wordplay at all — the shortest clue in the grid and one of the harder ones to trust.

27 across ASSAILANT

Mugger is idiot upset by six footer (9)

Definition: Mugger · charade

  • idiot → ASS an ass is a fool
  • upset → AIL to ail someone is to trouble or upset them
  • six footer → ANT an insect has six legs, and the crossword's insect of choice is the ant

'Six footer' is not a tall man but a six-legged one. Count the legs whenever a height turns up in a clue — it nearly always means an insect.

28 across PENDANT

Quibbler holds new medallion (7)

Definition: medallion · container

  • Quibbler → PEDANT a pedant quibbles over small points
  • new → N N = new

PEDANT and the answer are a famous near-pair, one letter apart, which is why the quibbler turns up so often in clues of this shape.

29 across BEATEN

Hammered bachelor put away (6)

Definition: Hammered · charade

  • bachelor → B B = bachelor, as in BA and BSc
  • put away → EATEN to put away a big meal is to eat it

'Hammered' pulls towards drunkenness, and the wordplay's 'put away' pulls the same way. The definition needs the other sense: thoroughly defeated.

Down

1 down MISSTEP

Fail to attend precious flipping trip (7)

Definition: trip · charade + reversal

  • Fail to attend → MISS to miss a lecture is to fail to attend it
  • precious → TEP a precious one is a PET — a darling — and those three letters turn over

'Precious' is working as a noun, the household darling, not as an adjective. Setters lean on that shift constantly, and it is what hides the reversal.

2 down SOCKS

Request for help involving Calvin Klein footwear (5)

Definition: footwear · container

  • Request for help → SOS SOS is the distress call, a request for help
  • Calvin Klein → CK the label's initials, as printed on the waistband

Calvin Klein is here for his monogram, not his clothing line, though the surface is banking on you picturing the underwear.

3 down EXUBERANT

Former ride-share company worker is vivacious (9)

Definition: vivacious · charade

  • Former → EX an ex is a former partner, and EX- is the prefix meaning former
  • ride-share company → UBER Uber is the ride-hailing firm
  • worker → ANT worker = ANT, the insect that works

'Worker' is almost always ANT in a cryptic, occasionally BEE. Bank that one — it comes up more often than any other creature.

4 down RETRACT

Withdraw from English area after start of revolution (7)

Definition: Withdraw · charade + first letter

  • revolution → R the first letter of 'revolution'
  • English → E E = English, as in the school subject
  • area → TRACT a tract is an area of land

The political surface is a decoy: 'revolution' is wanted for its opening letter alone, and a tract of land supplies everything else.

5 down RATIO

Traitor I love in proportion (5)

Definition: proportion · charade

  • Traitor → RAT a rat is an informer or turncoat
  • I → I taken as itself
  • love → O love is a score of nothing in tennis, written as a nought

Tennis supplies the cryptic's favourite O: love is nil, and nil is drawn as a nought. It turns up in a clue most weeks.

6 down BARBARIAN

Pub ban before Scotsman gets savage (9)

Definition: savage · charade

  • Pub → BAR a bar is a pub
  • ban → BAR to bar something is to ban it
  • Scotsman → IAN Ian is the Scottish form of John and the crossword's standard Scotsman

Two identical pieces out of two unrelated words, one a place and one a prohibition — and a Scotsman in a grid is nearly always IAN.

7 down TEETER

Former X contributor without sway (6)

Definition: sway · deletion

  • without → W 'W out' is the letter that has to go
  • Former X contributor → TEETER X used to be Twitter, so someone posting there was a TWEETER — now minus that W

'Without' is one of the great cryptic double-crosses. The rebranding is doing the other half of the work: X only became X in 2023.

8 down DEFEND

Protect upstanding US agent on border (6)

Definition: Protect · charade + reversal

  • US agent → DEF a FED is an American federal agent, here stood on his head
  • border → END the end of a field is its border or edge

The surface paints an honourable officer on patrol; 'upstanding' is really a compass direction in disguise. FED is the standard American agent.

14 down AFTERNOON

PM behind expression of hesitation regarding accepting refusal (9)

Definition: PM · charade + container

  • behind → AFT aft is the rear end of a ship, so behind
  • expression of hesitation → ER ER is the noise made while stalling for time
  • refusal → NO a flat refusal is NO
  • regarding → ON a book on birds is a book regarding birds

In a clue stuffed with hesitation and refusal, 'PM' reads as a Prime Minister. It is the clock abbreviation, post meridiem — after midday.

16 down RECOGNISE

Try rising during sincere broadcast for Grant (9)

Definition: Grant · anagram + container + reversal

  • sincere → SINCERE these seven letters are the material to be scattered
  • Try → OG a try is a GO, rising in a down clue so it reads upwards

The capital G invites you to think of a man called Grant, and the definition hides at the end behind a link word.

18 down ENTRANT

Competitor in event ran tirelessly (7)

Definition: Competitor · hidden word

  • event ran tirelessly → ENTRANT running straight across three words: evENT RAN Tirelessly

'Tirelessly' is lovely misdirection — it sounds like an instruction to remove something, and it is there to supply a single T.

19 down LIAISE

Is a lie tricky to communicate? (6)

Definition: communicate · anagram

  • Is a lie → LIAISE the six letters of IS A LIE rearranged

The question mark carries the joke: the clue asks whether a lie is hard to pass on, while quietly handing over its own letters.

20 down CHEAT ON

Be unfaithful to Charlie with passion and touching (5,2)

Definition: Be unfaithful to · charade

  • Charlie → C C is Charlie in the NATO alphabet
  • passion → HEAT the heat of the moment — heat is passion
  • touching → ON a hand on a shoulder is a hand touching it

Every piece is a straight synonym, so the difficulty is all in the surface, which sounds like an affair being confided rather than assembled.

21 down SHRIMP

Small person parking next to quiet verge (6)

Definition: Small person · charade

  • quiet → SH SH is the noise you make to hush someone
  • verge → RIM the rim of a cup is its verge or edge
  • parking → P P = parking, as on the road sign

The definition is both words together, and the wordplay starts at 'parking' — a clean split that the roadside surface works hard to hide.

23 down AT SEA

Disorganised leaders of army training session elicit apathy (2,3)

Definition: Disorganised · first letters

  • army training session elicit apathy → ATSEA the opening letters of all five words, in order

Five words in a row each giving one letter is a long acrostic, and the run has to begin immediately after the indicator — no gaps allowed.

25 down AWAIT

Anticipate a pressure for auditor (5)

Definition: Anticipate · charade + homophone

  • a → A taken as itself
  • pressure → WAIT pressure is WEIGHT, and WEIGHT said aloud is what gets written down

Only the second piece is heard rather than spelt, so the A sits there untouched. A homophone need not swallow the whole answer.
