1 across HAMLET

Play in twisted tree sporting something on your head (6)

Definition: Play · container + reversal

  • sporting something on your head → HAT the thing you sport on your head, and it opens up after two of its three letters
  • tree → ELM the elm is the crossword's short tree, and it goes in the other way round

'Play in twisted tree' reads as an actor performing up a trunk. The definition stops after the very first word, and everything after it is instruction.

4 across FRIENDLY

Warm, new filling cooked with the last bits of special pastry (8)

Definition: Warm · container + last letters

  • cooked → FRIED food cooked in a pan has been fried, and the word opens up before its final letter
  • new → N n is the standard abbreviation for new
  • special pastry → LY speciaL and pastrY each give up the letter they end on, in that order

'Warm, new filling cooked' reads as a pie straight from the oven, which is exactly how the definition hides: 'Warm' on its own is the whole of it.

9 across LAUNCH PAD

Countdown supporter's vigorous handclap taking in the final vowel (6,3)

Definition: Countdown supporter · anagram + container

  • handclap → HANDCLAP the anagram fodder, eight letters
  • the final vowel → U the vowels run A, E, I, O, U, so the last of them is a U

The italics make Countdown the television programme and its fans. The countdown that matters is the one before a rocket leaves the ground.

11 across SNEAK

Grass snake's tail moved (5)

Definition: Grass · anagram

  • snake → SNAKE the five letters of the reptile, with only the final one changing place

'Grass snake' is one creature in the surface and two jobs in the parse: the definition stops after the first word and the reptile is raw material.

12 across HERB

Self-driving car that is made to leave, leaves (4)

Definition: leaves · deletion

  • Self-driving car → HERBIE the Volkswagen Beetle with a mind of its own from the Disney films
  • that is → IE i.e. is Latin id est, 'that is', and these are the two letters shown the door

The joke is that 'leave' and 'leaves' sit a word apart meaning different things: one is an eviction, the other is what you chop into a sauce.

13 across CHRISTMAS

Stupidly rich, devoted man joins mother and sons for feast-day (9)

Definition: feast-day · anagram + charade

  • rich → CHRI the four letters of the word, rearranged
  • devoted man → ST a saint is the devoted man, and st is how a saint's name is written
  • mother → MA ma is the familiar word for a mother
  • sons → S s is the abbreviation for son, kept singular while the surface talks of several

Read the opening three words as one description of a wealthy churchgoer and the clue never comes apart. 'Stupidly' belongs to 'rich' alone.

15 across SNAPCHAT

Application shown by a police constable in tin hat (8)

Definition: Application · container + charade

  • tin → SN Sn is the chemical symbol for tin, from the Latin stannum
  • a → A the article at face value, one letter
  • police constable → PC PC is the rank a British bobby holds, printed in front of the name
  • hat → HAT the word is used as itself, three letters lifted from the clue

'Tin hat' is a soldier's helmet, which is why it reads as a single thing. The parse needs it as two, and prising the phrase apart is the whole difficulty.

17 across PLANT

Schedule the closure of paint factory (5)

Definition: factory · charade + last letter

  • Schedule → PLAN to schedule something is to plan it
  • paint → T the letter that word closes on

'The closure of paint factory' reads as a line of industrial news, which is what keeps the last word from looking like the definition it is.

20 across DANCE

Jig, shake and carouse without stopping (5)

Definition: Jig · anagram + outer letters

  • and → DAN the three letters of the joining word, shaken into a new order
  • carouse → CE the first and last letters of the word, once the middle has been taken out

'Without stopping' reads as carousing all night, and that is the trap: it is not about carrying on but about a filling being removed.

21 across HONEYDEW

Melon that's hard held up by weedy criminal (8)

Definition: Melon · charade + anagram

  • hard → H H is the grade stamped on a hard pencil
  • held up by → ON what rests on a support is held up by it, and the preposition is taken as its two letters
  • weedy → EYDEW the five letters of the word, rearranged

'Melon that's hard' sounds like one phrase about unripe fruit. The definition is the first word alone, and 'hard' is already doing wordplay.

22 across SPYMASTER

My parse's wrong, assuming 2's first letter is M? (9)

Definition: M? · anagram + container + first letter

  • My parse's → MYPARSES the anagram fodder, eight letters counting the s of the possessive
  • 2's first letter → T write the numeral out as a word and it starts with a T

'2' looks like a cross-reference, and 2 down really does begin with M, which is the trap. The number wants spelling out instead.

25 across PAST

Last course of Italian meal is left unfinished (4)

Definition: Last · deletion

  • course of Italian meal → PASTA the pasta course comes before the main one at an Italian table

'Last course' looks like a single phrase about pudding. The definition is the first word on its own, and the course belongs to the wordplay.

28 across OUNCE

Snow leopard's attack quietly carried out (5)

Definition: Snow leopard · deletion

  • attack → POUNCE a cat's attack is a pounce
  • quietly → P p is piano in music, the direction to play quietly, and it is the letter taken away

The whole clue is a picture of the animal it names, a big cat pouncing without a sound, which is what makes the definition so hard to see as one.

29 across ABRUPTEST

A British rugby union player's opening try is most unexpected (9)

Definition: most unexpected · charade + first letter

  • A → A the article at face value, one letter
  • British → B B is the abbreviation for British, as in BBC
  • rugby union → RU RU is how the fifteen-a-side code is written on a fixture list
  • player's opening → P the first letter of player
  • try → TEST a test is a try in the sense of a trial or an attempt

The rugby scene is a decoy: 'try' has to be read as an attempt rather than a score, and the definition is the superlative at the end.

30 across YIELDING

Admitting defeat, yankee fellow's excused from playing cricket? (8)

Definition: Admitting defeat · deletion + charade

  • yankee → Y yankee is Y in the radio alphabet used for spelling names out loud
  • playing cricket → FIELDING the side that is not batting is fielding
  • fellow → F f is the abbreviation for fellow, as after the name of a Royal Society member, and it is the letter excused

The question mark is the setter conceding that fielding is only one of the things done at cricket: a part of the game is standing in for all of it.

31 across WRITER

Columnist, say, who sets things straight on the radio (6)

Definition: Columnist, say · homophone

  • who sets things straight → WRITER someone who sets things straight puts them right, which makes them a righter

'Columnist, say' looks as though 'say' is the sound instruction. It is the by-example flag, and the real instruction waits at the other end of the clue.

1 down HOLY

Virtuous, with passion, but no heart (4)

Definition: Virtuous · deletion

  • with passion → HOTLY doing something with passion is doing it hotly
  • heart → T the middle letter of that five-letter word is a T, and it is what goes

The surface offers a passionate but heartless lover. In the parse 'heart' is a position rather than a feeling, and the comma marks where the definition stops.

2 down MOUSE

Something clicked with inspirational person, inspiring love (5)

Definition: Something clicked with · container

  • inspirational person → MUSE the muse is the classical figure who supplies an artist with ideas
  • love → O love is a score of nothing at tennis, written as a round O

'Inspirational' and 'inspiring' come from the same root and the clue leans on that: one of them is a synonym to be converted, the other an instruction.

3 down ESCABECHE

Taxi covered in melted cheese and spicy marinade (9)

Definition: spicy marinade · container + anagram

  • cheese → ESECHE the same six letters in a new order, splitting either side of the vehicle
  • Taxi → CAB a cab is a taxi, three letters landing in the middle

Every other word in the surface is food, which makes 'Taxi' the odd one out and the place to start: it is the only thing here nobody would eat.

5 down RADAR

Mix the tips of asparagus, radish, radicchio and dried pulses (5)

Definition: pulses · anagram + first letters

  • asparagus, radish, radicchio and dried → ARRAD five words, each handing over the letter it opens with, in the order they are listed

The list reads like a salad, and the 'and' is fodder alongside the vegetables — setters count it as a word like any other, which is what makes the letters come out.

6 down EASES

Each sufferer originally put on drugs gets better (5)

Definition: gets better · charade + first letter

  • Each → EA ea is the shopkeeper's abbreviation for each, as on a price list
  • sufferer → S the first letter of the word, and nothing else of it
  • drugs → ES E is ecstasy, so a plural of them is ES

The surface is a patient on medication and 'gets better' finishes it so smoothly that it never looks like a definition sitting at the end.

7 down DREAMLAND

Dozer's head with fantastic realm inside? That's it! (9)

Definition: Dozer's head with fantastic realm inside? That's it! · container + anagram + first letter + &lit

  • Dozer's head → D the head of a word is its first letter, so the sleeper gives up a D
  • realm → REAML the same five letters, shaken into another order
  • with → AND 'with' and 'and' both join two things, so one can be written for the other

An all-in-one: the sentence describes the place and builds it at the same time, and 'with' works twice over, as joinery in the surface and as three letters.

8 down YIKES

Definition: I'm afraid · anagram

  • Key is → KEYIS the anagram fodder, five letters including the little verb

'Key is faulty' sounds like a complaint about a lock or a piano, and the trap is reading 'Key' as the definition when it is the raw material.

10 down PACMAN

Mature boy holding up a boring PC game (6)

Definition: game · container + charade

  • PC → PC two letters taken at face value, and they come apart
  • a → A the article at face value, one letter drilled into the middle
  • Mature boy → MAN a boy who has matured is a man

The whole sentence reads as a mugging in an amusement arcade. Every word of it is instruction except the last, and that last word is the definition.

14 down USED

The Independent editor is exhausted (4)

Definition: exhausted · charade

  • The Independent → US the paper this puzzle appears in, speaking of itself as us
  • editor → ED ed is the short form of editor, as in a letter to the ed

A setter is allowed to point at his own paper, and the italics are the tell: the title is not there for the surface but to be read as 'we'.

16 down ANNOYANCE

Having dropped diamonds, Danny Ocean's in a state of confusion and anger (9)

Definition: anger · deletion + anagram

  • diamonds → D d is the card player's abbreviation for the suit, and it is the letter dropped
  • Danny Ocean → ANNYOCEAN the name without that letter leaves nine to be rearranged

Danny Ocean is the thief George Clooney plays in the Ocean's Eleven films, and the diamonds he drops here are a suit of cards rather than loot.

17 down PIED PIPER

Dutch cake decorator carrying baked product is a draw for kids (4,5)

Definition: a draw for kids · charade

  • baked product → PIE a pie is the baked product, riding on top of everything else
  • Dutch → D D is the dictionary's abbreviation for Dutch, used in word histories
  • cake decorator → PIPER the one who pipes icing onto a cake

He led Hamelin's children away behind his pipe, which is the joke in 'a draw for kids' — and the legend is German, so 'Dutch' is only ever a letter.

18 down TOWN

Cowdenbeath perhaps to emerge victorious after losing one (4)

Definition: Cowdenbeath perhaps · deletion

  • to emerge victorious → TOWIN to emerge victorious is to win, and the little word in front comes along too
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one, and this is the letter that goes

Cowdenbeath is a small place in Fife, best known outside Scotland for its football club, and 'perhaps' is what turns the name into a general word.

19 down HORROR

The beginning or the end of Highlander? Or The Shining? (6)

Definition: The Shining · charade + first letter + last letter

  • The beginning → H the opening letter of the title that follows
  • or → OR the little word is lifted straight out of the clue, two letters
  • the end of Highlander → R the letter that title finishes on
  • Or → OR the same two letters again, capitalised here only to start a sentence

Two films and only one of them defines: Highlander is there to be taken apart letter by letter, while Kubrick's title is an example of the genre.

22 down STORY

Jesus repeatedly exposed political figure's lie (5)

Definition: lie · charade + middle letter

  • Jesus → S strip the outside letters twice over and a single letter is left standing in the middle
  • political figure → TORY a Tory is a member of the British Conservative party

The surface has Jesus catching out a politician, which is why nobody looks at the name as five letters waiting to be peeled — twice.

23 down AHEAD

I see star chopping out line in front (5)

Definition: in front · charade + deletion

  • I see → AH 'ah' is the noise of understanding, the spoken form of 'I see'
  • star → LEAD the star of a film has the lead role
  • line → L l is the abbreviation for line, as in a reference to a poem, and it is the letter chopped out

'I see star' reads as an astronomer's note. Both halves of it are wordplay, and the definition is waiting at the far end of the clue.

24 down TRAIN

Creative works put up in sequence (5)

Definition: sequence · reversal + charade

  • Creative works → ART art is the collective word for creative work, and it goes up the grid
  • in → IN the little word at face value, two letters

'In sequence' reads as one phrase and then splits in two: the first word is already letters, and only the second is the definition.

26 down SHEET

The woman regularly went to get bed linen (5)

Definition: bed linen · charade + alternate letters

  • The woman → SHE the pronoun that stands in for the woman
  • went → ET the second and fourth letters of the word, the ones an even count picks up

'The woman regularly went' reads as a habit, and that is what disguises 'regularly' as a description of her rather than an instruction about letters.

27 down STIR

Move location of screw (4)

Definition: Move · double definition

  • Move → the small shift someone makes without quite waking, or what a spoon does to soup
  • location of screw → in British slang a screw is a prison officer, so the location is the jail he works in

Four words and no wordplay at all. 'Screw' is not a fastening here, and the second definition is a place rather than an action, which is what hides the join.
