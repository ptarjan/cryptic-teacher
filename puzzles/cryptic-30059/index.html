Across

7 across CHARIOT

Hoot passing steamer in 9 (7)

Definition: 9 · charade

9 → CHARIOT: 9 across is RACE, and the chariot race was the great spectacle of the Roman circus, run with two wheels and four horses.

  • steamer → CHA cha is tea, the cup that arrives steaming
  • Hoot → RIOT a hoot and a riot both mean something hilarious
  • passing — One thing that passes another finishes up right alongside it — a loose join, promising only that the two pieces touch.

The surface is a ship sounding its horn in fog. Cha for tea reached English from the same Chinese word as chai, and it is standard crossword furniture.

8 across PANCAKE

17 across 9? (7)

Definition: 17 across · double definition

17 across → PANCAKE: a pancake is proverbially flat, and with 9 across it names the Shrove Tuesday event run while tossing one.

  • 17 across → PANCAKE solve 17 across first: it hands you the adjective this griddle-thin thing is proverbially used to illustrate
  • 9 → PANCAKE 9 across is RACE, and Shrove Tuesday has a famous one run while tossing something in a frying pan

Both halves are cross-references, so this clue falls last: 17 across says what a pancake is like, 9 across says what a pancake gets carried through in February.

9 across RACE

Run – and whizz! (4)

Definition: Run · double definition

'Run' → RACE: a run is a contest over a distance, and 'whizz' catches the other verb sense, to move so fast you tear past.

  • Run → RACE horses and athletes run in the sense of competing over a distance
  • whizz → RACE to whizz along is to travel at real speed

The keystone of the puzzle: a dozen other clues point at 9, each naming a different kind of one. Two ordinary senses here, a contest and sheer speed.

10 across GREYHOUND

Track associated with 12 down 9 (9)

Definition: 9 · charade

9 → GREYHOUND: 9 across is RACE, and greyhound racing, run on an oval behind a mechanical hare, is a sport in its own right.

  • 12 down → GREY solve 12 down first: a grey is one of them, named for the colour of its coat
  • Track → HOUND to track somebody relentlessly is to hound them
  • associated with — Things associated stand together, and that is all this join promises — it never says which piece leads, so the crossing letters settle it.

'Track' looks like the definition — a dog track — and is really a verb. Paul then makes you solve 12 down before this one will give up its first half.

12 across HUMAN

I’m thinking to enter old ruling dynasty in 9 (5)

Definition: 9 · container

9 → HUMAN: 9 across is RACE, and the human race is the standing phrase for our species — one of the few races nobody competes in.

  • old ruling dynasty → HAN the Han ruled China for four centuries either side of year one
  • I’m thinking → UM the noise a person makes while thinking, written out
  • to enter — To enter a place is to go inside it, so the piece holding the instruction moves in among the other's letters.

The dynasty is split after a single letter, which is where insertions get hard: the eye expects the inserted piece to land somewhere nearer the middle.

13 across DUELLIST

Challenger with gun anticipated getting shot still (8)

Definition: Challenger with gun · charade + anagram

'Challenger with gun' → DUELLIST: the man who issues the challenge and then settles it at dawn, pistol in hand, at twenty paces.

  • anticipated → DUE a train anticipated at four is due at four
  • still → LLIST five letters handed over to be rearranged
  • getting shot — Something shot is wrecked — shot to pieces — and wrecked letters have to be rebuilt in a different order.

'Getting shot still' sounds like a photographer at work and is two jobs at once: the shooting is the instruction, and 'still' is the word it wrecks.

15 across IRON

Fee, one third off – decrease? (4)

Definition: decrease? · deletion

'decrease?' → IRON: read as de-crease, since taking the creases out of a shirt is ironing it, and the question mark flags the split.

  • Fee → FEE the word fee itself, all three letters of it, is the raw material
  • one third off → FE lose one of those three and FE is left, which is how chemistry writes the metal
  • one third off — On the surface that is a discount; on the letters it means one of the word's three characters, a third of them, is taken away.

Two jokes in four letters. FE is the chemical symbol, so a fee cut by a third names the metal — and 'decrease' is really de-crease, what the appliance does to a shirt.

16 across THREE-LEGGED

9 on and on and on? (5-6)

Definition: 9 · charade

9 → THREE-LEGGED: 9 across is RACE, and the three-legged race is the school sports-day event run by two children with their ankles tied.

  • on and on and on? → THREE the same little word is named over and over, and how many times is what the first half of the answer counts
  • on → LEGGED in cricket the on side is the leg side, so every mention supplies one leg

The surface is somebody droning away. 'On' is cricket's name for the leg side of the wicket, and 9 across turns three of them into a sports-day event.

17 across FLAT

Boring 9 (4)

Definition: Boring · double definition

'Boring' → FLAT: a flat joke falls dull and lifeless; with 9 across it becomes the level season of horse racing, run without fences.

  • Boring → FLAT a dull, lifeless performance is described this way, as is a joke nobody laughs at
  • 9 → FLAT 9 across is RACE: British racing splits into the jumps and the season run on the level, and this word names the second

Both halves are everyday, but the second needs 9 across first. The flat is the summer code, run without fences, as against National Hunt racing over jumps.

18 across SEALSKIN

Snake is swimming around lake – hide! (8)

Definition: hide · anagram + container

'hide' → SEALSKIN: a hide is an animal's skin taken off whole, and this is that same pelt named for the creature it came from.

  • Snake is swimming → SEASKIN seven letters resettled into a new order
  • lake → L L is the map-maker's lake, as on an atlas beside Windermere
  • swimming — A snake swimming holds no fixed shape from one moment to the next, and neither do the letters it is made of.
  • around — What goes around something closes over it, so the resettled letters finish up on both sides of the single one.

The dash is the join. 'Hide!' reads as an order to conceal yourself and means a pelt, and the exclamation mark is there to keep you reading it the wrong way.

20 across PIECE

Musical creation where harmony heard? (5)

Definition: Musical creation · homophone

'Musical creation' → PIECE: a single composition is called a piece, the everyday word for one work rather than for a whole genre.

  • harmony → PIECE harmony between people is peace, and said aloud that word is spelt a second way
  • heard — What is heard is taken in by ear, so you write the spelling that matches the sound rather than the word printed.

'Where harmony heard' poses as a concert hall, and it is the sound of calm that gives the composition. Musical surfaces are where this pair always hides.

21 across SUGAR BEET

Plant eater bugs cuckoo (5,4)

Definition: Plant · anagram

'Plant' → SUGAR BEET: the white-rooted crop grown across East Anglia and refined into much of Britain's sugar, so the general word covers it.

  • eater bugs → EATERBUGS nine letters to be rearranged
  • cuckoo — Cuckoo means mad, and letters sent mad will not stay in the order they were handed to you.

'Plant eater' reads as one word, herbivore, and has to be broken after the first — the standard trap when a setter starts a clue with a two-word phrase.

22 across DRAG

Boring thing, 9 (4)

Definition: Boring thing · double definition

'Boring thing' → DRAG: a tedious chore is 'a drag', and with 9 across it is the drag race, two cars sprinting a quarter-mile.

  • Boring thing → DRAG any tedious chore gets called this — a real one, as people say
  • 9 → DRAG 9 across is RACE, and two cars over a quarter-mile from a standing start make one of these

The same four letters do very different work either side of the comma: a chore you cannot get out of, and a quarter-mile sprint between two cars.

24 across SCREW-ON

Team welcomed by child able to be connected (5-2)

Definition: able to be connected · container

'able to be connected' → SCREW-ON: a screw-on fitting is one you attach by turning it onto a thread, so it names the connectable kind.

  • child → SON a child is a son
  • Team → CREW the team that works a boat, or a film set, is the crew
  • welcomed by — To welcome somebody is to take them in, so the piece doing the welcoming closes around the other one.

'Team welcomed by child' sounds like a story from a school match. Wrapping a three-letter word around a four-letter one leaves almost nothing showing outside.

25 across STONE ME

Partial recollection of theme, not so by George! (5,2)

Definition: by George! · hidden word + reversal

'by George!' → STONE ME: both are exclamations of astonishment, the answer a Cockney oath heard as surprise rather than as a threat.

  • theme, not so → STONEME the letters lie consecutively across these words, running the other way
  • Partial — Only part of the words that follow is wanted, and that part is a run of letters lying inside them.
  • recollection — A recollection brings something back, and letters brought back are read the other way about.

Both halves are mild oaths: 'by George' swears by the patron saint, while the answer is the Cockney exclamation Tony Hancock turned into a catchphrase.

Down

1 down WHOA

Stop pain in the ear? (4)

Definition: Stop · homophone

'Stop' → WHOA: the shout that brings a horse to a standstill, so it defines stopping as an instruction rather than as a plain verb.

  • pain → WHOA pain is woe, and woe said aloud is what a rider shouts to pull a horse up
  • in the ear — Anything arriving in the ear is heard rather than read, so what you write down is a spelling that matches the sound.

'Pain in the ear' poses as earache. Fitting that the sound of misery turns out to be the command that halts a horse, in a puzzle this full of racing.

2 down FRIEDMAN

Economist, possible dish for cannibal? (8)

Definition: Economist · charade

'Economist' → FRIEDMAN: definition by example — Milton Friedman, the Chicago monetarist whose name the solver supplies from the trade named.

  • possible dish → FRIED a dish may be described by how it was cooked, and this is the commonest way
  • for cannibal → MAN what a cannibal eats is man

Milton Friedman, the monetarist of the Chicago school, is the economist wanted; the surface carves his surname into a menu, which is the entire joke.

3 down NOGGIN

Measure of alcohol in two alcoholic drinks (6)

Definition: Measure of alcohol · charade

'Measure of alcohol' → NOGGIN: an old quarter-pint measure of spirits, and still the word for a small one poured for somebody.

  • two alcoholic drinks → NOG the first of the two is nog, the strong ale that also flavours a Christmas eggnog
  • two alcoholic drinks → GIN the second is gin, the spirit half of a G and T

One phrase has to yield both drinks, and 'two' is the only signal that the answer arrives in halves — no other word tells you where to cut.

4 down CASHMERE

Silver no better than fabric (8)

Definition: fabric · charade

'fabric' → CASHMERE: the soft wool combed from Kashmir goats and woven into cloth, the region's name spelt the English way.

  • Silver → CASH silver is coin, and coin in hand is cash
  • no better than → MERE something no better than a copy is a mere copy

'Silver no better than' reads as a jeweller sneering at second-rate metal. Both halves are ordinary synonyms once the sentence is broken at the right point.

5 down ACTUAL

Very true, origin forgotten (6)

Definition: Very · deletion

'Very' → ACTUAL: the older adjectival sense heard in 'the very man' — this identical one, real rather than supposed.

  • true → FACTUAL a true account is a factual one
  • origin forgotten → F the F standing at the front is the letter that goes
  • origin forgotten — A word's origin is where it begins, and forgetting it means the opening letter never gets written down.

'Very' is the definition, not an intensifier: 'the very man' means the actual man, the identical one. The surface passes it off as praise for a story.

6 down SKID

Vicious circles ending in deadlock, lose control (4)

Definition: lose control · container

'lose control' → SKID: once the tyres let go the car stops answering the wheel, which is precisely that loss of control.

  • Vicious → SID Sid Vicious of the Sex Pistols, summoned by his surname as crosswords like to do
  • ending in deadlock → K the last letter of deadlock
  • circles — To circle something is to go the whole way round it, so the letter named next ends up enclosed.
  • ending in — The ending of a word is its final letter, so only the last one of deadlock is taken.

The punk bassist hides behind an adjective: 'Vicious circles' reads as a stock phrase and is really a name with an instruction attached to it.

11 down ENDURANCE

9 run, ace played after finishing line (9)

Definition: 9 · charade + anagram

9 → ENDURANCE: 9 across is RACE, and an endurance race — Le Mans, the Marathon des Sables — is won by lasting rather than by pace.

  • finishing line → END the finishing line of anything is its end
  • run, ace → URANCE six letters handed over to be resettled
  • played — A tune played by ear comes out in a fresh arrangement every time, and so do these six letters.
  • after — After puts the shuffled letters behind the piece named last in the clue, so the finish stands at the front.

The surface passes for a tennis report. Paul hides the join by putting the finish first, which is the last place a solver looks for the start of an answer.

12 down HORSE

Reportedly rough 9 (5)

Definition: 9 · homophone

9 → HORSE: 9 across is RACE, and horse racing is what 'the races' means by default, from the Derby to the Grand National.

  • rough → HORSE a rough voice is a hoarse one, and that sound is spelt a second way
  • Reportedly — What is reported is passed on by voice, so the word wanted is the one you hear and not the one printed.

Two letters apart and a world apart: a ragged throat gives the animal that half this grid is quietly about, and the only instruction is 'Reportedly'.

14 down SPACE

      9 (5)

Definition: 9 · &lit

9 → SPACE: 9 across is RACE, and the space race was the cold-war contest between America and the Soviet Union to reach orbit and the moon.

  •       → SPACE the clue is a printed gap and nothing else — what the typesetter left in front of the 9 is itself the wordplay

Nothing is printed but the cross-reference, and the nothing is the point: the blank before 9 is a SPACE, and SPACE plus RACE is the contest to reach the moon.

16 down TAKE ROOT

Youngster on tour of Korea working to become firmly established (4,4)

Definition: to become firmly established · anagram

'to become firmly established' → TAKE ROOT: a seedling that takes root cannot be shifted afterwards, and the phrase carries over to ideas and habits.

  • Youngster → TOT a small child is a tot
  • Korea → KOREA the country contributes its own five letters, whole and unabbreviated
  • on tour — A youngster on tour is never in the same place twice, and letters that keep moving arrive in a new order.
  • working — Dough that is worked gets pushed about until it holds another shape, which is what happens here to the letters.

Eight letters out of two innocent words, with the definition running all the way to the full stop — 'working' belongs to the wordplay, not to the youngster.

17 down FIELDING

Satirist square or deep, perhaps? (8)

Definition: Satirist · double definition

'Satirist' → FIELDING: Henry Fielding, who wrote Shamela and Tom Jones; and square or deep name positions, examples of fielding flagged by 'perhaps'.

  • Satirist → FIELDING Henry ——, the Georgian novelist of Tom Jones and Shamela, who aimed his satire at his fellow writers
  • square or deep, perhaps? → FIELDING square leg and deep midwicket are places to stand in cricket, and 'perhaps' flags them as examples of the wider job

Two worlds in one word: a Georgian novelist, and the cricket field, where square and deep describe where a player stands rather than what shape he is.

20 down POTATO

9 that’s eyed (6)

Definition: 9 · double definition

9 → POTATO: 9 across is RACE, and a potato race is that old sports-day event; 'eyed' fits because the sprouting buds are called eyes.

  • 9 → POTATO 9 across is RACE, and there is a sports-day event of that name run gathering vegetables into a bucket
  • that’s eyed → POTATO the sprouting buds on the skin of this vegetable are called its eyes

Paul gives you a vegetable with eyes and a sports-day event nobody runs any more, in which competitors fetch potatoes one at a time into a bucket.

21 down SACK

9 boot (4)

Definition: 9 · double definition

9 → SACK: a sack race is the hopping event at a school sports day, while 'boot' takes the other sense — to sack is to dismiss.

  • 9 → SACK 9 across is RACE: the sports-day event run with both feet inside a bag
  • boot → SACK to give somebody the boot is to dismiss them, and this is another word for the same shove

Four letters covering a school sports day and a dismissal, with nothing but the numeral to tell you the two halves are separate senses.

23 down ARMS

9 heats won’t start (4)

Definition: 9 · deletion

9 → ARMS: 9 across is RACE, and the arms race was the cold-war competition to stockpile more weapons than the other side had.

  • heats → WARMS to heat something is to warm it
  • won’t start → W the opening W is the letter that never gets written
  • won’t start — A word that will not start has lost its opening letter, so you begin reading it at the second one.

'Heats' does double duty on the surface, since heats are also the qualifying rounds before a final — which is the reading Paul wants you to take.
