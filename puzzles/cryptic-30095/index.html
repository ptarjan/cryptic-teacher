Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,095

Across

9 across NANNY GOAT

Big Apple’s best ever on bread – and butter (5,4)

Definition: butter · charade

  • bread → NAN the Indian flatbread, spelt without the second A as often as with it
  • Big Apple’s → NY New York's nickname; the apostrophe-s is the surface's grammar only
  • best ever → GOAT greatest of all time, the sporting acronym of the last decade

'Bread and butter' is the ready-made phrase the surface leans on, and the dash is where the clue quietly stops being about food at all.

10 across E COLI

Possible sickener, incomplete story of environmentalism? (1,4)

Definition: Possible sickener · charade + deletion

  • environmentalism → ECO eco- is the standard prefix for anything green
  • incomplete story → LI a story is a lie, and it is cut off before the end

The enumeration (1,4) is the tell: a single letter followed by a word is nearly always a species or a designation, and this one is behind most food-poisoning scares.

11 across POPE LEO

Father, forward in motion … (4,3)

Definition: Father · container

  • motion → POO a motion is what a doctor calls a bowel movement, and the nursery word for one is three letters
  • forward → PELE the Brazilian who played at centre-forward and is still the game's biggest name

'Motion' is a doctor's euphemism rather than movement, which is where the joke lives, and the ellipsis hands the sentence on to 12 across, where the same man waits under his family name.

12 across PREVOST

… priest entering job (7)

Definition: … · container

  • job → POST a post is a job, as in 'a teaching post'
  • priest → REV Rev is the abbreviation that goes in front of a clergyman's name

The clue has no definition of its own: the leading ellipsis borrows the one next door, where the Father is named. Elected in 2025, he was born Robert Prevost and is the first American to hold the office.

13 across AIDE

Deputy Viking perhaps flayed (4)

Definition: Deputy · deletion

  • Viking perhaps → RAIDER a Viking is one example of a raider, and 'perhaps' is what marks it as an example
  • flayed → the outermost letter at each end is peeled away, and both happen to be the same letter

'Perhaps' is doing real work: without it the clue would be claiming that Viking and raider mean the same thing, and a definition by example always has to own up.

14 across DUSSELDORF

European city recovering from disappointment, investing not as much (10)

Definition: European city · charade + container + reversal

  • from → FRO fro is the old form of the word, surviving now only in 'to and fro'
  • disappointment → DUD a dud is a shell that fails to go off, hence anything that flops
  • not as much → LESS the plain comparative, and it goes inside the three letters above

Everything is assembled in clue order and then read backwards, which is the only way the German spelling comes out. Reverse the answer and the three pieces line up left to right exactly as written.

16 across I ASK YOU

A burden for a Titan in debt – Lord have mercy! (1,3,3)

Definition: Lord have mercy · container + charade

  • debt → IOU the scrap of paper that admits you owe money; it splits to take the rest
  • A → A the opening word is taken at face value, one letter
  • burden for a Titan → SKY Atlas was the Titan condemned to hold up the heavens for ever

Atlas holds up the heavens, not the globe he is usually drawn carrying, and the debt is the surface's excuse for a note nobody wants to be handed.

17 across JUGULAR

Describing a vessel – whimsically or not? (7)

Definition: Describing a vessel · substitution

  • whimsically → JOCULAR jocular is the adjective for joking or whimsical, and its tail survives intact
  • a vessel → JUG a jug is a vessel, and it takes over the first three letters

Two words one substitution apart: go for one and you are in a fight, be the other and you are only joking. The vessel in the definition is a vein, not something you pour from.

19 across GARDEN HOSE

Kid’s knocked over shed one rebuilt – a wind-up for the horticulturalist? (6,4)

Definition: a wind-up for the horticulturalist · reversal + anagram

  • Kid’s knocked over → GAR to rag someone is to tease them, and those three letters are then turned round
  • shed one → SHEDONE anagram fodder, seven letters
  • rebuilt → anagram indicator

'Kid' and 'wind-up' both mean teasing, so the clue reads as one long joke about pranks, and only the horticulturalist points at the thing coiled up in the shed.

22 across FLAT

Building unit razed to the ground? (4)

Definition: Building unit · double definition

  • Building unit → FLAT British English for a set of rooms occupying one storey
  • razed to the ground → FLAT levelled: what is left of a building after the bulldozers

One word for a home and for the demolition of one, which is the whole joke. The question mark signals that the second half is a definition with a wink rather than a dictionary entry.

24 across END USER

After embroidering, sender wraps uniform for purchaser (3,4)

Definition: purchaser · anagram + container

  • sender → SENDER anagram fodder, six letters
  • uniform → U U is uniform in the NATO alphabet, the version pilots say
  • embroidering → anagram indicator

Nothing in the surface is doing anything but sewing. The NATO alphabet repays memorising: uniform, victor, whisky and the rest turn up as single letters everywhere.

25 across LEFTIST

Pink paper is tucked inside permit, red (7)

Definition: red · container + charade

  • permit → LET to let is to permit, and the word opens up to take the rest
  • Pink paper → FT the Financial Times has been printed on pink newsprint since 1893
  • is → IS the word is taken at face value, two letters as they stand

Two colours in one clue and both are political jokes: the pink is real newsprint belonging to a famously unsocialist paper, and the red at the end is a century-old nickname.

26 across MANGO

I don’t want you, old bean, in fruit … (5)

Definition: fruit · charade

  • old bean → MAN 'old bean' is a dated way of addressing a chap, like 'old chum'
  • I don’t want you → GO the two-letter order you give someone you want rid of

Read the wordplay as speech and it is one impatient sentence addressed to a friend, which is exactly the trick 27 across then repeats with a ruder verb.

27 across MANGETOUT

… and similarly in veg? (9)

Definition: veg · charade

  • similarly → MAN the same fellow is being addressed as in the clue before
  • similarly → GETOUT and told to leave again, rather less politely this time

French for 'eat it all', and the flat pod really is eaten whole, skin and peas together. The English reading is the previous clue's dismissal repeated with the volume turned up.

Down

1 down ON SPEAKING TERMS

Quite friendly person playing with mates, man paying a visit (2,8,5)

Definition: Quite friendly · anagram + container

  • person → PERSON anagram fodder, six letters taken as they stand
  • mates → MATES five more letters of fodder, joined to the six above
  • man → KING the pieces on a chessboard are men, and the one the mates are aimed at is the crowned piece

The surface is a five-a-side kickabout with your friends; the wordplay is a chessboard, where mates are checkmates and the piece that receives them stays whole while everything else is shuffled around it.

2 down IN SPADES

How one might make bid, terribly (2,6)

Definition: How one might make bid · double definition

  • How one might make bid → IN SPADES at bridge every bid names a suit, and this is the highest of the four
  • terribly → IN SPADES this half wants the idiom for having a quality in overwhelming quantity

Two card senses of one phrase, and the comma is the seam. 'Terribly' is the adverb of degree — terribly clever, terribly kind — not the adverb of disaster.

3 down CYCLE

Revolution trammelled by democracy, clearly (5)

Definition: Revolution · hidden word

  • democracy, clearly → CYCLE the letters run unbroken from the tail of the first word straight into the head of the second

'Revolution' is politics on the surface and mechanics in the answer. Hidden words love a comma, because a solver's eye stops at one instead of reading across it.

4 down HONOLULU

Old drama served up with opera in state capital (8)

Definition: state capital · charade + reversal

  • Old drama served up → HONO O is the abbreviation for old and NOH the classical Japanese drama; the pair are then read upwards
  • opera → LULU Berg's unfinished work, one of the standard operas in crosswordland

Two art forms doing letter work and neither is English: NOH from Japan, a German opera from Vienna. 'Served up' is only fair because the entry runs downwards.

5 down STAPES

Small strips in small bone (6)

Definition: small bone · charade

  • Small → S S is the size printed on every clothing label
  • strips → TAPES strips of adhesive or magnetic material

The word 'small' is used twice on purpose, once as a laundry abbreviation and once as part of the definition, which is why the second one reads as scenery.

6 down GENEALOGY

Descent as intolerance of spirit reported? (9)

Definition: Descent · homophone

  • intolerance of spirit → GENEALOGY an intolerance is an allergy and the spirit is a gin; run the two words together and say them quickly

'Spirit' looks ghostly and 'descent' looks like a fall, and both belong to the family tree instead. Long homophones only work said at speed; slow down and the two versions come apart.

7 down KOSOVO

Country in defeat like that, 5-0 (6)

Definition: Country · charade

  • defeat → KO a knockout is how a boxing match is lost
  • like that → SO so = in that manner, as in 'hold it so'
  • 5-0 → VO the Roman numeral for five, then the nought written as a letter

The surface is a thrashing on the pitch, and the scoreline is the wordplay: numbers here are shapes to be read, not quantities to be counted.

8 down FIFTY-FIRST STATE

LIST, say, how Trump sees Canada? (5-5,5)

Definition: how Trump sees Canada · charade

  • LIST → FIFTYFIRST L is Roman fifty and I is one, so the opening pair of letters counts out an ordinal
  • LIST → STATE ST is the everyday abbreviation on envelopes and maps, here for a member of the union rather than a street

Four letters of clue do all the work. Read LIST as a piece of writing and there is nothing there; read it as numerals plus an abbreviation and it is a Manhattan address and a political jibe at once.

15 down CYBERSHOP

Book in cypher so encrypted for etailer (9)

Definition: etailer · anagram

  • Book → B b. is the standard short form for book in references
  • cypher so → CYPHERSO anagram fodder, eight letters
  • encrypted → anagram indicator

A sentence about codes containing an instruction to make a code: 'encrypted' reads as pure scene-setting, which is precisely why it is easy to walk past.

17 down JOSTLING

Fraction outside of second, swimmer pushing hard? (8)

Definition: pushing hard · container + charade

  • Fraction → JOT a jot is the smallest possible amount, as in 'not a jot'
  • second → S s is the abbreviation for the unit of time
  • swimmer → LING a long north-Atlantic food fish, cod's cousin

'Swimmer' is crossword shorthand for a fish, and a handful of short ones — eel, ide, dace, ling — come round again and again. Worth learning as a set.

18 down LOLLIPOP

Something possibly sugared in water turned up, sweet (8)

Definition: sweet · container + reversal

  • water → POOL a body of water, and it opens up to take the four letters below
  • Something possibly sugared → PILL the sugared pill of the proverb, a nasty thing made easier to swallow

Every word of the surface is about sweetening, which is what keeps the water out of sight, and the operations happen in order: insert first, then read the whole column upwards.

20 down RIDING

Sound clothes I had on (6)

Definition: on · container

  • Sound → RING the noise a bell makes, a noun here rather than the adjective the surface suggests
  • I had → ID I'd is the contraction, and the apostrophe is dropped as usual

A one-word definition made of a preposition is the hardest kind to spot, and this one is hiding at the very end of the sentence, where nobody looks for meaning.

21 down HAREMS

Run over manuscript for secluded groups (6)

Definition: secluded groups · charade

  • Run → HARE to hare off is to run flat out
  • manuscript → MS MS is the librarian's abbreviation for a manuscript

'Run over' reads as one phrase about road accidents and is really two instructions, a verb and a position. Setters split ready-made phrases like this constantly.

23 down OFTEN

Routinely concerned with all one’s toes? (5)

Definition: Routinely · charade

  • concerned with → OF the preposition for what a thing is about, as in 'a study of birds'
  • all one’s toes → TEN the standard count across two feet

The question mark is an apology for the toes: the count only holds if nothing has gone wrong, and that cheerful hedging is very much this setter's manner.
