Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,135

Across

1 across IDEA

In retrospect, ChatGPT etc destructive, vacuous at heart? There's a notion (4)

Definition: a notion · container + reversal + outer letters

  • ChatGPT etc → AI ChatGPT and its rivals are all artificial intelligence, and 'etc' asks for the class rather than the one product
  • destructive → DE hollowed out, the word keeps only its outer letters

'In retrospect' looks like an essayist clearing his throat before an opinion. It is an instruction, and so is every other word before the question mark.

4 across SOBA

Strings of buckwheat, Asian primarily? (4)

Definition: Strings of buckwheat, Asian primarily? · &lit + first letters

  • Strings of buckwheat, Asian → SOBA four words, four initials, taken in the order they are written

An &lit: the sentence is a description of the thing and the recipe for spelling it at the same time, which is why nothing is left over.

8 across WHITER THAN WHITE

In the raw with the bananas - extremely chaste (6,4,5)

Definition: extremely chaste · anagram

  • In the raw with the → WHITERTHANWHITE fifteen letters, every one of them from those five short words

The surface is a nudist joke and reads as one sentence, so the five words before the dash never look like material. The enumeration is the check: fifteen letters exactly.

11 across TOPPING

Knocking off chocolate sauce perhaps (7)

Definition: Knocking off · double definition

  • Knocking off → TOPPING British slang for killing someone, the same underworld register as the clue's own phrase
  • chocolate sauce perhaps → TOPPING whatever is poured over the top of a pudding, of which the sauce is one instance

Two idioms with nothing in common but their letters, and the clue slides from one to the other without a join. 'Perhaps' is the flag that the sauce is only an example.

12 across FLEAPIT

Give gratuity to a little fellow over in seedy venue (7)

Definition: seedy venue · charade + reversal

  • Give gratuity to → TIP the few coins left for a waiter
  • a → A the clue's own letter, joining the pieces either side of it
  • little fellow → ELF the small chap of folklore, three letters of him

A fleapit is British for a shabby old cinema, the sort whose seats you would rather not inspect too closely. The surface, a tip for a small waiter, is pure decoy.

13 across HURRICANE

Raunchier, but laughably so? That's a big blow (9)

Definition: a big blow · anagram

  • Raunchier → HURRICANE nine letters, and the same nine both times

One-word anagrams are an Everyman signature. 'That's' is the join: everything before it is material and everything after it defines.

14 across OVOID

Empty after having had nothing - like an egg? (5)

Definition: like an egg? · charade

  • nothing → O O is the zero on any scoreboard, so nothing is a circle
  • Empty → VOID an empty space is a void — a plain synonym, not an instruction

The trap is 'Empty' at the front, which reads as an order to hollow a word out. It is a synonym, and the clue is a plain two-part build.

15 across PLAIN

Unattractive lowland (5)

Definition: Unattractive · double definition

  • Unattractive → PLAIN the kind adjective for a face nobody would call handsome
  • lowland → PLAIN a broad flat stretch of open country — the noun sense

Two words and no machinery at all. They share a spelling and nothing else: one describes a face, the other a landscape, and neither is flagged as the definition.

16 across OENOPHILE

Merrily I open butts of French Pomerol to imbibe? (9)

Definition: Merrily I open butts of French Pomerol to imbibe? · &lit + anagram + last letters

  • I open → IOPEN five letters lifted straight out of the clue, waiting to be shaken
  • French Pomerol to imbibe → HLOE the last letter of each of those four words, in the order they are written

Pomerol is a red from Bordeaux, so the surface is a wine buff's confession — and every word of that confession is also machinery, which is what makes it an &lit.

19 across LEECHES

Third character in John Cleese play, Drains (7)

Definition: Drains · anagram

  • Third character in John → H J, O, H — count three characters into the name and stop
  • Cleese → CLEESE the comedian's surname, six letters to be rearranged with the one above

'Third character' invites you to count actors rather than letters, and the definition is the last word rather than the play's title it is dressed up as.

21 across WEEPING

Small, small sound: sound of sorrow (7)

Definition: sound of sorrow · charade

  • Small → WEE the Scottish word for small, and a fixture of these puzzles
  • small sound → PING the little noise a microwave makes when it finishes

The repetition is the disguise: 'small' is used twice for two different jobs and 'sound' twice for two more. Only the last three words define.

22 across GET OUT OF MY SIGHT

Avoid Everyman's daft appearance: begone! (3,3,2,2,5)

Definition: begone! · charade

  • Avoid → GET OUT OF to get out of a duty is to avoid it
  • Everyman's → MY the setter signs every puzzle Everyman, so his own possessive is the two letters MY
  • daft appearance → SIGHT someone who looks a sight looks ridiculous

Everyman puts himself in nearly every puzzle he sets, and the possessive is always MY. Once you have that, the outer pieces are two ordinary phrases laid end to end.

23 across ISLE

Geographical feature that is around small lake? The reverse! (4)

Definition: Geographical feature · container

  • that is → IE ie is the Latin id est, the standard shortening for that is
  • small → S S is the usual one-letter form of small, as on a clothes label
  • lake → L L is lake on a map
  • The reverse! → surface only, and a wink at the geography: land does not sit around a small lake, the water sits around the land

The letters do exactly what the clue says, and the flourish at the end is about the picture rather than the parse — the surface has the water and the land the wrong way round.

24 across NERO

Tyrant, somewhat ungenerous (4)

Definition: Tyrant · hidden word

  • somewhat ungenerous → NERO four consecutive letters inside the long word at the end, and finding where they start is the job

Everyman finishes with a gift: an emperor whose name has been a byword for cruelty since the first century, sitting untouched inside a word about meanness.

Down

2 down DIETITIAN

Food guru initiated reforms (9)

Definition: Food guru · anagram

  • initiated → DIETITIAN nine letters in and nine out, with three Is among them to place

The surface is a wellness company announcing changes, and 'initiated reforms' reads as one achievement — which is what hides the fact that the first of those words is raw material.

3 down ANTIGUA

Short time enthralled by wild iguana somewhere in W Indies (7)

Definition: somewhere in W Indies · anagram + container

  • wild iguana → ANIGUA the lizard's six letters shaken up, then opened to take a captive
  • Short time → T T is the one-letter shortening of time, which is what 'short' is asking for

A lizard and a stopwatch make a surface with nothing to say, which is the tell. Everyman almost always has one geographical answer, and this is the puzzle's.

4 down STAFFIE

Man alternately bites dog (7)

Definition: dog · charade + alternate letters

  • Man → STAFF to man a desk is to staff it — the verb, not the noun
  • bites → IE the second and fourth letters, where the counting lands

'Man bites dog' is the old newsroom test of what counts as news, and the clue leaves that picture intact. A staffie is Britain's pet name for a Staffordshire bull terrier.

5 down BOWIE

What musician may take regularly: mike for musician (5)

Definition: musician · charade + alternate letters

  • What musician may take → BOW a performer takes one at the end of a set, and a string player takes one in hand
  • mike → IE the second and fourth letters of the microphone's short name

The setter uses 'musician' twice on purpose: the first one is wordplay and the last one defines. The trap is treating the whole opening phrase as the definition.

6 down SWITCH

Exchange whip (6)

Definition: Exchange · double definition

  • Exchange → SWITCH the verb sense: two things trade places, or you move from one to the other
  • whip → SWITCH a thin flexible rod cut from a tree and used for beating — the noun sense

Two words, no indicator, nothing to shuffle. A verb and a noun set side by side, with the clue refusing to say which of them is doing the defining.

7 down PELTED

Rained heavily - wearing fur? (6)

Definition: Rained heavily · double definition

  • Rained heavily → PELTED rain coming down hard is said to do this, hammering on the roof
  • wearing fur? → PELTED a pelt is an animal's skin with the fur still on it, so a creature in one is described by the same six letters

The dash joins two senses with nothing in common. The first is the ordinary weather word; the question mark on the second admits that it is the setter playing.

9 down IMPERMANENT

Grim perm an entertainer hides in passing (11)

Definition: passing · hidden word

  • Grim perm an entertainer → IMPERMANENT eleven consecutive letters running across four words, beginning part-way into the first

A grim perm and an entertainer make an odd little picture, which is the giveaway: Everyman is not describing anything here, only supplying letters for the run to cross.

10 down IMPROVISING

I'm in favour of six heads of state issuing numbing generalisations departing from script (11)

Definition: departing from script · charade + first letters

  • I'm → IM the clue's own contraction, taken at face value
  • in favour of → PRO to be pro something is to be in favour of it
  • six → VI the Roman numeral for six
  • state issuing numbing generalisations → SING four initials in the order the words appear

The surface is a politician saying nothing at length. 'Heads of state' reads as one phrase — presidents and prime ministers — and prising it apart is the whole trick.

14 down OPPRESSOR

Proposers involved tormentor (9)

Definition: tormentor · anagram

  • Proposers → OPPRESSOR nine letters with two Ps, two Rs, two Ss and two Os to find places for

Two abstract nouns with one soft word between them: 'involved' has to be the signal, because neither noun can possibly define the other.

15 down PHLEGM

Cool Prime Minister circumventing hot Member (6)

Definition: Cool · container + charade

  • Prime Minister → PM PM is the standard shortening for the head of a British government
  • hot → H H is the tap marked hot, and the letter does that duty throughout crosswords
  • Member → LEG a member is a limb — an arm or a leg, and here the three-letter one

'Member' beside a Prime Minister reads as an MP, and it is a limb. The definition is the old medicine: this is the humour that gave English the word phlegmatic.

16 down ONSHORE

Riding, it seems, with son going up to the coast (7)

Definition: to the coast · charade + anagram

  • Riding, it seems → ON riding is being ON a HORSE, and the phrase hands over both words — this one first
  • with son going up → SHORE the HORSE of that same phrase, with S for son moved to the front of it

The trap is reading 'son' as a fresh piece to be added. Its letter is already there in the animal, and the instruction only says where it moves to.

17 down NEWSMAN

Journalistic chap seen in every direction: gee! (7)

Definition: Journalistic chap · charade

  • every direction → NEWS north, east, west and south — the four compass points, in the order that spells them
  • gee! → MAN both are exclamations of mild surprise: 'Gee!' and 'Man!' do the same job in speech

The compass in that order is one of the oldest devices in the craft and worth owning outright. The second half is a swap of one exclamation for another.

18 down EIGHTS

In the E End, loathes rowers (6)

Definition: rowers · homophone

  • loathes → HATES to loathe something is to hate it
  • In the E End, loathes → EIGHTS drop the aitch and what is left, said aloud, is written the way this entry is spelt

The E does double duty, standing for East and winking at the accent. Rowing crews are named by their size, so a boat of eight oars is simply an eight.

20 down HOURS

Time for you and me, did you say? (5)

Definition: Time · homophone

  • for you and me → HOURS what belongs to you and me is ours, and an unsounded aitch at the front is all that separates that from the spelling wanted

Everything is in the question mark: read as a statement the clue makes no sense, and read as a question it is asking you to listen. The H is silent.
