Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,881

Setter: Deri · Published: Sunday 15 March 2026 · Difficulty: Moderate (harder than 14% of the puzzles here) · Grid: 15×15

Across

1 across BATTER

Who's in pub besetting teetotal European? (6)

Definition: Who's in · container

  • pub → BAR the pub of the surface is its bar, and the three letters open up after the second one
  • teetotal → TT TT is the standard shorthand for teetotal, the pledge to drink nothing at all
  • European → E E is the usual single letter for European

The surface asks who is drinking; the definition asks who is at the crease. A teetotaller inside a pub is the joke holding both readings together.

4 across CELLARS

Corks regularly seen around Fitzgerald's booze-filled rooms? (7)

Definition: booze-filled rooms · container + alternate letters

  • Corks regularly → CRS the first, third and fifth letters of the word, read in order
  • Fitzgerald's → ELLA Ella Fitzgerald the jazz singer supplies her first name, which drops in after the opening letter

'Fitzgerald's' looks like a possessive and the apostrophe is surface work only: what the wordplay wants is the singer's first name, not anything she owns.

9 across FIEND

Massive fan of sci-fi endorsing Banks (5)

Definition: Massive fan · hidden word

  • sci-fi endorsing → FIEND five consecutive letters run straight across the space between the two words

Iain Banks wrote literary novels and, as Iain M. Banks, science fiction, so the capital letter looks like a tribute to a writer rather than an instruction.

10 across LIKE CRAZY

Be attracted to eccentric with a will (4,5)

Definition: with a will · charade

  • Be attracted to → LIKE to be attracted to somebody is to like them
  • eccentric → CRAZY an eccentric person is crazy in the mild, affectionate sense of the word

Two everyday synonyms laid end to end, and the join is invisible because the sentence reads so smoothly as a description of somebody's temperament.

11 across LOOSE ENDS

John Cleese's latest two characters complete special BBC radio show (5,4)

Definition: BBC radio show · charade + last letters

  • John → LOO John is the American word for the lavatory, and the British word for the same room is three letters
  • Cleese's latest two characters → SE the final two letters of the surname, kept in order
  • complete → END to complete a task is to end it, verb for verb
  • special → S S is the usual single letter for special

Loose Ends is Radio 4's Saturday-evening arts and conversation programme. Cleese supplies letters rather than comedy, which is what makes the surface look like a tribute.

12 across SPIKE

Sleeps around, initially eschewing drug (5)

Definition: drug · charade + reversal + first letter

  • Sleeps around → SPIK a kip is British slang for a sleep, and the plural is turned back to front
  • initially eschewing → E the first letter of the word that follows

'Sleeps around' reads as one racy phrase, and the wordplay wants it as two jobs: a slang plural, then the instruction to turn it.

13 across GO ON

Turn over waffle (2,2)

Definition: waffle · charade

  • Turn → GO your turn in a game is your go
  • over → ON the two prepositions swap freely — you tell somebody over the phone or on the phone

'Turn over' looks like an instruction to reverse something, which is the trap in a three-word clue; it is really two separate synonyms, one word each.

14 across LITTLE

Pinch Mini (6)

Definition: Pinch · double definition

  • Pinch → a pinch of salt is the amount you can hold between finger and thumb, which is to say a very small quantity
  • Mini → a mini skirt or a mini break is a small version of the thing, so the prefix is the same idea again

Two words of clue and no wordplay at all: the setter's economy is the joke, and Syd Little of Little and Large is looking across at 3 down.

16 across GYM

Room accommodating squatters? (3)

Definition: Room accommodating squatters · cryptic definition

  • Room accommodating squatters → read straight, this is a building somebody has moved into without permission, and that housing-page picture is what the clue wants you to see first
  • squatters → the setter's reading makes them exercisers instead: a squat is the knees-bent lift done with a barbell, and the people doing it need a hall with equipment in it

There is no wordplay to unpick here. The whole clue is the definition, and the joke is that the second reading of 'squatters' is entirely straight-faced.

19 across BEN

Height of Boeing occasionally overlooked (3)

Definition: Height · alternate letters

  • Boeing occasionally overlooked → BEN the first, third and fifth letters of the aircraft maker, with the others passed over

A ben is a mountain peak in Scotland — Ben Nevis is the tallest of them — so the three letters name a height instead of measuring one.

21 across EARFUL

Roasting chicken, having removed its head (6)

Definition: Roasting · deletion

  • chicken → FEARFUL chicken means cowardly, and a cowardly person is fearful
  • having removed its head → the F at the front is what goes, leaving the six letters behind it

Roasting a chicken is the picture, and both halves of it are false: the roasting is verbal and the chicken is a coward rather than a bird.

22 across TEEN

Youth essentially brightening poet's evening (4)

Definition: Youth · charade + middle letter

  • essentially brightening → T the middle letter of the eleven, six in from either end
  • poet's evening → EEN e'en is the poetic contraction of evening, the one preserved in Hallowe'en

Poets' contractions are a standing crossword resource, worth learning as a set: e'en for evening, o'er for over, ne'er for never.

25 across MATHS

Son finishing off dull hour in school lesson (5)

Definition: school lesson · charade

  • dull → MAT a mat finish is a dull one, the paint that does not shine
  • hour → H h is the abbreviation for hour
  • Son → S S is the standard single letter for son

'Dull' wants the paint-tin sense, spelled with one T. The word that follows the son is not part of him — it is the instruction about where he goes.

26 across TIGHT-ARSE

Get a shirt undone — one buttoned-up (5-4)

Definition: one buttoned-up · anagram

  • Get a shirt → GETASHIRT nine letters of raw material; the garment belongs to the surface only

The surface undoes a shirt and the definition does it back up, which is the joke: the buttoned-up person of the second half is the mean or repressed sort.

27 across PUT ON AIRS

Broadcast satisfactory, assume a haughty manner (3,2,4)

Definition: assume a haughty manner · charade

  • Broadcast → PUT ON AIR to broadcast a programme is to put it on air
  • satisfactory → S S is the grade meaning satisfactory on a school report

'Broadcast' is a favourite anagram signal, and that is the trap here: it is a plain synonym, and the phrase it hands over is three words long.

28 across LATHI

Partly flat, Hindu's wooden weapon (5)

Definition: wooden weapon · hidden word

  • flat, Hindu's → LATHI five letters run straight on across the comma and into the next word

The comma and the apostrophe are the camouflage: punctuation counts for nothing in the letters, so the run reads through them as if they were not there.

29 across PUDDING

Party brought about noise next to golf course (7)

Definition: course · reversal + charade

  • Party brought about → PUD the DUP is Northern Ireland's Democratic Unionist Party, and its three letters are turned round
  • noise → DIN a din is a continuous loud noise
  • golf → G golf is G in the NATO spelling alphabet

'Golf course' is a single thing in the surface and two separate pieces underneath. In Britain the sweet course of a meal is called the pudding whatever is served.

30 across CAECUM

Neither finishing much, each having twisted part of gut (6)

Definition: part of gut · deletion + reversal

  • each → CAE the word loses its final letter, and the three that remain are read from the far end
  • much → CUM this one is docked in the same way and turned round too

'Neither finishing' governs both words at once, which is the piece most solvers miss: it is not one deletion but two, and each word pays the same toll.

Down

1 down BAFFLEGAB

Incoherent talk from a loud female on feeding baby cut short (9)

Definition: Incoherent talk · charade + container + deletion

  • baby cut short → BAB the infant loses its last letter, and the three that remain part company after the first
  • a → A the article joins in as the single letter it is written with
  • loud → F f is forte in a score, the composer's instruction to play loud
  • female → F F is the standard abbreviation for female
  • on → LEG in cricket the leg side of the field is also called the on side, so on is LEG

Cricket's leg side is the convention doing the work here, and it is well buried: the surface is a nursery scene, and nothing in it points at a field.

2 down TREMOLO

Tool REM deployed for musical effect (7)

Definition: musical effect · anagram

  • Tool REM → TOOLREM the capitalised band and the implement next to it are seven letters of raw material, not a description

Two bands in one clue, and neither of them matters: R.E.M. is there because the letters are useful, and the effect wanted is a violinist's, not a guitarist's.

3 down EDDIES

Dust devils? Large maybe, then small (6)

Definition: Dust devils · charade

  • Large maybe → EDDIE Eddie Large was half of Little and Large, the British comedy double act, so the surname clues a forename by example
  • small → S S is the clothing-label abbreviation for small

Large and small read as a pair of adjectives, which is the trap: one is a comedian's surname and the other a size on a label. Little is in the grid too, at 14 across.

4 down CAKES

Patisserie items are wrapped in piece of cling film (5)

Definition: Patisserie items · container + first letter

  • piece of cling → C the opening letter is the piece taken off
  • are → A an are is the metric unit of area, a hundred square metres, and its symbol is a
  • film → KES Kes is Ken Loach's 1969 film about a boy and a kestrel, a fixture of British screens

'Cling film' reads as one thing, the kitchen wrap, and the setter splits it down the middle: cling gives up a letter and film becomes a title on its own.

5 down LOCKSTEP

Rugby player huffs over tight formation (8)

Definition: tight formation · charade + reversal

  • Rugby player → LOCK a lock is the forward who packs down in the second row of a rugby scrum
  • huffs over → STEP a pet is a fit of sulks — to be in a pet is to be in a huff — and the plural is turned round

'Huffs' is the sulking kind, not the breathing kind: a pet is a sulk, the sense that survives in the phrase 'in a pet'.

6 down AMAZING

Shocking, American's scorn undermining male in support group (7)

Definition: Shocking · container + charade

  • support group → AA AA is Alcoholics Anonymous, the best known support group of them all
  • male → M M is the standard abbreviation for male, and it drops between the two letters
  • American's scorn → ZING a zing, in American usage, is a barbed one-liner delivered at somebody's expense

The apostrophe reads as 'American is' on the surface and as 'the American word for' in the wordplay, which is where the scorn comes from.

7 down SAYLE

E.g. exhausted live stand-up comedian from Liverpool? (5)

Definition: stand-up comedian from Liverpool · charade + outer letters

  • E.g. → SAY e.g. means 'for example', and one everyday word does the same job in a sentence: 'say, an apple'
  • exhausted live → LE the outer letters of the four-letter word, its middle emptied out

Alexei Sayle, the Liverpool-born comedian and actor, was a leading figure in the alternative comedy boom of the early 1980s.

8 down SLINKIER

More close-fitting silken pants I must wear with Romeo (8)

Definition: More close-fitting · anagram

  • silken → SILKEN six letters of raw material; the fabric is there for the surface alone
  • I → I the speaker joins the mixture as the single letter used to write them
  • Romeo → R R is Romeo in the NATO spelling alphabet

'Pants' works twice over: a garment in the surface sentence and, in the wordplay, the slang for hopeless that tells you the letters are in the wrong order.

15 down THUGGISH

Like a rough gust, high on the rocks (8)

Definition: Like a rough · anagram

  • gust, high → GUSTHIGH eight letters of raw material: the weather and the state of mind are surface only

A rough, in older British usage, is a hooligan or a ruffian, which is what makes the adjective fair; 'on the rocks' is the drink the surface is pouring.

17 down MANNERISM

Habit of guy, beginning to moan when interrupted by flipping alarm (9)

Definition: Habit · container + reversal + first letter

  • guy → MAN a guy is a man, plain synonym
  • flipping alarm → NERIS a siren is the alarm, and its five letters are turned round
  • beginning to moan → M the first letter of the verb, and it ends up right at the back

'Habit of guy' reads as a single phrase — a man's way of carrying on — and that is the trap: the definition stops at the first word, and the guy belongs to the wordplay.

18 down HELSINKI

Beatles movie mostly set on island somewhere in Finland (8)

Definition: somewhere in Finland · charade + deletion

  • Beatles movie mostly → HEL Help! is the band's 1965 film, and it gives up its final letter
  • set → SINK the sun sets, or sinks, below the horizon — the same movement under either verb
  • island → I I is the map-maker's abbreviation for island

'Set on island' reads as a film's location, which is the surface's job; the wordplay takes the words one at a time, a synonym and then an abbreviation.

20 down NOTATED

Scored ten at Old Trafford in the end — fantastic! (7)

Definition: Scored · anagram + last letter

  • ten → TEN the number contributes its own three letters
  • at → AT the preposition is raw material too, taken at face value
  • Old → O O is the standard abbreviation for old
  • Trafford in the end → D the last letter of the ground's name

The surface is a match report from Old Trafford, Manchester's famous stadium; the wordplay ignores the place entirely and treats its words as letters.

23 down ERRATIC

Unpredictable Clapton perhaps covering Supergrass? (7)

Definition: Unpredictable · container

  • Clapton perhaps → ERIC Eric Clapton the guitarist gives up his first name; the surname is only there to point at him
  • Supergrass → RAT a supergrass is British slang for an informer who names many others at once, and a rat is the same idea in one syllable

Supergrass were a Britpop band, which is why they sit capitalised beside a guitarist; the wordplay wants the police-informer sense instead.

24 down STELLA

McCartney possibly excellent right away (6)

Definition: McCartney possibly · deletion

  • excellent → STELLAR the word here means excellent — literally 'of the stars', from the Latin for star, then figuratively outstanding
  • right → R R is the crossword shorthand for right, and it is the letter that leaves

'Right away' reads as immediately, which is the trap: the two words are separate instructions. Stella McCartney, Paul's daughter, is a fashion designer.

25 down MOP UP

Tidy mess made by Englishman in Australia, as it were (3,2)

Definition: Tidy · reversal

  • Englishman in Australia → MOP an Englishman down under is a POM in Australian slang, and these three letters are that word written the other way round
  • as it were → UP the entry carries its own instruction: this is a down clue, and reading letters up the grid is what turns them round

This clue runs backwards. Instead of the wordplay building the answer, the answer read as wordplay builds the Australian nickname, and 'as it were' is the nudge that says so.

26 down THING

Weak and ultimately disappointing cup of tea (5)

Definition: cup of tea · charade + last letter

  • Weak → THIN weak tea is thin, and the sense carries over to a weak argument as well
  • ultimately disappointing → G the last letter of the adjective

The definition is the idiom, not the drink: 'not my cup of tea' means not what I care for, and the setter hides it behind a weak brew.
