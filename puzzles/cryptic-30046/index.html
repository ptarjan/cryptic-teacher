Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,046

Across

1 across HIGH FIVES

Congratulations, having lofted sixes, almost? (4,5)

Definition: Congratulations · charade

  • lofted → HIGH lofted = high up in the air
  • sixes, almost → FIVES numbers that almost reach sixes

The surface is pure cricket — batters lofting sixes — but no letters move at all: 'almost sixes' are simply fives, and the question mark owns up to the arithmetic joke.

6 across CHAFF

Tease bird a small distance off (5)

Definition: Tease · deletion

  • bird → CHAFF a CHAFFINCH, one of Britain's commonest garden birds, with the INCH removed
  • a small distance → INCH the letters taken off the bird

To chaff someone is to tease them good-naturedly — the banter sense, not the wheat husks the farming-flavoured surface suggests.

9 across BRUIN

Disaster, going after black bear (5)

Definition: bear · charade

  • black → B as in pencil grades
  • Disaster → RUIN goes after the B

Bruin — Dutch for brown — is the bear's name in the medieval Reynard fables, and crosswords have kept it alive ever since.

10 across GERMANDER

Manipulate voting arrangement, cancelling all-out rally in plant (9)

Definition: plant · deletion

  • Manipulate voting arrangement → GERRYMANDER the base word, before surgery
  • all-out rally → RY RALLY with ALL out — and this RY is what gets cancelled

A deletion built from another deletion: the letters to be removed are themselves manufactured by an earlier removal, which is rare and worth slowing down for. Germander is a family of flowering plants.

11 across IDA

Princess on stage may sound like a duck (3)

Definition: Princess on stage · homophone

  • a duck → IDA the eider is the sea duck famous for its down

Princess Ida is Gilbert and Sullivan's operetta — hence 'on stage'. Say IDA aloud and you get 'eider', the duck whose down fills your duvet.

12 across TRISYLLABLE

Unfortunately still barely functioning as an example (11)

Definition: functioning as an example · anagram

  • still barely → STILLBARELY anagram fodder, 11 letters
  • Unfortunately → anagram indicator

The definition is self-referential: count the beats in 'func-tion-ing' — three, so the word is itself an example of a trisyllable.

14 across SCHLOSS

Academy halved deficit in grand house (7)

Definition: grand house · charade + deletion

  • Academy halved → SCH SCHOOL cut to its first half
  • deficit → LOSS a loss = a deficit

Schloss is the German for a castle or stately home — the grand house. An English academy, halved, opens a German answer.

15 across DISTEND

Detectives are liable to exaggerate (7)

Definition: to exaggerate · charade

  • Detectives → DIS Detective Inspectors
  • are liable → TEND 'they tend to' = 'they are liable to'

To distend is to swell or inflate, and blowing something up beyond its true size is exactly what exaggeration does — that shared image is what makes the definition fair.

16 across STARKER

More grim to be nearly naked (7)

Definition: More grim · deletion

  • naked → STARKER informal British slang for stripped bare, one letter longer than the answer with an extra S on the end — drop it

Stark means bleak, so its comparative answers 'more grim'. STARKERS is gloriously British slang for naked — setters adore it, so file it away.

19 across INNARDS

Youngster with Guardian wanting whiskey in pubs shows guts (7)

Definition: guts · container + deletion

  • Youngster with → with the Guardian alongside, this describes a WARD
  • Guardian → WARD a youngster with a guardian is a ward — the italics wink at this very newspaper
  • whiskey → W W in the phonetic alphabet; 'wanting' removes it, leaving ARD
  • pubs → INNS the ARD goes inside

The italicised Guardian is the setter's wink: a youngster with a guardian is a WARD in the legal sense. Whiskey is the radio operator's W, not the drink the pubs suggest.

22 across EVELYN WAUGH

Bizarrely we envy almost all that’s horrible in author (6,5)

Definition: author · anagram + deletion

  • we envy → WEENVY anagram fodder
  • almost all → AL ALL stopping short of its last letter
  • that’s horrible → UGH the exclamation of disgust joins the fodder

Evelyn Waugh wrote Decline and Fall and Brideshead Revisited, and the surface reads like a review of his satire: we do envy almost all that's horrible in him.

23 across VAR

One checks whistleblower in French department (3)

Definition: One checks whistleblower · double definition

  • One checks whistleblower → VAR the Video Assistant Referee reviews the on-pitch referee's decisions
  • French department → VAR one of the French departments along the Mediterranean coast in Provence, named after the river running through it

The whistleblower is not a leaker but the referee holding an actual whistle, second-guessed from the VAR booth every football weekend.

24 across PLAYHOUSE

Wendy’s theatre? (9)

Definition: theatre · double definition

  • Wendy’s → a child's toy Wendy house, named for Wendy in Peter Pan, is a miniature den for pretend games
  • theatre → loads of real theatres use this exact word as their proper name

The Lost Boys build a little house around Wendy in Peter Pan, which is how toy houses got the name — and since Peter Pan began as a play, the question mark is well earned.

26 across AFOOT

Under way a short distance (5)

Definition: Under way · double definition

  • Under way → AFOOT an old idiom for something already under way, as in a famous detective's saying
  • a short distance → AFOOT twelve inches make a common unit of length; stick the indefinite article in front of it to build the answer

Split the answer and 'a foot' really is a short distance. 'The game's afoot' was Shakespeare's line in Henry V long before Sherlock Holmes borrowed it.

27 across RHOMB

Foreign character doctor found in shape (5)

Definition: shape · charade

  • Foreign character → RHO the Greek letter
  • doctor → MB Bachelor of Medicine, from the Latin Medicinae Baccalaureus

A rhomb is a rhombus with its Latin tail docked. Crossword doctors come in many flavours — DR, MO, GP and, as here, MB.

28 across DROITWICH

Opposite of gauche, that missing husband in spa town (9)

Definition: spa town · charade + deletion

  • Opposite of gauche → DROIT French: gauche is left, droit is right
  • that → WHICH the pronoun
  • husband → H the abbreviation removed from WHICH

Droitwich Spa in Worcestershire made its name on salt and brine baths. Droit, the French for right, also gives English 'adroit' — the opposite of gauche in both senses.

Down

1 down HOBBITS

Ferret wounded small creatures in a hole (7)

Definition: creatures in a hole · charade

  • Ferret → HOB a hob is a male ferret
  • wounded → BIT past tense of bite
  • small → S standard abbreviation

Tolkien's opening line — 'In a hole in the ground there lived a hobbit' — makes the definition fair. HOB for a male ferret is a crossword staple worth filing away.

2 down GOULASH

Try united batter and stew (7)

Definition: stew · charade

  • Try → GO have a go = have a try
  • united → U as in Man U
  • batter → LASH to batter = to lash, as a verb

The surface wants 'batter' to be the cake mixture next to the stew, but it is the verb — to lash. U for united comes straight off the football results.

3 down FUNCTION KEY

Social gathering vital for one usually on laptop (8,3)

Definition: one usually on laptop · charade

  • Social gathering → FUNCTION a function is a formal social event
  • vital → KEY key = vital, as in 'a key decision'

The definition is by example: F1 to F12 run along the top of a laptop keyboard, so a function key is 'one usually on laptop'.

4 down VEGGIES

Selective eaters may have struggles to eat this protein? (7)

Definition: Selective eaters · container

  • struggles → VIES vies = struggles, competes
  • this protein → EGG the protein that VIES swallows

Veggies here are the vegetarians, not the vegetables — the selective eaters. The joke is that the wordplay acts out the surface: VIES really does have to eat an EGG.

5 down STRAYED

Had accommodation across river, then wandered off (7)

Definition: wandered off · container

  • Had accommodation → STAYED stayed = had accommodation, as at a hotel
  • river → R map-key abbreviation

STAYED spans the R just as the surface has the lodgings sitting across the water. R for river is one of the commonest single-letter abbreviations.

6 down CIA

Agency’s brief goodbye (3)

Definition: Agency · deletion

  • goodbye → CIA CIAO, the Italian goodbye, stopped just before the O

The Central Intelligence Agency, made by cutting a farewell short. In a three-letter entry, 'brief' doing the trimming is nearly always the whole trick.

7 down AUDIBLE

Bail due to be reviewed in hearing (7)

Definition: in hearing · anagram

  • Bail due → BAILDUE anagram fodder, 7 letters
  • to be reviewed → anagram indicator

The surface is a courtroom, and 'hearing' completes the legal picture so well that it hides the definition: 'in hearing' — within earshot — is AUDIBLE.

8 down FOR DEAD

Having crossed river, shot by arrow in the head, and left like this? (3,4)

Definition: left like this? · charade + deletion

  • Having crossed river → FORD a ford is where you cross a river
  • the head → EAD HEAD with its top letter shot away — 'EAD

In a down entry the first letter of HEAD really is its head, and the arrow shoots it off. The whole grisly scene — river forded, arrow in the head — leaves someone for dead.

13 down LOSING HEART

Despairing, how ‘miserable’ becomes me (6,5)

Definition: Despairing · double definition

  • Despairing → to lose heart is to despair
  • ‘miserable’ becomes me → strip the heart — every inner letter — out of MISERABLE and only M…E survives: 'me'

The second definition is an instruction acted out on the word itself: MISERABLE losing its heart leaves just ME. 'Becomes me' adds a layer of false modesty to the gloom.

16 down SLEEPER

Spy’s simple earring (7)

Definition: Spy · double definition

  • Spy → SLEEPER a long-term undercover agent who lies low for years before being activated
  • simple earring → SLEEPER a plain ring worn to keep a new piercing open

Espionage and jewellery, with nothing in common but the word. Railway sleepers would make it a triple — English works this word hard.

17 down AGES AGO

Way back, wise guy’s first to drop a board game (4,3)

Definition: Way back · charade + deletion

  • wise guy’s → AGES SAGE'S — a wise guy's — without its opening S
  • a → A straight from the clue
  • board game → GO the ancient Japanese board game

'Way back' is the adverb, as in 'way back when'. Note that SAGE keeps its possessive S when its own first letter drops — the apostrophe simply disappears.

18 down REWOUND

Turned back, taking opposite directions in circle (7)

Definition: Turned back · container

  • opposite directions → EW east and west, a facing pair on the compass
  • circle → ROUND takes the EW in

'Turned back' as a tape or a clock is rewound. Compass points are a setter's favourite way to smuggle in E, W, N and S — 'opposite directions' always means a facing pair.

19 down IN UTERO

Happening before delivery to change routine (2,5)

Definition: Happening before delivery · anagram

  • routine → ROUTINE anagram fodder, 7 letters
  • to change → anagram indicator

'Delivery' is the midwife's, not the postman's: in utero means in the womb, so before delivery. A neat single-word anagram — ROUTINE rearranged completely.

20 down RAVIOLI

Run a brief fiddle making pasta (7)

Definition: pasta · charade + deletion

  • Run → R cricket scorecard abbreviation
  • a → A straight from the clue
  • fiddle → VIOLI VIOLIN — a fiddle — cut short

Fiddle and violin are the same instrument, one word posher than the other; only the N is lost. The surface's 'brief fiddle' sounds like a minor swindle, which is the misdirection.

21 down STRETCH

A way to heave and strain (7)

Definition: strain · charade

  • A way → ST a street
  • to heave → RETCH to retch is to heave, in the queasy sense

'Strain' defines STRETCH as verb or noun. The surface implies hauling on a rope, but the heaving here is the stomach's — to retch.

25 down HUB

Partner dropping by in centre of activity (3)

Definition: centre of activity · deletion

  • Partner → HUBBY an informal husband
  • by → BY the letters dropped from HUBBY

The letters to remove are sitting right there in the clue: hubby drops 'by', and the deletion hides in plain sight as the visitor 'dropping by'.
