Across

8 across KINGSTON

More than one man mountain somewhere in 15 (8)

Definition: somewhere in 15 · charade

  • More than one man → KINGS chess and draughts call their pieces men, and kings are the grandest of them
  • mountain → TON informally, a mountain of work is a ton of it

Paul splits the phrase 'man mountain' in two and clues each half separately — the misdirection is in the join.

9 across OPERA STAR

Love and prayer in 15 breaks through for Willard White, perhaps (5,4)

Definition: Willard White, perhaps · charade + container

  • Love → O love is zero in tennis
  • prayer in 15 → RASTA a Rastafarian — Jamaica's man of prayer
  • for → PER as in 'per head' = 'for each' — even this little word is wordplay

The step that unlocks it is reading 'prayer' as a person rather than something said. 'For' looks like joinery and is building material — Paul rarely wastes a word.

11 across MONTEGO BAY

Python catches boa wandering by say, somewhere in 15 (7,3)

Definition: somewhere in 15 · container + anagram

  • Python → MONTY Monty Python — the comedy troupe, not the snake
  • boa → OBA the snake that really is here, sent 'wandering'
  • say → EG 'say' = for example = e.g.

The joke is that the Python is a comedy troupe and the boa is only letters — two snakes in one clue and neither of them real. 'Say' for e.g. is a workhorse worth banking.

12 across TROPHY

Hard left turns approaching end of rally – something for the winner? (6)

Definition: something for the winner? · charade + reversal + last letter

  • Hard → H H = hard, as on pencils
  • left → PORT the nautical left
  • end of rally → Y 'end of' takes the last letter of 'rally'

'Turns' spins the whole of H + PORT round in one go before it meets the tail of 'rally'.

14 across WELL DONE

Hats off, supply a wrap (4,4)

Definition: Hats off · charade

  • supply → WELL a well is a source or supply, as in 'a well of sympathy'
  • a wrap → DONE film-set slang: 'that's a wrap' means it's finished — done

Read 'supply' as a noun and 'a wrap' as the end of filming, and the congratulation assembles itself.

15 across JAMAICA

Was she not given a choice to speak for country? (7)

Definition: country · homophone

  • Was she not given a choice → JA MAKE ’ER said aloud, 'ja make 'er?' asks whether she was forced to go

The old music-hall gag: 'My wife's gone to the West Indies.' 'Jamaica?' 'No, she went of her own accord.'

17 across STREWTH

Oath’s not a pretence, would Aussie say? (7)

Definition: Oath · homophone

  • ’s not a pretence → ’S TRUTH what is no pretence is the truth — ''s truth', short for 'God's truth'

'Strewth' contracts 'God's truth', and the truth is exactly what a pretence is not.

22 across PATOIS

Old man’s taken to island, 15 by the sound of it? (6)

Definition: 15 by the sound of it? · charade

  • Old man → PA your old man is your pa
  • to → TO supplies its own letters

PA is packed off: 'to' gives itself and 'island' shrinks to IS — a gentle build under a lovely definition.

23 across MATCHMAKER

One struck by god, Cupid (10)

Definition: Cupid · charade

  • god → MAKER the Maker — God as creator

'One struck by god' sounds like a thunderbolt victim; really it is a struck match beside the Maker.

25 across USAIN BOLT

Favourite from 15 in sprint past team featuring Gatlin, Gay etc? (5,4)

Definition: Favourite from 15 · charade

  • team featuring Gatlin, Gay etc? → USA Justin Gatlin and Tyson Gay sprinted for the United States

26 across ACKEE AND SALTFISH

A fantastic island chef takes dish from 15 (5,3,8)

Definition: dish from 15 · anagram

  • A → A the first of the sixteen anagram letters
  • island chef takes → ISLAND CHEF TAKES the rest of the fodder for 'fantastic' to cook up

'Fantastic' cooks all sixteen letters at once — a whole island chef goes into the pot for Jamaica's national dish.

1 down LISTERIA

Bacterium in rum real, is it? (8)

Definition: Bacterium · anagram

  • real, is it → REAL IS IT the eight anagram letters, posing as the setter's own doubt

'Rum' means odd or strange — a classic anagram signal — and the mock-hesitant 'is it?' is not doubt but fodder.

2 down AGAR

Setter in a dress that’s short (4)

Definition: Setter · charade + deletion

  • a → A the article supplies its own letter
  • dress → GAR a dress is GARB; 'that's short' cuts off the final letter

The surface dangles a crossword setter in a frock; the real setter works in the kitchen.

3 down STUMPY

Belly squeezed by fifth columnist in squat (6)

Definition: squat · container

  • Belly → TUM your tum is your belly
  • fifth columnist → SPY a fifth columnist is an enemy agent working within — a spy

A fifth columnist really is a spy, so the wordplay hides inside a plain description rather than an instruction. 'Squat' is wanted as an adjective, not as the crouch.

4 down UNKNOWN

Nobody drunk now nobody drinks! (7)

Definition: Nobody · hidden word

  • drunk now nobody → UNKNOWN the answer runs straight across all three words: drUNK NOW Nobody

'Drinks' pictures the phrase swallowing the answer whole — a hidden word stretched across three words of a strangely teetotal scene.

5 down COVERLET

Activation of lever in bed to get warmer at night? (8)

Definition: warmer at night? · anagram + container

  • lever → VERLE 'activation of' sets the lever's letters moving
  • bed → COT a cot is a bed — it wraps around the shuffled lever

An anagram tucked inside a container: the activated LEVER climbs into the COT. The question mark excuses the playful definition.

6 down SECOND-BEST

Inferior boss, decent after training (6-4)

Definition: Inferior · anagram

  • boss, decent → BOSS DECENT the ten anagram letters

'After training' does the rearranging — the boss emerges decent, but still only second-best.

7 down GAWAIN

Knight guzzling absinthe, originally on the wagon (6)

Definition: Knight · charade + first letters

  • guzzling absinthe → GA 'originally' takes just the two initial letters
  • the wagon → WAIN a wain is an old word for a farm wagon

'On the wagon' means teetotal, which is exactly what the absinthe is there to contradict. In a down clue 'on' simply stacks one piece above another.

13 down PLASTIC BAG

Thing concluding film featuring final lightweight item (7,3)

Definition: lightweight item · charade + container

  • Thing → BAG slang: your bag is your thing, as in 'not my bag'
  • film → PIC a pic is a picture — a film
  • final → LAST this is what PIC 'features' — LAST goes inside

'Thing' for a bag comes straight out of 'not my bag', and it is the best moment in the clue. 'Concluding' is positional rather than definitional: it says where a piece goes, not what it means.

16 down CHIPMUNK

Rodent taking wodge around lunchtime? (8)

Definition: Rodent · container

  • wodge → CHUNK a wodge is a thick lump — a chunk
  • lunchtime? → IPM 1pm, with a Roman numeral I standing for the 1

'Lunchtime?' is 1pm — written with a Roman I — a lovely bit of setter's sleight of hand.

18 down TRIFLING

Frivolous throw, cast and pitch? (8)

Definition: Frivolous · charade

  • throw, cast and pitch? → TRIFLING three ways of saying FLING — so, a tri-FLING

Count the synonyms: throw, cast and pitch are three flings, and the question mark admits the cheek.

19 down CHUKKAS

Periods on horseback where British king into himself? (7)

Definition: Periods on horseback · container + charade

  • British → UK the United Kingdom
  • king → K K = king, as in chess notation
  • himself → CHAS Chas, short for Charles — the British king himself

Reading 'himself' as the king's own familiar name is the step that unlocks it. The clue also reads straight through as a description of polo, which is the fun of it.

21 down ABACUS

Counter when a god denied church (6)

Definition: Counter · charade + deletion

  • a god → A BACCHUS A, then Bacchus, the Roman god of wine
  • church → CH the abbreviation that 'denied' strips out of Bacchus

Bacchus loses his CH — denied church — and what remains counts beads, not blessings.

22 down PERUKE

Ralph snatches ‘er wig (6)

Definition: wig · container

  • Ralph → PUKE to ralph is slang for to vomit — to puke
  • ‘er → ER 'her' with the aitch dropped

Not a man's name at all: 'ralph' is slang for being sick, and PUKE grabs the dropped-aitch 'er.

24 down BEEF

Bellyache – something in 15’s patty? (4)

Definition: Bellyache · double definition

  • Bellyache → BEEF to bellyache is to gripe — to beef
  • something in 15’s patty? → BEEF the meat inside a Jamaican beef patty

Two meanings split by the dash: the grumble, and the filling of Jamaica's best-loved pastry.
