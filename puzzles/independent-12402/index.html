Across

1 across SCAREDY-CAT

Love to escape staggering day care cost for big baby (7-3)

Definition: big baby · anagram + deletion

big baby -> SCAREDY-CAT: playground name-calling for a coward, casting the frightened person as an infant rather than as anyone actually young.

  • day care cost → DAYCARECOST eleven letters handed over whole, one of which is about to walk out
  • Love → O love is the tennis scoreline of nothing, written as the round letter that escapes
  • to escape — whatever escapes is no longer in your hands, so the letter named beside it leaves the mix before anything else happens
  • staggering — a stagger is a walk that cannot hold a line, and letters made to stagger will not hold their printed order either

Love = O is one of the conventions worth owning outright. The surface sells you a childcare invoice; the (7-3) enumeration is what gives the hyphenated playground insult away.

6 across FANG

Root of foreign affairs' west facing perspective (4)

Definition: Root · hidden word + reversal

Root -> FANG: the old anatomical word for the embedded root of a tooth, as well as for the pointed tooth itself.

  • foreign affairs' → FANG four consecutive letters straddle the gap between these two words, and they have to be picked up from the right
  • of — a piece 'of' a phrase comes from within it, a quiet way of saying the letters are already printed there rather than needing to be built; loose, and the only such signal the clue offers
  • west facing perspective — a view that faces west looks leftwards, and in an across entry leftwards means the letters are collected from the right-hand end backwards

'Root of' reads as an instruction to take an initial letter, and 'perspective' looks like it must be the definition. Neither is true: the definition is the bare first word.

10 across NOSED

Pried and snooped around when out of work (5)

Definition: Pried · anagram + deletion

Pried -> NOSED: both past-tense verbs for poking into what is not your business, as in nosing around somebody's desk.

  • snooped → SNOOPED seven letters lifted whole from the clue, two of which will not survive
  • work → OP OP is opus, a composer's work, and that pair comes out before the shuffle
  • around — letters sent around are sent every which way, with nothing left holding them in the order the clue printed
  • out of — taking one thing out of another leaves the second short by exactly the letters that were taken

'Pried and snooped' looks like a pair of synonyms padding each other out. It is a definition followed by its fodder, and the second word is there to be taken apart.

11 across ADMIRABLE

Belting delivered by officer guarding British base (9)

Definition: Belting · container + charade

Belting -> ADMIRABLE: British slang praising a thing as first-rate, the same verdict as the answer delivers in a more measured voice.

  • officer → ADMIRAL the navy's most senior officer, and the word that opens up to take something in
  • British → B B is the usual abbreviation for British, and it is the letter being guarded
  • base → E e is the base of natural logarithms, 2.718..., which mathematicians write as one letter
  • guarding — a guard stands around whatever it protects, so the letter named next goes inside the officer's word rather than beside it

'Belting' is British slang for excellent, as in a belting goal, and has nothing to do with a strap. 'Base' is mathematics, not the army camp the surface wants.

12 across ANTENNAE

Things to detect before Queen's brought back through (8)

Definition: Things to detect · container + reversal

Things to detect -> ANTENNAE: an insect's paired feelers, the organs it senses the world with, plural to match the pair every bug carries.

  • before → ANTE ante is Latin for before, the word inside antenatal and the poker ante
  • Queen → ANNE Anne is the queen crosswords reach for when ER will not fit, and she is turned round
  • brought back — an across answer reversed reads from its right-hand end, so a queen brought back arrives with her letters in that order
  • through — a piece that goes through a word passes into the middle of it instead of joining on at either end

The definition is all three opening words: an insect's feelers are how it reads the world, so 'detect' is what they do rather than an instruction to you.

13 across GOT AT

Leave rubbish uncovered (3,2)

Definition: uncovered · charade

uncovered -> GOT AT: to get at the truth is to reach and expose it, so both past-tense verbs mean brought to light.

  • Leave → GO to leave is to go, the plainest of substitutions
  • rubbish → TAT tat is British for cheap, shoddy junk, the stuff a bad market stall is full of

The clue reads as an instruction about putting the bins out. The definition is the final word on its own: to get at the truth is to reach it.

15 across TEST BED

Spooner's top unruly youth once put on trial here? (4,3)

Definition: put on trial here? · spoonerism

put on trial here? -> TEST BED: the rig an engine or a design is tried out on, so anything on trial is literally put on one.

  • top → TEST top is best; Spooner sends its B across to the other word and takes that word's T in exchange
  • unruly youth once → BED a Ted was a Teddy boy, the drape-jacketed British youth of the 1950s — its T went the other way, leaving B
  • Spooner's — the Reverend Spooner was famous for swapping the opening sounds of two words, so the first sounds of the pair change places

Spooner clues are solved backwards. You hunt for the phrase the entry turns into when its two opening sounds trade places, and only the definition tells you where to start.

17 across SUNBURN

Overheating complaint returning new students chafe about - on the contrary! (7)

Definition: Overheating complaint · container + reversal

Overheating complaint -> SUNBURN: 'complaint' in its medical sense of an ailment, this one being what too much heat does to skin.

  • new → N N is the standard abbreviation for new
  • chafe → RUB to chafe is to rub, and this is the piece that ends up enclosed
  • students → NUS the NUS is Britain's National Union of Students
  • returning — an across answer that returns is read from its right-hand end back to its left, so the assembly is built and then collected the other way
  • about - on the contrary! — 'about' on its own would wrap the chafing round the students; the contradiction turns that inside out, so the students wrap round the chafing instead

The exclamation does real work. 'On the contrary' flips the containment the words state, and only once that is settled is the whole thing collected in reverse.

19 across CASHIER

Discharge when ready, oddly forgotten sidearm (7)

Definition: Discharge · charade + alternate letters

Discharge -> CASHIER: the formal verb for dismissing an officer from the service in disgrace, one dismissal word standing for another.

  • ready → CASH the ready, or ready money, is old British slang for money in hand
  • sidearm → IER the second, fourth and sixth letters of the word, in the order they stand
  • oddly forgotten — the letters standing in the odd positions are the forgotten ones, which leaves only those in the even positions

'Discharge' looks like a pistol going off, which is exactly what a sidearm is for. It is the military verb for stripping an officer of a commission.

21 across HOLDALL

Perhaps family seat's hiding ancient baggage (7)

Definition: baggage · container

baggage -> HOLDALL: a soft bag big enough for everything you are carrying, one of the items the collective word covers.

  • family seat → HALL the Hall is the big house of an English estate, the family's seat in the county
  • ancient → OLD ancient means old, and these three letters are the ones tucked inside
  • Perhaps — a hall is only one kind of family seat, and 'perhaps' is the flag saying the word is an example rather than a synonym
  • hiding — whatever you hide, you put inside something else, so those letters sit within the word beside them

'Ancient baggage' is very nearly an insult about a person, which is the surface's whole trick. The definition is the last word standing on its own.

22 across PAGED

Called to attend case of genie inhabiting bedsit? (5)

Definition: Called to attend · container + outer letters

Called to attend -> PAGED: to page someone is to summon them by name, over a hotel's public address system or a hospital bleeper.

  • bedsit? → PAD a bedsit is a single rented room, and pad is the informal word for the place you live
  • genie → GE the first and last letters of the word, which are its case
  • case of — a case is what encloses a thing, so what you keep is the shell of the word and not its contents
  • inhabiting — an inhabitant lives inside the place named, so these letters are set within the other word

A genie in a bedsit is a lovely picture and a pure decoy: 'case of' is an instruction rather than part of the story, and the definition is the first three words.

24 across PARANOIA

This obsession's the norm - put out a broadcast! (8)

Definition: This obsession · charade + homophone

This obsession -> PARANOIA: the fixed, unshakable conviction that other people are out to get you, an obsession in the clinical sense.

  • the norm → PAR par is the standard score for a golf hole, so being on par is being at the norm
  • put out a → ANOIA to put somebody out is to annoy them, and those two words run together into five letters once you say them aloud
  • broadcast — a broadcast is heard rather than read, so what counts is the sound the words make and not the way they are spelt

'This' belongs to the definition, not the surface. Someone insisting that their suspicion is perfectly normal is the joke the whole sentence is built to tell.

27 across LAMBRUSCO

Meat course briefly cooked in red wine (9)

Definition: red wine · charade + anagram + deletion

red wine -> LAMBRUSCO: definition by example, the lightly fizzy red of Emilia-Romagna standing in for the whole category.

  • Meat → LAMB lamb is the meat, and it survives exactly as it is
  • course → COURS a course is a dish within a meal, served here one letter short
  • briefly — a word given briefly stops before it is finished, so its last letter never arrives
  • cooked — cooking breaks ingredients down and remakes them into something else, and letters cooked come out in a new arrangement

The whole surface is a menu, which is what hides the fact that only the first word comes through intact. 'In' is a link, not a container.

28 across GOOFY

Game of rugby's finale's being featured in comic strip (5)

Definition: being featured in comic strip · charade + last letter

being featured in comic strip -> GOOFY: definition by example, naming Disney's long-eared dog who has run in newspaper strips since the 1930s.

  • Game → GO go is the ancient board game of black and white stones
  • of → OF the preposition at face value, two letters lifted from the clue
  • rugby → Y the closing letter of the word, which is where its finale sits
  • finale — the finale is the last thing to happen, so only the closing letter of the word in front of it is used

'Game of rugby' reads as one phrase and is really three separate scraps. 'Being' is the definition's noun in the cartoon sense: a character, not a creature.

29 across STYX

Place that's fouled by waters in the nether regions? (4)

Definition: waters in the nether regions? · charade

waters in the nether regions? -> STYX: the river the dead are ferried across in Greek myth, so it is literally underworld water.

  • Place that's fouled → STY a sty is where pigs are kept, so it is a place fouled by definition
  • by → X three by four is written 3 x 4, so 'by' is the multiplication cross

'Nether regions' means the underworld, not anatomy, and the question mark warns that the definition is describing the place rather than naming it.

30 across ADVERTISER

English right invested in consultant influencer (10)

Definition: influencer · container

influencer -> ADVERTISER: one who sets out to sway what people think and buy, which is the whole business of buying advertising space.

  • consultant → ADVISER a consultant is an adviser, and this is the word that opens up
  • English → E E is the usual abbreviation for English
  • right → RT Rt is the abbreviation in Rt Hon, the style of a British privy counsellor
  • invested in — money invested in a fund goes into it and stays there, so these letters are placed within the word rather than alongside it

'Influencer' is doing double duty: the modern job title on the surface, and its plain older sense of somebody who sways opinion in the definition.

1 down SINK

Degenerate psychiatrist Human Resources shunted out (4)

Definition: Degenerate · deletion

Degenerate -> SINK: both verbs for slipping downwards in character, as in sinking into idleness or degenerating into it.

  • psychiatrist → SHRINK a shrink is the informal name for a psychiatrist, short for head-shrinker
  • Human Resources → HR the personnel department is HR in every office, and that pair is what gets shoved out
  • shunted out — to shunt something out is to shove it off the line and away, and what is shoved away is simply gone from the letters

The surface is an office restructure. The definition is the verb 'degenerate', not the adjective, and saying that swap out loud is what stops you hunting for a noun.

2 down ARSONISTS

Those illicitly using Tinder? (9)

Definition: Those illicitly using Tinder? · cryptic definition

Those illicitly using Tinder? -> ARSONISTS: the whole clue is one sly definition, naming fire-raisers through the kindling their crime needs.

  • Tinder? → capitalised, it reads as the dating app, where using it illicitly suggests somebody cheating on a partner
  • Those illicitly using → the setter's reading takes tinder as dry kindling, so the people using it unlawfully are the ones deliberately starting fires

The capital letter is the only lie in the clue. Lower-cased, tinder is the scrap of dry stuff you light a fire with, and using it unlawfully is a crime with a name.

3 down ENDUE

Provide experience rookies primarily wanted (5)

Definition: Provide · deletion + first letter

Provide -> ENDUE: a formal, faintly archaic verb, since to endue someone with courage is to furnish or invest them with it.

  • experience → ENDURE to experience hardship is to endure it, six letters before one goes missing
  • rookies → R the opening letter of the word, and the one that has to drop out
  • primarily — what comes primarily comes first of all, so nothing but the opening letter of the word is being asked for
  • wanted — a thing wanted is a thing lacking, the older sense of want, so that letter is missing from what you build

'Provide experience' reads as one phrase about training new staff, which is the trap: only the first word is the definition and the second is fodder.

4 down YEARNED

Craved last bit of roly-poly taken home (7)

Definition: Craved · charade + last letter

Craved -> YEARNED: both past-tense verbs for wanting something badly, though yearning leans towards longing for a person or a place.

  • roly-poly → Y the final letter of the hyphenated word, which is where its last bit sits
  • taken home → EARNED take-home pay is what you have earned once the deductions have gone
  • last bit of — the last bit of a word is its closing letter, so a single character comes across and none of the rest

A roly-poly is a British suet pudding rolled up with jam, so the surface is all nursery food while the wordplay wants a payslip.

5 down ARMLESS

Unappealing church left without members (7)

Definition: without members · deletion

without members -> ARMLESS: 'member' in its old anatomical sense of a limb, so anything lacking them has no arms.

  • Unappealing → CHARMLESS someone unappealing has no charm whatsoever
  • church → CH CH is the standard abbreviation for church, and that pair is what departs
  • left — someone who has left has departed, so the two letters named beside it walk out of the word they were in

'Member' here is the old word for a limb, the sense that survives in 'dismember'. That is what makes the definition fair rather than parliamentary.

7 down AMBIT

Play, not bothering to use golf range (5)

Definition: range · deletion

range -> AMBIT: the scope a thing reaches, so a law's ambit is the range of situations it covers rather than any distance.

  • Play → GAMBIT a gambit is an opening play in chess, a pawn sacrificed for position
  • golf → G golf is G in the radio alphabet, and that G is the letter dropped
  • not bothering to use — what you cannot be bothered to use is left out, so the letter named beside it never joins the build at all

Golf and play pull the surface towards a driving range. The letter comes from the radio alphabet that pilots and soldiers spell with, not from the sport.

8 down GREAT-UNCLE

Get confused with nuclear family member (5-5)

Definition: family member · anagram

family member -> GREAT-UNCLE: definition by example, a grandparent's brother being one relative among the many the general phrase covers.

  • Get → GET three letters straight off the page, used for their shapes and not their sense
  • nuclear → NUCLEAR seven more from the clue as printed, making ten for the enumeration
  • confused — letters that have been confused have been muddled together, so not one of them can be trusted to stay where it was printed

'Nuclear family' is the phrase the surface builds and the joke underneath it: the relative the letters spell is precisely the one a nuclear family leaves out.

9 down ORIGINAL

First dole payment served up here - a pound! (8)

Definition: First · charade + reversal

First -> ORIGINAL: the sense of earliest rather than novel, as the original of a document is the first one, before any copy.

  • dole payment → GIRO the giro was the cheque British unemployment benefit was paid by, and it goes in backwards
  • here → IN 'is she in?' means is she here, so the two words swap freely
  • a → A the article at face value, one letter lifted from the clue
  • pound → L L is the pound sign, from the Latin libra
  • served up — in a down entry, anything served up is read from the foot of the column towards the head, so the word arrives the other way round

'First dole payment' reads as one phrase about a benefits claim, which is how the definition hides: it is the single opening word and everything after it is money.

14 down STOCKPILES

Shares buildings with squirrels (10)

Definition: squirrels · charade

squirrels -> STOCKPILES: the verb borrowed from the animal's hoarding habit, so both mean laying things aside in quantity for later.

  • Shares → STOCK stock is what a shareholder holds, the shares themselves
  • buildings → PILES a pile is a grand, imposing building, as in a stately pile

'Squirrels' is a verb here, not the animals the surface puts in the roof: to squirrel something away is to hoard it out of sight.

16 down BLINDERS

Covers boxing learner's knockout performances (8)

Definition: knockout performances · container

knockout performances -> BLINDERS: British sporting slang, a blinder being a display brilliant enough to dazzle everyone watching.

  • Covers → BINDERS a binder is the stiff cover a set of loose papers is kept in
  • learner → L L is the plate a British driver displays before passing the test
  • boxing — to box a thing is to shut it in a box, so the letter named next is enclosed by the word in front of it

'Boxing learner' puts a novice in the ring, and 'knockout' keeps him there. The definition is sporting slang instead: to play a blinder is to be dazzlingly good.

18 down UNANIMOUS

Renegotiated a sum Union and all agreed (9)

Definition: all agreed · anagram

all agreed -> UNANIMOUS: describing a vote every single person backed, so both say that not one voice dissented.

  • a sum Union → ASUMUNION nine letters across three words, exactly the enumeration, taken as printed
  • Renegotiated — a deal renegotiated comes back with its terms rearranged, and letters treated the same way come back in a new order

The surface is a pay round at the negotiating table. 'Union' keeps its capital purely to look like a body in the room; it is fodder.

20 down REPOSED

Serene bride so perfectly portrayed in the mirror (7)

Definition: Serene · hidden word + reversal

Serene -> REPOSED: both adjectives for being wholly at rest, a reposed face being one with nothing troubling it.

  • bride so perfectly → REPOSED seven consecutive letters run across these three words, and they are picked up starting from the right
  • portrayed in — a portrait sits inside its frame, and this one sits inside the clue's own words rather than being built out of them
  • the mirror — a mirror hands the same letters back in the opposite order, so the run has to be collected from its far end

The wedding photograph is entirely the surface's invention. Nothing here is being described, only spelled, and the letters have been printed in front of you all along.

21 down HORMONE

Discovered more bread mainly bolsters husband's testosterone? (7)

Definition: testosterone? · charade + middle letters + deletion

testosterone? -> HORMONE: definition by example, testosterone being one of the chemical messengers the body's glands release.

  • husband → H H is the standard abbreviation for husband, and it is propped up by the rest
  • more → OR the middle pair of the word, left behind once its covering letters come off
  • bread → MONE bread is slang for money, of which only the main part is kept
  • Discovered — read as dis-covered: the covering comes off, so the outside letters go and only the middle of the word is left
  • mainly — the main part of a word is what survives, which leaves it one letter shy at the end
  • bolsters — a bolster props something up from underneath, so in a down entry these letters sit below the piece they support

The question mark is load-bearing: testosterone is one example of the thing, not a synonym for it, which is what makes the short definition fair.

23 down GAMMY

Match cut short, setter's injured (5)

Definition: injured · charade + deletion

injured -> GAMMY: British informal for a limb that is lame or painful, as in a gammy leg after a bad tackle.

  • Match → GAME a match between two sides is a game, and its final letter is the one cut
  • setter's → MY the setter is the person writing the clue, so anything of the setter's is mine
  • cut short — a word cut short stops before its end, so what joins the build is one letter less than the whole

'Setter's' means belonging to me: the setter is the voice talking to you, and that convention turns up in nearly every cryptic you will solve.

25 down NIGHT

Before dawn, time first sign of haunting spirit turned up … (5)

Definition: Before dawn · charade + reversal + first letter

Before dawn -> NIGHT: the stretch of darkness that ends when the sun comes up, so it is exactly the time preceding daybreak.

  • time → T T is the physicist's symbol for time
  • haunting → H the opening letter of the word, taken as the first sign of it
  • spirit → GIN a spirit is a distilled drink, and gin is the one the ghostly surface keeps quiet about
  • first sign of — the first sign of a word is the letter it opens with, and that single character is all this piece contributes
  • turned up — a down entry turned up is read from its foot towards its head, so the pieces are laid out and then climbed back through

'Spirit' is the pivot: the surface wants a ghost and the wordplay wants the bottle. The trailing dots hand the ghost story straight on to the next clue.

26 down DYER

… one ensuring shades stay put - sounding portentous (4)

Definition: one ensuring shades stay put · homophone

one ensuring shades stay put -> DYER: the trade that fixes colour into cloth so the hue will not wash out, 'shades' meaning tints.

  • portentous → DYER something portentous warns of disaster and so is dire; say that word aloud and spell it the other way
  • sounding — a word sounding like another is judged by ear, so you write down what you hear and not what is printed

The leading dots carry 'shades' over from the ghosts of the previous clue. Here they are colours, and the definition is the whole phrase describing a trade.
