Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,057

Across

1 across BYPASS

Sort of surgery that’s built outside town (6)

Definition: Sort of surgery · double definition

  • Sort of surgery → the heart operation that reroutes blood around a blocked artery
  • built outside town → the road laid round the edge so traffic never has to come through the middle

One word doing the same job in two worlds, which is why the clue reads as a single sentence: traffic sent round an obstruction, blood sent round an obstruction. The surgeons borrowed the roadbuilders' term.

4 across PLANCK

Physicist from college welcomed by board (6)

Definition: Physicist · container

  • board → PLANK a board of sawn timber, and it opens up after four letters
  • college → C c. is the standard abbreviation for college

'Board' is the trap: a board of governors is the picture the surface paints, a length of wood is what the wordplay wants. Max Planck's constant sits at the root of quantum theory.

9 across TRIPLE WORD SCORE

In corridor we slept badly, but it’s a bonus to be on Red Square (6,4,5)

Definition: a bonus to be on Red Square · anagram

  • corridor we slept → CORRIDORWESLEPT all three words at face value, fifteen letters to redeal

Red Square is Moscow on the surface and a premium square on a Scrabble board underneath; the capital letters are doing all the lying. Fifteen letters of fodder is a gift once you spot it.

10 across PUTSCH

Revolutionary outbreak shut PC up (6)

Definition: Revolutionary outbreak · anagram

  • shut PC → SHUTPC the word and the two initials at face value, six letters to rearrange

'Shut PC up' reads as one instruction to a noisy computer, which hides the join. German lends English this word for a sudden armed grab at power — Munich's beer-hall attempt of 1923 is the famous one.

11 across NUISANCE

Inconvenience of one square being in the shade (8)

Definition: Inconvenience · container

  • the shade → NUANCE a nuance is a shade of meaning, and the word parts after two letters
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one
  • square → S s is the standard abbreviation for square, as in a street address

The surface puts you in a garden square on a hot afternoon. The clue means shade in the other sense entirely, a fine gradation of colour or of meaning.

12 across CHESTNUT

Horse box, large cask on back (8)

Definition: Horse · charade + reversal

  • box → CHEST a chest is a box you keep things in
  • large cask → NUT a tun is a large cask, and here it is written from its far end

'Horse box' looks like one thing, a trailer, and is really the definition sitting next to the first chunk. The same word names the reddish-brown coat, the tree, and the joke told once too often.

14 across SPIRIT

Animation of Irish in light rain (6)

Definition: Animation · container

  • light rain → SPIT spitting is what rain does when it is barely falling at all
  • Irish → IR ir. is the standard abbreviation for Irish

'Irish in light rain' paints a damp afternoon in Galway, and that picture is the whole job of the surface. Animation here means liveliness, nothing to do with cartoons.

15 across YOGURT

Dairy product finally baffling in your starter for ten (6)

Definition: Dairy product · container + last letter + first letter

  • your → YOUR the word taken as written, and it opens after two letters
  • finally baffling → G the last letter of baffling
  • ten → T the first letter of ten

'Your starter for ten' is the University Challenge catchphrase, and the clue snaps it in half: 'your' is a whole word to build into, while 'ten' lends nothing but its initial.

18 across PERVERSE

For a line of poetry, deliberately obscure (8)

Definition: deliberately obscure · charade

  • For a → PER per means for each — five pounds per head is five pounds for a head
  • line of poetry → VERSE a verse is a line, or a run of lines, of poetry

The comma is the only thing marking the split, and the surface hides it well: 'For a line of poetry' reads as an introduction rather than as two chunks of the answer.

21 across SUPPLIER

Caterer is purple with rage (8)

Definition: Caterer · anagram

  • is purple → ISPURPLE both words at face value, eight letters for the pile — the little verb is material, not joinery

'Purple with rage' is a set phrase and the clue quietly splits it, taking one half as raw material and the other as the instruction. Set phrases are where setters hide the seam.

22 across PISCES

Go round church for a sign from heaven (6)

Definition: a sign from heaven · container

  • Go → PISS the vulgar verb for going to the lavatory, and it splits before its last letter
  • church → CE CE is the Church of England, the standing abbreviation for church

'A sign from heaven' sounds like an omen or a thunderbolt, and means one of the twelve star signs, the fishes. The rude little verb underneath is doing all the work.

24 across FALKLAND ISLANDS

Make landfall, and kiss ground in remote region (8,7)

Definition: remote region · anagram

  • landfall, and kiss → LANDFALLANDKISS all three words go into the pile, fifteen letters to redeal

Sailors reaching land and kissing the earth is one clean picture, and every letter of it is material — including the little 'and', which looks like joinery and is not.

25 across ERRORS

Fears not starting boobs (6)

Definition: boobs · deletion

  • Fears → TERRORS fears are terrors, and this is the word before its front letter comes off

'Fears not' reads as archaic reassurance, which is the whole disguise — it is really an instruction to dock a letter. 'Boobs' is the schoolboy word for blunders, not anything ruder.

26 across BETTER

Much recovered, I’ll wager (6)

Definition: Much recovered · double definition

  • Much recovered → the comparative of well — 'much' is the giveaway, since it only ever qualifies a comparative
  • I’ll wager → one who lays a bet, the agent noun that goes with the verb

Two senses that share a spelling and nothing else: the health one, where you are on the mend, and the racing one, where you are the person holding the ticket.

Down

1 down BOROUGH

Town’s nasty smell unhealthy (7)

Definition: Town · charade

  • nasty smell → BO body odour, the two initials the deodorant adverts made everybody know
  • unhealthy → ROUGH feeling rough is feeling ill, off colour rather than coarse

The apostrophe-s looks possessive, as though the town owned the smell. It is only an equals sign, joining the definition to the two chunks that follow.

2 down PEPYS

Diarist looks furtively for audience (5)

Definition: Diarist · homophone

  • looks furtively → PEPYS to peep is to look furtively, and the seventeenth-century spelling of the surname is nothing like the noise it makes

Samuel Pepys said his own name 'peeps', which is the whole joke. Old surnames keep spellings that stopped matching their sounds centuries ago, and setters live on them.

3 down STEPHEN

He spent wildly for an old king (7)

Definition: an old king · anagram

  • He spent → HESPENT both words go into the pile at face value, seven letters to redeal

'He spent wildly' is a character sketch, and a sketch is where fodder hides. The definition is deliberately vague: any old king, and only the letters say which.

5 down LADDIES

Women protecting daughters and sons maybe (7)

Definition: sons maybe · container

  • Women → LADIES the everyday polite word, and it parts after two letters
  • daughters → D d is the standard abbreviation for daughter

The plural is paid for inside the word: the inserted D lands right beside the D the women already had, so a pair of daughters sits in the middle. 'Maybe' flags sons as one example of many.

6 down NECTARINE

Fruit, entire can demolished (9)

Definition: Fruit · anagram

  • entire can → ENTIRECAN both words at face value, nine letters for the pile

A whole tin being crushed is a picture, and the comma after 'Fruit' is the only sign that the definition has already finished. One-word definitions at the front are easy to read straight past.

7 down KARACHI

Dakar, a Chinese controlled city (7)

Definition: city · hidden word

  • Dakar, a Chinese → KARACHI the run starts halfway through the first word and finishes inside the third, straight across both gaps

The surface is geopolitics — a west African capital, a port under Chinese control — and the country it makes you think of is the wrong one on purpose. Only 'city' is doing definition duty.

8 down BONNET

Headgear of good French fabric (6)

Definition: Headgear · charade

  • good French → BON bon is the French for good, and naming the language is what licenses a foreign word
  • fabric → NET net is the open-weave cloth of curtains and veils

'French' points backwards at 'good', not forwards at 'fabric', and that hinge is the whole difficulty. A nationality in a clue is usually there to license a word from that language.

13 down STUD POKER

A game to stir up the boss? (4,5)

Definition: A game · charade

  • the boss → STUD a boss is the raised knob on a shield or a door, which is also a stud
  • to stir up → POKER the iron kept by the fire, the thing that does the prodding

The question mark licenses the whimsy: read as a compound, the two chunks describe an implement for prodding a knob. The definition is the card game dealt with some cards face up.

16 down OVULATE

Produce eggs to value specially (7)

Definition: Produce eggs · anagram

  • to value → TOVALUE both words go into the pile, seven letters in all

'To' is material here, not joinery, and that is the trap: the eye reads 'to value' as an infinitive and hands the clue two letters it needs.

17 down THIN AIR

Into which to disappear up Everest? (4,3)

Definition: Into which to disappear up Everest? · cryptic definition

  • up Everest → the surface reading, and a true one: the higher you climb the less atmosphere there is to breathe
  • Into which to disappear → the setter's reading — the everyday phrase for vanishing without trace, where what you vanish into is no place at all

Both readings run at once, which is what a cryptic definition lives on: the idiom for vanishing, and the literal fact about altitude. The question mark is the setter admitting there is no separable wordplay to find.

18 down PARADE

Show off row of shops (6)

Definition: Show off · double definition

  • Show off → the verb sense: to march a thing up and down in front of everybody, deliberate display
  • row of shops → the noun British estates use for their little terrace of shops, a shopping street in miniature

Both senses come off the same drill ground: soldiers marching past for show, and shops lined up in one straight row. It is the estate-agent sense that catches solvers out.

19 down RIPOSTE

I contribute online in extremely rude retort (7)

Definition: retort · container + outer letters

  • extremely rude → RE the outer letters of rude, and they part company to let the rest sit between them
  • I → I the pronoun taken as the letter it is
  • contribute online → POST to post is to put something up on a site for everyone to read

'I contribute online' is a whole sentence on the surface and two separate pieces underneath. The word began in fencing, as the return thrust made straight after a parry.

20 down SLENDER

Slim line in transmitter (7)

Definition: Slim · container

  • transmitter → SENDER whoever transmits a message is the sender, and the word opens after one letter
  • line → L l is the standard abbreviation for line

'Transmitter' suggests a radio mast and means only the person at the other end of the message. Two very short pieces make this one hard to break at the right place.

23 down SMART

Domestic equipment now may be so fashionable (5)

Definition: Domestic equipment now may be so · double definition

  • Domestic equipment now may be so → the new sense: a fridge or a speaker with a chip in it that talks to your phone
  • fashionable → the old sense, about how a person looks — well turned out, in style

'Now' is the honest part of the clue: the computing sense arrived in about a decade, and a kettle you can start from the bus would have made no sense before it.
