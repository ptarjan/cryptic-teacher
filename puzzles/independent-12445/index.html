Across

1 across TAKE IT OR LEAVE IT

You shouldn't have holiday sex with wretched Keir to please yourself (4,2,2,5,2)

Definition: please yourself · charade + anagram

  • You shouldn't have → TA what you say when handed a present, in its shortest form: ta, meaning thanks
  • Keir to → KEITOR the Labour leader's first name and the little word after it, six letters between them
  • holiday → LEAVE leave is the time off work a soldier or an employee is granted
  • sex → IT it has been a coy word for sex since the days of the It girl

Both 'ta' and 'you shouldn't have' are what you say when someone hands you a present, which is the joke holding the front of the clue up.

9 across SOMEDAY

One quits newspapers possibly drowning in sauce sooner or later (7)

Definition: sooner or later · deletion + container

  • sauce → SOY soy is the sauce in the little bottle on the table
  • newspapers → MEDIA newspapers are one branch of the media
  • One → I the Roman numeral for one, and the letter that walks out

The 'one' quitting is a numeral rather than a resigning journalist, and 'possibly' is the flag that newspapers stand for something broader.

10 across STRANGE

To choke, losing heart in Wimbledon, is hard to explain (7)

Definition: hard to explain · deletion

  • To choke → STRANGE to choke someone is to STRANGLE them, and this is that verb with one letter pulled out
  • Wimbledon → L nine letters, so the fifth stands at the dead centre

The surface is a player bottling it on Centre Court, and Wimbledon is in the clue to be counted rather than played.

11 across ASTRAY

Vauxhall car approaching yard is off course (6)

Definition: off course · charade

  • Vauxhall car → ASTRA the Astra is Vauxhall's long-running family hatchback
  • yard → Y Y is the abbreviation for a yard on a ruler

Vauxhall is a car marque, a London district and a football ground; only the badge on the bonnet is wanted here.

12 across STEADIED

Made firm second date arranged with bombshell (8)

Definition: Made firm · charade + anagram

  • second → S s is the second on a stopwatch
  • date → TEAD the four letters of the word, set out in a new order
  • bombshell → IED an IED is an improvised explosive device, a home-made bomb

A bombshell is a blonde on the surface and a piece of ordnance in the wordplay, and 'second date' is a romance rather than an abbreviation plus fodder.

14 across OPPOSES

Republicans originally redacted models' faces (7)

Definition: faces · deletion + charade

  • Republicans → OP the Republicans are the Grand Old Party, the GOP, docked of the letter it opens with
  • models' → POSES a model poses for the camera

GOP for the American Republicans is the piece to bank here, and 'faces' is the definition working hard to look like a noun about cheekbones.

17 across CLIENTS

Extremely cool remix of Strokes' Last Nite for those that pay (7)

Definition: those that pay · charade + outer letters + last letter + anagram

  • cool → CL the letters at either edge of the word, with the middle discarded
  • Strokes' → S the last letter of the band's name, and nothing else of it
  • Nite → NITE the song title's four letters, ready to be rebuilt

The Strokes released Last Nite in 2001, and the apostrophe does double duty: possession on the surface, and a pointer to one letter in the wordplay.

20 across LEISURE

The French certain to embrace current convenient opportunity (7)

Definition: convenient opportunity · container + charade

  • The French → LE the definite article as the French write it
  • certain → SURE someone certain of a thing is sure of it
  • current → I I is the physicist's symbol for electric current

'Current' is the electrical kind, not the present-day kind, and the French article is a crossword staple worth banking alongside LA and LES.

21 across ASTYLAR

Tips from angry sub: "Trying your luck annoys readers wanting columns" (7)

Definition: wanting columns · first letters

  • angry sub: "Trying your luck annoys readers → ASTYLAR the seven words each hand over the letter they begin with, in the order they are printed

A sub-editor's columns are inches of newsprint and an architect's are pillars, which is the whole of the joke; the quoted grumble is the fodder.

22 across TOGETHER

United’s lead cut by player’s mistake ... (8)

Definition: United · container

  • lead → TETHER the lead you walk a dog on is a tether
  • player’s mistake → OG OG is how a scoreline records an own goal

The capital U makes it a football club and the ellipsis runs the sentence on into 25 across, so nothing in the surface admits it is a clue about dogs.

25 across ANKARA

... short cross Rayan Aït-Nouri initially returned back to Donnarumma for City (6)

Definition: City · charade + deletion + first letters + reversal + last letter

  • cross → ANK an ANKH is the looped Egyptian cross, one letter shorter here
  • Rayan Aït-Nouri → AR the initials of the defender's two names, read the other way round
  • Donnarumma → A the letter the goalkeeper's name ends on

The dots carry the sentence on from 22 across, and the surface is football commentary: both players named turn out for the other City in the clue.

29 across BONDAGE

Who's licensed to kill twisted, barely legal kink? (7)

Definition: kink · charade

  • Who's licensed to kill → BOND the licence to kill belongs to 007, the agent Ian Fleming named
  • twisted → surface only: it colours the sentence and hands over no letters
  • barely legal → AGE being of age is what makes a thing legal, and barely of age is only just that

Bond has held his licence since 1953, and 'barely legal' is the surface leering at the age of consent while quietly naming a three-letter word.

30 across ON A ROLL

List of elite US students said to be doing well (2,1,4)

Definition: doing well · homophone

  • List of elite US students → ON A ROLL American schools post an honor roll of their best students, and an English mouth drops the aitch and runs the words together

The American spelling matters: an honour roll would not sound the same. Said aloud without the aitch, the words re-divide into a different phrase entirely.

31 across THEATRICAL AGENT

Professional representative could be awful charlatan getting cut repeatedly: first 12.5% of earnings (10,5)

Definition: Professional representative · anagram + deletion + first letter

  • charlatan → CHARLATAN all nine letters go into the mix
  • getting → GETTI the seven-letter word loses its final letter, then loses the next one as well
  • earnings → E eight letters, so an eighth of them is the first letter and no more

The percentage is a letter count in disguise, and the charlatan taking repeated cuts of someone's earnings is the surface's joke about commission.

Down

1 down TESLA COIL

Labour introducing shifting scale that makes sparks fly (5,4)

Definition: that makes sparks fly · container + anagram

  • Labour → TOIL labour is toil, the plain word for hard graft
  • scale → ESLAC the five letters of the word, shifted into a new order

Labour with a capital L is the party and a scale is a fish's or a musician's; the wordplay wants hard work and a measuring device instead.

2 down KEMPT

Orderly looked after nurses close to sanatorium (5)

Definition: Orderly · container + last letter

  • looked after → KEPT to look after a thing is to keep it
  • sanatorium → M the letter the word finishes on

The surface is a hospital ward, where an orderly is a member of staff. The definition wants the adjective, a word almost nobody uses except with un- in front of it.

3 down INDIA

Third off price for ham? (5)

Definition: for ham? · middle letter + substitution

  • price → I the third of its five letters, and the middle one

Radio hams spell letters out with the international alphabet, so one letter and a whole word are the same thing said two ways. The pork and the discount are pure surface.

4 down ORYX

One beastly Conservative's unguarded kiss (4)

Definition: One beastly · deletion + charade

  • Conservative's → ORY a Conservative is a TORY, and with its 's that is five letters; the two on sentry duty at either end walk off
  • kiss → X the X scribbled at the foot of a letter

The apostrophe-s looks like possession and is really the fifth letter of the fodder, which is what makes the sentries land where they do.

5 down LISZT

Key expert's cant in hearing (5)

Definition: Key expert · homophone

  • cant → LISZT to cant is to tilt, and a ship that tilts is said to list; that is the word being spoken

The surface is a locksmith or a piano tuner talking jargon. 'Cant' also means insincere talk, which is the sense the clue wants you to hear first.

6 down AERIALIST

TV show possibly exposed sporting star as one who gets high (9)

Definition: one who gets high · container + deletion

  • star → A LIST the A-list is where the famous names sit, so an A-lister is a star
  • TV show → ERIA a SERIAL is one kind of TV show, with the letter at each end taken off

'Sporting star' reads as an athlete and wants a celebrity, and 'one who gets high' points at a trapeze rather than a habit.

7 down ENNUI

Europeans disturbed by news on international lack of interest (5)

Definition: lack of interest · container + charade

  • Europeans → EU the union the Europeans built, in its two-letter form
  • news → NN N is the abbreviation for new, and a doubled letter is how crosswords write its plural
  • international → I I is the standard short form of international, as on a fixture list

Being disturbed by news is what happens to a reader and what happens to the letters, which is the whole of the surface's trick.

8 down TREAD

Cover of The Modern Age sampling Queen track (5)

Definition: track · container + charade + outer letters

  • The → TE the letters at the two edges of the word, its covers
  • Modern Age → AD AD, anno domini, is the era we are still counting in
  • Queen → R R for Regina, the Latin the monarch signs with

The italics make The Modern Age one Strokes title, and the wordplay refuses to read it that way: the first word and the other two are handled separately.

13 down ASHE

American woman who won five Grand Slam titles (4)

Definition: who won five Grand Slam titles · charade

  • American → A A is the standard abbreviation for American
  • woman → SHE the pronoun you use of a woman

The wordplay is two small pieces, so the whole clue rests on one false statement: it calls the player a woman, and the player was not one.

15 down PHI

Character from The Odyssey that’s acidic? (3)

Definition: Character from The Odyssey · charade

  • acidic → PH how acid a liquid is gets measured on the pH scale
  • acidic? → I Roman one: a reading of 1 on that scale is about as acid as liquids get

Homer wrote in Greek, so a character from his poem is a letter of that alphabet rather than a hero. The scale runs 0 to 14, and the numeral is written the Roman way.

16 down STUTTGART

Four times Sugar wasted capital in Baden-Württemberg (9)

Definition: capital in Baden-Württemberg · anagram

  • Four times → TTTT t is the scientist's symbol for time, and four of them are asked for
  • Sugar → SUGAR the whole word goes into the mix

The Sugar of the surface is Alan, and 'four times' looks like a frequency until you notice that time has a one-letter symbol.

17 down CIAO

Meloni's opener: "Trump's intelligence? Zero!" (4)

Definition: Meloni's opener · charade

  • Trump's intelligence → CIA the Central Intelligence Agency answers to the American president
  • Zero → O a nought is written as a round O

Giorgia Meloni is Italy's prime minister, so the word she opens with is Italian, and the surface's joke about presidential intelligence is doing the misdirection.

18 down NIL

Collar's missing a zip (3)

Definition: zip · deletion

  • Collar's → NIL to collar a suspect is to NAIL them, and this is that word one letter lighter

The surface is a jacket with a broken fastening; the definition is the American slang for nothing, and 'collar' has to be read as a verb.

19 down STREAMLET

Insignificant banker's unacceptable service on the back of jet (9)

Definition: Insignificant banker · charade

  • jet → STREAM a jet of water is a stream of it
  • unacceptable service → LET a serve that clips the net is a let and will not be accepted, so it is taken again

Banker for river is one of the oldest crossword conventions, on the grounds that a river is the thing with banks; the surface's bonus and bank job are decoys.

22 down TIBET

Half-hearted old Tory enforcer upset region in dispute (5)

Definition: region in dispute · deletion + reversal

  • old Tory enforcer → TEBIT Norman TEBBIT was the Conservatives' chairman and hard man, here with one of the twins at his centre gone

Tebbit was Margaret Thatcher's party chairman, the 'on yer bike' man; the setter likes the name for the pair of letters sitting at its middle.

23 down GENRE

Eagle going around after golf class (5)

Definition: class · reversal + charade

  • golf → G golf is how the radio alphabet says G
  • Eagle → ENRE an ERNE is a sea eagle, its four letters turned about

The surface is a round of golf, where an eagle is two under par; the wordplay wants the bird, and the erne is the crossword's standard one.

24 down ELEMI

Space kept by priest for fragrant resin (5)

Definition: fragrant resin · container

  • priest → ELI Eli is the Old Testament priest who brought up Samuel
  • Space → EM an em is the printer's space, as wide as the letter M is cast

Two conventions worth banking: the biblical three-letter priest, and the typesetter's em, which turns up whenever a clue mentions space or width.

26 down NYALA

New York city cops heading to apprehend animal (5)

Definition: animal · container + charade + first letter

  • New York → NY the state's two-letter postal abbreviation
  • city → LA LA is the shorthand for Los Angeles
  • apprehend → A the letter this word starts with

'New York city cops' reads as one police force and splits into two separate abbreviations, while 'cops' turns from a noun into the verb that does the enclosing.

27 down ALONE

Gay, maybe rampant and single! (5)

Definition: single · reversal

  • Gay → ENOLA the B-29 that carried the Hiroshima bomb was the Enola Gay, named for the pilot's mother

The exclamation mark is there to keep you reading 'gay' as an adjective; the wordplay wants the surname, and the first name that famously goes in front of it.

28 down SOMA

Huxley's soporific book on revolution (4)

Definition: Huxley's soporific · reversal

  • book → AMOS AMOS is one of the twelve minor prophets and a book of the Old Testament in its own right

Short books of the Bible are worth learning as spare letters, and 'revolution' keeps the surface political while doing something purely mechanical.
