Across

1 across BEASTIE

Wine-drinking insect, creepy-crawly (7)

Definition: creepy-crawly · container

A beastie is the jokey word for a bug or small creature, which is exactly what a creepy-crawly is.

  • insect → BEE the crossword's standard three-letter insect, the one that lives in a hive
  • Wine → ASTI Asti is the sparkling white from Piedmont, and four handy letters
  • drinking — What you drink goes down inside you, so the wine's letters end up swallowed by the insect's.

Asti and the bee are both crossword regulars: an Italian sparkler whenever wine is mentioned, a bee whenever an insect is. Learn the pair and clues like this open in seconds.

5 across HASHISH

Pot is as hot, thrice stirred (7)

Definition: Pot · anagram

'Pot' is slang for cannabis, and hashish is the resin pressed from the same plant — one street name answering another.

  • is as → ISAS four letters of fodder, exactly as they stand
  • hot, thrice → HHH h is the tap-label abbreviation for hot, and 'thrice' asks for three of them
  • stirred — Stirring a pot mixes up whatever is in it, so these letters will not stay in the order they are given.

What makes this look impossible is that half the fodder is spelled by an abbreviation: three separate aitches, one for each hot the clue orders.

10 across BABY

Not Old Spice! (4)

Definition: Not Old · double definition

As a modifier the word means young or small, the opposite of old; and it is also the stage name of one of the five Spice Girls.

  • Not Old → the negative does the defining: the word wanted is the one meaning young rather than aged
  • Spice → the group's members took nicknames — Scary, Sporty, Ginger, Posh and one more, and it is the one more that is wanted

The surface is Old Spice the aftershave, which keeps the four-word clue inside the puzzle's theme. The Spice who is not the old one is Emma Bunton's Baby.

11 across FINANCIERS

Nuts cracked by trendy bankers (10)

Definition: bankers · container

A financier lends and invests other people's money for a living, which is the banker's trade — plural answering plural.

  • Nuts → FANCIERS a nut for something is a fancier of it — pigeon nuts are pigeon fanciers
  • trendy → IN in = trendy, as in the in-crowd and the in thing
  • cracked by — Cracking a thing opens it up, and whatever did the cracking goes into the gap it made.

'Nuts cracked' is a single picture and the clue quietly splits it in two, which is why the fodder hides so well: these nuts are enthusiasts, not almonds.

12 across COCOON

Wrap up, wearing 14? (6)

Definition: Wrap up · charade

To cocoon someone is to wrap them up in snug layers, the way the silkworm's case encloses what is inside it.

  • 14 → COCO Chanel's four-letter scent — 14 across tells you what class of thing to look for
  • wearing → ON what you have on is what you are wearing

Split after four letters, the answer reads as a line from a perfume counter: Coco, on. The question mark is there to carry that whimsy.

13 across PENDANTS

Underwear, inside which butt – and dangly things (8)

Definition: dangly things · container

A pendant is an ornament hung from a chain so that it swings free, which makes the plural a set of things that dangle.

  • Underwear → PANTS in Britain pants are underwear rather than trousers
  • butt → END the butt of a rifle or a cigarette is its blunt end
  • inside which — The relative pronoun points back at the word just given, and 'inside' says the next piece goes within it.

The surface is doing its best to embarrass you, which is exactly how a plain insertion goes unnoticed. 'Butt' is the innocent word: the end of a thing.

14 across FRAGRANCE

Opium, say, kid smuggled into country (9)

Definition: Opium, say · container

Opium is an Yves Saint Laurent perfume, so 'say' flags it as one example of the class the answer names.

  • country → FRANCE the clue's country, taken whole before anything is hidden in it
  • kid → RAG to rag someone is to tease them, which is what kidding them is
  • smuggled into — Smuggling something into a place hides it inside, which is where these letters have to go.

'Opium, say' opens a drug-smuggling story and is really the definition. This is the puzzle's key: four other clues are defined only by the number 14.

16 across DAISY

Flower remains bathed in hours of sunlight (5)

Definition: Flower · container

A daisy is the small white and yellow flower of every lawn, so the general word names it by example.

  • hours of sunlight → DAY the hours between sunrise and sunset
  • remains → IS 'he remains calm' says no more than 'he is calm'
  • bathed in — Anything bathed in a liquid is surrounded by it, so this word closes over the letters put between them.

In a cryptic, 'flower' is usually a river — a thing that flows — so here the plain botanical reading is itself the misdirection.

17 across ASTIR

Stuck in Belfast, Irishman busy (5)

Definition: busy · hidden word

Astir means up and about, out of bed and already moving — the state of somebody busy rather than at rest.

  • Belfast, Irishman → ASTIR a run of five starting at the fifth letter of the city and carrying on over the comma
  • Stuck in — Something stuck in a phrase is lodged there whole, so the letters sit together and in their given order.

Two proper nouns side by side make good cover, because the eye takes them as a place and a person rather than as a string of letters to read across.

19 across SOUTH-EAST

Going west, has set out – a different way? (5-4)

Definition: a different way · anagram

'Way' means direction rather than route or method, and south-east is one of the compass's — the question mark admits the vagueness.

  • has set out → HASSETOUT nine letters of fodder, with the sun setting in the surface
  • Going west — To go west is British slang for coming to grief: a thing that has gone west has fallen apart, and so have these letters.

'Going west' looks like a reversal instruction, and an answer that is itself a compass point makes that even more tempting. The phrase is slang for falling apart.

23 across CASANOVA

Stellenbosch country certainly not embraced by wine lover (8)

Definition: lover · container + charade

Giacomo Casanova's memoirs made his name the standard word for a tireless seducer, so 'lover' is the eponym in everyday use.

  • wine → CAVA cava is Spain's sparkling wine
  • Stellenbosch country → SA Stellenbosch is the wine town outside Cape Town, so the country is South Africa, SA
  • certainly not → NO an emphatic refusal is a plain no
  • embraced by — An embrace closes round whatever it holds, so the drink named at the end wraps itself over the other letters.

Wine is mentioned three ways in one sentence — a South African region, a Spanish sparkler, and a lover of the stuff — and the definition is the single last word.

24 across NOUGAT

Sweet wrappers from Ouagadougou and Giza filling empty net (6)

Definition: Sweet · container + outer letters

Nougat is the chewy honey-and-nut confection found in the middle of a chocolate bar, and in Britain any such confection is a sweet.

  • empty net → NT NET with its middle taken out
  • Ouagadougou → OU the outer letters of the capital of Burkina Faso
  • Giza → GA the outer letters of the Egyptian city
  • wrappers from — A wrapper goes round the outside, so only the first and last letters of each name are taken.
  • empty — An empty container keeps its walls and loses everything between them, which is what happens to the middle letters.
  • filling — Filling something means going inside it, so these letters sit within the ones just emptied.

Two African cities and an empty net make a sentence about nothing at all, which is the point: every proper noun here is only a source of letters.

26 across INTIMATELY BECKHAM

14 to spray around tackle, he-man? I’m not posh, buy it! (10,7)

Definition: 14 · anagram + deletion

14 is FRAGRANCE, and Intimately Beckham is the scent sold under the footballer's name — one product standing in for the general word.

  • tackle, he-man? I’m → TACKLEHEMANIM thirteen letters of fodder, straight off the clue
  • buy it → BYIT BUY without its U, and then IT
  • to spray around — Spraying a scent sends the droplets everywhere at once, so the letters land in a new order.
  • not posh — Posh is U in the old labelling of upper-class English, so 'not posh' means the U is missing.

Posh Spice is in the surface twice over: she is the letter U being taken out, and she is married to the man whose scent this is.

27 across BLUE STRATOS

14, also Brut, set to be splashed all over (4,7)

Definition: 14 · anagram

14 is FRAGRANCE, and Blue Stratos was a men's aftershave of the seventies — one brand answering the general word.

  • also Brut, set → ALSOBRUTSET eleven letters of fodder, Brut being another aftershave and so on theme
  • to be splashed all over — Splashing a liquid all over throws it in every direction, so the letters end up anywhere but where they started.

Brut is the Fabergé aftershave the boxer Henry Cooper sold on British television, so the fodder is made of the same stuff as the answer.

28 across ANAGRAM

As ‘Curious’, Britney Spears, is for presbyterians? (7)

Definition: As ‘Curious’, Britney Spears, is for presbyterians? · cryptic definition

The whole clue is an illustration of the word: shuffle one name's letters and the other appears, which is the relation being named.

  • As ‘Curious’ → the quoted word is the scent the singer put her name to, which keeps the surface inside the puzzle's theme
  • Britney Spears, is for presbyterians → the two names hold exactly the same thirteen letters in a different order, and the clue is asking you to name that relation

Britney Spears and Presbyterians are the most celebrated pair in the business, and Paul has hung his theme on it: Curious is her perfume, so the surface stays on scent.

2 down ENAMOUR

Charm in a second cracking magical character up (7)

Definition: Charm · container + reversal

To enamour someone is to fill them with love, and to charm them is the same act of winning affection.

  • magical character up → ENUR a rune is both a letter of the old Norse alphabet and a magic sign, turned round here
  • a second → AMO 'a mo' is a moment — half a mo, back in a mo
  • cracking — Cracking a thing splits it open, and the split is where the other letters get wedged.
  • up — This is a down entry, so a piece written 'up' is read from the bottom and comes out back to front.

'Cracking' reads as praise, as though the clue were reviewing a magical character in a novel. It is the instruction that splits the turned-round word open.

3 down SAY-SO

Express then: green light (3-2)

Definition: green light · charade

A say-so is the permission or authority to go ahead, which is just what giving the green light grants.

  • Express → SAY to express a view is to say it
  • then → SO 'so' introduces what follows from something — and so, and then

The colon does the work of a link word, and both halves are such ordinary words that neither looks like fodder. 'Then' for SO is worth filing away.

4 down INFANTA

Princess seemingly enjoying orange bubble bath? (7)

Definition: Princess · charade

An infanta is the Spanish and Portuguese title for a king's daughter who is not the heir — a princess by another name.

  • enjoying → IN the only way to enjoy a bath is to be in it
  • orange bubble bath → FANTA the orange fizzy drink: all bubbles, if not the sort you wash in

The picture is invented purely for the joke — a royal child sitting in a tub of orange fizz — and the question mark owns up to it.

6 down AWNING

Canopy breaking, roof shearing off (6)

Definition: Canopy · deletion

An awning is the canvas canopy wound out over a shop front or a caravan door to keep sun and rain off.

  • breaking → DAWNING when day breaks it is dawning — the word taken whole, before anything is lost
  • roof → in a down entry the roof is the letter at the top, here the D
  • shearing off — To shear is to cut clean away in one stroke, so the part named simply goes and nothing replaces it.

'Canopy breaking' reads as a tent coming down in the wind, which hides both jobs: 'breaking' is a word to be taken apart, not a description of the canopy.

7 down HAI KARATE

Short poem on a rank 14 (3,6)

Definition: 14 · charade + deletion

14 is FRAGRANCE, and Hai Karate was a brand of aftershave — one particular scent standing in for the general word.

  • Short poem → HAIK a haiku is the Japanese three-line poem, here minus its final letter
  • a rank → ARATE the clue's own A, then RATE — to rank things is to rate them
  • Short — A word called short is docked of a letter, so the poem's name arrives one letter light.
  • on — In a down entry, one thing sitting on another is above it, so these letters come first.

Hai Karate was a cheap British aftershave of the early seventies, advertised with the gag that wearing it obliged you to fend women off — hence the martial art in the surface.

9 down INSPECTORATES

Test teams heading for Pakistan, cricket, say, talks about that (13)

Definition: Test teams · container + charade + first letter

An inspectorate is an official body that examines and tests — schools, prisons, factories — so several of them really are teams that test.

  • cricket, say → INSECT a cricket is one kind of insect, and 'say' marks it as an example
  • heading for Pakistan → P the opening letter of the country's name
  • talks → ORATES to orate is to talk formally, at length and in public
  • heading for — A heading sits at the top of what it names, so only the opening letter of the country is wanted.
  • about that — 'About' means round, and 'that' points back at the letter just taken — the insect closes over it.

The surface is an England cricket tour and not one word of it means that: the teams are official bodies, and the cricket is the insect that chirps in the grass.

15 down GRIMACING

Dark art of server, visibly in pain (9)

Definition: visibly in pain · charade

To grimace is to screw your face up, which is how somebody in pain shows it without saying a word.

  • Dark → GRIM a grim mood, or a grim joke, is a dark one
  • art of server → ACING the tennis server's art is acing — serving balls the other player cannot touch

'Dark art' wants to be read as one phrase, sorcery or a waiter's black arts, and it is two pieces from two different worlds.

18 down SHANNON

Pipe down, soon crossing new river (7)

Definition: river · charade + container

The Shannon is Ireland's longest river, so the bare word 'river' defines it by example rather than by meaning.

  • Pipe down → SH sh! is what you say to make somebody be quiet
  • soon → ANON anon is the old word for soon — 'I will come anon'
  • new → N n is the standard abbreviation for new
  • crossing — One thing crossing another cuts through the middle of it, so the letter lands inside the word.

A whole instruction to be quiet compresses into two letters here, and the river runs 240 miles across Ireland — the longest in either island.

20 down TINTYPE

Old still, preserve order (7)

Definition: Old still · charade

A tintype is a Victorian photograph made straight onto a metal plate, and a still is a single photograph as against a moving picture.

  • preserve → TIN to tin food is to preserve it in a sealed can
  • order → TYPE an order of animals, or of architecture, is a type of it

'Old still' sounds like a whisky-maker's kit rusting in a barn, and 'preserve order' reads as one phrase about policing. Both halves are disguised the same way.

21 down SPATULA

Kitchen implement: it has inspired me to gather up last bit of fondant! (7)

Definition: Kitchen implement · container + last letter

A spatula is the flat flexible blade used for scraping out bowls and turning food, which the general phrase names exactly.

  • it → SA 'it' is sex appeal in the old sense, and SA is the abbreviation crosswords keep alive
  • me → PAUL the setter signs this puzzle Paul, so 'me' is his name
  • fondant → T the final letter of fondanT, the icing of the surface
  • has inspired — To inspire is literally to breathe in, so what comes next is taken inside the letters already there.
  • to gather up — Gathering things up is taking them into your arms, so this piece closes round what follows it.
  • last bit of — The last bit of a word is its final letter, and that single letter is all this phrase hands over.

Setters use 'me' for their own pen name, and this one has built himself into the word: two nested insertions, with the icing landing dead centre.

22 down SONATA

Piece noted through observations on a tangent (6)

Definition: Piece · hidden word

A sonata is a composition for one or two instruments, so 'piece' is the plain musical sense of an everyday word.

  • observations on a tangent → SONATA a run of six beginning on the last letter of the first word and crossing three gaps
  • noted through — To note a thing is to spot it, and spotting it through a phrase means reading straight across the words.

A hidden word that crosses three word breaks is the hardest kind to see, and the musical surface — noted, observations — keeps your eye on meaning rather than letters.

25 down UZBEK

Of Asian citizens, fifth of which live in European country (5)

Definition: Of Asian citizens · container + charade + fifth letter

Uzbekistan sits in Central Asia, so Uzbek is the adjective belonging to one nation of Asian citizens — a definition by example.

  • European country → UK the United Kingdom, taken as its two-letter form
  • fifth of which → Z 'which' points back at citizens, and its fifth letter is the Z
  • live → BE to live is to be — let live, let be
  • fifth of — Counting five letters into the word just named picks out a single letter and discards the rest.
  • in — The plainest container instruction there is: whatever lives in the country sits inside its letters.

'Fifth of which' sends you back to a word the definition has already used, which is why the clue feels as though it has nothing left to give you.
