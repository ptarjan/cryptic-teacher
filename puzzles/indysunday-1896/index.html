Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,896

Across

1 across COSSET

Baby group starts with some trigonometry (6)

Definition: Baby · charade

  • some trigonometry → COS cos is the short form of cosine, one of the three standard trig functions
  • group → SET a set is a group of things, the mathematician's word for a collection

The surface is a toddler class. Both halves of the wordplay are maths words, which is the only reason 'group' is sitting next to 'trigonometry' at all.

4 across DECAMP

Hit with tear gas from behind, quietly scarper (6)

Definition: scarper · reversal + charade

  • Hit with tear gas → DECAM someone hit with tear gas has been MACED, and that word is turned round here
  • quietly → P p is piano in music, the mark telling a player to be quiet

Mace is a brand of tear-gas spray that became a verb for using it, so five of the six letters arrive in one word before it is spun round.

8 across DISHARMONY

Clashing plate host eats on (10)

Definition: Clashing · charade + container

  • plate → DISH a dish is a plate, or the food served on one
  • host → ARMY a host is a great multitude, the biblical sense that also means an army
  • on → ON the two letters go in whole, between the multitude's third and fourth

'Host' is the multitude, not the man handing round the plates, and that single word is where the whole surface picture comes from.

9 across MAID

Help island cutting bananas (4)

Definition: Help · container

  • bananas → MAD bananas is slang for crazy, and the three letters open up after two
  • island → I I is the standard map abbreviation for island

The clue reads as a shopping trip and none of it is about fruit or holidays. 'Help' is the noun for a servant, not the verb it looks like.

10 across BLOW HOT AND COLD

Three types of buffet show indecision (4,3,3,4)

Definition: show indecision · charade

  • Three types of buffet → BLOW to buffet is to strike, so a buffet is a blow — the sense with no food in it
  • types of buffet → HOTANDCOLD the other two are the ways a spread is served, and the pair comes with its own linking word

Counting a punch among the buffets is the joke: one word carries a strike and a laid table at once, and supplies every part of the answer.

11 across GROTTO

Underground shelter arrived at with input from Republican (6)

Definition: Underground shelter · container

  • arrived at → GOTTO to have got to a place is to have arrived at it; the word splits after its first letter
  • Republican → R R is the letter American usage prints beside a Republican politician's name

The politics is a decoy — no party is involved past the single letter — and the shelter is as likely to be a garden folly as a real cave.

13 across AMARETTO

Not drinking after a dreadful experience with old liqueur (8)

Definition: liqueur · charade

  • a → A the article is taken as the letter it is
  • dreadful experience → MARE a mare is British slang for a nightmare, a thoroughly bad time of it
  • Not drinking → TT TT is short for teetotal, said of someone who never touches alcohol
  • old → O O is the standard abbreviation for old

Reading it as one sentence about a hangover is the trap. It is four separate pieces, and 'old' is here only to hand over its usual letter.

15 across CONSOMME

Prisoner's muddled memo that's clear for Spooner? (8)

Definition: that's clear for Spooner · charade + anagram

  • Prisoner's → CONS a con is a convict, and the possessive apostrophe-s hands over a letter too
  • memo → OMME the four letters of MEMO, stirred into a different order

The capital S promises the Reverend and his swapped syllables, and delivers none. Read him as a spooner, someone wielding a spoon, and the clear soup is the answer.

17 across TANACH

Hebrew bible Ruth can attempt in part, reading backwards (6)

Definition: Hebrew bible · hidden word + reversal

  • Ruth can attempt → TANACH six consecutive letters, read the wrong way, spanning the end of one word and the start of the next

'Ruth' is a book of the Hebrew bible, so it reads as more of the definition rather than as the letters doing the work. That is the whole trap.

19 across OBSERVATION CAR

Jockey in bar over Ascot where there are track-side views (11,3)

Definition: where there are track-side views · anagram

  • in bar over Ascot → INBAROVERASCOT fourteen letters of fodder, exactly the length the enumeration asks for
  • Jockey → anagram indicator

Ascot is a racecourse, so 'track-side' sounds like horses. The track is a railway one, and the answer is a carriage built for watching it go by.

22 across STYE

Infection small yet spreading (4)

Definition: Infection · anagram

  • small → S S is the clothing-label abbreviation for small
  • yet → YET three more letters of fodder, taken exactly as they stand
  • spreading → anagram indicator

Four letters and every word working: 'small' is the size on a label rather than an adjective describing how bad the infection is.

23 across PROVIDENCE

God's will supporting study into sin (10)

Definition: God's will · charade + container

  • supporting → PRO to be pro something is to be in favour of it, supporting it
  • sin → VICE a vice is a sin or moral failing, and it splits after two letters
  • study → DEN a den is the small room at home you shut yourself away to work in

It reads as a sermon and is built from three flat synonyms. 'Study' is the room rather than the activity, which is the piece most solvers reach for last.

24 across CLOSED

Exclusive clubs get rid of head of diversity (6)

Definition: Exclusive · charade + first letter

  • clubs → C C is the card player's abbreviation for the suit of clubs
  • get rid of → LOSE to lose something is to get rid of it
  • head of diversity → D diversity supplies its opening letter only

'Head of diversity' is a job title in the surface and a single letter in the wordplay, and the sentence about a members' club depends on you reading the first.

25 across LEFTIE

Sinister type didn't eat filling in pies (6)

Definition: Sinister type · charade + middle letters

  • didn't eat → LEFT the food you did not eat is what you have gone and left
  • pies → IE the two letters in the middle of the word, with the crust taken off

The surface promises a villain and there is none: sinister is Latin for left, a sense heraldry still uses, so the type meant is a political one.

Down

1 down CLIMBER

Sportsperson physically flexible and cold at the top (7)

Definition: Sportsperson · charade

  • cold → C C is the standard abbreviation for cold, the letter on a tap
  • physically flexible → LIMBER limber describes a body that bends and stretches easily

The clue names the supple half first and only then says where the cold letter goes, which is a habit of down clues worth getting used to.

2 down SCHMO

Scot mostly takes His Majesty for a fool (5)

Definition: a fool · container + deletion

  • Scot mostly → SCO SCOT drops its last letter, and what remains opens up after its first two
  • His Majesty → HM HM is the formal abbreviation for His Majesty, as in HM Government

Nothing here is Scottish once the clue is parsed. The word itself is American slang out of Yiddish, which is why an insult this blunt can be clued so briskly.

3 down EARTHWORM

Ground elder's back - what, more soil improver? (9)

Definition: soil improver · anagram + last letter

  • elder's back → R the closing letter of ELDER joins the letters waiting to be jumbled
  • what, more → WHATMORE eight letters of fodder, which with the one above makes the nine wanted
  • Ground → anagram indicator

Ground elder is a real garden weed, and that is the picture the setter is selling. The two words are doing entirely separate jobs here.

4 down DOYEN

Senior member aching after party (5)

Definition: Senior member · charade

  • party → DO a do is a party or function in British usage
  • aching → YEN a yen is an aching longing for something

Both halves are short words hiding in plain sight: the party is the British do rather than a political one, and 'aching' has to be read as a noun.

5 down COME CLEAN

One half of Macclesfield stupidly admit criminal activity (4,5)

Definition: admit criminal activity · anagram + deletion

  • One → ONE the word itself goes into the mix whole, all three letters
  • half of Macclesfield → MACCLE Macclesfield is a Cheshire town of twelve letters, and its first six join the fodder
  • stupidly → anagram indicator

Half of a word means half of its letters, and nothing in the surface says which half — the count is what settles it.

6 down MAILLOT

Jersey put in the post with sale item (7)

Definition: Jersey · charade

  • put in the post → MAIL to mail something is to put it in the post
  • sale item → LOT a lot is the numbered item an auctioneer puts up for sale

'Jersey' pulls hard towards the Channel Island, and the sale and the post keep that shipping picture going. It is the garment.

7 down CONTRACEPTION

Carrie having squeezed out four, mechanism to limit that? (13)

Definition: mechanism to limit that · container + outer letters

  • mechanism → CONTRAPTION a contraption is a mechanism or gadget, and it opens up after six letters
  • Carrie having squeezed out four → CE the four letters inside the name go, leaving her first and her last

The definition's own noun is doing double duty: 'mechanism' names what the answer is and also supplies the gadget word the leftover pair squeezes into.

12 down TASTELESS

Naff stuff primarily in gallery? Not so (9)

Definition: Naff · charade + container + first letter

  • stuff primarily → S stuff gives up its opening letter and nothing else
  • gallery → TATE the Tate is Britain's national collection of modern and British art, four letters that open after two
  • Not so → LESS less is what you have when there is not so much of something

The question mark is doing real work: 'Not so' has to be read as a comparison, meaning not so much, rather than as a flat contradiction.

14 down AUTHORISE

Agree to gold even if more money (9)

Definition: Agree to · charade

  • gold → AU AU is the chemical symbol for gold
  • even if → THO even if means though, and tho' is its clipped poetic spelling
  • more money → RISE a rise is the British word for an increase in pay

Nothing in the clue admits that the middle piece is shortened; the letter count is what tells you. The surface pretends to be a haggle over wages.

16 down ORBITAL

M25 maybe spoken about little (7)

Definition: M25 maybe · container

  • spoken → ORAL an oral exam is a spoken one, and the word opens after two letters
  • little → BIT a bit of something is a little of it

'Spoken' looks like a homophone marker and is not — it is just the adjective oral, opened up. The M25 is the motorway ringing London.

18 down CORACLE

Hide boat with start of cyclone forecast (7)

Definition: Hide boat · charade + first letter

  • start of cyclone → C cyclone hands over its opening letter and nothing more
  • forecast → ORACLE an oracle is a source of prophecy, one who forecasts what is coming

'Hide' looks like a verb telling you something is concealed, and nothing is: it is the animal skin the little Welsh river boat is covered with.

20 down VAPID

12's fast, making the top five (5)

Definition: 12's · substitution

  • five → V V is five in Roman numerals, a single letter that fills one square
  • fast → APID rapid means fast, and it surrenders its first letter to the newcomer

A number on its own points at another clue for the definition, so solve 12 down first; the wordplay here is one letter swapped at the head.

21 down CHEAT

Male cat has caught rat, perhaps (5)

Definition: rat, perhaps · container

  • cat → CAT the animal goes in whole and splits open after its first letter
  • Male → HE he is the pronoun standing in for any male

The trap is reading 'Male cat' as one thing, a tom. Split the two words and the sentence still works: the male is what the cat has caught.
