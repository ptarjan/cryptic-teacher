Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,158

Setter: Everyman · Published: Saturday 27 June 2026 · Difficulty: Tough (harder than 67% of the puzzles here) · Grid: 15×15

Across

1 across MARY

Woman to spoil end of party (4)

Definition: Woman · charade + last letter

  • to spoil → MAR to mar something is to spoil or damage it
  • end of party → Y the last letter of partY

A four-letter clue with two chunks and no indicator worth the name is Everyman easing you in, and the surface, a woman ruining the fun, holds together perfectly.

3 across OPPROBRIUM

In favour of British getting into narcotic juice: disgrace! (10)

Definition: disgrace · charade + container

  • narcotic juice → OPIUM opium is the dried juice of the poppy, so the two words are one thing, not two
  • In favour of → PRO pro = in favour of, as in the pros and cons
  • British → BR br. is the standard shorthand for British

'Narcotic juice' looks like two separate pieces of wordplay and is a single definition. Once the poppy is spotted, the drug wraps round the other three letters and the exclamation mark is pure surface.

9 across IOTA

Letter from Athens returned to you in Paris (4)

Definition: Letter from Athens · reversal

  • to you in Paris → IOTA in French, 'to you' is a toi, and naming a French city is the standard way of asking for French

'Letter from Athens returned' reads as post going back unopened, and both halves are doing something else: Athens means the Greek alphabet and the letters travel backwards.

10 across PERSISTENT

According to sibling, canvas is fixed (10)

Definition: fixed · charade

  • According to → PER per = according to, as in 'per your email'
  • sibling → SIS sis is the familiar short form of sister
  • canvas → TENT canvas is what one is made of, and 'under canvas' means camping

Three ordinary words in a row with nothing marking the joins, which is what makes a long charade hard: the surface is a remark about an artist's stretched canvas and never stumbles.

12 across CIRCULATION

Number of readers an issue for first-aider (11)

Definition: Number of readers · double definition

  • Number of readers → a newspaper's reach is measured by how many copies get out to buyers
  • an issue for first-aider → first aid is taught as airway, breathing and then keeping the blood moving

'Issue' is placed to keep you in the newsroom, where it means an edition; for the first-aider it means a problem, and the two trades share one word without a join between them.

15 across UNMASKS

Kim disheartened; Susan sadly shows up (7)

Definition: shows up · anagram + outer letters

  • Kim disheartened → KM the name loses its central letter and keeps the two either side
  • Susan → SUSAN anagram fodder, five letters
  • sadly → anagram indicator

Two ordinary first names are the hardest fodder to spot, because nothing about Kim or Susan looks like raw material until you count the letters against the enumeration.

16 across INSULIN

Regulator cutting short abuse: cool (7)

Definition: Regulator · charade + deletion

  • abuse → INSUL an insult is abuse, and it is cut short by one letter
  • cool → IN in = fashionable, what is cool this season

The surface is a watchdog clamping down on trolls and keeping its head, so all three parts read as one sentence about conduct while none of them is about the body.

17 across SUNDIAL

Take time here on cloudless day (7)

Definition: Take time here on cloudless day · cryptic definition

  • Take time here → the surface invites you to be unhurried; the setter means take a reading of the hour off the object itself
  • on cloudless day → it tells you nothing at all under an overcast sky, which points at what powers it

There is no wordplay to hunt for here, only one sly sentence. 'Cloudless day' is more than scenery: the instrument is named for the very thing it needs to work at all.

19 across SOPRANI

Singers giving appeasing gift to Indian princess (7)

Definition: Singers · charade

  • appeasing gift → SOP a sop is something handed over to placate someone, as in a sop to Cerberus
  • Indian princess → RANI a rani is an Indian queen or princess, the feminine of raja

The English plural would end in -OS, and the crossing letter shows the setter has gone to the opera house for the Italian one instead. That last letter is the whole difficulty.

20 across NEANDERTHAL

Palaeolithic man tilled earthen land (11)

Definition: Palaeolithic man · anagram

  • earthen land → EARTHENLAND anagram fodder, eleven letters
  • tilled → anagram indicator

The picture of a man working his fields is tens of thousands of years too late for the answer, whose people hunted and gathered and tilled nothing at all. That gap is the joke.

23 across HEN PARTIES

Spooner's to corral lusty types for women's gatherings (3,7)

Definition: women's gatherings · spoonerism

  • to corral → PEN to corral livestock is to pen it
  • lusty types → HEARTIES hearties are hearty, full-blooded fellows, as in 'me hearties'
  • Spooner's → HEN PARTIES say those two words with their opening sounds traded, P for H

Work out the plain version first and the swap is mechanical. Everyman signposts the device by name, which is the convention: a spoonerism always has someone in the clue doing the talking.

24 across CUBE

Cut into pieces: eight or 27, perhaps (4)

Definition: Cut into pieces · double definition

  • Cut into pieces → in the kitchen you chop meat or vegetables into small even blocks
  • eight or 27, perhaps → two multiplied by itself three times, and three treated the same way

'Cut into pieces' looks exactly like an anagram instruction, which is the trap; it is a plain definition, and the second one arrives as arithmetic. The question mark admits the numbers are only examples.

25 across STICKY TAPE

Aggro given by ultimately snarky, flippant imitator: this'll help you hold things together (6,4)

Definition: this'll help you hold things together · charade + last letters

  • Aggro → STICK aggro is British slang for trouble, and to give someone stick is to give them a hard time
  • snarky, flippant → YT the closing letters of the two adjectives, in the order they are written
  • imitator → APE to ape someone is to imitate them

'Ultimately' governs both adjectives at once, and that is where solvers stall: take one last letter and the count comes up short by one.

26 across MEWS

Overheard one inspiring announcements from Persians (4)

Definition: announcements from Persians · homophone

  • one inspiring → MEWS one of the nine Muses is what inspires a poet, and said aloud she is spelled quite differently

The capital P wants you to think of people from Persia. The Persians here are cats, and what they are announcing is dinner.

Down

1 down MAIN COURSE

With a prune, mint sauce or nuts will give you substantial fare (4,6)

Definition: substantial fare · anagram + deletion

  • mint → MIN the herb is pruned, so its final letter goes
  • sauce or → SAUCEOR anagram fodder, seven letters, taken as they stand
  • nuts → anagram indicator

Prune, mint sauce and nuts read as a list of things served alongside, which is the trap: 'nuts' is the instruction, not the food, and the pruning happens to a word.

2 down RETIREMENT

Entire term ghastly - not going to work any more (10)

Definition: not going to work any more · anagram

  • Entire term → ENTIRETERM anagram fodder, ten letters
  • ghastly → anagram indicator

The surface is a schoolteacher complaining about a bad term, and every word of that complaint is either raw material or the instruction to shuffle it.

4 down PREFABS

Assembled accommodation in anticipation of the Beatles (7)

Definition: Assembled accommodation · charade

  • in anticipation of → PRE pre- is the prefix meaning before, so anticipating something is standing in front of it
  • the Beatles → FABS the Beatles were the Fab Four, and the Fabs for short

Everyman is dating himself twice over: the Fabs belong to the sixties and the houses to the years after the war, when factory-built homes went up in weeks on bombed streets.

5 down ROSSINI

Musician, somewhat gross, in isolation (7)

Definition: Musician · hidden word

  • gross, in isolation → ROSSINI the run starts inside the first word and carries straight on across the comma

'Somewhat gross' does what a good hidden-word clue must: it reads as an aside about the musician, and the letters sit in plain sight while you look for a synonym.

6 down BUSINESS PARK

Public transport lines not beginning to reach flash commercial area (8,4)

Definition: commercial area · charade + deletion

  • Public transport → BUS the everyday public vehicle, and the crossword's shortest one
  • lines → INES LINES with its opening letter gone
  • flash → SPARK a flash of electricity is a spark

'Flash commercial area' reads as one phrase about somewhere expensive, and the split falls in the middle of it. Buses and lines keep the surface on the transport network throughout.

7 down ITEM

About time: Everyman twice sent up as 'particular' (4)

Definition: particular · charade + reversal + container

  • Everyman twice → IEM the setter signs himself both ME and I; take the two in that order and send them back up the column
  • time → T t = time, the standard abbreviation

Everyman naming himself in his own clue is a signature move, and 'twice' is the whole trick: he answers to ME and to I, which is two pieces rather than one repeated.

8 down MATE

Primarily: marry / amigo / tisane / ending! (4)

Definition: amigo · first letters

  • marry / amigo / tisane / ending → MATE the opening letter of each of the four listed words, read in order

Every word in the list also means the answer: to marry is to mate, mate is chess for the ending, and mate is a South American tisane. The setter has clued it four times and spelled it once.

11 down QUESTION MARK

Doubt value of pre-euro currency? (8,4)

Definition: ? · charade

  • Doubt → QUESTION to question something is to doubt it
  • pre-euro currency → MARK Germany spent the mark until the euro arrived in 2002

The definition is the last character of the clue rather than a word in it: the setter writes the thing itself and leaves you to notice. Read without it, the sentence is nothing but wordplay.

13 down BLEAK HOUSE

Empty bath containing ooze onto river: that's novel (5,5)

Definition: novel · container + charade + outer letters

  • Empty bath → BH the word for the tub keeps only its two outer letters
  • ooze → LEAK water that oozes out where it should not is a leak
  • river → OUSE the Ouse is an English river and a setter's favourite, being almost all vowels

The whole surface is a plumbing disaster, which is a long way from Dickens. Learn the river: with four letters and three vowels it turns up constantly, usually clued as bluntly as this.

14 down INFIELDERS

Cricketers arranged friendlies (10)

Definition: Cricketers · anagram

  • friendlies → FRIENDLIES anagram fodder, ten letters
  • arranged → anagram indicator

Three words, one of them the definition and one the instruction, so the fodder has to be the remaining one. 'Arranged friendlies' is such natural sports-desk English that neither job shows.

18 down LEAPT AT

Eagerly approached meadow with dung covering rabbit's tail (5,2)

Definition: Eagerly approached · charade + container + last letter

  • meadow → LEA lea is the poets' and setters' word for a meadow
  • dung → PAT a cowpat is a pat of dung dropped in a field
  • rabbit's tail → T the last letter of rabbiT, its tail end on the page

Two pieces of country vocabulary worth keeping: a lea is a meadow and a pat is dung. The rabbit is in the field for the surface and lends nothing but its final letter.

19 down SIDLE UP

Furtively approach drink, hiding empty (5,2)

Definition: Furtively approach · container

  • drink → SUP to sup is to drink, and supper takes its name from it
  • empty → IDLE an idle boast or an idle threat is an empty one

'Drink, hiding empty' is a whole little scene of someone concealing a drained glass, and both nouns in it are synonyms doing wordplay instead.

21 down THUS

So it's hours being regularly wasted (4)

Definition: So · alternate letters

  • it's hours → THUS take the second letter and then every second one after it, counting the s of the contraction as a letter like any other

The apostrophe earns its keep: the count runs through it as though it were not there, which is what stops the fodder looking like fodder in so short a clue.

22 down ANTI

Antagonistic to some characters from Pleasant Island (4)

Definition: Antagonistic · hidden word

  • Pleasant Island → ANTI the old European name for Nauru, in the Pacific, carries the letters wanted straight across the gap between its two words

A capital letter mid-clue usually means a proper noun, and this one is a colonial name for a Pacific island - pleasant enough on the surface to hide something hostile.
