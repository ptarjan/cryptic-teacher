Across

1 across PERFIDIOUS

Faithless son mostly purified after conversion (10)

Definition: Faithless · anagram + deletion

  • son → SO the word with its final letter left off
  • purified → PURIFIED eight more letters of fodder, taken straight from the clue

The surface is a tale of religious redemption, and both halves of it are fodder: a word docked of one letter, then a whole word, ten letters between them.

6 across JUNG

Psychiatrist's new, cooler chambers (4)

Definition: Psychiatrist · container

  • cooler → JUG cooler and jug are both prison slang, so one can stand in for the other
  • new → N the usual single-letter abbreviation

'Cooler chambers' reads like refrigeration and is two separate jobs: a slang word for jail, and an instruction to put something inside it.

10 across PIQUE

Nelson on track to lose Trafalgar, initially upset (5)

Definition: upset · deletion + first letter

  • Nelson on track → PIQUET Nelson Piquet won three Formula One titles, so the Nelson on a track is the Brazilian, not the admiral
  • Trafalgar → T its opening letter is the one that has to go

Nelson and Trafalgar pull hard towards the sea battle, and that is the trap: the naval hero has nothing to do with it beyond lending his first name to a racing driver.

11 across NUMBER TWO

Second name, Brown, ridiculously pronounced (6,3)

Definition: Second · charade + homophone

  • name → N the standard abbreviation, as on a form
  • Brown → UMBER umber is an earth pigment, the brown of raw and burnt umber on a paint chart
  • ridiculously → TWO ridiculously expensive is too expensive, so the adverb means the same as the word you say here

The surface hands you a former Prime Minister and a middle name, and neither is wanted: 'Brown' is a colour and the last piece is a sound, not a spelling.

12 across PIGTAILS

Plaits braided either side of one girl's head? (8)

Definition: Plaits braided either side of one girl's head · &lit + anagram + container + first letter

  • Plaits → PLAITS six letters of fodder, counted before they are rearranged
  • one → I the Roman numeral
  • girl's → G the letter at the front of the word

Every word works twice: read once it is a plain description of a hairstyle, read again it is fodder, a numeral and an initial with instructions on where each goes.

13 across AMAZE

Floor area I swept clear of corn (5)

Definition: Floor · charade + deletion

  • area → A the usual abbreviation, as in the formula for a rectangle
  • corn → MAZE corn here is MAIZE, the crop, once one letter has been taken out of it
  • I → this is the letter that gets swept out, standing in the clue as an innocent pronoun

A farmyard surface of threshing floors and swept grain. 'Floor' is at the front doing the defining, in its knocked-flat sense rather than anything underfoot.

15 across AVIGNON

Article about wine circle touring grand Provençal location (7)

Definition: Provençal location · container

  • Article → AN the indefinite article written out in full
  • wine → VIN French for wine, which the southern setting invites
  • circle → O the letter you draw as a ring
  • grand → G a grand is a thousand, and G is how the slang is written

Two insertions, one inside the other: the wine word opens up for a single letter, and the article closes round the lot. The surface is a wine-tasting trip.

17 across SCRUPLE

Screw up when cutting motorway reservation (7)

Definition: reservation · deletion

  • Screw up → SCRUMPLE to scrumple a piece of paper is to screw it up into a crushed ball
  • motorway → M British motorways are numbered with an M, as in the M1 and the M25, and that letter is the one cut

The surface has roadworks in it, which is exactly what the parse needs you to forget. The long word for crumpling is the one to find first.

19 across VIEWING

Rival neighbours secure good survey (7)

Definition: survey · charade

  • Rival → VIE to rival someone is to vie with them
  • secure → WIN to secure a seat or a contract is to win it
  • good → G the usual one-letter abbreviation, as on a school report

The surface is a feud over a fence, and 'neighbours' is not part of that picture at all: it is the setter telling you the pieces just sit together.

21 across DEMETER

Divine cleared by device that measures divinity (7)

Definition: divinity · charade + outer letters

  • Divine → DE the two letters at the outside of the word, with everything between them gone
  • device that measures → METER a meter is what measures a supply of gas, electricity or parking time

'Divine' and 'divinity' sit at opposite ends looking like the same idea. One is the definition; the other is just a word to be hollowed out.

22 across DELHI

City trader having charcuterie delivered (5)

Definition: City · homophone

  • trader having charcuterie → DELHI the shop that sells cold meats and cheeses is a deli, and said aloud it needs one more letter written down

Charcuterie makes you think of France, and the sound lands in India instead. The extra letter is silent, which is the whole of the trick.

24 across LUNCHEON

Turkey put aside for 50 in staff meal (8)

Definition: meal · substitution

  • staff → TRUNCHEON a truncheon is the short stick a British police officer carries
  • Turkey → TR TR is Turkey's international vehicle code, the pair being moved out of the way
  • 50 → L the Roman numeral for fifty, taking the vacated place

A canteen surface, with the bird and the staff belonging to the same picture. Both are pulled apart: one becomes two letters of a country code, the other a police weapon.

27 across RECESSIVE

Niche setter's inclined to retire? (9)

Definition: inclined to retire · charade

  • Niche → RECESS a niche in a wall is a recess
  • setter's → IVE the setter speaks as I, so the contraction is I've with the apostrophe dropped

'Setter' means the person who wrote the clue, one of the oldest conventions going, and it almost always turns into the single letter I.

28 across CARER

Having dropped some ecstasy, calling nurse? (5)

Definition: nurse · deletion

  • calling → CAREER a calling is the career you feel drawn to follow
  • some ecstasy → E E is the drug's usual letter, and 'some' takes just one of the two sitting side by side

A hospital surface with a drug in it, and the drug is only ever one letter. The question mark admits that the definition is an example rather than a match.

29 across NEED

Miss, once dead, oddly forgotten (4)

Definition: Miss · alternate letters

  • once dead → NEED take every second letter across the two words, counting from the second letter of the first

An epitaph on the surface. The comma after 'Miss' is doing a lot of work: it makes the word look like a form of address rather than the definition.

30 across AGGRESSION

Punk gigs are about embracing singular hostility (10)

Definition: hostility · anagram + container

  • gigs are → GIGSARE seven letters of fodder in the clue's own words
  • about → ON a book about birds is a book on birds, so the two words swap
  • singular → S the grammarian's abbreviation, as in sing. and pl.

'Punk gigs' reads as one phrase and is really instruction plus fodder, which is why the first word feels like scene-setting. The last word is the definition.

Down

1 down PIPE

Butt which smoker will use repeatedly? (4)

Definition: Butt · double definition

  • Butt → PIPE in old measures a butt is a cask of ale or wine holding around a hundred gallons, nothing to do with cigarettes
  • which smoker will use repeatedly → PIPE the second sense is the one at the bar, refilled night after night rather than thrown away

'Butt' invites the cigarette end, and that is the wrong smoking word entirely: it is the cask sense, which is why the answer is a thing you do not stub out.

2 down REQUISITE

Essential queries being processed with IT (9)

Definition: Essential · anagram

  • queries → QUERIES seven letters of fodder
  • with IT → IT the capitals are the computing department, and both letters join the shuffle

A helpdesk surface: queries going through IT. The capital letters look like emphasis and are really just two more letters for the pile.

3 down ID EST

That is finally lost after Roman dates? (2,3)

Definition: That is · charade + last letter

  • Roman dates → IDES the Ides were fixed days in the Roman month, the middle of March being the famous one
  • lost → T the closing letter of the word

The clue pretends to be about something mislaid on a calendar. Latin lives on in English as the abbreviation i.e., which is what the two words shorten to.

4 down INN SIGN

Pub swinger? (3,4)

Definition: Pub swinger · cryptic definition

  • swinger → the surface sense is a person with an adventurous love life, propping up the bar
  • Pub swinger → read flatly instead: the painted board hanging from a bracket over the pavement, which really does swing in the wind

Two words and no wordplay to take apart, so the work is choosing which sense of 'swinger' the setter means. The question mark is the nudge.

5 down UNMASKS

At Le Moulin Rouge, a monsieur requests reveals (7)

Definition: reveals · charade

  • a → UN the French indefinite article
  • monsieur → M M. is how the title is abbreviated in French, as Mr is in English
  • requests → ASKS to request something is to ask for it

A masked ball at a cabaret is the picture, and it is doing double duty: the setting is the instruction to think in French for the first two pieces.

7 down ULTRA

Extreme resistance, Middle Eastern ruler barely suppresses (5)

Definition: Extreme · container + deletion

  • Middle Eastern ruler → ULTA the ruler is a SULTAN, stripped of the letter at each end
  • resistance → R R is the physicist's symbol for resistance

'Extreme resistance' reads as one phrase in the surface and splits in the parse: the first word defines, the second is a single letter to be buried.

8 down GOOSEBERRY

Fruit company discomfiting lovers? (10)

Definition: Fruit · double definition

  • Fruit → GOOSEBERRY the sharp green fruit of a thorny garden bush, the one that goes into fools and crumbles
  • company discomfiting lovers → GOOSEBERRY in British usage, to play this is to be the third person tagging along with a courting couple

'Fruit company' looks like one phrase and a possible tech joke. Split it after the first word and both halves define the answer.

9 down HEXAGRAM

Hydrogen emitted originally by a pulse star? (8)

Definition: star · charade + first letter

  • Hydrogen → H the chemical symbol
  • emitted → E the opening letter of the word
  • by → X three by four is three x four, so 'by' is the multiplication sign
  • a → A taken as it stands
  • pulse → GRAM gram is the chickpea, one of the pulses, and a staple word in Indian cooking

The surface builds an astronomy picture out of five separate pieces, one of them a letter hiding as an ordinary preposition. A pulsar is the thing you are meant to see.

14 down SALVADORAN

LA road snarled with vans from Central America (10)

Definition: from Central America · anagram

  • LA road → LAROAD six letters of fodder, the city abbreviated as it always is
  • vans → VANS four more letters joining the tangle

A Los Angeles traffic jam on the surface, and the setter has picked the one American city whose initials are also fodder. 'Snarled' does the work of both pictures.

16 down NOISIEST

Blast on site is most deafening (8)

Definition: most deafening · anagram

  • on site is → ONSITEIS eight letters of fodder, three little words of the clue running together

'Blast on site' is a building-works headline, and the first word is the instruction rather than part of it. The definition is the superlative at the end.

18 down POTPOURRI

Scientists bearing crucible spill mixture (9)

Definition: mixture · charade

  • crucible → POT a crucible is the pot a chemist melts things in
  • spill → POUR to spill liquid is to pour it out, deliberately or not
  • Scientists → RI the RI is the Royal Institution in London, the body Faraday worked at and where the Christmas Lectures are given

A laboratory accident on the surface, and the pieces run the other way up: the scientists are supporting the other two from below, as a down clue allows.

20 down GOLFING

Drive Heather around following a round or two? (7)

Definition: a round or two · charade + container

  • Drive → GO drive is the get-up-and-go that keeps someone pushing on
  • Heather → LING ling is the heather that covers British moorland, and the capital letter makes it look like a woman
  • following → F f. is the reference-book abbreviation for following, as in see page 12f.

'Drive' and 'round' both belong to the sport, which is the joke: one of them is defining and the other is a piece of the build. Heather is a plant, not a passenger.

21 down DANSEUR

Chap in tights torn asunder (7)

Definition: Chap in tights · anagram

  • asunder → ASUNDER seven letters of fodder, the whole of the last word

'Torn asunder' is a set phrase, so the two words look welded together; the setter has split them into instruction and fodder.

23 down LUCRE

Clubs putting on attraction that's said to be filthy (5)

Definition: that's said to be filthy · container

  • attraction → LURE an attraction is a lure, the thing that draws you in
  • Clubs → C C is the suit on a bridge scorecard, and the capital is there to hint at a nightclub

The definition is an idiom rather than a synonym: the setter points at a stock phrase and lets you supply the word that always comes after the adjective.

25 down HOCKS

Pawns beginning to fall in surprise (5)

Definition: Pawns · cycling

  • surprise → SHOCK a surprise is a shock, and all five letters stay in the clue's order once the front one has moved

Chess pieces are the surface and no help at all. 'Pawns' is a verb here, and the wordplay is one letter taking a walk down the column.

26 down IRON

Maybe decrease in tension, originally brought about (4)

Definition: Maybe decrease · hidden word + reversal

  • tension, originally → NORI four consecutive letters straddle the comma, starting near the end of the first word and running into the second

The definition is the joke: read 'decrease' as de-crease, an undoing of creases, and 'Maybe' is there to license the split. The surface is about relaxing.
