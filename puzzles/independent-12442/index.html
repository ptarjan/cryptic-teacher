Across

1 across AUTHOR

Steel, for example, and half of that gold-plated? (6)

Definition: Steel, for example · container + first letters

  • gold → AU AU is the chemical symbol for gold
  • half of that → TH the front half of THat
  • gold → OR or is gold in heraldry, the second coat

One word for gold does the job twice under two different conventions, which is what a plating means: a coat on this side and a coat on that.

4 across SMACKERS

People who dole out corporal punishment and kisses (8)

Definition: People who dole out corporal punishment · double definition

  • People who dole out corporal punishment → read the verb as striking: anyone who smacks a child is one of these
  • kisses → read the same verb as the noisy sort of kiss, the one you can hear across a room

Two senses of one verb, with no letters to assemble: to smack is both to strike and to kiss loudly.

10 across RE-EMERGED

Notoriously thirsty thespian drinking gallons in lake showed up again (2-7)

Definition: showed up again · container

  • Notoriously thirsty thespian → REED Oliver Reed, the actor as famous for drinking as for acting
  • gallons → G G is the abbreviation for gallons
  • lake → MERE a mere is a lake - Windermere is one

The enumeration (2-7) is a tip in itself: a hyphen after two letters nearly always means a prefix, and here it is the one meaning again.

11 across TRUER

Straighter French street runs through outskirts of Tréguier (5)

Definition: Straighter · container + outer letters

  • Tréguier → TR the first and last letters of Tréguier
  • French street → RUE rue is the French for street

Tréguier is a real Breton town, which is why a French street sits so comfortably inside it.

12 across RESIN

Hardened sap to offend another time? (5)

Definition: Hardened sap · charade

  • another time → RE RE- is the prefix that means over again
  • offend → SIN to sin is to offend against a commandment

The question mark flags the joke: to offend a second time would be to re-sin, a word no dictionary carries but the letters do.

13 across EVIDENTLY

Bump into nasty Yankee apparently (9)

Definition: apparently · container + charade

  • nasty → EVIL evil is nasty at full strength
  • Yankee → Y Y is Yankee in the NATO alphabet
  • Bump → DENT a bump in a car door is a dent

Yankee for Y comes from the NATO alphabet, which will supply a letter for any clue that needs one - Alpha, Bravo, Charlie and the rest.

14 across FORESTS

Out of uniform, alien visitors taking breather inside woods (7)

Definition: woods · deletion + container

  • alien visitors → UFOS unidentified flying objects are the visitors from elsewhere
  • uniform → U U is uniform in the NATO alphabet, and this is the letter that leaves
  • taking breather → REST taking a breather is having a rest

'Out of uniform' reads as soldiers off duty and does its real work by stripping a single letter from the flying saucers.

16 across EARN

Be paid to study but not back in school (4)

Definition: Be paid · deletion + last letter

  • study → LEARN to study a subject is to learn it
  • school → L the final letter of schooL, the one to drop

'Back in school' looks like one phrase about term starting and is really two instructions: which end of a word, and which word.

19 across IMPS

Cheeky devils - one's hugging representative (4)

Definition: Cheeky devils · container

  • one's → IS one is the single stroke I, and the 's comes along with it
  • representative → MP your representative in the Commons is an MP

An apostrophe-s left standing at the end is a favourite way of handing over two letters, and it stays put while the other piece moves in.

21 across WELCOME

We'll shortly attend reception (7)

Definition: reception · charade + deletion

  • We'll → WEL WE'LL run together is WELL, docked of its final letter
  • attend → COME to attend a party is to come to it

The apostrophe is the whole disguise: 'We'll' is a promise on the surface and four letters to trim in the wordplay.

24 across COURTROOM

Royal gathering fell back where bench is (9)

Definition: where bench is · charade + reversal

  • Royal gathering → COURT the court is the monarch's household gathered round
  • fell → MOOR a fell is northern moorland

'Fell' is a noun here, a Lakeland hill, and not the past tense of fall the surface is selling.

25 across THANE

Old Scottish lord occupying Elizabethan estate (5)

Definition: Old Scottish lord · hidden word

  • Elizabethan estate → THANE ElizabeTHAN Estate carries the letters straight across the gap between the words

Shakespeare put the word in everybody's head, and the setter has hidden it inside a queen from the wrong century entirely.

26 across IDIOM

Man introduced by retiring police officer displaying unique turn of phrase (5)

Definition: unique turn of phrase · charade + reversal

  • police officer → ID a DI is a detective inspector, turned round here
  • Man → IOM IOM is how the Isle of Man writes itself on an address

'Man' is the island rather than the male, and the clue hides that by standing a person and a policeman on either side of it.

27 across STATEMENT

Custer essentially had soldiers close to fort position at bank (9)

Definition: position at bank · charade + middle letters + last letter

  • Custer → ST the middle pair of cuSTer
  • had → ATE had lunch is ate lunch
  • soldiers → MEN soldiers are men under arms
  • fort → T the closing letter of forT

Custer's last stand supplies the whole surface, and 'had' is the piece most solvers walk past - a past tense about eating, not possession.

28 across REGISTRY

Judge once more screening foreign fighter's record collection (8)

Definition: record collection · container

  • Judge once more → RETRY to judge a case a second time is to retry it
  • foreign fighter's → GIS a GI is an American soldier, foreign from here, and the 's rides along

GI comes from 'government issue', stamped on American kit, and it has meant an American soldier ever since.

29 across KERNEL

Core group of mariners trapped by bottom of boat (6)

Definition: Core · container

  • bottom of boat → KEEL the keel is the timber running along a boat's bottom
  • group of mariners → RN the Royal Navy is RN

'Bottom of boat' looks like an instruction to take a final letter and is nothing of the kind - it is a plain synonym.

Down

1 down AIRCRAFT

Skill in pursuit of express plane (8)

Definition: plane · charade

  • express → AIR to air a grievance is to express it
  • Skill → CRAFT craft is skill of hand, as in craftsmanship

'Express' hides a verb, and the moment you read it as one the surface's fast train falls apart.

2 down TREASURE

Half-heartedly confirm offering support for Tesla's valued helper (8)

Definition: valued helper · charade + deletion

  • Tesla → T T is the symbol for the tesla, the unit of magnetic flux
  • confirm → REASURE to reassure is to confirm what someone hoped, and REASSURE with half of its twin middle letters gone leaves this

The surface points at Nikola Tesla and his indispensable assistant; the wordplay wants only the unit named after him, one letter of it.

3 down OCEAN

Expert putting head down and getting stuck into dealing with Neptune's orbit (5)

Definition: Neptune's orbit · container + cycling

  • Expert → ACE an ace is an expert at whatever it is
  • dealing with → ON 'on' means about - a lecture on birds is one dealing with birds

'Head down' is doing two jobs: a diligent worker on the surface, and on the grid a letter with a journey to make.

5 down MIDWIFE

When youth has gone, warehouse's initially replacing learner who helps with deliveries (7)

Definition: who helps with deliveries · substitution + first letter

  • When youth has gone → MIDLIFE the stretch of years after youth is midlife
  • learner → L L is the plate on a learner's car, and it is the letter that goes
  • warehouse's → W the first letter of warehouse moves in to take its place

L for learner comes off the plates on a British car, and it is worth knowing for good - clues want that letter constantly.

6 down CATHEDRAL

Crashing car halted building of church (9)

Definition: building of church · anagram

  • car halted → CATHEDRAL the nine letters of CAR HALTED put back another way

Nothing in the surface admits to a place of worship: 'building' reads as work in progress right up until you see it is the definition.

7 down EQUITY

In fairness, gave up drug first before completion of therapy (6)

Definition: fairness · charade + last letter

  • drug → E E is ecstasy, the crossword's standard drug
  • gave up → QUIT to quit is to give up
  • therapy → Y the closing letter of therapY

'First' looks like part of the drug reference and is really positional, telling you where its letter belongs rather than which letter to take.

8 down STRAYS

Carrier picked up by exhausted services for homeless creatures (6)

Definition: homeless creatures · container + outer letters

  • services → SS the two end letters of ServiceS, the middle used up
  • Carrier → TRAY a tray is what carries the drinks

Nothing here is military: 'services' is only a word to hollow out, and 'carrier' wants the thing a waiter holds.

9 down EGRETS

Fliers postmaster general's carrying around (6)

Definition: Fliers · hidden word + reversal

  • postmaster general's → EGRETS postmaSTER GEneral holds the letters, read back up the entry

A reversed hidden word is the hardest of all to spot, because the letters are sitting in plain sight and only the direction of travel is odd.

15 down SOMETIMES

Occasionally call for assistance covering personal break (9)

Definition: Occasionally · container

  • call for assistance → SOS the distress call is SOS
  • personal break → ME TIME me-time is time kept for yourself

Me-time is new enough that it still looks like surface chat, and the distress call splits cleanly round it.

17 down SOYA BEAN

Originally, offers by upwardly mobile online marketplace confined to hospital food (4,4)

Definition: food · container + reversal + first letter

  • offers → O the opening letter of offers
  • online marketplace → YABE eBay is the online marketplace, climbing the entry
  • hospital → SAN a san, short for sanatorium, is the sickbay in every boarding school

SAN for hospital is worth pocketing: it comes from sanatorium and turns up wherever a clue wants three letters for a ward.

18 down PEDESTAL

Base spread evenly over broken slate (8)

Definition: Base · charade + alternate letters + anagram

  • spread → PED the second, fourth and sixth letters of sPrEaD
  • slate → ESTAL the five letters of slate rebuilt

'Spread evenly' is one phrase with two jobs: a breakfast-table picture on the surface, and an instruction to count letters in twos.

20 down SPONSOR

Back amendment of Spooner’s, losing vote in the end (7)

Definition: Back · anagram + deletion + last letter

  • Spooner’s → SPOONERS the reverend's name with its 's, eight letters to start from
  • vote → E the last letter of votE, the one that goes

Spooner is the red herring of the puzzle: no sounds are swapped anywhere, and his name is simply the fodder, one letter lighter.

21 down WOMBAT

Aussie native's bill cut up (6)

Definition: Aussie native · charade + reversal

  • bill → TAB the bill run up at a bar is the tab
  • cut → MOW to cut the grass is to mow it

A whole charade can be turned at once rather than piece by piece: build the two words in clue order, then read the lot upwards.

22 down ACHIER

I reach out and it's more painful (6)

Definition: more painful · anagram

  • I reach → ACHIER the six letters of I REACH in a fresh order

The surface sounds like a confession about stretching too far, and every word of it except the definition is either fodder or the nudge to shuffle it.

23 down OUTING

Excursion got uni in trouble (6)

Definition: Excursion · anagram

  • got uni → OUTING the six letters of GOT UNI rearranged

'Uni' is modern enough to pass as student chatter, which is exactly why it goes unnoticed as fodder.

25 down THEME

Article has limited space for subject (5)

Definition: subject · container

  • Article → THE the definite article
  • space → EM an em is the printer's space, as wide as the letter M is

Printers measure spaces in ems and ens, after the widths of M and N, and that little pair turns up in clues constantly once you know it.
