1 across CLEANSE

Remove dirt from winding lanes in borders of Cheshire (7)

Definition: Remove dirt from · anagram + container + outer letters

Remove dirt from → CLEANSE: the thorough verb for washing a thing clean, stronger than 'clean' and used of wounds and skin.

  • Cheshire → CE the outer pair of CheshirE, an English county in the north-west
  • lanes → LEANS the five letters of LANES put in a new order
  • winding — A winding lane bends one way then another, and letters told to wind will not stay in the order they were handed over.
  • in — Whatever is described as in something else sits inside it, so the reshuffled letters land between the pair already found.
  • borders of — The borders of a county are its outer edges, so only the opening and closing letters of the word are wanted.

Scrubbing the mud off twisting country lanes at the edge of Cheshire.

'Winding lanes' reads as one picture of country roads, and its two words belong to opposite halves of the wordplay: one is the instruction, the other the material.

5 across SCRAWLS

Son slowly moves illegible pieces of handwriting (7)

Definition: illegible pieces of handwriting · charade

illegible pieces of handwriting → SCRAWLS: the plural noun for hasty scribbles, writing done too fast for anyone to read.

  • Son → S s is the standard abbreviation for son, the one used on a family tree
  • slowly moves → CRAWLS traffic that moves slowly crawls

A boy shifting a pile of unreadable papers at a snail's pace.

'S' for son is one of the family abbreviations crosswords lean on constantly, alongside 'w' for wife and 'd' for daughter.

10 across FAIL

Lack success following trouble (4)

Definition: Lack success · charade

Lack success → FAIL: the ordinary verb for coming up short of what was attempted, not the narrower school sense of a failed exam.

  • following → F f. is the abbreviation for 'following' used in page references
  • trouble → AIL to trouble somebody is to ail them, as in 'what ails you?'

Coming to grief in the wake of some trouble.

'Following' looks like a positional instruction telling you where the next piece goes, and it is a letter of the answer in its own right.

11 across ON ONE'S GAME

Cricket side with individual batters lately plucky and playing well (2,4,4)

Definition: playing well · charade + last letter

playing well → ON ONE'S GAME: the sporting idiom for somebody performing at their best, in the form they are capable of.

  • Cricket side → ON in cricket the on side, or leg side, is the half of the field behind the batter's legs
  • individual → ONE an individual is a single one
  • batters → S the last letter of batterS
  • plucky → GAME somebody game for anything is plucky
  • lately — Lately points at the latter end of things, so the closing letter of the word before it is the one taken.

A cricket team whose batters have lately been brave and in good form.

Cricket fills the surface and only its first two words are cricketing wordplay; 'batters' is there for its final letter alone.

12 across SHOWER

Group not up to much in brief fall of rain (6)

Definition: Group not up to much · double definition

Group not up to much → SHOWER: British slang for a hopeless rabble; brief fall of rain → SHOWER, the short burst of wet weather.

  • Group not up to much → British slang, out of forces use, for a collection of useless people
  • brief fall of rain → read straight: a short burst of wet weather that soon passes over

A useless crowd caught out in a quick downpour.

'What a shower!' is the British insult behind the first half, a hopeless rabble, and the weather filling the rest of the clue keeps it out of sight.

13 across NOMINATE

Put forward number in team possibly (8)

Definition: Put forward · charade + anagram

Put forward → NOMINATE: to propose a person's name for a post or a prize, the formal verb for putting a candidate up.

  • number → NO no. is the standard short form for number
  • in team → MINATE the six letters of IN TEAM put in a new order
  • possibly — Possibly says the letters beside it could come out in any arrangement, not only the one printed.

Suggesting a squad number for somebody who might make the team.

'Number in team' reads as the digit on a shirt, and 'possibly' is carrying an instruction rather than hedging about who gets picked.

14 across BRIDLEWAY

Bid lawyer formulated to get track for riders (9)

Definition: track for riders · anagram

track for riders → BRIDLEWAY: a British public right of way that horse riders may legally use, named from the bridle they ride with.

  • Bid lawyer → BIDLAWYER the nine letters that get reordered
  • formulated — A formula is drawn up by setting its parts out in a chosen order, so the letters beside it are arranged anew.

An offer drawn up by a solicitor that ends up producing a horse trail.

'Bid lawyer' sits together in the surface as an offer somebody drafted, and 'formulated' looks like the rest of that phrase while it is really the instruction.

16 across WINDY

Secure day avoiding area affected by gusts? (5)

Definition: affected by gusts? · charade + deletion

affected by gusts? → WINDY: exposed to strong blowing wind, the adjective for a day or a hillside the wind keeps hitting.

  • Secure → WIN to secure a prize is to win it
  • day avoiding area → DY area is A, and DAY with that letter kept out leaves D…Y
  • avoiding — To avoid something is to keep clear of it, so the letter named is left out of the word in front.

Getting hold of a safe day out, keeping clear of the region the gales are hitting.

'Area' is A in crossword shorthand, out of maps and mathematics, and here it is a letter to take away rather than one to add.

17 across STASH

Secret consignment put in chest as highlighted (5)

Definition: Secret consignment · hidden word

Secret consignment → STASH: a quantity of something hidden away, the word for a hoard of drugs or loot kept out of sight.

  • chest as highlighted → STASH the letters run consecutively from inside the first word across the two after it
  • put in — Something put in a place ends up inside it, so the letters are sitting inside the words that follow.

A smuggled parcel packed into a chest, exactly as the marking showed.

'Secret' describes the consignment in the surface and describes what the wordplay has done with the letters, the one adjective covering both.

19 across BY DEFAULT

Dub a lefty misguided through lack of positive action? (2,7)

Definition: through lack of positive action? · anagram

through lack of positive action? → BY DEFAULT: describing what comes about simply because nobody did anything to prevent it.

  • Dub a lefty → DUBALEFTY the nine letters that get reordered
  • misguided — Somebody misguided has been led the wrong way, and letters led the wrong way come out in the wrong order.

Writing off a left-winger as thoroughly misguided.

'Dub' is the verb for giving somebody a name, which is what makes the surface read as name-calling rather than as nine letters to reorder.

23 across MARRIAGE

A grim era sadly for union (8)

Definition: union · anagram

union → MARRIAGE: the joining of two people in law, 'union' as a formal bond rather than a workers' organisation.

  • A grim era → AGRIMERA the eight letters that get reordered
  • sadly — Sadly says things have gone badly for the words beside it, and letters things have gone badly for do not keep their order.

Looking back sadly on a bleak stretch of history.

'Union' pulls towards trade unions in a clue this gloomy, and the older sense — two people joined — is the one wanted.

24 across VAPOUR

Mist in Virginia – and rain (6)

Definition: Mist · charade

Mist → VAPOUR: water hanging in the air as a fine cloud, 'mist' in its physical, steam-like sense.

  • Virginia → VA VA is the postal abbreviation for the state of Virginia
  • rain → POUR when it rains hard it pours

Fog hanging over Virginia, followed by heavy rain.

'Rain' is a verb here, which is why it can sit at the end of the clue and still be wordplay rather than a second definition.

26 across SABBATICAL

Southern sailor, one in Baltic at work, has period of leave (10)

Definition: period of leave · charade + anagram

period of leave → SABBATICAL: the stretch of paid leave a university grants a scholar, originally one year in every seven.

  • Southern → S s is the standard abbreviation for southern
  • sailor → AB an AB is an able seaman, the Royal Navy rating crosswords use for any sailor
  • one in Baltic → BATICAL one is A, and the letters of A BALTIC come out in a new order
  • at work — Letters described as at work are busy doing something, and what they are busy doing here is taking up new places.

A southern seaman working somewhere in the Baltic earns himself some time off.

The surface is entirely nautical and 'Baltic' is the one sea word in it contributing letters rather than meaning.

27 across NAGS

Badgers and horses in poor condition (4)

Definition: Badgers · double definition

Badgers → NAGS: pesters somebody over and over; and horses in poor condition → NAGS, the dismissive word for tired old horses.

  • Badgers → the verb: keeps on at somebody until they give in
  • horses in poor condition → read straight: worn-out old animals fit for very little

Badgers alongside a few broken-down horses.

'Badgers' reads as the striped animals, matching the horses beside it, and it is the verb — to badger somebody is to keep on at them.

28 across SLEEPER

One covertly placed in overnight train (7)

Definition: One covertly placed · double definition

One covertly placed → SLEEPER: an agent planted long in advance and left inactive; overnight train → SLEEPER, the service with berths for the night.

  • One covertly placed → an agent planted inside an organisation and left dormant for years
  • overnight train → the night service with berths, where you book a bunk rather than a seat

A spy quietly slipped aboard the night train.

Both senses come out of lying dormant: one waits years for orders, the other carries passengers who are asleep.

29 across SPECTRE

Special form of therapy to do with ghostly figure (7)

Definition: ghostly figure · charade

ghostly figure → SPECTRE: an apparition, the literary word for a ghost seen as a shape rather than felt as a presence.

  • Special → SP sp is the standard short form for special
  • form of therapy → ECT ECT is electroconvulsive therapy, the shock treatment
  • to do with → RE re means concerning, as at the head of a memo

A specialised course of treatment for somebody who keeps seeing an apparition.

'Special' looks like an adjective describing the therapy, and it is a piece of the answer on its own; the clue holds back its definition until the last two words.

2 down LEATHER

State of agitation about origin of exotic material made from skin (7)

Definition: material made from skin · container + first letter

material made from skin → LEATHER: animal hide tanned into something durable, the everyday name for skin turned into a workable fabric.

  • State of agitation → LATHER a lather is the froth soap works up, and a person worked up is in one
  • exotic → E the first letter of Exotic
  • about — To go about a thing is to go round it, so the word in front closes over the letter named next.
  • origin of — The origin of something is where it begins, so its opening letter is the one taken.

Someone in a flap about where an exotic import came from.

'State' looks like the start of a definition — a country, perhaps — and it is nothing of the kind; the definition is the whole tail of the clue.

3 down ALLOW

Permit shown in general lowdown (5)

Definition: Permit · hidden word

Permit → ALLOW: the verb for letting something happen, matching 'permit' used as a verb rather than the noun for a licence.

  • general lowdown → ALLOW the letters sit consecutively across the join between the two words, in the order given
  • shown in — Something shown in a place is already sitting there, so the letters are read straight out of the words that follow.

A permit turning up in the general gossip.

'Lowdown' is the informal word for inside information, which keeps the surface sounding like an office briefing rather than a letter hunt.

4 down SCOURGE

Celt almost on impulse is cause of great trouble (7)

Definition: cause of great trouble · charade + deletion

cause of great trouble → SCOURGE: the word for something that afflicts a whole population, a plague or a pest rather than a minor nuisance.

  • Celt almost → SCO a Scot is a Celt, and SCOT written almost to the end stops a letter short
  • impulse → URGE a sudden impulse to do something is the urge to do it
  • almost — Almost means falling just short of the whole, so the word gives up its final letter.
  • on — In a down clue one thing sitting on another is written above it, so this piece comes first.

A Scotsman acting on the spur of the moment and causing havoc.

'Celt' names a whole family of peoples and a Scot is only one of them, which is a definition by example and quite fair.

6 down CREAMS

ointments → CREAMS: the tubes of medicated preparation rubbed into the skin, the everyday word for such salves.

  • Best → CREAM the cream of something is its best part, as in the cream of the crop
  • shop → S the first letter of Shop
  • principally — Principally means first and foremost, so the leading letter of the word beside it is the one taken.

The best shop to visit if ointment is mainly what you want.

'Best' looks like a superlative adjective opening a definition, and it is a noun instead — the cream of the crop.
