Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,124

Across

1 across EASY CHAIRS

Where to comfortably enjoy Spooner's schmaltzy music (4,6)

Definition: Where to comfortably enjoy · spoonerism

  • schmaltzy music → CHEESY AIRS schmaltzy is cheesy, and airs are tunes — that is the plain pair Spooner is handed
  • Spooner's schmaltzy music → EASY CHAIRS say those two words with their opening sounds exchanged: the CH crosses over to the second word and the long E is left at the front of the first

The surface offers a sentimental concert; the Reverend's swap turns it into furniture. 'Airs' for tunes is the old sense that makes the plain version sound natural.

6 across HEWS

Talked of colours in prunes dramatically (4)

Definition: prunes dramatically · homophone

  • colours → HEWS colours are hues, and that word said aloud can be written a second way

The surface is a gardener attacking a shrub, and 'talked of' quietly moves the wordplay from ink to sound. Hewing is axe-work, so 'dramatically' is fair warning.

9 across MANAGERIAL

Concerning boss's hidey-hole, say, an American in retirement (10)

Definition: Concerning boss · charade + reversal

  • hidey-hole → LAIR a lair is an animal's hidey-hole
  • say → EG eg = say, in the sense 'for example'
  • an → AN the article taken at face value
  • American → AM am. is the standard shortening of American

Everything is assembled forwards and then read backwards, so the clue runs left to right while the entry is built right to left. 'Say' = EG is worth keeping; it turns up constantly.

10 across ASIA

Perhaps ChatGPT's a step back for most of humanity (4)

Definition: most of humanity · charade + reversal

  • Perhaps ChatGPT's → AIS ChatGPT is one instance of AI — 'perhaps' flags the example — and the 's brings an S with it
  • a → A the article at face value

The surface argues that machines are a backward step for us all, and the answer is a continent. Modern crossword shorthand: a named chatbot is there to give you AI.

11 across NO HARM NO FOUL

Dodgy moolah for nun: forgive the transgression (2,4,2,4)

Definition: forgive the transgression · anagram

  • moolah for nun → MOOLAHFORNUN anagram fodder, twelve letters — moolah is slang for money

'Dodgy moolah for nun' paints a small scandal and the phrase that comes out waves the whole thing away, which is the joke of the surface.

15 across EARACHE

What makes a lug smart? (7)

Definition: What makes a lug smart? · &lit + charade

  • a lug → EAR lug is northern and Scottish slang for the thing you hear with
  • smart → ACHE to smart is to sting or hurt — the verb, not the adjective the surface suggests

Read as a question it is a riddle, read as instructions it is two words laid end to end, and both readings land on the same thing. That double duty is what &lit means.

16 across POT-HERB

Swap other basket - that contains culinary flavouring (3-4)

Definition: culinary flavouring · hidden word

  • Swap other basket → POTHERB the letters run on consecutively from the end of the first word, straight through the second and into the start of the third

'Swap' invites you to rearrange the letters of what follows, and that is the trap: nothing moves here, and the whole answer has been lying in plain sight all along.

17 across IMPINGE

Everyman's to send text: Everyman's beginning to intrude (7)

Definition: to intrude · charade + first letter

  • Everyman's → IM Everyman is what this setter calls himself, so 'Everyman's' is I'M
  • to send text → PING to ping somebody is to send them a quick message
  • Everyman's beginning → E the letter the setter's name starts with

Everyman signs his own clues, so his name means both I'M and the letter it opens with — the same word doing two different jobs a few syllables apart.

19 across SPIDERY

Abrupt notion: run into intelligence agent like Black Widow? (7)

Definition: like Black Widow? · container + deletion

  • intelligence agent → SPY a spy is an intelligence agent, and it is this word that gets opened up
  • Abrupt notion → IDE a notion is an IDEA, docked of its last letter
  • run → R r = run, the cricket scorer's abbreviation

Black Widow is the Marvel spy, which is why the definition sits so quietly at the end. The clue wants the other black widow, the one with eight legs.

20 across TURKISH TOWEL

What gets wetter when it dries up in Istanbul? (7,5)

Definition: What gets wetter when it dries up in Istanbul? · cryptic definition

  • What gets wetter when it dries → the old riddle: whatever does the drying takes on all the water it removes, so it ends up soaked
  • up in Istanbul → 'dries up' looks like one phrase about a drought; split it and the rest is only saying which country the thing comes from

The paradox is literally true rather than a trick of words, and Istanbul is there to supply the nationality — the plain word on its own would be four letters short.

23 across ATOM

Tiny thing seen in first half of dictionary (4)

Definition: Tiny thing · charade

  • first half of dictionary → ATOM a dictionary runs from A to Z, so the half you reach first stops at M, and that span written out as a range is the wordplay

The wordplay takes no letters out of the word 'dictionary' at all — it is the range the entries cover, written as you would say it.

24 across AUSTRALIAN

Gold pin Arthur's taken back - is he from Perth? (10)

Definition: he from Perth · charade + reversal

  • Gold → AU au is the chemical symbol for gold, from the Latin aurum
  • pin → NAIL a nail is a pin you hammer in
  • Arthur's → ARTS Art is the familiar short form of Arthur, and the 's comes along with it

Perth is the trap — there is one in Scotland too, and an Arthur nudges you towards it. The reversal runs across both of the last two pieces at once, not just the pin.

25 across TANK

Cistern to fail - at great cost (4)

Definition: Cistern · double definition

  • Cistern → the plain noun: a large container for holding water
  • to fail - at great cost → the verb, borrowed from American sport and the box office, where a film that flops expensively is said to do this

A dash is all that separates the two definitions, which is why the clue reads as one sentence about a plumbing disaster.

26 across STREET FOOD

Trendy dining option: Coronation chicken? (6,4)

Definition: Trendy dining option · charade

  • Coronation → STREET Coronation Street is Britain's longest-running television soap, so the name supplies the word that follows it
  • chicken → FOOD chicken is one example of the general category, so the category is what the word gives you

Coronation chicken is a real dish, invented for the Queen's coronation in 1953, and that is the whole misdirection: those two words belong to different halves of the clue.

Down

1 down ELMS

Key limes regularly seen in trees (4)

Definition: trees · alternate letters

  • Key limes → ELMS take every second letter across the two words, starting with the second letter of the first

Key limes are a real variety, small and sharp, which is what keeps the surface innocent — nothing about the fruit suggests woodland.

2 down SINS

Seven in number. Satanic? Originally (4)

Definition: Seven in number. Satanic? Originally · &lit + first letters

  • Seven in number. Satanic → SINS the initial letters of those four words, taken in the order they stand

Read straight, the clue describes the deadly seven and who they were traditionally blamed on; read as wordplay, each word simply hands over its first letter.

3 down COGNOSCENTI

Unimportant employee with no trace - bit of inkling they know (11)

Definition: they know · charade + first letter

  • Unimportant employee → COG a cog in the machine is the standard image for a worker who counts for little
  • no trace → NOSCENT a trace is a scent, so those two words are written out as they stand
  • bit of inkling → I the letter INKLING opens with

The surface grumbles about a nobody in the office and the answer is the opposite — the people in the know. It is Italian for 'those who know'.

4 down AIRFARE

Broadcast: food's price to go up (7)

Definition: price to go up · charade

  • Broadcast → AIR to air a programme is to broadcast it
  • food → FARE fare is what is set before you to eat, as in bill of fare

The colon makes it read as a headline about grocery inflation, so 'price to go up' looks like a verb phrase about rising costs rather than a noun.

5 down ROAD MAP

Perform hiphop about old admiral? I'll tell you where to go (4,3)

Definition: I'll tell you where to go · container + charade

  • Perform hiphop → RAP to rap is to perform hiphop, and this is the word that gets opened up
  • old → O o = old, the usual abbreviation
  • admiral → ADM adm. is the standard shortening of the naval rank

'I'll tell you where to go' reads as the opening of an argument, which is the misdirection — it is a flat statement of what the thing does for a driver.

7 down EAST OF EDEN

English feasted on novel - one from 1952 (4,2,4)

Definition: one from 1952 · anagram

  • English → E e = English, the standard abbreviation
  • feasted on → FEASTEDON anagram fodder, nine letters, with the E joined on

'Novel' is the trap: it looks like the definition sitting where a definition would sit, and it is the anagram signal, leaving 'one' to carry the definition.

8 down STABLE BOYS

So let baby cavort; they won't frighten the horses (6,4)

Definition: they won't frighten the horses · anagram

  • So let baby → SOLETSBABY anagram fodder, ten letters — the printed version dropped the S from 'lets' and the Observer corrected it online the same day; without that S the fodder is a letter short

Count the fodder before you trust it: as printed it is one letter shy, and the paper reissued the clue with 'lets' that Sunday. The horses are the giveaway.

12 down OUTSIDER ART

Creation on the fringes: bizarrely, it's our trade (8,3)

Definition: Creation on the fringes · anagram

  • it's our trade → ITSOURTRADE anagram fodder, eleven letters

The surface is a couple of craftspeople describing their line of work, and the answer is what galleries call art made right outside the art world.

13 down REGISTRANT

One signing in and restarting after revision (10)

Definition: One signing in · anagram

  • restarting → RESTARTING anagram fodder, ten letters

'Signing in' and 'restarting' both belong to the same computer screen, which is what makes the definition so hard to see as a definition.

14 down PROPER NOUN

May, say, but may ... not (6,4)

Definition: May, say, but may ... not · cryptic definition

  • May, say → with its capital, May is a month and a surname — a name for one particular thing, which is the test being applied
  • but may ... not → in lower case the same three letters are an ordinary verb of permission, no kind of name, and so fail that test

One word inspected twice. A capital at the start of a clue is invisible, since every clue starts with one, which is why 'May, say' looks like an example of something else.

18 down EXHAUST

Sap getting a kind of pipe (7)

Definition: Sap · double definition

  • Sap → the verb sense: to sap somebody is to drain their strength away
  • a kind of pipe → a noun and a different word altogether — the tube that carries burnt gases out of an engine

The two senses share a spelling and nothing else, and 'getting' is there only to join them. 'A kind of' is the usual flag that a category, not a synonym, is being pointed at.

19 down SCOTTIE

Small bed with couple and pooch (7)

Definition: pooch · charade

  • Small → S s = small, as on a clothing label
  • bed → COT a cot is a small bed
  • couple → TIE to couple two things is to tie them together — the verb, not the pair

A nationality falls out of the first two pieces by accident, which is half the pleasure: 'small bed' has nothing to do with Scotland and produces it anyway.

21 down CIAO

A friendly 'hello' in Naples - or quite the opposite (4)

Definition: A friendly 'hello' in Naples · double definition

  • A friendly 'hello' in Naples → Naples is doing no more than telling you which language to greet somebody in
  • quite the opposite → the very same word is what Italians say on leaving, so the greeting doubles as a farewell

One word for both ends of a meeting is unusual in English, which is why the clue can define it twice and still read as a single sentence about Italy.

22 down ENID

Maybe Blyton's a bit uneven? I daresay (4)

Definition: Maybe Blyton · hidden word

  • uneven? I daresay → ENID the letters run on from the end of the first word, through the one-letter word, and into the start of the last

'A bit uneven' reads as mild criticism of the writing, which is the innocent surface a hidden word needs, and the capital I helps disguise the join.
