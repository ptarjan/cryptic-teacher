Across

1 across BACKLIGHT

Rugby player lacking weight put in the shade? (9)

Definition: put in the shade? · charade

  • Rugby player → BACK a back is a rugby position, behind the forwards
  • lacking weight → LIGHT light = lacking weight

To backlight something is to light it from behind, throwing its face into shadow — so the question-marked definition is fair. Rugby's BACKS are a crossword staple for 'player'.

6 across MOTTO

Words to live by, not initially arse-backwards (5)

Definition: Words to live by · deletion + reversal

  • arse → OTTOM BOTTOM (arse) 'not initially' — drop the B

Translate 'arse' to BOTTOM first, then obey both instructions: lose the first letter, read it backwards. The rude idiom 'arse-backwards' smuggles the reversal indicator past you.

9 across GILET

Joe, for one, allowed a padded jacket (5)

Definition: a padded jacket · charade

  • Joe, for one → GI a Joe is an example of a GI — an American soldier
  • allowed → LET let = allowed

'For one' signals definition by example: Joe, as in GI Joe, is one particular GI. The French-derived gilet has become the standard word for the padded bodywarmer.

10 across RAIN DANCE

Call for monsoon? Mumbai resident dropping one in a hurry (4,5)

Definition: Call for monsoon? · deletion + container

  • Mumbai resident → INDIAN an Indian
  • one → I I = one — this is what gets dropped
  • a hurry → RACE to race = to hurry; it wraps round the rest

INDIAN drops an I and squeezes into RACE. The question-marked definition is the ritual dance performed to summon rain — a monsoon, keeping the Mumbai surface honest.

11 across MISERY GUTS

Tigress, with ‘Yum!’, mauled grumbler (6,4)

Definition: grumbler · anagram

  • Tigress → TIGRESS anagram fodder
  • ‘Yum!’ → YUM joins the fodder — ten letters in all

'Mauled' is both what a tigress does and what has happened to the letters. A misery guts is British slang for a habitual complainer.

12 across TYPE

Some charity, perhaps, would be kind (4)

Definition: kind · hidden word

  • charity, perhaps → TYPE hidden across the join: chariTY PErhaps

'Some' tells you to take only part of what follows, and the answer straddles the comma. 'Charity, perhaps' is pure camouflage — only its letters matter.

14 across PAINTED

Showed desire to embrace one in make-up? (7)

Definition: in make-up? · container

  • Showed desire → PANTED panted (after) = showed desire
  • one → I I = one, embraced

PANTED takes in I. 'In make-up?' is a fair adjectival definition — a painted face is a made-up one, as in 'painted lady'.

15 across DATASET

US lawyer ready to accept thanks for information (7)

Definition: information · charade + container

  • US lawyer → DA DA = district attorney
  • thanks → TA ta = thanks, accepted in the middle
  • ready → SET set = ready

DA and SET accept TA between them. DA for 'US lawyer' and TA for 'thanks' are two of the most reused scraps in cryptics — worth banking.

17 across AT LARGE

A goal almost entirely securing first place in league, in general (2,5)

Definition: in general · charade + deletion + container + first letter

  • A → A taken as itself
  • goal → TARGE TARGET (goal) 'almost entirely' — last letter off
  • first place in league → L the first letter of 'league'

TARGE(t) secures L and follows A. The sense needed is 'the public at large' — in general — not the fugitive meaning the surface hints at.

19 across DREYFUS

Duke’s decline largely involving unknown figure in French scandal (7)

Definition: figure in French scandal · charade + deletion + container

  • Duke → D D = duke
  • decline → REFUS REFUSE (decline) 'largely' — most of it
  • unknown → Y y, algebra's unknown, taken inside

D + REFUS(e) involving Y. Alfred Dreyfus was the wrongly convicted officer at the centre of the affair that split 1890s France — the 'figure in French scandal'.

20 across ITCH

Desire to lose, discarding diamonds (4)

Definition: Desire · deletion

  • to lose → DITCH to ditch = to lose, get rid of
  • diamonds → D D = diamonds, the card suit — this is what's discarded

DITCH sheds its D. Card-suit abbreviations (D, H, C, S) are standard currency, and the definition is 'desire' as in an itch to travel.

22 across PERMITTING

Allowing style’s suitable, but not fine (10)

Definition: Allowing · charade + deletion

  • style → PERM a perm is a hairstyle
  • suitable → FITTING fitting = suitable
  • fine → F f = fine, as on pencils — this is what's dropped

PERM plus FITTING shorn of its F. Definition and answer agree in form — 'allowing' for PERMITTING — which is exactly the agreement a fair setter owes you.

25 across AMBIGUOUS

Pompous Unionist blocking a timid one, mostly? That’s of doubtful value (9)

Definition: of doubtful value · charade + container + deletion

  • Pompous → BIG big = pompous, self-important
  • Unionist → U U = Unionist, the political abbreviation
  • a timid one → AMOUS A MOUSE (a timid one), 'mostly' — the E drops

BIG + U block A MOUS(e). 'Of doubtful value' defines fairly: what is ambiguous is of uncertain meaning or worth.

26 across AMIGO

Friend I’m backing to invest in a success (5)

Definition: Friend · reversal + container

  • I’m → MI I'M sent backing
  • a success → A GO a go = a success, as in making a go of things

Reversed I'M invests in A GO. Amigo is the Spanish loan-word English uses for a pal — a one-word definition hiding at the front.

27 across CLANG

Big noise in family group estranging nearly all? (5)

Definition: Big noise · charade + deletion

  • family → CLAN a clan is a family
  • group → G GROUP, estranging nearly all of its letters, keeps only its G

'Family group' looks like one unit, but only 'family' gives CLAN; 'group' then estranges nearly all of itself down to a single G. The question mark owns up to that playful liberty.

28 across GALAPAGOS

Drink in the past contributing to atmosphere in islands (9)

Definition: islands · charade + container

  • Drink → LAP to lap = to drink, as a cat does
  • in the past → AGO ago = in the past
  • atmosphere → GAS gas = atmosphere; it takes the rest in

'Contributing to' is a gentle container signal, easy to read as pure surface. Darwin's islands answer to a one-word definition while the wordplay wears all the disguise.

1 down BY GUM

How stamps are affixed? Crikey! (2,3)

Definition: How stamps are affixed? · double definition

  • How stamps are affixed? → BY GUM stamps are stuck on by gum — read literally
  • Crikey! → BY GUM by gum! — a dated exclamation of surprise

A whimsical double definition: the literal reading (stamps really are affixed by gum) and the exclamation, a politely softened 'by God!' still heard in northern England.

2 down CELESTIAL

Starry Parisian is one active in political group (9)

Definition: Starry · charade + container

  • Parisian is → EST 'is' in French — est
  • one → I I = one
  • active → A a = active
  • political group → CELL a cell is a small political unit; it wraps round the rest

EST + I + A sit inside CELL — the sleeper-unit sense of 'political group'. 'Parisian is' for EST is a piece of French vocabulary worth banking.

3 down LITERATURE

University brought in rare title, fantastic writing (10)

Definition: writing · anagram

  • University → U U = university, 'brought in' to the mix
  • rare title → RARETITLE anagram fodder, nine letters

An anagram of RARE TITLE with U folded in. The surface about a university awarding an odd degree keeps 'fantastic' looking like praise rather than an instruction.

4 down GARAGED

Book salvaged from rubbish daughter stored (7)

Definition: stored · deletion + charade

  • Book → B B = book — this is what gets salvaged (taken out)
  • rubbish → GARAGE GARBAGE (rubbish) minus that B
  • daughter → D d = daughter

'Salvaged from' means the B is lifted out of the rubbish, not added to it. The definition is the innocuous last word: a garaged car is a stored one.

5 down TWISTED

Peculiar warped wits hosted by talks provider? (7)

Definition: Peculiar · anagram + container

  • wits → WIST WITS 'warped' — anagrammed
  • talks provider? → TED TED talks; the ? nods to defining by brand

TED — the talks brand — hosts warped WITS. The definition and the anagram indicator sit shoulder to shoulder, so 'Peculiar warped' reads like one description.

6 down MODE

A way with meter and rhyme? (4)

Definition: A way · charade

  • meter → M m = metre, in its American spelling
  • rhyme? → ODE an ode is a poem — 'rhyme' loosely, hence the ?

The surface promises poetry, but 'meter' is just the abbreviation M and the ode is the crossword's all-purpose poem. A mode is simply a way of doing something.

7 down TUNNY

Fish like the location where fish are readily shot? (5)

Definition: Fish · double definition

  • Fish → TUNNY a tunny is the tuna
  • like the location where fish are readily shot? → TUNNY tun + -y: like a TUN, a barrel — as in shooting fish in a barrel

As sun gives sunny, tun gives TUNNY: barrel-like — and a barrel is where fish are proverbially easy to shoot. The plain definition is the everyday name for the tuna.

8 down OPERETTAS

Stereo apt to distort musical works (9)

Definition: musical works · anagram

  • Stereo apt → STEREOAPT anagram fodder, nine letters

The surface grumbles about hi-fi equipment; the answer is Gilbert-and-Sullivan territory. 'Apt to distort' hides the anagram instruction inside a plausible complaint.

13 down STREET LAMP

Definition: by the way, it’s illuminating · container + reversal

  • whisky → TLAM MALT (whisky), sent 'up' this down entry

STREEP swallows reversed MALT. The whole closing phrase defines: a street lamp is illuminating and stands by the way — the wayside — not a conversational aside at all.

14 down PHARISAIC

Definition: hypocritical · anagram + container

An anagram of A CHAP IS placed about RI. The Pharisees are the New Testament's byword for showy piety, which is how PHARISAIC comes to mean hypocritical.

16 down SUFFICING

Definition: appearing to be enough · reversal + charade

  • following → FF ff = 'and following', as in page references
  • cake decoration → ICING icing

'Promoting' works as a reversal because the entry runs downwards — promotion sends US up the grid. The scholarly abbreviation ff for 'following' is the clue's best disguise.

19 down DAMOSEL

21 down COBRA

Definition: Government committee · charade

  • firm → CO co. = company, a firm
  • support → BRA a bra is a support

COBRA is the UK government's emergency committee, named for the Cabinet Office Briefing Rooms. CO for 'firm' and BRA for 'support' are both crossword standards.

23 down GROSS

Definition: Unrefined · double definition

  • Unrefined → GROSS gross = coarse, unrefined
  • Imperial measure? → GROSS a gross = twelve dozen, 144 — an old traders' quantity

A gross — twelve dozen — belongs to the old pre-metric world of counting, so 'Imperial' carries a question mark. The other sense is plain: coarse, crude, unrefined.

24 down AGOG
