Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,898

Across

1 across INCREASINGLY

More and more entered for race, processed one by one (12)

Definition: More and more · charade + anagram

  • entered → IN to be entered for something is to be in it
  • race → CREA the four letters of the contest, coming out of the machine in a different order
  • one by one → SINGLY doing things one by one is doing them singly

'Entered for' does two jobs at once: on the surface it signs the runners up, and in the wordplay it hands over a preposition and then a contest to be jumbled.

9 across GUBBINS

Return of bad code trashes device (7)

Definition: device · reversal + charade

  • bad code → GUB a fault in a program is a bug, and it comes back to front
  • trashes → BINS to bin something is to trash it

Reading 'bad code' as one idea rather than two words is the step that opens the clue; the surface is a virus wrecking the hardware.

10 across MASCARA

Hair thickener Cliff brought into male support group (7)

Definition: Hair thickener · charade + container

  • male → M M is the standard abbreviation for male
  • support group → AA Alcoholics Anonymous is the support group a setter means
  • Cliff → SCAR a scar is a bare rock face on a hillside, and the capital letter is a red herring

Both halves of the surface mislead: the capital makes you think of the singer, and 'male support group' sounds like a men's club rather than a letter and a meeting.

11 across THROB

Strong beat via bass (5)

Definition: Strong beat · charade

  • via → THRO going via a place is going through it, and 'thro'' is the old contraction of that
  • bass → B B is the abbreviation for bass on a score or an amplifier

Nothing tells you to shorten anything: 'thro'' is simply an accepted spelling, one of the poetic contractions (o'er, e'en) that setters treat as ordinary words.

12 across EASTBOUND

European surprise about British making move towards China? (9)

Definition: making move towards China? · charade + container

  • European → E E is the abbreviation for European
  • surprise → ASTOUND to astound somebody is to surprise them thoroughly, and this word opens up
  • British → B B is the abbreviation for British

The question mark is the setter admitting that heading east is not specifically heading for China — it is one destination out of many in that direction.

13 across WATERMARK

Figure on banknote Washington designate a Republican king (9)

Definition: Figure on banknote · charade

  • Washington → WA WA is the postal abbreviation for the American state
  • designate → TERM to term something is to designate it by a name
  • a → A taken as it stands
  • Republican → R R is the abbreviation for Republican
  • king → K K is the king on a chessboard and in a pack of cards

The surface sets up the dollar bill and its president, but Washington is only two letters here — the state, not the man — and the rest is a run of small conventions.

15 across TONGA

Indian's cart excellent on Ganges' bridges (5)

Definition: Indian's cart · hidden word

  • excellent on Ganges' → TONGA five letters in a row that start inside the first word and finish inside the third

Everything in the surface is scenery — a bargain, a river, a bridge — and the answer, a cart you hire outside an Indian station, sits in the middle of it untouched.

16 across AWASH

Receding somewhat, Noah saw Ararat barely sticking out of the water (5)

Definition: barely sticking out of the water · hidden word + reversal

  • Noah saw Ararat → AWASH a run of five letters lies across the joins here, written in the opposite direction

All the Genesis furniture is what makes the letters hard to see: the flood is scenery, and the answer is lying backwards underneath it.

18 across HOMEOPATH

Ineffective therapist in work at hospital (9)

Definition: Ineffective therapist · charade

  • in → HOME somebody who is in is at home — 'is she in?'
  • work → OP OP is the abbreviation for an operation, a surgeon's piece of work
  • at → AT two letters taken as they stand
  • hospital → H H is the abbreviation for hospital, as on a road sign

'In' for HOME hides best of the four, because it looks like the smallest of link words rather than a piece of the charade.

20 across ENTRANCES

Ways in which empty nester can't see changes? (9)

Definition: Ways in · anagram + outer letters

  • nester → NR the first and last letters, with the middle hollowed out
  • can't see → CAN'T SEE seven letters taken as they stand, the apostrophe ignored

Read as English the clue is a parent who cannot spot what has altered at home; read as wordplay it is a hollowed-out word and a phrase thrown together.

22 across LEMON

American Tour de France winner mostly in yellow (5)

Definition: yellow · deletion

  • American Tour de France winner → LEMOND the first American to win the race, in 1986; his surname arrives with one letter to spare

The cyclist writes his surname with a capital M in the middle, which is part of why it hides so well inside an ordinary colour word.

24 across STRATUM

Fact that's doubtful about river level (7)

Definition: level · charade + container

  • Fact → STAT a stat is a figure quoted as hard fact, and this word opens up
  • river → R R is the map-maker's abbreviation for river
  • that's doubtful → UM the noise you make when you are not sure of something

Two very different registers side by side: a clipped modern abbreviation for a fact, and the noise of hesitation written down as letters.

25 across THAWING

How cute crush squeezes, becoming friendlier (7)

Definition: becoming friendlier · container

  • crush → THING having a thing for somebody is having a crush on them
  • How cute → AW the noise you make at something adorable

The surface is all teenage romance, and so is the wordplay: one piece is the crush itself, the other is the noise made about it.

26 across BENEFACTRESS

Donor putting lock on account after complaint about Nigel's capital (12)

Definition: Donor · container + charade

  • complaint → BEEF to have a beef with somebody is to have a complaint
  • Nigel's capital → N the capital letter the name begins with, not a city
  • account → AC AC is the standard abbreviation for account
  • lock → TRESS a tress is a lock of hair

'Nigel's capital' is misdirection worth learning: it sounds like a country's chief city and means the letter the name starts with.

Down

1 down INGOT

Cast bar where Batman can be found scorning bad acting (5)

Definition: Cast bar · deletion

  • where Batman can be found → IN GOTHAM the caped crusader patrols one particular city, and the wordplay puts him in it
  • bad acting → HAM an actor who lays it on far too thick is a ham

'Bad acting' is the piece to spot: three letters that come out of the city and leave the metal behind. The surface sounds like a critic sneering at a superhero film.

2 down CABARET

Jazz fan bored by plain acts at club (7)

Definition: acts at club · container

  • Jazz fan → CAT a cat is the hip word for a jazz player or devotee
  • plain → BARE a bare wall and a plain wall are the same undecorated thing

Beat-era slang does the work: 'cat' for a jazz enthusiast is worth banking, because setters reach for it whenever jazz turns up in a surface.

3 down EDINBURGH CASTLE

Scots keep Red Sea nightclub running ... (9,6)

Definition: Scots keep · anagram

  • Red Sea → RED SEA six letters taken exactly as they stand
  • nightclub → NIGHTCLUB nine more, straight off the page and into the mix

There is no Red Sea nightclub to picture — the phrase exists to be taken apart. 'Scots keep' reads as a nation hanging on to something and means a stronghold.

4 down SASSENACH

... Englishman there sticks around, entertained by band (9)

Definition: Englishman there · container + reversal

  • band → SASH a sash is the band of cloth worn across the chest
  • sticks → SENAC sticks you walk with are canes, and they arrive written the other way round

The row of dots carries 'there' back to the previous answer, so 'there' is Scotland — and the word is what a Scot calls a visitor from south of the border.

5 down NAMES

Nearly all notice guy rolling big cheeses (5)

Definition: big cheeses · charade + deletion + reversal

  • notice → SE to notice something is to see it, and the last of those three letters is given up
  • guy → MAN a guy is a man, the everyday sense of the word

Two small pieces are assembled and only then turned over, which is the order to keep straight. 'Big cheeses' is the tip-off that the answer is about people.

6 down LAST BUT NOT LEAST

Mixture of table salt to nuts ultimately significant? (4,3,3,5)

Definition: ultimately significant? · anagram

  • table salt → TABLE SALT nine letters taken as they stand
  • to nuts → TO NUTS six more, the little word swept into the mix as well

The definition splits neatly in two: 'ultimately' is the coming-last half and 'significant' the not-being-least half, so the phrase answers it word for word.

7 down SATURN

Orbiting body like spinning wheel (6)

Definition: Orbiting body · reversal + charade

  • like → SA 'like' is AS, and here it is written the other way about
  • wheel → TURN to wheel round and to turn are the same movement

Two-letter words hiding in plain sight are a setter's favourite: 'like' belongs to a small set (as, so, qua) that hands over letters without ever looking like wordplay.

8 down HARD CASH

Plastic card in mess, ATM ejecting it? (4,4)

Definition: ATM ejecting it? · anagram

  • card → CARD four letters taken as they stand
  • mess → HASH to make a hash of something is to make a mess of it

The joke is that plastic is the opposite of what the slot spits out, and 'Plastic card' looks like one phrase when it is really an instruction followed by its material.

13 down WHAT ELSE

Women can't stand university, that's inevitable (4,4)

Definition: that's inevitable · charade

  • Women → W W is the abbreviation for women, as on a door
  • can't stand → HATE if you can't stand something, you hate it
  • university → LSE the London School of Economics, whose initials setters lean on constantly

Said as a rhetorical question rather than a statement, the answer is a shrug: of course that happened. The surface pretends it is a complaint about student life.

14 down KAMA SUTRA

Adult guide reviewed Amtrak when crossing northern America (4,5)

Definition: Adult guide · anagram

  • Amtrak → AMTRAK the American passenger rail company, six letters taken as they stand
  • northern America → USA the northern portion of the Americas is the States, and its three letters join the mix
  • when crossing → surface only: what the trains do to the country, and it adds no letters

'Adult guide' is a shop-shelf euphemism rather than a description of a manual, and the railway and the country are in the clue only for the letters they carry.

17 down ATTIRE

Clothes dry in breeze, barely wet (6)

Definition: Clothes · container + middle letter

  • breeze → AIR a breeze is moving air, and this word opens up to take something in
  • dry → TT TT is teetotal, the crossword's standard dry
  • wet → E strip the outside off this three-letter word and a single letter is left

'Barely' is the joke: it means bare, not scarcely, and stripping a word bare is exactly what a clue about clothes ought to ask for.

19 down ARMOIRE

Sleeve Doris cut in odd places on wardrobe (7)

Definition: wardrobe · charade + alternate letters

  • Sleeve → ARM the sleeve of a coat is its arm
  • Doris → OI the name gives up the letters standing first, third and fifth
  • on → RE 'on' in the sense of 'concerning' is RE, the heading at the top of a memo

The surface has Doris altering a garment. 'On' for RE is worth banking — a paper on a subject is a paper re it, and setters use the pair constantly.

21 down COMBE

Valley searched methodically but no diamonds (5)

Definition: Valley · deletion

  • searched methodically → COMBED police going over a field inch by inch are doing exactly this, and the word arrives one letter too long
  • diamonds → D D is the card player's abbreviation for the suit

The surface is a prospector coming home empty-handed, which is the joke: the missing diamond is a single letter rather than a stone.

23 down NEGUS

Hot drink for example accepted by rising star (5)

Definition: Hot drink · container + reversal

  • star → NUS the nearest star is the sun, and here it is climbing the grid
  • for example → EG e.g. is the Latin abbreviation for 'for example'

Port or sherry with hot water, sugar and spice was a standard Victorian party drink, which is why it turns up in Dickens and in crosswords more often than in pubs.
