Across

1 across GRAMMAR

Rules regarding language ultimately applying to crash damage (7)

Definition: Rules regarding language · charade + last letter

Grammar is the body of rules governing how a language is put together, so the definition is the plain sense of the word.

  • ultimately applying → G the closing letter of 'applying'
  • crash → RAM to ram something is to crash into it
  • damage → MAR to mar is to damage or spoil
  • ultimately — 'Ultimately' means at the very end of things, so it points at the final letter of the word beside it.

Small print about wording that ends up governing what a collision costs.

'Crash damage' is a single insurance phrase in the surface, and the clue quietly splits it into two unrelated synonyms that happen to sit next to each other.

5 across WHISTLE

Sound from police, perhaps, sealing off street in time (7)

Definition: Sound from police, perhaps · container

A whistle is the shrill blast a police officer blows, and 'perhaps' marks that as one example of a sound they might make.

  • time → WHILE a while is a stretch of time
  • street → ST ST is the standard abbreviation on any address
  • sealing off — Sealing a place off closes it in on every side, so the word named next is shut inside another.
  • in — 'In' puts the piece before it within the piece after it, rather than alongside.

Police cordoning off a road for a spell.

The surface has officers taping up a street, so 'sealing off' reads as police work rather than as a direction about where one word goes inside another.

9 across TRIAL

Manner adopted by officer backing court-martial? (5)

Definition: court-martial · container + reversal

A court-martial is a trial held under military law, so the definition points at the answer through a single example of it.

  • officer → LT LT is the rank abbreviation for a lieutenant
  • Manner → AIR an air is the manner someone carries themselves with
  • adopted by — To adopt someone is to take them into your own household, so the officer takes the other piece inside.
  • backing — Backing is going back the way you came, and in an across entry that means the letters read from the right.

An officer whose bearing is what got him hauled up before a military court.

'Court-martial?' is one particular species of the thing rather than a synonym for it, which is what the question mark is quietly admitting.

10 across KARATEIST

Chopper whirling at a strike (9)

Definition: Chopper · anagram

A karateist practises karate, whose signature blow is the chop, so 'chopper' names the person by the strike they deliver.

  • at a strike → ATASTRIKE nine letters of fodder
  • whirling — Whirling spins something so fast that you can no longer tell which part was where, which is what happens to these letters.

A helicopter circling above a picket line.

'Chopper' wants to be the helicopter the surface is flying, and it means a person who chops. Karateist really is the noun for someone who practises karate.

11 across COOT

Nellie getting twice the love in bed (4)

Definition: Nellie · container

Both words are dated slang for a simpleton: this is the coot of 'you old coot' rather than the black waterbird of the same name.

  • bed → COT a cot is a small bed
  • twice the love → O love is the tennis score of nil, written as a nought, and a second one joins the one the bed already carries
  • in — 'In' puts the letter before it inside the word after it, rather than next to it.

Nellie enjoying a double helping of affection at bedtime.

'Twice the love' does not ask for two letters to be inserted — one goes in, landing beside the one that was already sitting there.

12 across DISHARMONY

Mains out of sync with hydro makes for conflict (10)

Definition: conflict · anagram

Disharmony is the state of not getting along, which is conflict in its abstract sense of discord between people or ideas rather than open fighting.

  • Mains → MAINS five letters of fodder
  • hydro → HYDRO five more, shuffled together with the first five
  • out of sync — Things out of sync have slipped out of the order they should keep, which is exactly what happens to these letters.

The national grid and the hydroelectric stations failing to keep in step.

Mains and hydro belong to the same electrical picture, so the sentence reads as a technical complaint about power supply rather than as ten letters to rearrange.

14 across ANYONE

An old article about cricket side, no matter who’s involved? (6)

Definition: no matter who’s involved · charade + container

Anyone means any person at all, which is precisely what 'no matter who' says — the answer supplies the person the phrase deliberately leaves open.

  • An → AN the clue's own first word, used as it stands
  • old article → YE YE is the archaic form of 'the', as in ye olde shoppe
  • cricket side → ON the on side is the half of a cricket field lying behind the batter's legs
  • about — 'About' means round the outside of a thing, so the article wraps itself round the cricket term.

An old newspaper piece about a cricket team, whoever happened to be playing.

'Article' is the grammarian's article rather than a piece of writing, and cricket's 'on' side is the leg side, the half behind the batter as they stand.

15 across BLUEBIRD

One flies in America? Low offer’s about right (8)

Definition: One flies in America · charade + container

The bluebird is a North American thrush, so 'one flies in America' points at it by where it lives instead of naming the family.

  • Low → BLUE feeling low is feeling blue
  • offer → BID a bid is an offer at auction
  • right → R R is the standard abbreviation for right
  • about — 'About' wraps the offer round the outside, leaving the other letter sitting in its middle.

A lowball offer being judged more or less fair.

'Low offer's about right' reads as one flat verdict on a cheap bid, which is why none of its three words looks like machinery.

18 across HARLOTRY

A good many brought in to hound prostitutes’ activity (8)

Definition: prostitutes’ activity · container

Harlotry is the trade of a harlot, the old word for a prostitute, so the definition names the activity rather than the woman.

  • hound → HARRY to harry someone is to hound or harass them
  • A good many → LOT a good many of something is a lot of it
  • brought in — To bring something in is to carry it inside, so these letters are set down in the middle of the other word.

A crowd brought in to chase somebody down.

Harry sounds like a first name in the surface and is a verb meaning to harass, which is the reading the wordplay needs and the surface never suggests.

20 across PARADE

Display cut including promotional item (6)

Definition: Display · container

A parade is a public display — of troops, of floats, or of one's learning — so the two words swap as noun or as verb.

  • cut → PARE to pare is to cut or trim
  • promotional item → AD an ad is the standard shortening of an advertisement
  • including — To include something is to have it inside you, so the shorter piece sits within the longer one.

A budget cut that still leaves room for advertising.

'Display' serves as both noun and verb, and so does the answer, so the definition gives away nothing about which of the two is wanted.

23 across CONFORMITY

Doing well in town, following the crowd (10)

Definition: following the crowd · container

Conformity is going along with what everyone else does, so 'following the crowd' is the idiom for the very same behaviour.

  • town → CITY a city is a town, on the large side
  • Doing well → ONFORM a player doing well is on form, the British sporting phrase
  • in — 'In' places what comes before it inside what comes after, so the phrase sits within the town.

Somebody prospering in town by doing exactly what everybody else does.

'Doing well in town' reads as one continuous statement about a person's fortunes, which is why the join between its two ideas is invisible.

24 across MESS

Eatery in Times Square (4)

Definition: Eatery · hidden word

A mess is the room where soldiers or sailors eat together, so 'eatery' is the general word standing in for a specifically military one.

  • Times Square → MESS four consecutive letters bridge the gap, starting two letters into the first word
  • in — 'In' says the letters are already sitting inside the words that follow and need only picking out.

Somewhere to grab a bite just off Times Square.

New York's landmark is a strong enough picture to stop you reading the two words as a run of letters, and that is the whole of the concealment.

26 across SENTIMENT

Living to accommodate mass emotion (9)

Definition: emotion · container

Sentiment is feeling as opposed to reason, so it is emotion in its milder, more considered sense rather than raw passion.

  • Living → SENTIENT a sentient creature is one capable of feeling, and so living
  • mass → M M is the physicist's abbreviation for mass
  • accommodate — To accommodate someone is to find room for them indoors, so the letter is housed inside.

A way of living that leaves room for feeling on a grand scale.

'Mass emotion' looks like one phrase — a crowd's shared feeling — and its two words belong to opposite halves of the clue.

27 across TIBIA

Bone somewhat twisted? I should probe that (5)

Definition: Bone · reversal + container

The tibia is the shinbone, the thicker of the two bones between knee and ankle, so 'Bone' defines it by its class.

  • somewhat → ABIT somewhat means 'a bit', and those four letters are turned round
  • I → I the pronoun stands for the single letter it is written with
  • twisted — Twisting a thing turns it round, so these letters come out running the other way.
  • should probe that — To probe is to push into something, so the pronoun is driven inside the letters just turned.

A slightly warped bone that somebody ought to examine.

'Somewhat' has to be swapped for a phrase before anything can be done to it, and that unsignalled first step is where the clue is hardest.

28 across COOKIES

Military leader acceptable - that is, beginning to supply biscuits (7)

Definition: biscuits · charade + first letter

Cookies are biscuits in American English — the same object under a different name on the other side of the Atlantic.

  • Military leader → CO the CO is the commanding officer in charge of a unit
  • acceptable → OK OK means acceptable
  • that is → IE i.e. is short for the Latin id est, 'that is'
  • beginning to supply → S the opening letter of 'supply'
  • beginning to — 'Beginning to' takes you only as far as the start of the word that follows, which is its first letter.

A commander pronounced acceptable, and now starting to hand out the biscuits.

Nothing in the sentence announces a join, because every piece is an abbreviation that also passes as an ordinary word of the surface.

29 across MIRACLE

Group of motorists in the distance for amazing experience (7)

Definition: amazing experience · container

A miracle is an event so extraordinary that it defies explanation, and 'amazing experience' is the weakened everyday use of exactly that word.

  • the distance → MILE a mile is a set distance
  • Group of motorists → RAC the RAC is the Royal Automobile Club, one of Britain's two big roadside breakdown organisations
  • in — 'In' drops the motorists inside the word that follows instead of leaving them beside it.

A convoy of drivers glimpsed far off down the road.

'In the distance' is one phrase in the surface and three separate jobs in the wordplay, with the definite article belonging to the measurement rather than the view.

1 down GATECRASH

Arrive without a ticket and go down after those attending (9)

Definition: Arrive without a ticket · charade

To gatecrash is to turn up at a party or event uninvited and unpaid, which is exactly arriving without a ticket.

  • those attending → GATE the gate at a football match is the crowd that turned up, and the money they paid at the turnstiles
  • go down → CRASH a plane or a market that goes down has crashed
  • after — In a down clue 'after' means further down the page, so the second piece is written underneath the first.

Turning up unpaid and then flopping down behind the people who bought seats.

'Gate' meaning the attendance at a sporting fixture is British sports-page vocabulary, and it does the same job in the picture as it does in the letters.

2 down ALIMONY

Post-match expense? Some will involve flash car (7)

Definition: Post-match expense · container

Alimony is the maintenance a divorced spouse pays, so it is the expense that arrives after a match — a marriage — has ended.

  • Some → ANY 'some' and 'any' are the same quantifier once a sentence turns into a question
  • flash car → LIMO a limo is the flashy hired car of choice
  • will involve — To involve something is to wrap it up in whatever you are doing, so the car ends up inside the other word.

The bill after a sporting fixture, some of which runs to a hired limousine.

'Match' is a marriage rather than a fixture, which is the whole misdirection, and 'will involve' passes as a prediction about costs instead of an instruction.

3 down MALT

Whisky? Macallan, Ardbeg, Lagavulin, Talisker, for starters (4)

Definition: Whisky? · first letters

Malt is whisky made from malted barley alone, so 'Whisky?' defines it by the wider class it belongs to, with the question mark marking that looseness.

  • Macallan → M the leading letter of the first of four distilleries
  • Ardbeg → A an Islay distillery, contributing its initial
  • Lagavulin → L another Islay name, handing over the letter it starts with
  • Talisker → T the Skye distillery, closing the run with its first letter
  • for starters — 'For starters' means to begin with, so only the opening letter of each name is being served.

A whisky lover reeling off the distilleries they would open the evening with.

All four names are Scottish single malt distilleries, so the definition is hiding among four things that are already examples of it, and the list reads as flavour.

4 down RAKE IN

Acquire Ecstasy, mostly sloping around (4,2)

Definition: Acquire · container + deletion

To rake in money is to acquire it fast and in quantity, the phrase used of profits rather than of a single purchase.

  • mostly sloping → RAKIN a stage or a windscreen that slopes is RAKING, here docked of its last letter
  • Ecstasy → E E is the standard abbreviation for the drug
  • mostly — 'Mostly' means most of it but not all, so the word gives up the letter it ends on.
  • around — Something placed around another thing encircles it, so these letters open up and take the drug inside.

Someone getting hold of a pill and then lounging about at an angle.

A rake is a slope — the pitch of a theatre stage or the angle of a windscreen — and that is the sense doing the work, not the garden tool.

5 down WORMHOLE

Old military group blocking entire interstellar transport option? (8)

Definition: interstellar transport option · container

A wormhole is a hypothetical tunnel folding two distant regions of space together, which is why fiction uses it to cross between stars.

  • entire → WHOLE whole means entire
  • Old → O O is the standard abbreviation for old
  • military group → RM RM is the Royal Marines, Britain's amphibious infantry corps
  • blocking — Anything blocking a passage sits in the middle of it, so these letters lodge inside the word that follows.

An ancient regiment barring the only route between the stars.

The question mark is doing real work: nobody has ever travelled by one, so the definition is offered as a possibility rather than as a fact.

6 down INTERREGAL

Bizarre triangle involving former Queen before another ruler is named? (10)

Definition: before another ruler is named · anagram

Interregal means falling between reigns, so it describes the stretch after one ruler has gone and before a successor is proclaimed.

  • triangle → TRIANGLE eight letters of fodder
  • former Queen → ER ER was Elizabeth Regina's royal cypher, and 'former' dates her as the previous monarch
  • Bizarre — Something bizarre is out of its normal shape, and letters out of their normal shape have been put in a new order.
  • involving — 'Involving' folds the extra pair of letters into the mixture rather than leaving them standing outside it.

An odd love triangle drawing in the late Queen, dated to the gap before the next monarch.

ER is the crossword's standing pair of letters for a queen, from Elizabeth Regina, and 'former' is there to date her rather than to change anything.

7 down TRIPOLI

African city’s mistake over distribution of oil (7)

Definition: African city · charade + anagram

Tripoli is the capital and largest city of Libya, on the North African coast, so the definition names it by continent and kind.

  • mistake → TRIP a trip is a stumble, and so a mistake
  • oil → OLI three letters dealt into a new order
  • over — In a down clue 'over' is vertical: the piece named first sits on top of whatever comes next.
  • distribution of — Distributing something deals it out, so these letters are dealt into an order the clue has not printed.

A north African capital's blunder over how the oil gets shared out.

Oil is the commodity the surface is arguing about and the raw material of the letters at the same time, so the word looks like part of the story.

8 down ENTRY

Nobles offering no Government access (5)

Definition: access · deletion

Entry is the act or right of going in, so 'access' is the same idea in its noun form — a way in rather than permission to use something.

  • Nobles → GENTRY the gentry are the British landowning class, ranked just below the titled nobility
  • Government → G G is the standard abbreviation for government, and it is the letter withheld
  • offering no — Offering none of a thing is refusing to supply it, so the letter named next is simply not there.

Landed families refusing to let anyone from Whitehall through the door.

'Offering no Government' passes as a political refusal and is an instruction to drop a single letter, which is the only mechanism in the clue.

13 down ANNO DOMINI

An agreement over small car for the present time (4,6)

Definition: the present time · charade

Anno Domini is the era our dates are counted in — AD — so 'the present time' is the age we are living through rather than this instant.

  • An → AN the clue's own opening word, taken as it stands
  • agreement → NOD a nod is how agreement is given without speaking
  • over → O O is the scorer's abbreviation for a cricket over, the six balls bowled from one end
  • small car → MINI the Mini is the British small car

A deal struck over a little car, signed off in the here and now.

'Over' looks like a positional instruction in a down clue and is a cricket abbreviation instead, and it is the one piece nothing in the sentence flags.

16 down DEEP SPACE

Hurry twice, in different directions, to reach interplanetary region (4,5)

Definition: interplanetary region · charade + reversal

Deep space is the near-empty region out beyond the planets, so 'interplanetary region' names the emptiness between worlds rather than any body in it.

  • Hurry → DEEPS a hurry is a SPEED, turned round to run the other way up the grid
  • twice → PACE 'twice' calls for a second word meaning hurry, and PACE goes in as it stands
  • in different directions — If the two words run different ways, one of them has to be written out backwards — and it is the first.

Rushing about in two directions at once in the hope of getting somewhere.

Both halves mean hurry and only one of them is disturbed; 'twice' is the clue's way of asking for a synonym it never prints.

17 down TRUMPETS

Tories gutted after politician among Right blares (8)

Definition: blares · container + outer letters

To trumpet something is to blare it out — to announce it loudly and publicly — so the two verbs are the same brazen noise.

  • Right → TRUE right in the sense of correct is true
  • politician → MP MP is a Member of Parliament
  • Tories gutted → TS 'Tories' emptied of its insides keeps only the letters at its two ends
  • gutted — A gutted fish has had its insides taken out, leaving only what was sitting on the outside.
  • among — Something among others is in the middle of them, so the politician lands inside the other word.
  • after — In a down clue 'after' means further down, so these letters follow underneath the rest.

The Conservatives hollowed out, with one member of theirs left among the party's right wing.

'Right' is not the political right the surface is arguing about but plain correctness, and it is the word that opens up to take the member inside.

19 down RUN INTO

Come across dispute - wife leaving couple (3,4)

Definition: Come across · charade + deletion

To run into someone is to meet them by chance, which is the 'come across' sense of stumbling upon rather than the sense of seeming a certain way.

  • dispute → RUNIN a run-in is a dispute or confrontation
  • couple → TWO a couple is two
  • wife → W W is the standard abbreviation for wife, and it is the letter that departs
  • leaving — If the wife is leaving, her letter walks out of the word she was standing in.

A quarrel breaks out and the wife walks out on the pair of them.

The two pieces do not break where the answer does: the dispute ends one letter past the gap, so the spaces move once the letters are laid down.

21 down ALEMBIC

Smart guy keeping IBM unsettled still (7)

Definition: still · container + anagram

An alembic is the gourd-shaped vessel alchemists distilled in, so 'still' names it by the job it does: distilling.

  • Smart guy → ALEC a smart alec is a know-all
  • IBM → MBI the computer firm's three letters, resettled
  • keeping — To keep something is to hold on to it, so the letters named next are held inside.
  • unsettled — Anything unsettled will not stay in the arrangement it was given, so these three letters settle somewhere new.

A clever operator keeping the computer giant permanently on edge.

'Still' closes the sentence reading as 'even so' and is the definition, and a noun at that — a piece of equipment rather than an adverb.

22 down STATIM

Gloves picked up, going round area right now (6)

Definition: right now · reversal + container

Statim means immediately in Latin, so 'right now' is the plain English of a word that survives in medicine and in crosswords.

  • Gloves → MITTS mitts are gloves, and here they are lifted up the grid
  • area → A A is the standard abbreviation for area
  • picked up — Picking a thing up lifts it, and in a down entry a lifted word is read from the bottom upwards.
  • going round — Going round something means encircling it, so the area ends up enclosed by the rest.

Someone snatching up their gloves and heading straight out.

Statim is the Latin for 'at once', the word behind the 'stat!' shouted in hospital dramas, and English keeps it almost only on prescriptions.

23 down COSEC

Little time to support company function (5)

Definition: function · charade

Cosec is the trigonometric function cosecant in its usual abbreviated form, so 'function' names it by the mathematical class it belongs to.

  • company → CO CO is the standard abbreviation for a company
  • Little time → SEC a sec is a second, time's smallest everyday unit
  • to support — Whatever supports another thing is underneath it, and underneath in a down entry means later in the answer.

A brief spell of time propping up a firm.

'Function' is mathematical rather than a party or a role, and cosec is the short form of cosecant, the reciprocal of the sine.

25 down STAR

Look hard, endlessly - glower? (4)

Definition: glower · deletion

Read as glow-er, the definition means a thing that glows, and a star is the original one — burning gas seen from an enormous distance.

  • Look hard → STARE to stare is to look hard at something
  • endlessly — Endlessly means without an end, so the word loses the letter it finishes on.

One person glaring at another and never letting up.

'Glower' is not the scowl the surface wants but glow-er, a thing that glows, which is why the setter leaves a question mark behind it.
