Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,889

Across

1 across MARIGOLD

Mother and doctor of many years bloom (8)

Definition: bloom · charade

  • Mother → MA ma is the nursery word for a mother
  • doctor → RIG to doctor a result is to rig it — fix it in advance
  • of many years → OLD anything of many years is old

'Doctor' looks like an instruction to shuffle the letters beside it. It is nothing of the kind here: it is a plain verb, handing over three letters of its own.

5 across UTMOST

Extreme mutt's barking outside back of Tesco (6)

Definition: Extreme · anagram + container + last letter

  • mutt's → MUTTS the dog's own five letters are the raw material, apostrophe and all
  • back of Tesco → O Tesco is a British supermarket chain, and only its final letter is wanted

The surface has a dog yapping behind a shop. Nothing is barking except the letters, and the supermarket is there to give up one character.

9 across PARADIGM

Yearly grub in batter for model (8)

Definition: model · container + charade

  • Yearly → PA PA is per annum, the yearly rate quoted on a savings ad
  • batter → RAM to batter a door down is to ram it
  • grub → DIG to grub about in the soil is to dig

'Grub in batter' sounds like something fried at a fairground. Both words are verbs here, and neither has anything to do with food.

10 across FISCAL

Dealing with revenue approximately on island in Florida (6)

Definition: Dealing with revenue · container + charade

  • Florida → FL FL is the two-letter postal code for Florida, and it opens up in the middle
  • island → IS IS is how an island is labelled on a map
  • approximately → CA ca. is circa, the 'approximately' put in front of a date

Florida does two jobs at once: it holds the holiday surface together and, split in half, it holds the wordplay too.

12 across SENNA

Princess first in salon, back for quick Brazilian (5)

Definition: quick Brazilian · charade + first letter + reversal

  • Princess → ANNE the Princess Royal, Anne, is the crossword's standing princess
  • salon → S the salon gives up its opening letter and nothing else

'Quick Brazilian' sounds like a beauty treatment booked at that salon. It is a person: Ayrton, three-time Formula One world champion, killed at Imola in 1994.

13 across TRACK DOWN

Find curtain rail on the floor (5,4)

Definition: Find · charade

  • curtain rail → TRACK the rail a curtain runs along is a curtain track
  • on the floor → DOWN a boxer on the floor is down

Both halves are so ordinary that the join hides in plain sight — there is no indicator anywhere to say two everyday words are being laid end to end.

14 across CHRISTMAS EVE

Shifting last of crops, harvest mice stocking up then? (9,3)

Definition: stocking up then? · anagram + last letter

  • last of crops → S the final letter of crops, taken on its own
  • harvest mice → HARVESTMICE eleven letters handed over whole, to be rearranged around that S

Harvest mice hoarding for winter is a perfectly good picture of autumn, and it is made entirely of letters: the night it points to is months later.

18 across LAND REGISTRY

Record holder with most titles? (4,8)

Definition: Record holder with most titles? · cryptic definition

  • Record holder → the surface offers an athlete who holds a world record; read the other way it is an office whose job is holding records
  • with most titles? → titles here are not championships won but legal titles — the documents proving who owns a piece of ground

There is nothing to take apart: the whole clue is one sentence read twice, sport on the surface and property law underneath.

21 across CHINATOWN

Bristly feature next to private dining area? (9)

Definition: dining area? · charade

  • Bristly feature → CHIN the part of a face that grows stubble
  • next to → AT waiting at the door is waiting next to it, so one little word swaps for the other
  • private → OWN your own room is your private one

Three ordinary words run together with no indicator anywhere, and the question mark is the setter conceding that the definition is a district, not a room.

23 across SHEER

Pipe down keel's centre runs straight down (5)

Definition: straight down · charade + middle letters

  • Pipe down → SH sh is the noise you make telling somebody to be quiet
  • keel's centre → EE the middle pair of a four-letter word
  • runs → R R is runs on a cricket scorecard

Every word here sounds nautical — pipes, keels, running straight down — and the pieces turn out to be a hush, two middle letters and a cricket abbreviation.

24 across TRAVEL

Extent of movement beginning to trouble composer (6)

Definition: Extent of movement · charade + first letter

  • trouble → T the opening letter of the word, and no more of it
  • composer → RAVEL Maurice Ravel, the French composer of Boléro

The definition is the engineer's word, not the holiday one: the travel of a lever or a piston is how far it can move.

25 across PHEASANT

Long trousers affected Ashe's game (8)

Definition: game · container + anagram

  • Long → PANT to pant for something is to long for it, and the word opens after its first letter
  • Ashe's → HEAS Arthur Ashe, the tennis champion, supplies four letters in a new order; the 's is the surface's possessive

'Ashe's game' reads as tennis, and the man's name is only there for its letters. 'Long' is a verb here, and 'trousers' is not a noun at all.

26 across PRETTY

Nice to see motorbike race in quarry (6)

Definition: Nice · container

  • quarry → PREY the quarry of a hunt is the creature being chased — its prey
  • motorbike race → TT the TT is the Isle of Man Tourist Trophy, raced on public roads

The capital N is only there because the clue starts, so 'Nice' is the plain adjective rather than the French city, and 'quarry' is an animal rather than a pit.

27 across APPENDIX

A writer Richard's mentioned added pages (8)

Definition: added pages · charade + homophone

  • A writer → APEN a writer is a pen, with the clue's own A in front of it
  • Richard's → DICKS Dick is the familiar form of Richard
  • A writer Richard's mentioned → APPENDIX said aloud at speed, those three little words run together into one long one

Nothing is spelled out here — the pieces only work once you say them, which is why the letters never quite add up on the page.

Down

1 down MOPISH

Down second flipping hot drink (6)

Definition: Down · charade + reversal

  • second → MO a mo is a moment — 'hang on half a mo'
  • hot → H H is the tap marked hot, and the crossword's letter for it
  • drink → SIP to drink slowly is to sip, and these three letters turn round with the H in front of them

'Flipping hot drink' reads as one grumble about a scalding cuppa. The three words are three separate jobs: a synonym, an abbreviation and an instruction.

2 down RARING

Enthusiastic clique supporting Gunners (6)

Definition: Enthusiastic · charade

  • Gunners → RA the Gunners are the Royal Artillery, the British Army's gun regiment
  • clique → RING a closed little group working together is a ring

The Gunners are also Arsenal, which is what the football surface wants you to hear; the letters come from the regiment the nickname was borrowed from.

3 down GODFATHER

Old king hides unexpected death of spiritual mentor (9)

Definition: spiritual mentor · container + anagram

  • Old king → GR GR is Georgius Rex, King George — the monogram still cast into old British postboxes
  • unexpected death of → ODFATHE seven letters of 'death of', rearranged and lodged between the king's two

'Old king' promises a story about Cole or Lear. It is two letters of royal shorthand, and everything between them is the same seven letters in a new order.

4 down LIGHTNING ROD

Conductor reducing weight of baton, saving energy (9,3)

Definition: Conductor · charade + deletion

  • reducing weight → LIGHTNING reducing weight is lightening, and it arrives one letter short
  • energy → E E is the physicist's letter for energy, and it is the letter that goes
  • baton → ROD a baton is a slim rod carried in the hand

A conductor with a baton is an orchestra, and both words have been borrowed from the wrong trade: the conductor is electrical and the baton is only a stick.

6 down THINK

Feel OK, when nothing is fine (5)

Definition: Feel · substitution

  • fine → THIN fine thread is thin thread — the width sense, not the weather one
  • OK → K OK is two letters, and only the second survives the swap

'Feel OK' reads as a question about your health, which is the trap: OK is not a state of mind here but a pair of letters, one of them replaced.

7 down ONCE-OVER

Check right after crossing (4-4)

Definition: Check · charade

  • right after → ONCE once can mean as soon as: 'once we've eaten' is right after we've eaten
  • crossing → OVER to get over a river is to cross it

'Right' begs to be the usual single letter for right. It belongs with 'after' instead, and both halves of this answer are ordinary words rather than abbreviations.

8 down TALENTED

Naturally good books accepted by story editor (8)

Definition: Naturally good · container + charade

  • story → TALE a story told is a tale
  • books → NT the books a crossword means are usually scripture: NT is the New Testament
  • editor → ED ED is the standing abbreviation for an editor, as printed on a masthead

The surface is a tidy scene in a publishing office, and every word of it is really shorthand — three stock abbreviations laid end to end with one tucked inside.

11 down PARTISANSHIP

Piano maker half polishes piano, preferring one side (12)

Definition: preferring one side · charade + deletion

  • Piano → P p is the musician's mark for piano, an instruction to play softly
  • maker → ARTISAN an artisan is someone who makes things by hand
  • polishes → SHI polishes is shines, and only the front of that word is taken
  • piano → P the second piano is the same single letter, arriving at the end

A piano maker polishing a piano is a lovely picture and a complete fiction: the two pianos are single letters, one at each end.

15 down AEROSPACE

Archer quickly comprehends flight technology (9)

Definition: flight technology · container

  • quickly → APACE to move apace is to move quickly, and the word opens up after its first letter
  • Archer → EROS Eros, the god of love, does his work with a bow and arrow

'Archer' looks like a surname — the novelist, or the radio family. It is the archer of Piccadilly Circus, the arrow-shooting god.

16 down BLACKTOP

Returned to power, Labour's leader admitted requirement to fix potholes (8)

Definition: requirement to fix potholes · container + charade + first letter

  • Returned → BACK an MP returned at an election is back in the House
  • Labour's leader → L the leader of a word is its first letter, and the party's is L
  • power → TOP the top of an organisation is where its power sits

The surface is election night — in Britain a candidate is 'returned' when elected — and the answer is what a council spreads on the road afterwards.

17 down INTIMATE

Close friend cheers up, parting eventually (8)

Definition: Close friend · container + reversal

  • eventually → INTIME something that happens eventually happens in time
  • cheers → AT cheers is ta, the everyday thank-you, and it is turned round before it goes in

'Cheers up' reads as a mood lifting. It is a two-letter thank-you standing on its head, and the definition wants a noun where the surface offers an adjective.

19 down HERALD

Announce that woman's hairless except head (6)

Definition: Announce · charade + deletion

  • that woman's → HER what belongs to that woman is hers, and the possessive gives three letters
  • hairless → BALD a head with no hair on it is bald
  • head → B the head of a word is its opening letter, and this is the one that comes off

'Hairless except head' sounds like a description of where the hair still is. It is an instruction: take a word and knock the front off it.

20 down PRE-TAX

Monsieur's ready with a kiss - potentially gross? (3-3)

Definition: gross · charade

  • Monsieur's ready → PRET Monsieur signals French, and prêt is the French for ready
  • a → A the clue's own word, taken at face value
  • kiss → X X is the kiss pencilled at the bottom of a letter

'Ready' is also slang for cash, which keeps the money surface running. And 'gross' is the accountant's word for the whole amount, not a verdict on the kiss.

22 down ADEPT

A little cyclist pedalled around showing proficiency (5)

Definition: showing proficiency · hidden word + reversal

  • cyclist pedalled → ADEPT five consecutive letters straddle the gap between these two words, and they are read from right to left

'A little cyclist' is not a comment on anybody's height: 'a little' means a small piece of what follows, which is where the letters are sitting.
