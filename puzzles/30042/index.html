Guardian Cryptic Crossword No 30,042

Across

1 across MOONSET

Disappearance of Ganymede, say, short time before dawn (7)

Definition: Disappearance of Ganymede, say · charade

  • short time → MO a mo = a moment, a short time
  • dawn → ONSET the dawn of something is its onset

Ganymede is a moon of Jupiter, so 'say' is doing real work: the definition is by example, and a moonset is the general case.

5 across SUN-LAMP

Lighter article from France entered into poetry competition by Penny (3-4)

Definition: Lighter · container + charade

  • article from France → UN the French indefinite article
  • poetry competition → SLAM a poetry slam; split S…LAM
  • Penny → P p = penny

'Lighter' is a noun meaning a thing that gives light, not the thing in your pocket, and that switch is the clue's one real joke.

9 across LILAC

Colour I observed in ring given a twirl (5)

Definition: Colour · container + reversal

  • I → I the letter I, slipped inside
  • ring → CALL give someone a ring = a call; I sits in it as CAL-I-L

'ring' is the telephone sense. And 'given a twirl' turns the whole assembly rather than any one piece — reversing after inserting is an order worth watching for.

10 across BUBBLE TEA

Black then beat blue fluid for drink (6,3)

Definition: drink · anagram

  • Black → B b = black, as on pencils
  • then → link word joining B to the fodder
  • beat blue → BEATBLUE anagram fodder, eight letters
  • fluid → anagram indicator

'Black then beat blue' reads as a decorating instruction, which is why none of it looks like fodder. B for black comes off the side of a pencil.

11 across RIPSNORTER

Wow, the snuff taker has snuffed it! (10)

Definition: Wow · charade

  • has snuffed it → RIP rest in peace — written for someone who has died
  • the snuff taker → SNORTER snuff is snorted, so its taker is a snorter

The joke is the entire surface: a snuff taker who has snuffed it. Both halves of the answer fall out of that one grim pun.

12 across CLEF

Musical symbol split, briefly (4)

Definition: Musical symbol · deletion

  • split → CLEF split = CLEFT; 'briefly' drops its last letter

'briefly' nearly always means drop the last letter — and 'split' is the word being cut, not an instruction to do the cutting.

14 across HEDGEHOPPER

Anything but a high-flier for the guillotine, did you say? (11)

Definition: Anything but a high-flier · homophone

  • the guillotine → HEDGEHOPPER the guillotine is a head-chopper; said aloud, 'head-chopper' becomes HEDGEHOPPER

The whole clue is one long joke — a guillotine is a head-chopper, and said quickly that becomes a flier who skims the hedges rather than climbing above them.

18 across SEISMOGRAPH

First of musicians in parish goes crazy, shaking recorder (11)

Definition: shaking recorder · anagram + first letter

  • First of musicians → M first letter of Musicians
  • parish goes → PARISHGOES anagram fodder, ten letters
  • crazy → anagram indicator

'shaking recorder' is a fair definition and a fine disguise: it reads as a nervous witness, while 'crazy' is the word actually doing the shaking.

21 across ASDA

Turning left, notices a superstore (4)

Definition: superstore · reversal

  • notices → ADS notices = advertisements
  • a → A the A from the clue

In an across clue, 'Turning left' means read backwards — the same job 'up' does in a down clue. That is the only instruction here.

22 across FISHMONGER

Obscene gesture keeps quiet doctor in the gutter? (10)

Definition: in the gutter? · container + charade

  • Obscene gesture → FINGER giving someone the finger; split FI…NGER
  • quiet → SH sh! = quiet
  • doctor → MO MO = medical officer

'in the gutter?' sounds like a moral judgement and describes a man gutting fish; the question mark is the setter enjoying himself. The obscene gesture is the finger.

25 across GALLIVANT

Bother, it having impeded vehicle – search for diversion? (9)

Definition: search for diversion? · charade + container

  • Bother → GALL gall = irritation, bother
  • it → IT split I…T around the vehicle
  • vehicle → VAN a van

To gallivant is to gad about, so the question mark carries a definition that is a description rather than a synonym. IT split around a VAN is the move to spot.

27 across TROTSKY

Red, pace ahead of blue (7)

Definition: Red · charade

  • pace → TROT a trot is a pace
  • blue → SKY sky blue

'Red' is a person here, not a colour, and both pieces come from ordinary words — the surface reads like a paint chart.

28 across LADY DAY

Case on driveway packed into car, end of holiday celebration in March (4,3)

Definition: celebration in March · outer letters + container + charade + last letter

  • driveway → DY its case — the outer letters — is D…Y
  • car → LADA the Lada; split LA…DA
  • end of holiday → Y last letter of holidaY

Lady Day, 25 March, is the feast of the Annunciation and an old quarter day; setters use it, so it is worth knowing. 'Case' meaning outer letters is the unusual instruction.

Down

1 down MILORD

Beer swilling if not noble (6)

Definition: noble · container

  • Beer → MILD mild is a traditional dark English beer; split MIL…D
  • if not → OR 'hurry, or you'll be late' means 'if not, you'll be late'

'if not' for OR is the substitution to learn — say 'hurry, or you'll be late' and you can hear it. The beer surface hides the nobleman completely.

2 down OXLIPS

Plants kiss where kiss planted, love coming first (6)

Definition: Plants · charade

  • love → O love = zero in tennis
  • kiss → X the X at the end of a letter
  • where kiss planted → LIPS you plant a kiss on the lips

'kiss' does two jobs a few words apart: first the X, then the thing you plant on lips. Oxlips are woodland primulas, cousins of the cowslip.

3 down SECOND HOME

Diabolical demon chose the other place? (6,4)

Definition: the other place? · anagram

  • demon chose → DEMONCHOSE anagram fodder, ten letters
  • Diabolical → anagram indicator

The definition is a joke rather than a synonym, and the question mark is the setter's apology for it. 'Diabolical' meanwhile suits the demon so well it stops looking like an instruction.

4 down TIBER

Italian flower again chewed up? (5)

Definition: Italian flower · reversal

  • again chewed → REBIT chewed again = RE-BIT

'flower' is crossword code for a river — a thing that flows — and it is one of the first conventions worth learning. This one is Italian.

5 down SUBGENERA

A grub seen wriggling in biology classes (9)

Definition: biology classes · anagram

  • A grub seen → AGRUBSEEN anagram fodder, nine letters
  • wriggling → anagram indicator — a fine image for a grub

'wriggling' is exactly what a grub does, so the indicator disappears into the sentence. Subgenera sit just below a genus in the biological order.

6 down NULL

Empty pan? You fill vessel, finally (4)

Definition: Empty · last letters

  • pan? You fill vessel → NULL final letters of paN, yoU, filL, vesseL

Four last letters in a row, and 'finally' at the very end is the only word telling you so. 'Empty pan?' reads as one phrase, which is how the definition hides.

7 down ANTELOPE

Resident of nest, fly off graceful animal (8)

Definition: graceful animal · charade

  • Resident of nest → ANT ants live in nests
  • fly off → ELOPE to elope is to fly off — to run away, classically to marry

ANT is the crossword's favourite three letters — worker, insect, resident of nest — and 'fly off' for elope keeps the whole surface about escape.

8 down PLATFORM HEELS

Villains waiting to board train, perhaps, in ‘70s attire (8,5)

Definition: ‘70s attire · charade

  • Villains → HEELS heels = villains, as in wrestling slang
  • waiting to board train, perhaps → PLATFORM where you stand waiting to board a train

HEELS for villains is wrestling slang, the piece most solvers need told once. The station surface gives nothing away, and the answer runs on into 26 across.

13 down POOH-POOHED

Big jobs for a reporter journalist sniffed at (4-6)

Definition: sniffed at · homophone + charade

  • Big jobs → POOHPOOH nursery slang for poos — 'for a reporter', they sound like POOH, POOH
  • journalist → ED ed = editor, the crossword journalist

'for a reporter' is the homophone signal: something as it would be spoken. The nursery joke does the rest, and ED is the usual crossword journalist.

15 down DIGNITARY

Big noise as good egg splits log (9)

Definition: Big noise · charade + container

  • good → G g = good
  • egg → NIT a nit is a louse's egg
  • log → DIARY a diary is a log; split DI…ARY

'good egg' is a compliment on the surface and two separate pieces underneath, G and NIT. Spotting that split is the whole difficulty.

16 down ESCARGOT

Slow mover races off before caught (8)

Definition: Slow mover · anagram + charade

  • races → ESCAR anagram of RACES, signalled by 'off'
  • caught → GOT caught = got

The slow mover is a snail, at least on a French menu — and 'races' pretending to be speed is the setter's joke at its expense.

17 down VINDALOO

Lentils and duck in red or white dish that’s hot (8)

Definition: dish that’s hot · container + charade

  • Lentils → DAL dal = the Indian lentil dish, lentils
  • duck → O a duck is a zero in cricket
  • red or white → VINO wine; split VIN…O

Duck for zero comes from cricket, and 'red or white' for wine keeps the surface at the dinner table. The fiddly part is two Os arriving from two different places.

19 down AGREED

You’re on peak of Annapurna, asking for a lot (6)

Definition: You’re on · charade + first letter

  • Annapurna → A its peak — the first letter in this down clue
  • asking for a lot → GREED greed is wanting, and asking for, a lot

'You're on' is what you say when a deal is struck, which makes it a fair definition even though it looks like a fragment. 'peak of' means top letter because the clue runs down.

20 down TRASHY

Worthless wood fills crack (6)

Definition: Worthless · container

  • wood → ASH ash is a wood
  • crack → TRY have a crack at = have a try at; split TR…Y

'crack' as a go at something is the substitution to spot. Everything else is a plausible sentence about DIY, which is what keeps it hidden.

23 down HOTEL

Accommodation in which weed and grass picked up? (5)

Definition: Accommodation · homophone

  • weed → HOE to weed the garden is to hoe
  • grass → TELL to grass on someone is to tell on them
  • in which → link words for the drug-deal surface

Weed and grass each have a straight sense and a drug sense, and the clue is built so the drug reading arrives first. 'picked up?' asks you to listen instead.

24 down PILS

Beer bringing hiccup from below (4)

Definition: Beer · reversal

  • hiccup → SLIP a slip = a small mistake, a hiccup
  • bringing → link word

'from below' means read upwards, which only makes sense in a down clue — one of those small rules that turns a clue from impossible into obvious.
