Across

1 across MARCO POLO

Merchant's parent company quietly invested in chocolate (5,4)

Definition: Merchant · container + charade

  • parent → MA ma is the everyday word for a mother
  • chocolate → ROLO a Rolo is a chocolate, and it opens up after its first letter
  • company → CO CO is the abbreviation on a firm's letterhead
  • quietly → P p is piano in music, the mark telling a player to be quiet

The surface sells you a corporate story of parents and investments, while the wordplay wants only a mother, a firm and a sweet.

6 across DALEK

Chipmunk back to irk exterminator? (5)

Definition: exterminator · charade + last letter

  • Chipmunk → DALE Chip and Dale are Disney's chipmunks, and this is the second of the pair
  • irk → K the letter standing at the back of irK

The question mark is doing real work: these creatures exterminate nobody by trade, they simply shriek the word.

9 across SLIME

Gooey substance produced by small tree (5)

Definition: Gooey substance · charade

  • small → S S is the label sewn into the smallest size
  • tree → LIME the lime is a common street tree

'Produced by' is only joinery: nothing is being manufactured, the two pieces simply sit end to end.

10 across THIRSTILY

New hairstylist cutting as intensely (9)

Definition: intensely · anagram + deletion

  • hairstylist → HAIRSTYLIST eleven letters to start from
  • as → AS the two letters cut away, leaving the nine wanted

The salon picture holds together because cutting is what stylists do - and it is also the instruction to lose two letters.

11 across NANKEEN

Buff relative given piercing (7)

Definition: Buff · charade

  • relative → NAN nan is what a great many people call their grandmother
  • piercing → KEEN a keen wind is a piercing one

'Buff' is neither the enthusiast nor the bare skin here but a colour, which is the last of its senses most solvers reach for.

13 across ORSINO

Play duke wrong wearing gold ring (6)

Definition: Play duke · container + charade

  • gold → OR or is gold on a coat of arms
  • wrong → SIN a wrong done is a sin
  • ring → O a ring is a circle, written as the letter O

Two conventions worth banking for good: OR is gold in heraldry, and a ring is always the letter O.

15 across SLEEP WITH

Make love to wife on vacation - it helps to exercise (5,4)

Definition: Make love to · anagram + outer letters

  • wife → WE wife with its insides away leaves the two outer letters
  • it helps → ITHELPS seven letters more, which with the WE make the nine wanted

The dash makes the second half look like a remark about the first; it is really the fodder and the instruction to shuffle it.

17 across CHILD

Youngster disheartened having lost clothing in luggage (5)

Definition: Youngster · deletion + outer letters

  • disheartened → CHILLED to be chilled is to be dispirited, and these seven letters are the word to work on
  • luggage → LE the letters clothing lUGGAGe are the L and the E at its two ends, and that pair is what goes

'Luggage' is not a synonym for anything here - it is the word on the page, and the answer loses the two letters standing at its ends.

18 across AZTEC

Detective follows street maps of Mexican empire (5)

Definition: Mexican empire · charade

  • street maps → AZ the A-Z is the book of street maps in every British glovebox
  • Detective → TEC a tec is a detective in old slang

Two pieces of vocabulary worth keeping: 'tec' for a detective, and street maps for the A-Z rather than a range of letters.

19 across LETTING ON

Revealing Latvian spirit stocked by pub (7,2)

Definition: Revealing · container + charade

  • Latvian → LETT a Lett is a Latvian
  • pub → INN an inn is a pub, and it opens up after its second letter
  • spirit → GO go is spirit in the sense of drive - 'plenty of go'

'Spirit' looks like the drink a pub would stock and turns out to be pep, which is where the clue hides its joke.

21 across TERROR

Fear agent's latest blunder (6)

Definition: Fear · charade + last letter

  • agent's → T the last letter of agenT
  • blunder → ERROR a blunder is an error

The surface reads as spy fiction; the wordplay wants one letter of the agent and the mistake he made, nothing more.

22 across SENSORS

Some detecting edits on the radio (7)

Definition: Some detecting · homophone

  • edits → SENSORS somebody who edits the objectionable out is a censor, and censors said aloud is what you write in

Two words that sound identical and share no work at all: one keeps things out of print, the other picks up heat and movement.

25 across SAN MARINO

Country concerned with Iran kidnapping leader of military while undergoing revolution (3,6)

Definition: Country · container + charade + reversal

  • concerned with → ON a book on birds is a book concerned with birds
  • Iran → IRAN the country named, which opens up before its last letter
  • leader of military → M the leading letter of Military
  • while → AS as means while - 'as I was walking'

The surface is Middle Eastern politics and the answer is a tiny republic inside Italy, reached only by reading the whole assembly backwards.

26 across PRANG

Crash parking Duster around November (5)

Definition: Crash · container + charade

  • parking → P P is the parking sign at the roadside
  • Duster → RAG a duster is a rag
  • November → N November is N in the radio alphabet

The capital letter is a decoy - a Duster is a car you could well crash - but the clue only ever wanted the cloth.

27 across SEDGE

Perennial hosed gently drinks (5)

Definition: Perennial · hidden word

  • hosed gently → SEDGE the answer runs across the join of hoSED GEntly

'Perennial' is the gardener's noun rather than the adjective - a plant that comes back each year, and this one likes wet ground.

28 across UNEARTHLY

Alien hunter lay camouflaged (9)

Definition: Alien · anagram

  • hunter lay → HUNTERLAY nine letters, exactly the length the answer asks for

'Alien' is doing adjective duty here: not a creature at all, but the quality a noise has when it belongs nowhere on earth.

Down

1 down MUSINGS

Greek character vocalises thoughts (7)

Definition: thoughts · charade

  • Greek character → MU mu is the twelfth letter of the Greek alphabet
  • vocalises → SINGS to vocalise a tune is to sing it

'Greek character' is a letter of the alphabet, not somebody from Athens, and that swap is the whole of the trick.

2 down REIGN

Command doctor to check king's file before operation's conclusion (5)

Definition: Command · container + charade

  • doctor → RIG to doctor a result is to rig it
  • king's file → E in chess the king starts on e1, so the e-file is his
  • operation's conclusion → N the letter operatioN concludes on

Two hospital words are doing other jobs: the doctor is a verb meaning to fiddle with, and the file is a chessboard's rather than a patient's.

3 down OWE

Palaeontologist dismissing Newton must pay (3)

Definition: must pay · deletion

  • Palaeontologist → OWEN the Victorian naturalist who coined the word 'dinosaur' has this surname
  • Newton → N N is the symbol for the newton, the unit of force

One scientist gives up a letter to another: the man supplies the word and the unit of force takes its symbol back out.

4 down OSTENSIBLE

Pretended back in Tokyo Biles managed to claim perfect scores (10)

Definition: Pretended · charade + last letter + anagram + container

  • Tokyo → O the letter at TokyO's back
  • Biles → BILES the gymnast's five letters are the fodder, and they resettle as S ... IBLE
  • perfect scores → TENS a perfect score in gymnastics is a ten, and more than one makes tens

Simone Biles at the Tokyo Olympics is the picture the clue paints; the wordplay wants her surname's letters and the marks she was given.

5 down OMIT

Pass with third in Geography at Cambridge University (4)

Definition: Pass · charade

  • Geography → O geOgraphy, counted in three letters
  • at Cambridge University → MIT MIT is the university in Cambridge, Massachusetts

The Cambridge the surface offers is the English one with the third-class degrees; the wordplay means the other one, across the Atlantic.

6 down DESTRUCTION

Death that is going without international uproar (11)

Definition: Death · deletion + charade

  • that is → IDEST id est is Latin for 'that is', the phrase behind i.e.
  • international → I I is the abbreviation for international, and it is the letter that goes
  • uproar → RUCTION a ruction is a noisy row

Latin is the key here: read 'that is' as id est rather than as plain English, and the letter to be dropped has somewhere to come from.

7 down LAICISING

Removing church influence from new Sicilian government (9)

Definition: Removing church influence · anagram

  • Sicilian → SICILIAN eight letters from the island's adjective
  • government → G G is government in the abbreviations, the ninth letter needed

Mafia politics is the picture, but 'new' is doing nothing more sinister than telling you the letters will not stay where they are.

8 down KEYWORD

Descriptor of island population left abandoned (7)

Definition: Descriptor · charade + deletion

  • island → KEY a key is a low island, as in the Florida Keys
  • population → WORLD the world means the people in it - 'the whole world knows'
  • left → L L is left, and it is the letter that is dropped

Both nouns are in disguise: an island that is never called an island, and a population read as the whole world.

12 down EXPECTORATE

Anticipate talk to get something off your chest? (11)

Definition: get something off your chest · charade

  • Anticipate → EXPECT to expect a thing is to anticipate it
  • talk → ORATE to orate is to talk at length in public

The definition is the joke: the idiom means unburdening yourself of a secret, and the medical sense means clearing the chest quite literally.

14 down WHAT IS MORE

Saw her cavorting with 5 in addition (4,2,4)

Definition: in addition · anagram

  • Saw her → SAWHER six letters to start from
  • 5 → OMIT the answer at 5 down joins in, four letters more

A number in a clue points at another entry: solve 5 down first and its four letters become part of the fodder here.

16 down ENTHRONED

English theatre production's ending with Herod falsely made king (9)

Definition: made king · charade + anagram + last letter

  • English → E E is English in the abbreviations
  • theatre → NT the NT is the National Theatre on the South Bank
  • Herod → HEROD the king of the nativity story, five letters of fodder
  • production's → N the letter productioN ends on, joining the fodder

Herod stands in the surface as a stage villain and in the wordplay as five loose letters - and the definition is what Rome did to him.

18 down ATTESTS

Bears witness to cycling examination taken (7)

Definition: Bears witness to · charade + cycling

  • taken → SAT an examination that has been taken has been sat
  • examination → TEST an examination is a test

The two pieces are assembled first and spun afterwards: the S at the front of the pair rolls round to the far end.

20 down NOSEGAY

No vehicle leaving with flowers (7)

Definition: flowers · charade + deletion

  • No → NO the word is taken at face value, two letters
  • vehicle → SEGWAY a Segway is the two-wheeled machine you ride standing up
  • with → W w is the standard short form of with, and it is the letter that leaves

'With' looks like joinery holding the sentence together and is really a letter to be taken away - the commonest trap in a deletion clue.

23 down OMAGH

Irish town house outside school erected (5)

Definition: Irish town · container + reversal

  • house → HO ho. is the estate agent's short form of house
  • school → GAM a gam is a school of whales

'School' is the whaling word rather than the classroom one, and spotting that is the step the whole clue turns on.

24 down LIEU

Place similar to Britain without any Kings? (4)

Definition: Place · charade + deletion

  • similar to → LIKE one thing similar to another is like it
  • Britain → UK UK is Britain on the back of a car
  • any Kings → KK K is king on a playing card, and both of the ones here are dropped

The question mark flags the liberty in 'any Kings': it means every K in sight, and there happens to be one in each half.

26 down PAR

Expected shots where young footballers might play kilometres away (3)

Definition: Expected shots · deletion

  • where young footballers might play → PARK a park is where a kickabout happens
  • kilometres → K k is the abbreviation for kilometre, and it is the letter that goes

Golf and football share one clue: the pitch is a park, and the distances on the road signs hand you the letter to remove.
