Independent Cryptic Crossword No 12,420

Across

1 across GINNEL

Local punching setter in narrow alleyway (6)

Definition: narrow alleyway · container

  • setter → GEL gel is what sets hair, so the setter here is the tube on the bathroom shelf
  • Local → INN your local is the pub down the road, and a pub is an inn

'Setter' is almost always the person who wrote the clue, which is exactly why it is worth doubting here — this one sets hair.

5 across TEASPOON

Notes OAP is terrible stirrer (8)

Definition: stirrer · anagram

  • Notes → NOTES five letters taken as letters, not as music or as money
  • OAP → OAP an old age pensioner joins the mix, initials and all

The surface accuses a pensioner of stirring up trouble, and the answer is what stirs your tea — a different sort of stirrer entirely.

9 across LING

Heather is untruthful, taking year out (4)

Definition: Heather · deletion

  • is untruthful → LYING somebody untruthful is lying
  • year → Y Y is the standard abbreviation for year, and it is the letter that goes

'Heather' reads as a woman's name in a sentence about honesty, and it is the plant — the heather that covers a British moor.

10 across VERSAILLES

Seller with Visa unusual in palace location (10)

Definition: palace location · anagram

  • Seller → SELLER six letters taken at face value, not as a person
  • Visa → VISA four more letters, capital V and all

A trader taking cards is a plausible little scene, which is what fodder wants to look like: two ordinary nouns and nothing to see.

11 across ADOLESCENT

When housing benefit gets bit of money for youth (10)

Definition: youth · container + charade

  • When → AS as means when — 'as I left' is 'when I left' — and it opens after its first letter
  • benefit → DOLE the dole is unemployment benefit
  • bit of money → CENT a cent is the small change of a dollar or a euro

The benefits-office surface is built entirely out of the wordplay's own words, so nothing in it is pointing at a person of that age.

12 across TENT

Quietly happy to leave rip-off shelter (4)

Definition: shelter · deletion

  • Quietly happy → CONTENT content is happy in a settled, undemonstrative way
  • rip-off → CON a con is a swindle, and it is the three letters that walk

'Quietly' looks like the usual signal for P, the musician's piano, and here it is simply half of a two-word definition.

13 across MAR-A-LAGO

Perhaps Robbie cut cakes as prepared in opulent mansion (3-1-4)

Definition: opulent mansion · container + deletion

  • Perhaps Robbie → MARGOT Margot Robbie is one Robbie among many, which is what 'perhaps' is warning you about
  • as prepared → ALA a la is the menu French for done in the style of, as in a la carte

Everything reads as a tea party: 'cut cakes' is a knife and a plate on the surface, and two separate instructions underneath it.

16 across RENNES

Shy singer picked up in French city (6)

Definition: French city · homophone

  • Shy singer → RENNES a wren is the small skulking songbird of the hedgerow, its W is silent, and French drops the ending of the city's name the same way

French keeps the -nes on the page and swallows it in the mouth, which is how six letters can sound like three.

18 across GOSSIP

Leave, then go back and chat (6)

Definition: chat · charade + reversal

  • Leave → GO to leave is to go
  • then go → PISS 'go' the second time is the lavatory euphemism, piss, and that is the word turned round

The same little verb is clued twice in two different senses, which is why 'go' can sit in the answer and in the wordplay without repeating itself.

20 across NEMATODE

New poem about repellent domesticated worm (8)

Definition: worm · charade + container + reversal

  • New → N N is the standard abbreviation for new
  • poem → ODE an ode is a poem, and with the N in front it makes the shell
  • domesticated → TAME a domesticated animal is tame, and this is the word that gets driven back

'Repellent domesticated worm' reads as one phrase about an unpleasant creature, and the two adjectives belong to quite different jobs.

22 across OBAN

Steal ring from East Scotland town (4)

Definition: Scotland town · charade + reversal

  • Steal → NAB to nab something is to steal it
  • ring → O a ring is a circle, drawn as the letter O

'East Scotland' looks like one place and is two jobs — the east belongs to the wordplay, and the town it hides is on the west coast.

24 across IN THE CLEAR

Hear client somehow not under suspicion (2,3,5)

Definition: not under suspicion · anagram

  • Hear client → HEARCLIENT ten letters at face value, a lawyer and a verb dissolved into fodder

A solicitor listening to a client is a scene the wordplay borrows whole, and the definition is everything left in the sentence.

27 across FAIR-HAIRED

Maybe blonde female character ran to the audience (4-6)

Definition: Maybe blonde · charade + homophone

  • female → F F is the standard abbreviation for female
  • character → AIR the air of a person or a place is its character — an air of menace, an air of calm
  • ran → HAIRED to hare off somewhere is to run flat out, and 'hared' said aloud is written the other way

The joke is that the soundalike is the hair itself: a running hare and a head of hair make exactly the same noise.

28 across NEON

Great English scientist, losing heart, produces gas (4)

Definition: gas · deletion

  • Great English scientist → NEWTON Isaac Newton is the great English scientist, and the W and T at his centre are what go

The definition is one small word at the end of a sentence about physics, which is where a setter puts it when the surface is doing all the shouting.

29 across COMPOTES

Desserts in school regularly loathed by son (8)

Definition: Desserts · charade + alternate letters

  • school → COMP a comp is a comprehensive school
  • loathed → OTE every second letter of lOaThEd
  • son → S S is the standard abbreviation for son

A miserable school dinner is the picture, and the three pieces come from three registers: slang, a letter trick and an abbreviation.

30 across RED FOX

Common UK mammal given baguettes possibly to eat, according to Spooner (3,3)

Definition: Common UK mammal · spoonerism

  • given → FED to be given something to eat is to be fed
  • baguettes possibly → ROCKS a baguette is a long rectangular cut of gemstone, and rocks is the trade's slang for stones
  • given baguettes possibly to eat → RED FOX trade the F and the R at the front of the two words and the sound that comes out is the answer

Baguettes look like lunch and are jewellery here, so the surface's whole job is to keep you standing in the bakery.

Down

2 down ILIAD

Epic tale of single boy going round India (5)

Definition: Epic tale · charade + container

  • single → I a single is one, and one is I in Roman numerals
  • boy → LAD a boy is a lad, and it opens up after its first letter
  • India → I India is I in the phonetic alphabet

Two different I's from two different conventions, a Roman numeral and a radio call sign, which is how the clue gets away with using one twice.

3 down NIGELLA

D'Artagnan finally brought up "All for one, and one..." — a bloomer (7)

Definition: a bloomer · charade + reversal + last letter

  • D'Artagnan → N the last letter of the fourth musketeer's name
  • All → ALL three letters lifted straight out of the motto
  • for one → EG 'for one' is how a clue says e.g., the abbreviation of 'for example'
  • and one → I one is I again, and ALL, EG and I then climb back up the entry together

The musketeers' motto is quoted for the surface and then read as three unrelated scraps of crosswordese, so the quotation marks help you not at all.

4 down LIVES

Survives very boring falsehoods (5)

Definition: Survives · container

  • falsehoods → LIES falsehoods are lies, and the word splits after its second letter
  • very → V V is the standard abbreviation for very

Three words, and the middle one is an instruction rather than an opinion — 'boring' is not describing the falsehoods at all.

5 down THREE-POINT TURNS

Oscar then interrupts wild driving manoeuvres (5-5,5)

Definition: driving manoeuvres · anagram

  • Oscar → O Oscar is O in the phonetic alphabet
  • then interrupts → THENINTERRUPTS fourteen letters at face value, which with the O makes the fifteen the grid wants

'Interrupts' reads as an instruction to push one piece into another, and that is the trap: here it is fourteen letters of raw fodder.

6 down APART FROM

Save a piece for eccentric male (5,4)

Definition: Save · charade + anagram

  • a piece → APART a piece of something is a part of it, and the 'a' comes along too
  • for → FRO the three letters of 'for' knocked out of their usual order
  • male → M M is the standard abbreviation for male

'Save' at the front looks like a verb about rescuing or economising, and it is really the preposition of 'all save one'.

7 down PELOTON

Exercises with group running in pack (7)

Definition: pack · charade

  • Exercises → PE PE is physical education, the school lesson where you exercise
  • group → LOT a lot is a group of things taken together
  • running → ON a play or a film that is running is on

'Running in' looks like one phrase about a race and is really two jobs: 'running' is its own piece and 'in' only joins the halves of the clue.

8 down OCEAN

Old tin cans ultimately pollute large body of water (5)

Definition: large body of water · charade + container + last letter

  • Old → O O is the standard abbreviation for old
  • tin → CAN a tin is a can, and it opens after its first letter
  • pollute → E the last letter of pollute

'Cans' is doing a verb's work in a sentence full of nouns — read it as 'puts in a tin' and the clue comes apart at once.

14 down AGO

Past master's second attempt (3)

Definition: Past · charade + second letter

  • master's → A the second letter of master, which is also the A of an MA
  • attempt → GO an attempt at something is simply GO, the word behind 'give it your best shot'

'Past master' is a set phrase for an expert, and reading those two words together is what stops the clue splitting where it should.

15 down APPLICANT

European ignored by pescetarian probing suitable candidate (9)

Definition: candidate · container + deletion

  • suitable → APT apt means suitable, and it parts after its second letter
  • pescetarian → PELICAN a pelican lives on the fish it scoops up, so it keeps to a pescetarian's diet
  • European → E E is the standard abbreviation for European, and it is the letter the bird loses

The bird is the joke: 'pescetarian' describes a diet, and only one creature anywhere in the clue actually keeps to it.

17 down END

Stop loan, wasting pound (3)

Definition: Stop · deletion

  • loan → LEND to loan something is to lend it
  • pound → L L is the pound's letter, from the Latin libra, as on the £ sign

Money and lending sit together so naturally that 'pound' reads as part of the loan rather than as one letter to be thrown out.

19 down SUNTRAP

Dictator getting a Parisian to replace American in hot spot (7)

Definition: hot spot · substitution

  • Dictator → SATRAP a satrap was a Persian provincial governor, long a byword for a petty tyrant
  • a Parisian → UN 'a' in Paris is un
  • American → A A is the standard abbreviation for American, and it is the letter that makes way

Satrap is worth banking: it turns up whenever a setter wants a dictator and the letters S, A, T, R, P.

21 down TALONED

Left alone dangerous bears with hooked claws (7)

Definition: with hooked claws · hidden word

  • Left alone dangerous → TALONED the end of 'left', all of 'alone' and the start of 'dangerous' run straight into one another

Three ordinary words in a row about wildlife, and the answer is lying across the joins between them without the surface twitching once.

23 down BRAVO

British eggs on the up? Excellent! (5)

Definition: Excellent! · charade + reversal

  • British → BR BR is the short form of British, familiar from British Rail
  • eggs → OVA ova are eggs in the biologist's Latin, and they are what climbs

'Eggs on' reads as a single phrase meaning urges on, which is exactly the wrong place to break the clue.

25 down ELDER

Church official's European articles (5)

Definition: Church official · charade

  • European articles → EL el is the Spanish for 'the'
  • European articles → DER der is the German for 'the', and two of Europe's articles are all the clue needs

'Articles' is plural for a reason: the clue wants two of them, from two languages, and neither one is the English 'a' or 'the'.

26 down ADOBO

Fuss over nasty smell in Filipino dish (5)

Definition: Filipino dish · charade

  • Fuss → ADO much ado about nothing — ado is fuss
  • nasty smell → BO BO is body odour

Both pieces are staples worth banking: 'ado' for fuss and BO for a smell turn up several times a week between them.
