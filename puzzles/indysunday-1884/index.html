Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,884

Across

1 across BAMBOO

Italian child leaving home with nothing shoots to eat (6)

Definition: shoots to eat · deletion + charade

  • Italian child → BAMBINO a bambino is an Italian baby or young child
  • home → IN somebody at home is in, and those are the two letters that go
  • nothing → O nothing is written as a round zero, the letter O

The surface is a teenager moving out with no money. Both of the crossword conventions here are worth owning: at home is IN, and nothing is O.

5 across SQUIDGE

Macabre S Korean survival show shunned by American press (7)

Definition: press · deletion

  • Macabre S Korean survival show → SQUIDGAME Squid Game is the Korean series in which contestants play playground games for their lives
  • by American → AM American shortens to Am, and that pair is what gets shunned

'Press' invites you to think of newspapers, which fits the surface about a television show and is the wrong sense entirely.

9 across BROWN BEAR

Yogi, say, won Barbera endless spoils (5,4)

Definition: Yogi, say · anagram + deletion

  • won → WON three of the nine letters, taken exactly as written
  • Barbera → BARBER the grape's name one letter shorter, and the other six letters of the fodder

Yogi was drawn by the Hanna-Barbera studio, so the fodder and the definition come out of the same cartoon; 'say' is what marks him as an example.

10 across WIPER

Arm waving in front of driver with decapitated snake (5)

Definition: Arm waving in front of driver · charade + deletion

  • with → W w is the standard short form of with, the one used in recipes and notes
  • snake → IPER the snake is a viper, and its head has been cut off

The definition is the whole picture of a windscreen fitting, described so plainly that it reads as surface, while 'with' quietly does duty as a single letter.

11 across AS FAR AS IT GOES

Though less than satisfactory, what EV's range is? (2,3,2,2,4)

Definition: Though less than satisfactory · double definition

  • Though less than satisfactory → the concession you make when something is true only up to a point: fine, but do not push it further
  • what EV's range is? → an electric vehicle's range is the distance it will cover on one charge, read here as plain measurement

EV is the everyday abbreviation for an electric vehicle. The first half is idiom and the second is literal, which is what makes the two readings feel so different.

14 across DISPOSE

Settle back in bed? OK, reluctantly (7)

Definition: Settle · charade + last letter

  • bed → D the last letter of the three, and all that is wanted
  • OK, reluctantly → ISPOSE a grudging agreement is I s'pose, with suppose worn down to one syllable

'Settle back in bed' reads as one comfortable phrase, and that is the trap: the definition stops after the first word.

16 across SWEATER

Top part of UK for cider and nice apple (7)

Definition: Top · charade

  • part of UK for cider → SW cider country is the West Country, Somerset and Devon, in the south-west corner of England
  • nice apple → EATER in a British greengrocer's an eater is an apple for eating raw, as against a cooker

'Top part of UK' sends you to Scotland, and the cider is what tells you to look south-west instead. Nothing here announces itself as wordplay.

17 across SHORE UP

Orpheus played for support underground, perhaps (5,2)

Definition: support underground, perhaps · anagram

  • Orpheus → SHOREUP the letters of the name, all seven of them, given a different order

Orpheus went down to the underworld to fetch Eurydice and played his way past its guards, so every word of the surface is his story.

19 across SCOURGE

Flog half of Scotch eggs, saving the last (7)

Definition: Flog · charade + deletion

  • Scotch → SCO the front half of the word, three letters out of six
  • eggs → URGE to egg somebody on is to urge them, and the S at the end is held back

A Scotch egg is one picnic item on the surface and two separate pieces of wordplay underneath, with 'eggs' as a verb rather than a food.

20 across DISINTERESTED

Exhume Caesar's heart with Catholic priest unconcerned (13)

Definition: unconcerned · charade + middle letters

  • Exhume → DISINTER to disinter a body is to dig it up again
  • Caesar's → ES the middle two letters of the name
  • Catholic priest → TED Father Ted, the priest in the Irish sitcom named after him

Father Ted is a 1990s sitcom about three Catholic priests on an island off Ireland, which is why a first name on its own can clue one.

23 across CHINA

Source of tea in tea cups? (5)

Definition: Source of tea · double definition

  • Source of tea → the country the leaf first came from, and still the name printed on the caddy
  • tea cups? → the fine white ceramic they are made of, which took its name from where Europe first imported it

Both halves are about tea and both point at the same word, which is why the clue reads as one short question rather than two definitions in a row.

24 across HAIRSPRAY

Hot breezes please setter in salon (9)

Definition: setter in salon · charade

  • Hot → H H is hot, the letter on the tap
  • breezes → AIRS an air is a light breeze, and the plural comes with it
  • please → PRAY pray is the old-fashioned please, as in pray tell me

'Setter' is the word to distrust: in a crossword it usually means the person who wrote the clue, and here it is a thing that sets hair.

26 across MATADOR

Deadly sportsman twirling stick at amateur (7)

Definition: Deadly sportsman · charade + reversal

  • stick → ROD a rod is a stick, and this is the piece before the turn
  • at → AT the clue's own preposition, taken for its two letters
  • amateur → AM am is the short form of amateur, as in am-dram

'Twirling stick' paints a majorette and 'deadly sportsman' an assassin with a racket. Neither picture is the one the clue means, and only one word is an instruction.

27 across SHREWD

Penetrating offensive reported after call for peace (6)

Definition: Penetrating · charade + homophone

  • call for peace → SH sh is the noise you make to quieten a room
  • offensive → REWD offensive behaviour is rude, and once that sound follows the hush it is spelt differently

'Penetrating offensive' reads as a military push, and that is the whole misdirection: the first word is the definition and the second is a noise.

Down

2 down ALOOF

Cool, trick answer entered upside down (5)

Definition: Cool · charade + reversal

  • trick → FOOL to trick somebody is to fool them
  • answer → A A is the short form of answer, the letter beside the solution in a quiz book

Nothing in the surface admits to being an instruction: it reads as a card trick going in the wrong way round, and the two pieces queue up in plain sight.

3 down BAN

Make criminal steal flip-flops (3)

Definition: Make criminal · reversal

  • steal → NAB to nab something is to steal it

The surface is beachwear being pinched. 'Make criminal' looks like a phrase about corrupting a person, and it means outlawing an act instead.

4 down OVERSEE

Suspect severe cop exposed witness unintentionally (7)

Definition: witness unintentionally · anagram + middle letter

  • severe → SEVERE six of the seven letters, handed over to be rebuilt
  • cop exposed → O take the C and the P off the outside of cop and a single letter is left

The everyday sense of the answer is to supervise, and that is the reading to put aside: the setter wants the older sense, seeing by accident.

5 down STRATUS

Cloud begins lifting around you (7)

Definition: Cloud · reversal + container

  • begins → STARTS to begin is to start, and this is the word before it is turned
  • you → U U is the text-message spelling of you

The surface is a weather report, where cloud lifting is exactly what you would expect it to say, so neither instruction word looks like one.

6 down UNWHOLESOME

Disorderly house women left, lacking family values? (11)

Definition: lacking family values · anagram

  • house women → HOUSEWOMEN ten of the eleven letters, taken straight from the two words
  • left → L L is left, as stamped inside a pair of boots

'Disorderly house' is a set phrase, which is why it reads as one thing and works as two: the adjective is the instruction and the noun is material.

7 down DEPOSITOR

Duke vacated seat taken by needy customer at bank (9)

Definition: customer at bank · charade + container + outer letters

  • Duke → DE the walls of the word, its middle emptied out
  • needy → POOR the needy are the poor, and these four letters open up to take the next piece
  • seat → SIT to seat somebody is to sit them down

A duke's seat is his country house, which is the picture the surface is painting; the wordplay wants the plain verb instead.

8 down EARN

Make vase out of your mug? (4)

Definition: Make · homophone

  • vase → EARN the vase is an urn, most often the one that holds ashes, and it is the sound of that word you want

The surface has you throwing a pot out of your own drinking cup, and 'make' looks like part of that instruction rather than the definition.

9 down BRANDISH

Wave's concave shape beneath hulls (8)

Definition: Wave · charade

  • hulls → BRAN the hulls of grain are its husks, milled off as bran
  • concave shape → DISH a shallow curve is a dish, which is how the satellite aerial got its name

Waves and hulls make the surface nautical and neither word is doing nautical work: one is the definition, the other is breakfast cereal.

12 down RHODE ISLAND

Smallest union member wobbly on his ladder (5,6)

Definition: Smallest union member · anagram

  • on his ladder → ONHISLADDER eleven letters across three words, which is the whole of the material

The Union is the United States, so a union member is one of the fifty; the surface's decorator teetering on a ladder has nothing to do with it.

13 down FRIENDLY

Helpful diner ordered in hurry (8)

Definition: Helpful · anagram + container

  • hurry → FLY to fly is to hurry, and this piece opens up to take the other letters
  • diner → RIEND the five letters of diner in a new arrangement

The surface is a restaurant evening, and 'ordered in' reads as one phrase about takeaway food rather than as two separate instructions.

15 down SNOWDRIFT

Bank blown up in winter? (9)

Definition: Bank blown up in winter? · cryptic definition

  • Bank → the surface offers a high street branch, which makes 'blown up' an explosion; a bank is also any heaped-up mass of stuff
  • blown up in winter? → the wind does the heaping here, piling what has fallen against a wall or a hedge until it is deep enough to close a lane

There is nothing to take apart. The clue is one picture read twice, a robbery on the surface and a blocked country road underneath.

18 down PANTHER

Potentially big game that woman starts with deep breath (7)

Definition: Potentially big game · charade

  • deep breath → PANT a pant is the breath you take when you are out of puff
  • that woman → HER her is the pronoun standing in for the woman already mentioned

'Potentially big game' reads as a hopeful sports fixture, which is exactly the wrong picture: the phrase belongs to hunting, not to the fixture list.

19 down SPECIES

Type that is limited by small muscles (7)

Definition: Type · charade + container

  • small → S S is small, the letter on a clothing label
  • muscles → PECS pecs are the chest muscles, short for pectorals
  • that is → IE i.e. is the Latin id est, and it means that is

'Small muscles' reads as one anatomical description and is really two pieces clued separately, which is what hides the join.

21 down THREW

Cast connected by phone to the audience (5)

Definition: Cast · homophone

  • connected by phone → THREW a switchboard puts you through, and that word said aloud is written another way entirely

'Cast' is a past tense here, which is what tells you the answer is one too. The trap is reading it as the people in a play, which the audience encourages.

22 down SCAM

Unreal opportunity, those backing this magic formula claim (4)

Definition: Unreal opportunity · last letters

  • this magic formula claim → SCAM four words, each contributing its final letter, in the order they are written

The surface is a dodgy pitch and the people behind it, so 'those backing' reads as supporters rather than as a direction to look at the ends of words.

25 down SIR

Man tapped on the shoulder is turning back to customer (3)

Definition: Man tapped on the shoulder · reversal + last letter

  • is → IS the clue's own verb, taken at face value for its two letters
  • customer → R the last letter of the word, and nothing more of it

A sword laid on each shoulder is a knighthood, so the definition is the ceremony; the rest of the sentence is shop-floor politeness and pure surface.
