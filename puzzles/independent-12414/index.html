Across

1 across CAVE

Separate from icy mass, save large bears sleeping in here? (4)

Definition: bears sleeping in here? · deletion

  • Separate from icy mass → CALVE a glacier calves when a chunk breaks away from it and floats off
  • large → L L is the standard abbreviation for large, and it is the letter dropped here

Melting ice is the picture the surface sells. The question mark flags a definition by description rather than a synonym: it names where a hibernating bear spends the winter.

3 across SANDCASTLE

Strand building that might have been shelled? (10)

Definition: that might have been shelled? · charade

  • Strand → SAND a strand is a beach, and the swap to the stuff that covers it is the setter's own looseness
  • building → CASTLE a castle is one kind of building, a general word pointing at a particular one

Both halves of the wordplay hide inside a single noun phrase, and the question-marked half is the definition: shelled means decorated with shells and also bombarded, which is what happens to a real castle.

10 across RANGE

Variety published conclusion to damning exposé on the front (5)

Definition: Variety · charade + last letter + first letter

  • published → RAN a paper ran a story, which is how it published it
  • damning → G the closing letter of damning
  • exposé → E the opening letter of the word, accent and all

The first word is italicised so it reads as the showbiz trade paper running the story. The definition is the plain noun underneath the italics.

11 across IMPERIOUS

Queen stops reprobate assuming command (9)

Definition: assuming command · container

  • reprobate → IMPIOUS an impious person has no reverence for anything, which is the reprobate the surface wants
  • Queen → ER ER is Elizabeth Regina, the crossword's standing abbreviation for a queen

The surface has a monarch blocking a scoundrel's promotion, so 'stops' reads as halting someone. In cryptic use it is what a plug does in a hole.

12 across INSWINGER

Gets to dump women's footballer making you cross? (9)

Definition: cross? · deletion + charade

  • Gets → INS one word for gets is WINS, which turns up here a letter short
  • women → W W is the standard abbreviation for women, as on a lavatory door, and it is the letter thrown out
  • footballer → WINGER a winger plays wide on the flank, the player who delivers balls into the box

The surface is a manager dropping a player. 'Cross' is the football noun rather than the mood, and the possessive apostrophe is hiding a single letter of wordplay.

13 across ODEON

Cinema shortened Christmas party after reflection (5)

Definition: Cinema · deletion + charade + reversal

  • Christmas → NOE Christmas is NOEL, arriving here without its last letter
  • party → DO a do is a party or function in British usage

Odeon is the best-known cinema chain in Britain, and the word is also Greek for a small theatre — a name that is both a brand and a common noun.

14 across HERE GOES

Brave people hiding perhaps? I'm gonna do it! (4,4)

Definition: I'm gonna do it! · container

  • Brave people → HEROES brave people are heroes, and the word is prised open in the middle
  • perhaps? → EG eg is the Latin abbreviation for 'for example', the crossword's usual reading of perhaps

The trap is taking 'perhaps' as the setter hedging about the bravery. It is fodder, and everything after the question mark is the definition.

16 across STITCH

Mend circumstances broken by tension (6)

Definition: Mend · container

  • circumstances → SITCH sitch is informal shorthand for the situation, the circumstances you find yourself in
  • tension → T T is the physicist's symbol for the tension in a rope or a string

The surface reads as a plot summary, circumstances broken by tension. Both content words are doing other jobs, one slang and one out of a physics textbook.

18 across NICKEL

Nothing keeps American away from cake piece (6)

Definition: piece · container + deletion

  • Nothing → NIL nil is nothing in a scoreline, and it opens after its first two letters
  • American → A A is the standard abbreviation for American, and it is the letter taken out
  • cake → CKE the word CAKE with the letter above removed from it

The surface has someone guarding a cake. 'Piece' is the old word for a coin — a fifty-pence piece — and the coin here is the United States five-cent one.

19 across TUBIGRIP

Fall over ending in you getting large bandage (8)

Definition: bandage · container + last letter

  • Fall over → TRIP to trip is to fall over, and this word splits open after its first letter
  • you → U the last letter of the word you
  • large → BIG big is large, and it follows the single letter into the split

The answer looks like a brand name because it is one: the elasticated tube sold in British chemists to hold a sprained joint together.

22 across FREES

Broadcast decorated band releases (5)

Definition: releases · homophone

  • decorated band → FREES a frieze is the carved band running along the top of a classical wall

The band is architectural rather than musical, and the definition is a verb: 'releases' looks like a plural noun about records, which is what the surface is counting on.

23 across EXISTENCE

Being sixteen, suspect taking coke not OK (9)

Definition: Being · anagram + deletion

  • sixteen → EXISTEN the seven letters of SIXTEEN, shuffled into a new order
  • coke → CE COKE loses the two letters named right after it
  • OK → OK the pair of letters struck out of the fizzy drink above

'Being sixteen' reads as an age and the drug keeps that picture going, but both are letters. The definition is the noun 'being', not the participle.

25 across MACHINIMA

Animated film a small hotel group briefly backed (9)

Definition: Animated film · charade + deletion + reversal

  • a → A the article is taken down as it stands
  • small → MINI a mini is a small one of whatever is being talked about
  • hotel → H hotel is H in the phonetic alphabet
  • group → CAM a group of people with a shared cause is a CAMP, and it stops a letter short

'A small hotel group' reads as one phrase out of the travel trade and is four separate pieces, which is the whole trick. The answer is animation shot inside video-game engines.

26 across ORRIS

Fragrant root repellent when peeled slowly at first (5)

Definition: Fragrant root · deletion + charade + first letter

  • repellent → ORRI repellent means HORRID, and the outside letters are pared away
  • slowly → S the opening letter of slowly

The root is dried and ground from certain irises for its violet smell, and it turns up in perfume and in gin — one of those words worth learning once.

27 across DRESS SENSE

Designer's clothing succeeded in 8 countries, ultimately eastern style (5,5)

Definition: style · charade + outer letters + container + last letter

  • Designer's → DR the first and last letters of DESIGNER, with the possessive ending left out of it
  • 8 → ESSEN the German city solved at 8 down, written out in full and then opened up
  • succeeded → S S is the standard abbreviation for succeeded, as in a line of monarchs
  • countries → S the last letter of countries
  • eastern → E E is the compass abbreviation for eastern

A clue with a cross-reference is worth saving until its neighbour is filled, since five letters then arrive free. The surface is a fashion house's export figures.

28 across RELY

Bank very heartless (4)

Definition: Bank · deletion

  • very → RELY very is REALLY, and the pair of letters at its centre are gone

Two words of wordplay is about as short as clues get. The definition is the verb in 'bank on someone', with nothing financial about it.

Down

1 down CORNICHON

Pickle from the south-west reportedly concerning (9)

Definition: Pickle · homophone + charade

  • from the south-west → CORNICH Cornwall is the county at England's south-western tip, so something from there is Cornish, and the sound is spelled with a CH here
  • concerning → ON a book on birds is a book concerning them

The word is French, which is why the spelling refuses to follow the sound, and the surface reads as a grumble about a pickle rather than a recipe.

2 down VENUS

Goddess given usual offers (5)

Definition: Goddess · hidden word

  • given usual → VENUS the run starts inside the first word and finishes inside the second, with nothing skipped in between

Read as English it is a sentence about generosity, which is what makes the letters easy to walk past: they never move at all.

4 down ATINGLE

Beer drinking can lead to group feeling excited (7)

Definition: feeling excited · container + first letter

  • Beer → ALE ale is beer, and this is the word that opens up to take the rest inside
  • can → TIN a can is a tin, the thing a tin-opener opens
  • group → G the opening letter of the word group

The trap is reading 'can lead to' as one phrase about consequences. The can is a noun, and only the two words after it are the instruction.

5 down DEPART

Make tracks from record inspired by Rush (6)

Definition: Make tracks · container

  • Rush → DART to dart is to rush, and this word is prised open after its first letter
  • record → EP an EP is an extended-play record, longer than a single and shorter than an album

Rush is capitalised so it reads as the rock band, and 'tracks' and 'record' keep that picture going. All three words are doing something plainer.

6 down AIRPORT FICTION

Trip to Cairo in February's beginning wild holiday romance? (7,7)

Definition: holiday romance? · anagram + first letter

  • Trip to Cairo in → TRIPTOCAIROIN thirteen letters of fodder, taken exactly as they stand
  • February's → F the month's opening letter makes the fourteenth

The trap is reading 'holiday romance' as a fling. It is the genre of paperback you buy on the way to the gate, and the question mark covers the jump.

7 down TWOSEATER

Transport for pair (sea otter and wife) swimming together (9)

Definition: Transport for pair · anagram

  • sea otter → SEAOTTER eight of the nine letters to be shuffled
  • wife → W W is the standard abbreviation for wife, as on a family tree

The bracket makes the animal and the spouse look like an aside about a wildlife pond. They are the whole of the fodder, and 'pair' in the definition is a count of seats.

8 down ESSEN

Scottish beast I released turns up in German city (5)

Definition: German city · deletion + reversal

  • Scottish beast → NESSIE the monster said to live in Loch Ness is Nessie, Scotland's most famous animal
  • I → I the letter I is the one let go, leaving five to be turned round

'Scottish beast' wants to be a stag. The city is in the Ruhr, Germany's old coal and steel heartland, and it is short and vowel-heavy enough that setters come back to it.

9 down MEN IN GREY SUITS

Unrecognisable figures try Guinness I brewed with me (3,2,4,5)

Definition: Unrecognisable figures · anagram

  • try Guinness I → TRYGUINNESSI twelve letters of fodder, the stout included
  • me → ME the setter writes themselves in for the last two

In British political shorthand the men in grey suits are the anonymous party grandees who arrive quietly to tell a leader their time is up.

15 down RECHERCHE

Child protagonist hurt twice without a choice (9)

Definition: choice · charade + deletion

  • Child protagonist → RECHER Lee Child writes the Jack Reacher thrillers, and the hero's name turns up here minus a letter
  • hurt → CHE to hurt is to ACHE, and the same letter is taken off again

Child is a thriller writer's surname, not a young person, which is the whole joke. The answer is French, worn into English for anything studiedly rare.

17 down HAPLESSLY

Unfortunately hard copy admits large and small secret (9)

Definition: Unfortunately · charade + container

  • hard → H H is the grade stamped on a hard pencil
  • copy → APE to ape someone is to copy them, and this word opens up before its last letter
  • large → L L is the standard abbreviation for large, the size on a clothing label
  • small → S S is the standard abbreviation for small, the size below medium
  • secret → SLY sly behaviour is secret and underhand

Five pieces for nine letters, which is why the surface has to work so hard: 'hard copy' and 'large and small' both read as ordinary phrases and neither is one.

20 down URINALS

Drawing naked friends, topless men can be seen going here (7)

Definition: men can be seen going here · deletion + charade

  • Drawing → URIN drawing here means luring, attracting someone in, and the first and last letters come off
  • friends → ALS friends are PALS, and the letter at the top drops away

The surface is a life-drawing class and the definition is the last five words, where 'going' carries the polite sense rather than the travelling one.

21 down REVISE

Go over hill lapping Tesla? (6)

Definition: Go over · container

  • hill → RISE a rise is a hill, a swell in the ground, and it opens after its first letter
  • Tesla? → EV a Tesla is an EV, an electric vehicle, and the question mark flags one make standing for the whole class

A tesla is also the unit of magnetic flux, which is the reading the capital letter invites. Here it is the car, and the question mark is the tell.

22 down FUMED

50% of ACME parts cartoon hunter cut were about to explode? (5)

Definition: were about to explode? · container + deletion

  • cartoon hunter → FUD Elmer Fudd is the shotgun-toting hunter of the Bugs Bunny cartoons, and one D is cut away
  • ACME → ME the back half of the word, and Acme is the mail-order firm that supplies every gadget in those cartoons

Acme's gadgets are the ones that blow up in the Road Runner's pursuer's face, which is where the exploding in the definition comes from.

24 down NURSE

Cherish new wallet when penniless? (5)

Definition: Cherish · charade + deletion

  • new → N N is the standard abbreviation for new
  • wallet → URSE a wallet is a PURSE, which arrives here short of the coin letter

The joke is that emptying the wallet costs it exactly one penny, and the definition is a verb of tending rather than anything to do with hospitals.
