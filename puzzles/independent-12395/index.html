Across

1 across BACKBENCH

Non-ministerial support new church receives to remain unchanged (9)

Definition: Non-ministerial · charade + container

  • support → BACK to back a person or a plan is to support it
  • to remain unchanged → BE to carry on exactly as you are is simply to be
  • new → N n is the standard abbreviation for new
  • church → CH ch is the standard abbreviation for church

The surface is a row about church appointments, which is what makes 'support' look like part of the definition instead of the first piece of the build.

6 across TABLE

Opener for Tottenham with skilful counter (5)

Definition: counter · charade + first letter

  • Tottenham → T the first letter of Tottenham
  • skilful → ABLE able means skilful at something

Tottenham's opener sounds like the first goal of a match, and it is the first letter of the word. 'Counter' is furniture here, not something that counts.

9 across ENAMOUR

On reflection, regret being transfixed by old guy's charm (7)

Definition: charm · container + reversal

  • regret → RUE to rue something is to regret it, and this word is pierced before its last letter
  • old → O o is the standard abbreviation for old
  • guy → MAN a guy is a man

'On reflection' opens the clue as though someone is thinking better of an old regret, and it is not part of that picture at all: it is the instruction.

10 across DOUGLAS

Man has this grim, unfinished vessel docked (7)

Definition: Man has this · charade + deletion

  • grim → DOU DOUR means grim, and it stops before its final letter
  • vessel → GLAS a GLASS is a drinking vessel, with its tail cut off

'Man' is crossword shorthand for the Isle of Man, which is why a three-word definition can point at a town without naming a country.

11 across DRINK

Something to refresh primarily damp and chilly venue (5)

Definition: Something to refresh · charade + first letter

  • damp → D the first letter of damp
  • chilly venue → RINK a rink is the venue you skate on, and it is kept cold

'Primarily damp and chilly' reads as one weather report; only its first two words belong together, and the chill has been lent to the venue instead.

12 across HALL

Had to pay to vacate holy room (4)

Definition: room · deletion

  • holy → HALLOWED hallowed ground is holy ground
  • Had to pay → OWED anyone who had to pay owed the money, and those four letters are the ones that move out

The whole of 'Had to pay' is the thing removed, which is why it reads as ordinary past tense rather than as a piece of machinery.

13 across TSAR

27 small parts for top Russian ... (4)

Definition: top Russian · container

  • 27 → TAR the answer to 27 across is another word for tar, the black sticky stuff
  • small → S s is the standard abbreviation for small, as on a clothing label

The numeral is a cross-reference, not a quantity: solve 27 across first and most of this one arrives with it. The dots run the surface on into 15 across.

15 across NEITHER

... not one in three broadcast (7)

Definition: not one · anagram

  • in three → INTHREE anagram fodder, seven letters
  • broadcast → anagram indicator

Broadcasting suggests something you listen to, which is the trap: this is the sowing sense. The dots at the front carry the surface on from 13 across.

16 across SOCCER

Popular pastime taking regular nips of Scotch - cheers! (6)

Definition: Popular pastime · charade + alternate letters

  • Scotch → SOC the first, third and fifth letters of ScOtCh
  • cheers → CER the first, third and fifth letters of ChEeRs

Everything after the definition is a drinks order - nips of Scotch, and cheers - which is why nobody reads 'regular' as an instruction the first time through.

18 across NET

X is regressing, that's clear (3)

Definition: clear · reversal

  • X → TEN X is ten in Roman numerals

The X is a numeral, not the unknown of algebra, and 'clear' is the accountant's word: what you clear after deductions is what you net.

20 across TINTIN

Comic character flipping two eggs (6)

Definition: Comic character · reversal

  • two eggs → NITNIT a nit is the egg a louse lays, and two of them stand side by side

Nit for a louse's egg is worth keeping, since setters use it constantly, and the doubling is what turns three letters into a Belgian reporter's name.

21 across RICOTTA

Cereal snubbed - too much American dairy product (7)

Definition: dairy product · charade + deletion

  • Cereal → RIC RICE is the cereal, cut short
  • too much → OTT OTT is over the top, British shorthand for excessive
  • American → A a is the standard abbreviation for American

'Cereal snubbed' sounds like a complaint about breakfast and 'too much American' like one about television; the definition is the last two words and nothing before them.

24 across LOOP

Ring John and Paul to begin with (4)

Definition: Ring · charade + first letter

  • John → LOO john and loo are both informal words for the lavatory, one American, one British
  • Paul → P the first letter of Paul

John and Paul put two Beatles in the surface, and only one of them stays a name; the other is a euphemism for the smallest room in the house.

26 across FORD

Crossing Doubtful Sound, maybe, Juliet's blown away (4)

Definition: Crossing · deletion

  • Doubtful Sound, maybe → FJORD Doubtful Sound is one of New Zealand's fjords, a long steep-sided sea inlet
  • Juliet → J Juliet is J in the radio alphabet

'Sound' looks like an instruction to listen and is a piece of geography instead: Doubtful Sound is a fjord in all but name, which the 'maybe' concedes.

27 across PITCH

Delap's last long throw (5)

Definition: throw · charade + last letter

  • Delap's → P Rory Delap was the footballer famous for enormous throw-ins, and his name ends in P
  • long → ITCH to long for something is to itch for it

A long throw is precisely what Delap was known for, so the surface is a football report and the definition sits in plain sight as its final word.

29 across ASEXUAL

Not interested in IT? (7)

Definition: Not interested in IT? · cryptic definition

  • IT → the capitals push you towards information technology, and someone with no time for computers
  • Not interested in IT? → in lower case 'it' has been a coy word for sex since the 1920s, when Clara Bow was the It girl, and that is the sense meant

There is no wordplay to take apart here, only two letters wearing capitals they have not earned. Everything rests on which sense of 'it' you read.

30 across SERVICE

Give assistance to gentleman reportedly failing (7)

Definition: Give assistance to · charade + homophone

  • gentleman → SER a gentleman is addressed as sir, and said aloud that word can be written this other way
  • failing → VICE a vice is a moral failing

A compliment and an insult stitched together, which is why the clue reads as gossip about a man rather than as a set of instructions.

31 across SMASH

Mum's mum admits defeat (5)

Definition: defeat · container

  • Mum's → SSH keeping mum is keeping quiet, and the noise for that is ssh
  • mum → MA ma is the informal word for a mother

'Mum' does two jobs a word apart, the silence and the mother, and taking both senses out of one small word is the whole trick of the clue.

32 across REPAYMENT

Means for getting even near empty works (9)

Definition: Means for getting even · anagram

  • near empty → NEAREMPTY anagram fodder, nine letters
  • works → anagram indicator

'Near empty works' paints a derelict factory, and the phrase is the fodder rather than the picture; counting its letters against the enumeration gives it away.

1 down BLEND IN

Provide receptacle for wine bottles to match the background (5,2)

Definition: to match the background · container

  • receptacle for wine → BIN a bin is where a cellar keeps its wine, and it opens up after its first letter
  • Provide → LEND to lend is to provide something on loan

'Provide receptacle for wine' reads as one instruction to fetch a glass. It is two separate synonyms, and the word between them is a verb telling you how they fit together.

2 down COALITION

Boxer embraced by Coot in unconventional union (9)

Definition: union · anagram + container

  • Coot in → COTION the same six letters re-set, opening up in the middle
  • Boxer → ALI Ali is the crossword's boxer, Muhammad Ali

Setters slip their own name into the grid: Coot signed this puzzle at the top and then handed his six letters over as fodder.

3 down BLOCKCHAIN

Digital records system - secure measure supporting bursar principally (10)

Definition: Digital records system · charade + first letter

  • bursar principally → B the first letter of bursar
  • secure → LOCK to lock something is to make it secure
  • measure → CHAIN a chain is an old unit of length, 22 yards, still the distance between cricket stumps

The surface is a school office - secure measures and a bursar - and nothing in it hints at cryptocurrency, which is where the definition has quietly landed.

4 down NORTHERN

Hornet flying with force from Inverness? (8)

Definition: from Inverness? · anagram + charade

  • Hornet → NORTHE the insect's six letters, resettled
  • force → RN RN is the Royal Navy, one of Britain's armed forces

A hornet flying with force is a vivid picture and a complete red herring. The only Scottish word in the clue is the city, and that is the definition.

5 down HUDDLE

Crowd disorder in which Henry's knocked out Mike (6)

Definition: Crowd · substitution

  • disorder → MUDDLE a muddle is a state of disorder
  • Mike → M Mike is M in the radio alphabet, and it is the letter that leaves
  • Henry's → H the henry, the unit of inductance, is written H, and it moves into the vacated place

Both names are letters rather than people: the radio alphabet and the physicist's unit are two conventions worth keeping, and a swap like this leaves the length unchanged.

6 down TAUT

Tense auditor's shown how (4)

Definition: Tense · homophone

  • shown how → TAUT someone shown how to do a thing has been taught, and said aloud that word is written a shorter way

Audit and audio come from the same Latin root for hearing, which is why an auditor is a sound signal in a crossword rather than an accountant checking books.

7 down BALLS

Playing Quidditch one needs such courage (5)

Definition: Playing Quidditch one needs · double definition

  • Playing Quidditch one needs → the wizards' game in the Harry Potter books is played with four of them, from the snitch to the bludgers
  • courage → the second sense is slang, the nerve to go through with something

The two halves join so smoothly that the clue reads as one sentence about bravery on a broomstick, and the join is exactly where the definition changes hands.

8 down ENSURER

Old queen's engaged trained nurse, one providing guarantees (7)

Definition: one providing guarantees · container + anagram

  • Old queen's → ER ER is Elizabeth Regina, the late Queen, and the pair comes apart
  • nurse → NSURE the same five letters in a new order

'Old queen' is ER on almost every crossword page, short for Elizabeth Regina, and the surface buries that royal pair inside a hospital scene.

14 down COACH PARTY

Travelling crowd celebrate on top of train? Quite the opposite (5,5)

Definition: Travelling crowd · charade

  • train → COACH to train someone is to coach them
  • celebrate → PARTY to party is to celebrate

'Train' looks like the vehicle a carriage belongs to and is a verb instead, and the last three words are the setter cheerfully undoing the order he has just given.

17 down CUT IT FINE

Allow little margin for error if using wordplay? (3,2,4)

Definition: Allow little margin for error · cryptic definition

  • Allow little margin for error → the plain reading and the definition: leaving yourself barely enough time or room to manage
  • if using wordplay? → the answer read backwards as instructions spells the little word 'if' - one letter from a two-letter word trimmed, one from an abbreviation

This is a reverse clue: instead of wordplay building the answer, the answer is wordplay. Cut IT and you have I; F is the pencil grade meaning fine.

19 down TRADES UP

Rudest PA sacked, invests in better (6,2)

Definition: invests in better · anagram

  • Rudest PA → RUDESTPA anagram fodder, eight letters
  • sacked → anagram indicator

A sacked PA is a complete office scene, and 'rudest' goes on working as an adjective right up until you count its letters against the enumeration.

20 down TELE-ADS

Marketing via mobiles possibly exhausted the Guides (4-3)

  • the → TE THE emptied of its middle letter leaves the two on the outside
  • Guides → LEADS whoever guides a group leads it

Exhausted Guides read as a hard day's hiking, and the capital G is surface dressing - the definition has already finished by then.

22 down ACHIEST

Area over unfinished main road suffering most (7)

Definition: suffering most · charade + deletion

  • Area → A a is the standard abbreviation for area
  • main → CHIE the main thing is the CHIEF one, without its final letter
  • road → ST st is street, the everyday road abbreviation

'Unfinished main road' is one phrase in the surface and two separate pieces in the wordplay, and the join between them is where the clue does its hiding.

23 down BOWLER

Person who delivers headgear (6)

Definition: Person who delivers · double definition

  • Person who delivers → in cricket, delivering is what you do with the ball at the batter, six times an over
  • headgear → the second sense is the stiff round black hat of the old City gent

One noun and two trades, run together so smoothly that the clue reads as a delivery van. The hat is named after Thomas and William Bowler, who made the first one.

25 down OPERA

Did surgery Edward wanted work in theatre? (5)

Definition: work in theatre? · deletion

  • Did surgery → OPERATED a surgeon who did surgery operated
  • Edward → TED Ted is short for Edward, and those three letters are the ones found missing

The joke is the operating theatre: surgery and the stage share the word, and the question mark is the setter admitting he is leaning on it.

28 down PUSH

Promote the sale of local American jams (4)

  • American → US US is the United States, the standard shorthand for American

'Local American jams' reads as a stall of homemade preserves, which is why the definition standing at the front of the clue is so easy to walk past.
