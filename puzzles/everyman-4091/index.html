Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,091

Across

1 across REAR WINDOW

Bring up opportunity to see Hitchcock movie (4,6)

Definition: Hitchcock movie · charade

REAR WINDOW is the title of a Hitchcock picture, so the definition names the answer by category — one instance of his films.

  • Bring up → REAR to rear a child is to bring it up
  • opportunity → WINDOW a window is a limited stretch of time in which something can be done

Someone raises the chance of an evening in front of an old thriller.

'Bring up' works twice over: it sounds like raising a subject and means raising a child. The film is Hitchcock's 1954 thriller, shot entirely from inside one apartment.

6 across CHEF

Revolutionary, loud apron-wearer (4)

Definition: apron-wearer · charade

A CHEF runs the professional kitchen, and the apron is that trade's uniform, so the definition names the answer by what it wears.

  • Revolutionary → CHE Che Guevara is the crossword's standing revolutionary
  • loud → F f is forte, the musical direction for playing loud

A loud-mouthed radical who happens to be wearing an apron.

Both halves are conventions worth banking: CHE is who a crossword means by a revolutionary, and F is the musician's forte, which turns up in dozens of clues a year.

9 across EXFOLIATES

Fixes ale to order and gets out of one’s skin (10)

Definition: gets out of one’s skin · anagram

To EXFOLIATE is to take the dead surface layer off, so the answer really does get something out of one's skin.

  • Fixes ale to → FIXESALETO ten letters of fodder, exactly the length being asked for
  • order — putting something in order means moving things into new positions, and the letters take up new positions here

A barman sorts the beer out to order while somebody leaps out of their skin.

'Gets out of one's skin' borrows the idiom about jumping out of it in fright, and means the beauty-counter thing instead: scrubbing the dead outer layer away.

10 across BEAU

Fancy fellow’s drop of bottled French water (4)

Definition: Fancy fellow · charade + first letter

A BEAU is a woman's admirer or a showy dresser, so 'fancy fellow' catches both the courting sense and the peacock one.

  • drop of bottled → B the first letter of bottled
  • French water → EAU eau is French for water, as on a bottle of eau minérale
  • drop of — a drop is the least amount of liquid you can take, so the word behind it parts with a single letter, its first

A dandy with a little bottled French mineral water.

The surface is all drinks trolley, and 'drop' is the word doing the damage — a measure of liquid on one reading, an instruction about letters on the other.

12 across CONCERT GRAND

Making racket – foregone conclusion – excellent: a huge piano! (7,5)

Definition: a huge piano · charade

A CONCERT GRAND is the largest grand piano made, the nine-foot instrument used on stage, so 'a huge piano' is plain description.

  • racket → CON a racket is a swindle run as though it were a business, and a swindle is a con
  • foregone conclusion → CERT a cert is British shorthand for a certainty — a dead cert
  • excellent → GRAND grand means splendid as well as large
  • Making → surface only — it holds up the reading about noise and hands over no letters

A string of exclamations: the noise, the inevitable outcome, and a magnificent instrument.

'Racket' is the pivot. The surface wants the din a big piano makes; the wordplay wants the criminal sense, a protection racket. 'Cert' is worth learning in its own right — British for a sure thing.

15 across CHUFFED

Acted like a steam train travelling over the moon (7)

Definition: Acted like a steam train travelling · double definition

CHUFFED is the past tense of chuff, the sound a steam engine makes, and it is also British slang for delighted, which is what 'over the moon' means.

  • Acted like a steam train travelling → a locomotive puffing along makes the noise its own verb is named after, and the clue wants that verb in the past tense
  • over the moon → the British idiom for delighted, the phrase footballers and lottery winners reach for

A steam engine puffing along, thoroughly pleased with itself.

Both halves define, so the sentence asserts nothing at all — and 'travelling over the moon' reads as one phrase, as though the engine were sailing past the sky.

16 across HAIRCUT

Hot air ignored? Do! (7)

Definition: Do · charade

A do is informal British for a hairdo — the styling itself — which is what a HAIRCUT gives you.

  • Hot → H h is the letter on a hot tap
  • air → AIR the word is taken at face value, all three letters of it
  • ignored → CUT to cut somebody is to snub them, to look straight past them in the street

Empty boasting is brushed aside, with an order to get on with it.

The definition is two letters long, which is what makes this hard: a do is a hairdo, and everything before it reads as a complete sentence about being snubbed.

17 across UNDEALT

Not fine, flaunted, displaying cards in deck like so (7)

Definition: cards in deck like so · anagram + deletion

UNDEALT means not yet handed out, which is the state of every card sitting in a deck nobody has touched.

  • Not fine → f is the pencil-grade abbreviation for fine, and 'Not' says leave that letter out
  • flaunted → LAUNTED the seven letters of flaunted once that f has gone
  • displaying — 'displaying' is a loose shuffle instruction — laying something out spreads it into a fresh arrangement — and the vagueness there is the setter's, not a gap in your solving

Something second-rate is being shown off, cards and all.

The definition is the whole tail of the clue and it points at a condition rather than naming it: cards still in the pack are exactly like so. F for fine comes off the side of a pencil.

19 across CHICAGO

Musical fashionable in the past (7)

Definition: Musical · charade

CHICAGO is the Kander and Ebb stage musical, so the definition points at the answer through its genre — one instance of the form.

  • fashionable → CHIC chic is stylish, borrowed straight from the French
  • in the past → AGO something in the past happened a while ago

A show that used to be in vogue and no longer is.

One word carries the entire definition, and it defines by example — CHICAGO is a particular show rather than the category. The city it is named for never gets a mention.

20 across MARIACHI BAND

Musicians from China barmaid enlivened (8,4)

Definition: Musicians · anagram

A MARIACHI BAND is the Mexican street ensemble of trumpets, violins and guitars, so 'musicians' is the plain category word.

  • China barmaid → CHINABARMAID twelve letters of fodder, matching the enumeration exactly
  • enlivened — to enliven something is to stir it up, and letters stirred up do not stay in the order they were handed to you

A barmaid from China brightens the whole pub up.

'From China' reads as a country of origin, and the players are Mexican — the words are in the clue only for their letters, which is what a long anagram counts on.

23 across EVER

Your crossword compiler is only half there – always (4)

Definition: always · deletion

EVER means at all times, so 'always' is a straight synonym — the sense in 'ever faithful', not the questioning 'did you ever?'

  • Your crossword compiler → EVERYMAN the setter of this Observer puzzle signs himself with one eight-letter name
  • only half there — 'only half there' says the name arrives at half length, so four of its eight letters are left behind

The compiler confesses to being only half present, as he always is.

Everyman writing his own name into a clue is a habit of this puzzle worth knowing, and 'only half there' is the joke twice: the letters are halved and the man is distracted.

24 across TRACK EVENT

Sporting fixture – racket bust – let rip (5,5)

Definition: Sporting fixture · anagram + charade

A TRACK EVENT is a running race on the track, as opposed to a field event, which makes it one kind of sporting fixture.

  • racket bust → TRACKE the six letters of racket, reshuffled
  • let rip → VENT to vent is to let rip, to let feeling out in a rush
  • bust — something bust has been broken up, and letters broken up have to be put back together in some other order

A tennis match where the racket breaks and tempers go with it.

'Racket' does the misleading: the surface makes it a piece of tennis equipment while the wordplay only wants its six letters. The rest of the answer is a synonym joined straight on the end.

25 across TIDE

Sea’s movement even, you say? (4)

Definition: Sea’s movement · homophone

The TIDE is the sea's twice-daily rise and fall, so 'sea's movement' names the answer by what it does.

  • even → TIDE a match that finishes even is tied, and that word said out loud is what you write in the grid
  • you say — 'you say' hands the clue to your ear: what counts is the noise the word makes, not the letters it is written with

A remark that the sea's movement is level, pending confirmation.

'Even' is a word for level water as well as for a drawn match, so the surface talks about a calm sea while the mechanism wants the scoreboard sense. The two spellings share their letters and sound identical.

26 across UNDERSTUDY

Stand-in actor to read European articles first (10)

Definition: Stand-in actor · charade

An UNDERSTUDY learns a part in order to go on when the lead cannot, which is exactly a stand-in actor.

  • European articles → UNDER the French indefinite article un and the German definite article der, laid end to end
  • read → STUDY to read a subject at university is to study it
  • first — 'first' sends the piece it follows to the front of the answer instead of leaving it where the clue puts it

An actor is told to get through the European papers before anything else.

'Read' is the British university sense — you read history at Durham — and 'first' is what reorders the halves, since the articles are named after the verb and belong in front of it.

Down

1 down REED

Does and bucks rampant in grass (4)

Definition: grass · reversal

A REED is a tall grass growing in marshes and riverbanks, so 'grass' is the plain category word for it.

  • Does and bucks → DEER does are the females and bucks the males of one and the same animal
  • rampant — in heraldry a beast rampant is rearing up on its hind legs, and anything rearing up in a down clue is read from the bottom of the light towards the top

Deer of both sexes charging about in long grass.

'Rampant' reads as wild behaviour and is really the heraldic pose, an animal up on its hind legs — which in a down light is an instruction to climb. A reed is a tall grass of wet ground.

2 down ALFA

One (or 17) before Romeo (4)

Definition: One (or 17) before Romeo · cryptic definition

In the NATO alphabet ALFA is the codeword for A: the first entry, and seventeen places before Romeo, the codeword for R.

  • One (or 17) → reads as a number or as a cross-reference to another entry in the grid; it is really a position in a list — first place, and also seventeen places back from R
  • before Romeo → Romeo is the NATO radio codeword for R, which is what fixes the list being counted along; the same two words also sit side by side on an Italian car badge

A note pointing at other numbers in the puzzle and at somebody called Romeo.

Romeo is the giveaway: it is a NATO radio codeword, so the counting runs along that alphabet, where A stands first and R stands eighteenth — one place along, or seventeen places back. ALFA also fronts a famous car marque.

3 down WALDORF SALAD

Walnut? Not half! And a little Discovery or Fuji’s cored, as prepared in diners, primarily (7,5)

Definition: Walnut? Not half! And a little Discovery or Fuji’s cored, as prepared in diners, primarily · &lit + charade + deletion + outer letters + first letters

Read straight through, the clue describes the dish itself: walnuts, cored apple, a menu staple — so the whole sentence is the definition.

  • Walnut? Not half! → WAL half of those six letters, taken from the front
  • a little Discovery → D Discovery is an English apple variety, and the first letter is all that is wanted
  • or → OR the joining word is taken at face value, both letters of it
  • Fuji’s cored → FS Fuji is another apple; cored, the word keeps only its outer letters
  • as prepared in → ALA à la is the menu phrase for 'in the style of' — how a kitchen says as prepared in
  • diners, primarily → D the first letter of diners
  • Not half — 'Not half' refuses to hand the whole word over, so only half of it turns up — and it is the front half
  • a little — 'a little' asks for the smallest possible piece of the word behind it, which is the single letter it opens with
  • cored — coring an apple takes the middle out and leaves the outside, so the word keeps only its outer letters
  • primarily — 'primarily' means first of all, so the word it follows gives up nothing but its initial

A cook running through the dish: walnuts in quantity, an apple cored, done the way diners do it.

Every word pays twice — the sentence lists the dish's real ingredients while spelling it out a letter or two at a time. Discovery and Fuji are apple varieties, and 'not half' is British for 'very much so'.

4 down NUANCED

Greek character danced topless with subtlety (7)

Definition: with subtlety · charade + deletion

NUANCED describes something full of fine shades and small distinctions, which is what doing a thing with subtlety produces.

  • Greek character → NU nu is the thirteenth letter of the Greek alphabet
  • danced topless → ANCED danced without the letter at its top
  • topless — 'topless' takes the top off, and the top of a word in a down clue is the letter it starts with, so that letter goes

A Greek dances bare-chested, and does it delicately.

'Greek character' looks like a person from Athens and asks for a letter of that alphabet instead — nu, mu, eta, phi and the rest are worth memorising, because setters return to them constantly.

5 down ON EARTH

A little yobbo near there, where you live (2,5)

Definition: where you live · hidden word

'Where you live' is the planet, and ON EARTH is the phrase for being on it — the same words that sharpen a question like 'what on earth?'

  • yobbo near there → ONEARTH seven letters running straight on from the end of the first word, through the whole of the second, into the start of the third
  • A little — 'A little' asks for part of what follows rather than all of it, which is how a run of letters buried in the words gets flagged

A minor thug hanging around near the place you call home.

The letters are sitting in the open a few words before the definition, and long hidden phrases hide best exactly here — inside an ordinary noun phrase, where you read the words instead of the letters.

7 down HIERARCHAL

Around end of dinner, hail her cab, mostly arranged in ranks (10)

Definition: in ranks · anagram + deletion + last letter + container

Something HIERARCHAL is ordered in tiers, each above the next, so 'in ranks' is the adjectival phrase it stands in for.

  • hail her cab, mostly → HIEARCHAL hail her ca — cab minus its final letter — is nine letters looking for a new order
  • end of dinner → R the last letter of dinner
  • Around — 'Around' puts one piece on both sides of another, so the assembled letters close over the single letter picked out first
  • end of — 'end of' points at the final letter of the word behind it and wants nothing else from that word
  • mostly — 'mostly' means nearly all of it, so the word arrives with its final letter left behind
  • arranged — arranging things means setting them out in an order somebody chose, and the order chosen here is not the one the clue gave

As dinner winds up, a taxi is flagged down for a departing guest.

'Arranged in ranks' reads as a single phrase and only its last two words define — 'arranged' is off doing the shuffling. The surface keeps that quiet by talking about seeing a guest into a cab.

8 down FOUNDATION

Undergarment that goes on before blusher (10)

Definition: Undergarment · double definition

A foundation garment is shapewear worn underneath, and foundation is also the make-up base applied before blusher: one word, two dressing-table senses.

  • Undergarment → the drapers' sense: the shaping garment worn beneath everything else, a corset or a girdle
  • that goes on before blusher → the cosmetics sense: the base coat spread on the skin before any colour goes over it

Getting dressed and made up, in the right order.

'Undergarment' is placed first to keep you in the wardrobe, so the make-up sense arrives late — and the two meanings of FOUNDATION never touch except in a phrase about getting ready.

11 down IGNITION KEYS

In turn, they’ll get you going (8,4)

Definition: In turn, they’ll get you going · cryptic definition

IGNITION KEYS are what you turn to start an engine, so 'in turn' and 'get you going' both read literally once the human sense is dropped.

  • In turn → reads as one after another, taking it in turns; what is meant is the turning itself, the twist of a wrist in a steering column
  • they’ll get you going → reads as encouragement from somebody keeping your spirits up; what is meant is starting a vehicle moving

Colleagues taking it in turns to jolly you along and get you motivated.

There is no separable wordplay to find: the whole clue is one definition with two readings. The plural is the hint, because a person who gets you going comes one at a time and IGNITION KEYS jangle in a pocket.

13 down SCRUMMIEST

Seconds, cheap in the extreme and most delicious (10)

Definition: most delicious · charade

SCRUMMIEST is the superlative of scrummy, so it means most delicious — the informal register, not the dictionary one.

  • Seconds → S s is the abbreviation for seconds on a stopwatch
  • cheap in the extreme → CRUMMIEST crummy means cheap and shoddy, and in the extreme pushes it to its superlative

Cut-price factory rejects turn out to taste wonderful.

'Seconds' reads as the flawed goods on a market stall and gives up the stopwatch abbreviation instead. Scrummy is British nursery slang for delicious, a child's squashing of scrumptious.

14 down DUNDERHEAD

Department’s leader reporting to top teacher, an idiot (10)

Definition: an idiot · charade + first letter

A DUNDERHEAD is a blockhead, an old word for someone slow-witted, so 'an idiot' is a straight singular synonym.

  • Department’s leader → D the first letter of department
  • reporting to → UNDER somebody who reports to you sits under you on the staff chart
  • top teacher → HEAD the head is what a British school calls its principal
  • leader — a leader is the one out in front, so the word hands over the letter standing at its front

A staffroom note: the department chief answers to the head, and someone is a fool.

The whole clue is built out of a school flow chart, which is what stops 'an idiot' at the end from looking like a definition — it reads as one more remark about the man.

18 down TEHERAN

Some wrote her a note somewhere in Iran (7)

Definition: somewhere in Iran · hidden word

TEHERAN is Iran's capital, so 'somewhere in Iran' defines it by category: a place in the country rather than the country itself.

  • wrote her a note → TEHERAN the letters run consecutively from the middle of the first word through to the start of the last
  • Some — 'Some' asks for a part of what follows and not the whole of it, which is the signal that a run of letters is sitting inside those words

Somebody left a short written message behind.

The spelling with the extra E is the older transliteration, and it is what makes this work — the modern Tehran would not fit the letters these words offer. 'Some' is the only hint that anything is buried.

19 down CUBICLE

Announced boxy workspace (7)

Definition: workspace · homophone

A CUBICLE is a partitioned-off desk space in an open-plan office, so 'workspace' is the plain noun for it.

  • boxy → CUBICLE a boxy thing is cubical, shaped like a cube — and said out loud that word is indistinguishable from the office one
  • Announced — 'Announced' means said out loud, so the clue is describing how the word sounds rather than how it is spelt

Somebody reads out a description of a square little office.

Two words, one sound, different spellings: CUBICAL with an A is the cube-shaped adjective, CUBICLE with an E the partitioned desk. Only your ear connects them.

21 down PERU

Foremost of peaks enveloping River Ucayali – here! (4)

Definition: here · first letters

The Ucayali rises and runs in PERU, so 'here' points at the country the clue has just been describing.

  • peaks → P the first letter of peaks
  • enveloping → E the first letter of enveloping — the word looks like an instruction to tuck something inside, and is not one
  • River → R the first letter of River, which is not the same as the usual r standing for river
  • Ucayali → U the first letter of the river's name
  • Foremost of — 'Foremost of' asks for whatever stands at the front of each word that follows, which is the letter it begins with

Mountains closing in around a South American river.

Two of the four words are booby traps: 'enveloping' looks like a container instruction and 'River' like the standard R, and both are only donating initials. The Ucayali is a Peruvian headwater of the Amazon.

22 down STAY

Remain conscious throughout Australia Day, finally (4)

Definition: Remain · last letters

To STAY somewhere is to remain there — the same verb in the same form, the everyday synonym.

  • conscious → S the last letter of conscious
  • throughout → T the last letter of throughout
  • Australia → A the last letter of Australia
  • Day → Y the last letter of Day
  • finally — 'finally' asks for the letter each word finishes on, taken in the order the words arrive

Keeping awake right through the national holiday.

'Remain conscious' reads as one phrase and only the first word defines; everything after it is a queue of words giving up their final letters. Australia Day is the country's national holiday, 26 January.
