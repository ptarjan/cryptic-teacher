Observer Everyman Crossword No 4,153

Across

1 across TAPHOUSE

Dance music in tavern (8)

Definition: tavern · charade

  • Dance → TAP tap is the dance done in metal-tipped shoes
  • music → HOUSE house is the four-to-the-floor club genre, and the word sits in front of 'music' just as naturally

The trap is reading 'Dance music' as one thing. Each of those two words is really half of a different compound, and the drinking is all in the definition.

5 across BRACES

Supports couples (6)

Definition: Supports · double definition

  • Supports → the verb: to prop up, or to steady yourself against a shock you can see coming
  • couples → the noun: a brace is a pair, two birds shot or two of anything, so the plural is pairs

Two words, two definitions and nothing in between. Read as a sentence it sounds like relationship counselling, and that is all the misdirection a clue this short has room for.

9 across SCOFF

Gobble up mockery (5)

Definition: Gobble up · double definition

  • Gobble up → the eating sense: to put food away fast and greedily
  • mockery → the derision sense, a jeer thrown at somebody

Two unrelated senses of one small word in a clue only three words long. 'Up' belongs to the first definition and not to the second, which is the whole of the difficulty.

10 across INSOLENCE

Son succeeding daughter in laziness and rudeness (9)

Definition: rudeness · substitution

  • laziness → INDOLENCE indolence is settled laziness, the habit of never stirring
  • daughter → D the standard abbreviation, and the letter that vacates the word
  • Son → S the standard abbreviation, moving into the place just vacated

Two nine-letter nouns a single letter apart is the whole joke. The surface reads as a family inheritance, and 'in laziness' looks like an insertion instruction rather than the word being altered.

12 across MAKE UP YOUR MIND

Decide to put cosmetics on solver: skin contains mark (4,2,4,4)

Definition: Decide · charade + container

  • put cosmetics on → MAKEUP cosmetics are the noun and applying them is the verb, spelt the same either way
  • solver → YOU the setter addressing whoever is holding the paper
  • skin → RIND a rind is the skin of a fruit or a cheese
  • mark → M the German currency abbreviated, one letter to drop in

'Skin contains mark' reads as an instruction to hunt for buried letters and is an insertion instead. 'Solver' meaning you is a device Everyman uses most weeks.

14 across ELECTORAL

Concerning political process, the Spanish muscle wanting bit of protection (9)

Definition: Concerning political process · charade + deletion + first letter

  • the Spanish → EL el is Spanish for 'the', the masculine form of the definite article
  • muscle → PECTORAL the pectoral is the chest muscle, the pec of the gym
  • protection → P the first letter of the word, the bit taken off it

'The Spanish' is the standard crossword way of asking for EL, the definite article of that language rather than anything about Spain; French and Italian articles are clued the same way.

15 across BLEAK

Primarily baleful, like Eeyore and knelling? (5)

Definition: Primarily baleful, like Eeyore and knelling? · &lit + first letters

  • baleful → B first letter of a word meaning menacing or full of gloom
  • like → L first letter, and the little comparison word is material like the rest
  • Eeyore → E first letter of the permanently downcast donkey in Winnie-the-Pooh
  • and → A first letter, taken from a conjunction that looks like pure grammar
  • knelling → K first letter of the slow tolling a bell does at a funeral

Every word after the instruction hands over its opening letter, and the sentence they spell is itself a fair description of the answer. The clue defines itself, which is what the question mark is signalling.

16 across INDIE

Cockney's language is not mainstream (5)

Definition: not mainstream · homophone

  • language → INDIE the language is Hindi, and in that accent it loses the aitch it starts with

The aitch is the whole clue. Hindi written down gets you nowhere; Hindi as a Cockney says it does, once you let the spelling change at the other end too.

18 across STEADIEST

'Extremely stable' Stateside freak (9)

Definition: Extremely stable · anagram

  • Stateside → STATESIDE nine letters meaning 'over in America', handed over whole

The quotation marks flag a famous American boast and carry no instruction at all, while 'Stateside' keeps that surface running and is quietly the only material in the clue.

20 across COME FROM BEHIND

Oh! Find Morecambe - without a struggle - unexpectedly win (4,4,6)

Definition: unexpectedly win · anagram + deletion

  • Oh! → OH the exclamation gives up both of its letters
  • Find → FIND four more letters, taken as they stand
  • Morecambe → MORECAMBE a seaside resort on the Lancashire coast, and the stage surname of the comedian Eric
  • a → A the single letter that 'without' takes straight back out again

Fifteen letters go in and fourteen come out. 'Without a struggle' reads as one phrase about an easy victory, and the setter has hidden both a deletion and the shuffling instruction inside it.

23 across VENEZUELA

Singing gondolier heard somewhere in S America (9)

Definition: somewhere in S America · homophone

  • Singing gondolier → VENEZUELA a gondolier poles his boat along the canals of Venice, and one who sings is a wailer: say the two words quickly together

The country's name means 'little Venice', which is why the joke lands so neatly: the explorers who named it thought the stilt houses out on the water looked Venetian.

24 across SABRE

Weapon hidden in flipping wastepaper basket (5)

Definition: Weapon · hidden word + reversal

  • wastepaper basket → SABRE five letters in a row straddle the space between the two words, and they run right to left

Everyman tells you outright that something is buried, which is generous. 'Flipping' is the word that costs you, because it reads as mild annoyance about the bin.

25 across RESIDE

Abide the dregs not having uniform (6)

Definition: Abide · deletion

  • the dregs → RESIDUE what settles at the bottom and is left over when the rest has gone
  • uniform → U uniform is U in the phonetic alphabet used over the radio

'Uniform' is the radio alphabet's U, from the same list that gives Romeo, Tango and Whisky. 'The dregs' is a noun here, not a complaint about the company.

26 across MENSWEAR

Everyman to promise to put away, for a start, nasty ties etc (8)

Definition: ties etc · charade + container + first letter

  • Everyman → ME the setter signing himself, as he does most weeks
  • nasty → N the first letter of the word and nothing else
  • promise → SWEAR to swear is to promise on oath

'Nasty ties etc' sounds like a verdict on somebody's wardrobe, but only 'nasty' is wordplay and the rest is the definition. Everyman means himself whenever his own name turns up.

Down

1 down TOSH

At heart, Photoshop's rubbish (4)

Definition: rubbish · middle letters

  • Photoshop's → TOSH ten characters counting the s, so three off each end leave four standing in the centre

The surface is a grumble about the software. That apostrophe-s reads as 'is' and is also counted as a letter, which is what puts the centre of the word where it needs to be.

2 down PROPANE

In favour of flat having no trace of laughing gas (7)

Definition: gas · charade + deletion + first letter

  • In favour of → PRO pro- is the prefix of support, as in pro-democracy
  • flat → PLANE a plane is a flat surface, the geometer's word for one
  • laughing → L the first letter of the word, and the one that goes

The surface offers you laughing gas, which is nitrous oxide and not the answer. 'Gas' is the definition standing on its own, and 'laughing' is there only to be reduced to a single letter.

3 down OFF-CENTRE

Fort with fence constructed, not in the middle (3-6)

Definition: not in the middle · anagram

  • Fort → FORT four letters of the material, taken as they stand
  • fence → FENCE five more, and the two words together are exactly the nine the enumeration asks for

A fort with a fence round it is a picture, not an instruction: nothing here goes inside anything. The (3-6) is the only sign that the answer arrives in two hyphenated parts.

4 down SLIPPERY SLOPE

Here skeletons may descend from on high; situation likely to worsen (8,5)

Definition: Here skeletons may descend from on high · double definition

  • Here skeletons may descend from on high → skeleton is the Winter Olympic sport whose riders go head-first down an iced chute, so these skeletons are athletes and what they come down is a steep sheet of ice
  • situation likely to worsen → the figurative sense, where one small concession is taken to lead inevitably to a worse one

The surface wants a haunted house with bones tumbling out of the attic. Swap that for the sliding sport and both halves of the clue are describing the same steep, icy thing.

6 down RULER

Person in charge appearing in the end, after you yell blue murder (5)

Definition: Person in charge · last letters

  • after → R the closing letter of a word that looks like joinery and is really material
  • you → U the closing letter of the pronoun
  • yell → L the closing letter of the shout
  • blue → E the closing letter of the colour
  • murder → R the closing letter of the crime, fifth in an unbroken run

'Yell blue murder' is a real idiom for kicking up a fuss, which is why the eye slides straight over it, and why 'after' reads as a link word rather than the first piece of the wordplay.

7 down CENTIME

Swiss bread's aroma absent at first - then I'm getting hint of edelweiss (7)

Definition: Swiss bread · charade + deletion + first letter

  • aroma → CENT an aroma is a scent, and the S it begins with is the one that goes missing
  • I'm → IM the contraction hands over both its letters as they stand
  • edelweiss → E the first letter of the alpine flower

'Swiss bread' is not a loaf: bread is slang for money, so the definition is a currency. The edelweiss and the mountain air are surface dressing, and the flower gives up one letter only.

8 down SPEED SKATE

Sportingly compete and see Kaepernick finally pasted, sadly (5,5)

Definition: Sportingly compete · anagram + last letter

  • see → SEE three letters of the mix, taken whole
  • Kaepernick → K the final letter of the American footballer's surname
  • pasted → PASTED six more letters, which with the other four make exactly ten

'Pasted' means thrashed in sport, so the surface stays on a beaten quarterback throughout. Nothing points to ice until you put the definition and the enumeration together.

11 down SHOULDER BLADE

Bear has weapon: it's wedged in your body (8,5)

Definition: it's wedged in your body · charade

  • Bear → SHOULDER to shoulder a burden is to bear it
  • weapon → BLADE a blade is a knife or a sword, the weapon named for its cutting part

'Bear' looks like the animal and 'wedged in' looks like an instruction to push something inside. Neither is true: the second half of the clue is all definition.

13 down REDISCOVER

Once more find echoey effect mostly overwhelming dance music (10)

Definition: Once more find · container + deletion

  • echoey effect → REVER reverb is the studio effect that makes a room sound live, and its final B is dropped
  • dance music → DISCO disco is a genre as much as it is a place to dance

Dance music turns up twice in this puzzle and the two clues want different genres out of it. 'Overwhelming' is doing a container's work here, not describing an emotion.

15 down BUDDHISTS

Curt biddy shuts out believers (9)

Definition: believers · anagram + deletion

  • biddy → BIDD a biddy is an old woman, mildly rude, and the Y at the end is cut away
  • shuts → SHUTS five letters taken whole into the mix

'Shuts out' reads as one phrasal verb and splits in two: the verb is material and the small word after it is the instruction. Nobody is being excluded from anything.

17 down DOMINOS

What may be played in pub as one of the Three Tenors is no good? (7)

Definition: What may be played in pub · charade + deletion

  • one of the Three Tenors → DOMINGO Placido Domingo sang alongside Pavarotti and Carreras as the Three Tenors
  • is → S the contracted 's that English writes for the verb, tacked on the end
  • good → G the abbreviation that 'no' takes away again

The game has been played in British pubs for two centuries, on a table under the darts board, and that is the world 'played in pub' is pointing at rather than anything operatic.

19 down ENNOBLE

Elevate, somehow, ankle bone - but not back - every now and then (7)

Definition: Elevate · anagram + deletion + alternate letters

  • ankle bone → ANKLEBONE nine letters, the material the rest of the clue then trims
  • back → AK the second and fourth letters of that word, which are the two taken away

The dashes invite you to lift 'but not back' out as an aside, when it is an instruction operating on its own last word. 'Elevate' is the peerage sense, raising somebody to the Lords.

21 down FAZED

Startled as first and last characters taken in by policeman (5)

Definition: Startled · container

  • policeman → FED a fed is an American federal agent, and so a policeman of a sort
  • first and last characters → AZ the characters are the letters of the alphabet, which runs from A to Z

'Characters' wants to be people, the sort a policeman deals with. Read the word as letters instead and the two ends of the alphabet are the piece you need.

22 down LEER

Act the lech and stagger up (4)

Definition: Act the lech · reversal

  • stagger → REEL to reel is to stagger about, the way a drunk does

Four letters turned round, with 'up' doing its proper job for a down entry. 'Lech' is British slang for a lecher, so acting the lech is ogling somebody.
