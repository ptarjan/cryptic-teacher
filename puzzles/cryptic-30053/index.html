Across

1 across TETRAGRAM

‘Fish’, ‘good’, ‘bash’ – one of those four-letter words (9)

Definition: one of those four-letter words · charade

  • ‘Fish’ → TETRA a tetra is the small bright fish of home aquariums
  • ‘good’ → G g is the standard abbreviation for good, as on a school report
  • ‘bash’ → RAM to bash into something is to ram it

The quotation marks are the instruction: each of the three words is being handed over as material rather than read for sense, and each of them really is four letters long.

6 across BIPED

Man, say, placing one page between the sheets? (5)

Definition: Man, say · container

  • the sheets → BED to be between the sheets is to be in bed, and the word opens after its first letter
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one
  • page → P p is the usual abbreviation for page

'Say' is the marker that the definition is only an example: a man is one kind of two-legged creature and far from the only one. The surface suggests something else entirely.

9 across BLITHERING IDIOT

Muppet Amin confined to more cheerful block? (10,5)

Definition: Muppet · charade + container

  • more cheerful → BLITHER blithe means cheerful, and the comparative just adds an R
  • block → INGOT an ingot is a block of cast metal, and it opens after its first three letters
  • Amin → IDI Idi Amin, the Ugandan dictator, hands over his first name

'Muppet' is playground slang for a fool, which the question mark quietly concedes. Amin's forename is the crossword's usual way of getting three awkward letters into a grid.

10 across ROCK

Brilliant music? (4)

Definition: Brilliant · double definition

  • Brilliant → ROCK a brilliant is a diamond cut to fifty-eight facets, and the trade's slang for a big stone is this
  • music → ROCK the loud guitar-and-drums kind, named for the way it rolls

Two words and two definitions, with nothing between them to say which is which — the question mark is the only sign that 'brilliant' is a noun here rather than praise.

11 across KRAKATOA

Old banger taking the biscuit with breakdown truck, would you say? (8)

Definition: Old banger · charade + homophone

  • taking the biscuit → KRAKA the biscuit is a cracker, and said aloud its opening is spelled quite differently
  • breakdown truck → TOA a breakdown truck tows, so it is a tower, and the sound runs straight on from the piece before

'Old banger' is a wreck of a car on the surface and something that banged in 1883 underneath: the eruption was heard thousands of miles away across the Indian Ocean.

14 across FLY AGARIC

Dash a cigar off that’s poisonous (3,6)

Definition: poisonous · charade + anagram

  • Dash → FLY to fly somewhere is to dash there
  • a cigar → ACIGAR anagram fodder, six letters

'Dash a cigar off' reads as one impatient gesture, which is exactly what hides the join between the two pieces. The toadstool meant is the storybook one: scarlet cap, white spots.

15 across NADIR

Some villain, a director, the lowest of the low (5)

Definition: the lowest of the low · hidden word

  • villain, a director → NADIR five letters in a row run from the tail of the first word, through the second and into the third

The comma after 'villain' is the disguise: it makes two separate people out of what is really one unbroken run of letters in a film-set sentence.

16 across ITCHY

Irritable and hot in Windy City (5)

Definition: Irritable · anagram

  • hot → H h is the letter on the hot tap
  • City → CITY anagram fodder, four letters

'Windy City' is Chicago and has nothing to do with the answer: the two words have to be prised apart, one as the instruction and one as the material.

18 across MULTIPLEX

Consider framing suggestion with old pictures (9)

Definition: pictures · charade + container

  • Consider → MULL to mull a thing over is to consider it, and the word opens before its last letter
  • suggestion → TIP a tip is a helpful suggestion
  • old → EX ex- is the prefix for a former anything

'Old pictures' reads as a run of classic films, and that is where the definition hides: only 'pictures' belongs to it, while 'old' is two more letters of wordplay.

20 across THE BIRDS

Intervals including notes a perfect fifth apart horrifying singers? (3,5)

Definition: horrifying singers? · container

  • Intervals → THIRDS a third is a musical interval, and the word opens after its second letter
  • notes a perfect fifth apart → EB count E up to B and you cover five steps of the scale, the interval called a perfect fifth

The clue is all music until the last two words, when it turns into Hitchcock. Even the definition keeps up the pretence, since birds sing and 'singers' still sounds musical.

21 across MWAH

Kiss and Wham on tour? (4)

Definition: Kiss · anagram

  • Wham → WHAM anagram fodder — the eighties pop duo, four letters

Kiss and Wham are both bands, which is the joke: only one of them is being used as a band, and the other is quietly the definition.

25 across SHORT AND CURLIES

One’s disempowered when had by those, Spooner’s Margaret, Temple and MacLaine? (5,3,7)

Definition: One’s disempowered when had by those · spoonerism

  • Margaret → SHORT Margaret Court won more grand slam titles than anyone; she hands her C over and takes SH in exchange
  • and → AND the joining word between the names stays exactly where it stands
  • Temple and MacLaine → CURLIES Shirley Temple and Shirley MacLaine make a pair of Shirleys, and they collect the C the tennis player gave up

Say the plain version first — a tennis champion and two actresses — and Spooner does the rest. What comes out is an idiom with nothing to do with any of them.

26 across EATER

Hog radiator, say, when first cut off (5)

Definition: Hog · deletion

  • radiator, say → HEATER a radiator is one kind of heater, which is what 'say' is marking, and this is the word before its front letter goes

'Say' is doing the work of a definition-by-example in the wordplay rather than the definition: the clue needs the general word first, and only then docks a letter from it.

27 across ARTILLERY

Arsenal’s feeble passing through channel (9)

Definition: Arsenal · container

  • channel → ARTERY an artery is the channel blood travels down, and it opens after its third letter
  • feeble → ILL somebody feeble is ill, weak from sickness

Arsenal are the Gunners, which is the joke the capital letter is trying to sell you. The arsenal wanted is the older sense: a store of weapons.

Down

1 down TIBER

Runner in European capital concerned with section going uphill (5)

Definition: Runner in European capital · charade + reversal

  • concerned with → RE re is the heading on a memo, meaning concerning
  • section → BIT a bit of something is a section of it

'Runner' is the crossword's standing word for a river, and the capital it runs through is Rome — neither is named, so the definition passes for an athlete abroad.

2 down TWITCHY

Bad speller in theory, extremely anxious (7)

Definition: anxious · container + outer letters

  • theory, extremely → TY the two ends of THEORY, its first and last letters, with the middle discarded
  • Bad speller → WITCH not somebody who misspells words but somebody who casts them, and wickedly

The joke is 'bad speller', which reads as a hopeless orthographer and means a caster of evil spells. Everything else in the sentence is machinery dressed as small talk.

3 down ACHE

Long speech finally wrapped up by wizard (4)

Definition: Long · container + last letter

  • wizard → ACE an ace is a wizard at something — the expert, not the conjuror — and the word opens after its second letter
  • speech finally → H the closing letter of speecH

'Long' opens the clue looking like an adjective sizing up the speech, and it is really the verb — to long for something. That switch is the whole difficulty.

4 down RARE

Red colour a revolutionary adopts (4)

Definition: Red · hidden word

  • colour a revolutionary → RARE four letters in a row bridge the end of the first word, the whole of the second and the start of the third

A colour and a revolutionary side by side look like two pieces of wordplay to solve, when the phrase is only a hiding place. 'Red' here is the steak, not the politics.

5 down MINOR SCALE

Noted progression without cabbage being mentioned? (5,5)

Definition: Noted progression · homophone

  • without → MINOR without means minus, and said aloud its closing S runs on into the next word instead of staying put
  • cabbage → SCALE the cabbage meant is kale, and it collects the S that the word before let go

The whole trick is where one word stops and the next starts: the sound never changes, only the gap moves. Say the two greengrocery words quickly and the music appears.

6 down BRICKING IT

Very anxious when building house? (8,2)

Definition: Very anxious · double definition

  • Very anxious → BRICKING IT the slang phrase for being frightened out of your wits
  • building house → BRICKING IT read literally, laying the courses that make the walls

The second reading is a joke rather than a dictionary sense, which is what the question mark concedes: a builder halfway up a wall is doing this in the innocent way.

7 down POINTED

Keen I observe, nothing to lose in school (7)

Definition: Keen · container + deletion

  • school → POD a school of whales is a pod, and it opens between its second and third letters
  • I → I the pronoun taken exactly as it stands, one letter
  • observe, nothing to lose → NTE to observe is to note, and 'nothing' is the O that goes

'Nothing to lose' reads as a gambler's shrug and is really naming the letter O. The school is a gathering of whales, not a building full of children.

8 down DOT MATRIX

Pattern has Dorothy frolicking in spring, did you say? (3,6)

Definition: Pattern · charade + homophone

  • Dorothy → DOT Dot is the familiar short form of Dorothy
  • frolicking in spring → MATRIX frolics are tricks and the spring month is May, and the two run together into one word when spoken

A dot matrix printer builds every character out of a grid of dots, which is the pattern meant — and Dorothy's nickname is a dot in its own right.

12 down UGLY SISTER

Plain character gets surly, I gathered (4,6)

Definition: Plain character · anagram

  • gets surly, I → GETSSURLYI anagram fodder, ten letters

The surface is a small domestic sulk, and 'I gathered' passes for 'so I understood' while doing the shuffling. Plain here is the opposite of beautiful, not of fancy.

13 down PRIMA DONNA

Photo briefly capturing mother, teacher and a princess (5,5)

Definition: princess · charade + container + deletion

  • Photo briefly → PRIN a photo is a print, docked of its last letter; what is left opens up before its own final N
  • mother → MA ma is the nursery word for mother
  • teacher → DON a don is a university teacher
  • a → A the word a, taken as it stands, landing at the very end

'Capturing' does double duty as what a camera does and as the instruction to hold something inside. The print is the darkroom kind, not the newspaper kind.

14 down FAIL TO SEE

Miss payment claiming one pocketed by singers (4,2,3)

Definition: Miss · container

  • payment → FEE a fee is a payment, and it splits after its first letter
  • singers → ALTOS altos are the singers between the sopranos and the tenors
  • one → I the Roman numeral for one

Two insertions nested one inside the other, and the surface never stops sounding like a dispute over an invoice. Only 'Miss' stands outside the wordplay.

17 down CHEROOT

Actress and singer also going up in smoke (7)

Definition: smoke · charade + reversal

  • Actress and singer → CHER Cher has an Oscar and a shelf of hit records, so she answers to both descriptions
  • also → TOO also means too, and this is that word before it is turned

'Going up in smoke' is one idiom on the surface and two separate instructions underneath. The turn stops short of 'in', which is doing nothing but joining.

19 down LOWLIFE

One defined by ‘scummy fellow’? (7)

Definition: One defined by ‘scummy fellow’? · &lit + anagram

  • One → I the Roman numeral for one, which the rest is built around
  • fellow → FELLOW anagram fodder, six letters

Every word works twice: read straight the sentence says what the answer means, and read again it is the recipe for building it. The quotation marks flag the phrase as material.

22 down HUSKY

Growly dog (5)

Definition: Growly · double definition

  • Growly → HUSKY of a voice that comes out low and rough, as after a night of shouting
  • dog → HUSKY the thick-coated sled breed of the Arctic

Two words, and each of them is a complete definition — there is no wordplay to find at all, which is how the clue can afford to be this short.

23 down SCUT

Stumpy tail – ‘s pruned (4)

Definition: Stumpy tail · charade

  • ‘s → S the letter is handed over on its own, inside quotation marks
  • pruned → CUT a pruned branch is one that has been cut

The dash does all the disguising: it turns a definition and two scraps of wordplay into what looks like a single passing remark about an animal.

24 down ORAL

Test of popular opinion to the north (4)

Definition: Test · hidden word + reversal

  • popular opinion → ORAL four letters in a row straddle the gap between these two words, and the entry climbs back up through them

A test of popular opinion is a poll, which is the answer the surface is offering and not the one wanted. Nothing has been rearranged here, only read the other way.
