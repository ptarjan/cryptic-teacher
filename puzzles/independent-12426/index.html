Across

1 across VAIN

Conceited American in Bordeaux? (4)

Definition: Conceited · container

  • Bordeaux? → VIN a Bordeaux is a wine, and wine in the town it comes from is vin
  • American → A A is the dictionary abbreviation for America or American

Bordeaux does two jobs at once: it names the drink and it sets the language you name it in. The question mark is the setter apologising for the French.

3 across ISLE OF MAN

Douglas here maintains svelte physique eating zero fat meals primarily (4,2,3)

Definition: Douglas here · container + first letters

  • maintains svelte physique → ISLEAN someone who maintains a svelte physique is lean, and 'is lean' is the phrase to write out
  • zero → O zero is written as the round O
  • fat meals primarily → FM the opening letters of Fat Meals

The trick is hearing 'maintains svelte physique' as words a person would say — is lean — rather than as a description of them. The whole diet surface exists to stop you doing that.

8 across ADD-ON

Bonus promotion almost over (3-2)

Definition: Bonus · charade + deletion

  • promotion → AD an ad is a promotion
  • over → DON over is DONE, and this stops one letter short of the E

The enumeration (3-2) is a hint in its own right: it promises a hyphenated compound, so you are looking for two pieces rather than one five-letter word.

10 across DRILLING

Practising dental work? (8)

Definition: Practising · double definition

  • Practising → DRILLING troops rehearsing formations, or pupils repeating exercises, are both practising through repetition
  • dental work → DRILLING the drill boring into a tooth is the dentist's own verb

Both halves are -ing words, which is what makes the seam invisible: the clue reads as one activity, a dentist rehearsing, and is really two definitions side by side.

11 across INSIGNIA

Endless, asinine rubbish about Instagram symbol (8)

Definition: symbol · anagram + container + deletion

  • asinine → ASININ asinine hands over everything but its last letter
  • Instagram → IG IG is how Instagram is written in a handle or a hashtag

The comma after 'Endless' is the disguise. It makes the word look like a second adjective describing rubbish, when its job is to dock a letter off the word that follows.

12 across SOLENT

Very fast body of water (6)

Definition: body of water · charade

  • Very → SO 'so tired' and 'very tired' are the same intensifier
  • fast → LENT Lent is the church's forty-day fast

'Fast' is the noun here, a stretch of going without food, not the adverb. 'Very' sitting in front of it is what pushes you towards speed.

15 across OBLATES

Old bishop behind society for religious people (7)

Definition: religious people · charade

  • Old → O O is the abbreviation for old, as in OAP
  • bishop → B B is the bishop in chess notation
  • behind → LATE a train running behind is running late
  • society → S S is the standard abbreviation for society

Three of the four pieces are single letters, and the one that is not hides behind a word you would naturally read as a position. Hunt the abbreviations first.

17 across OUTLAST

Have more staying power than half-hearted social reject (7)

Definition: Have more staying power than · substitution

  • social reject → OUTCAST an outcast is someone society has pushed out
  • half-hearted → L the middle letter of OUTCAST is C, a hundred in Roman numerals, and half of a hundred is L

Roman numerals are the mechanism: because C is 100 and L is 50, one letter can be halved into another. Setters work the same trick with D and M.

18 across TEENAGE

In secondary education? Rest and eat before start of exam (7)

Definition: In secondary education · charade + first letter

  • Rest → TEE a golf ball rests on a tee, so a tee is a rest
  • eat → NAG a worry that eats at you nags at you
  • exam → E the opening letter of Exam

'Rest and eat' sounds like advice for the night before a paper, which is the scene the surface wants. Both words are really nouns and verbs to be swapped for others.

20 across VANILLA

Pedestrian unlucky to meet a vehicle coming first (7)

Definition: Pedestrian · charade

  • vehicle coming first → VAN a van is the vehicle, and it leads despite being named last
  • unlucky → ILL ill luck is bad luck, so unlucky is ill
  • a → A the article taken at face value

'Pedestrian' is the adjective meaning dull, but the road-accident surface keeps it looking like a person on foot right up until the letters are in.

22 across KOALAS

End of combat that's unfortunate for indigenous Australians (6)

Definition: indigenous Australians · charade

  • End of combat → KO a knockout ends a fight, and KO is how it is written
  • that's unfortunate → ALAS alas is what you say when something is unfortunate

'End of combat' looks like an instruction to take one last letter, and 'indigenous Australians' points hard at people. Both readings are the setter's, and neither is the one you want.

23 across ELONGATE

Make extended remix of metal song principally lacking energy (8)

Definition: Make extended · anagram + deletion

  • metal → ETAL METAL with its leading M gone
  • song → ONG SONG stripped of its leading S the same way
  • energy → E E is the physicist's symbol for energy

Read 'principally lacking' as the instruction and 'energy' as one more letter for the pile. The temptation is the other way round: to take an E away and leave both words whole.

26 across AVIATORS

Pilots' map readers ignoring leader - not good (8)

Definition: Pilots · deletion

  • map readers → NAVIGATORS the ones who read the maps are the navigators
  • leader → N the letter leading NAVIGATORS is its N
  • good → G G is the standard abbreviation for good

The dash does a lot of quiet work: it makes 'not good' read as a judgement on the map readers rather than as a second instruction to drop a letter.

27 across TUTOR

Express disapproval over royal governess? (5)

Definition: governess · charade

  • Express disapproval → TUT tut is the noise you make to show disapproval
  • over → O O is the cricket scorer's abbreviation for an over
  • royal → R R is Rex or Regina, the royal signature

'Over' is not doing anything directional here — it is the cricketing abbreviation, which is why the three pieces simply run on in the order the clue gives them.

28 across KNACKERED

I'm not sure journalist's stalking ability is exhausted (9)

Definition: exhausted · charade

  • ability → KNACK a knack is the ability to do something easily
  • I'm not sure → ER er is the noise of someone who is not sure
  • journalist's → ED the journalist is the editor, ED

'Ability is exhausted' reads as one idea, a talent run dry, and that is what stops you cutting the clue at its last word, where the definition actually starts.

29 across UPON

Familiar with riding? (4)

Definition: Familiar with · double definition

  • Familiar with → UPON someone well versed in a subject is often said to be on top of it, right up to speed
  • riding → UPON a rider sits astride the horse, literally on top of it

The enumeration says one word and the first definition wants two, up on. Those four letters reading either way is the whole of the joke.

Down

1 down VLADIVOSTOK

Fifth and final poet returning to visit fine eastern city (11)

Definition: eastern city · charade + container + reversal

  • Fifth → V V is five in Roman numerals
  • final → LAST the final one is the last one
  • poet returning → DIVO Ovid is the crossword's stock poet, and he arrives backwards
  • fine → OK 'fine' and OK are the same shrug of approval

'Fifth and final' sounds like one phrase about a last verse and is really two separate pieces. Ovid is the setter's favourite poet because three of his four letters are vowels.

2 down INDUS

Asian runner visiting Germany and America (5)

Definition: Asian runner · charade

  • visiting → IN someone visiting is in, the way a doctor is in
  • Germany → D D is Germany's international car plate, for Deutschland
  • America → US US is the everyday short form of the country

No indicator anywhere: three pieces simply run on in clue order, which is the shape to look for when every word is a country or a preposition.

4 down SIDE ISSUES

Discuss idle site to be repurposed after decentralisation, and related matters (4,6)

Definition: related matters · anagram + deletion

  • Discuss → DISUSS DISCUSS has seven letters, so its centre is the C, and out it comes
  • idle → IE IDLE has an even middle, DL, and both letters go
  • site → SE SITE loses its middle pair IT the same way

Three words are hollowed out before anything is shuffled, which is why counting the clue's letters never reaches ten. The planning-committee surface hides both steps.

5 down ERIN

Ireland's old monarch trending (4)

Definition: Ireland · charade

  • old monarch → ER ER is Elizabeth Regina, and 'old' marks her as the reign before this one
  • trending → IN what is trending is in — in fashion, in vogue

ER is a crossword workhorse, and since the accession it needs flagging as the previous monarch: that is the job 'old' is doing, not describing an ancient king.

6 down FALLOUT

Consequences of following a large thug (7)

Definition: Consequences · charade

  • following → F f. is the footnote abbreviation for following
  • a → A the article is taken at face value as its own letter
  • large → L L is the clothing label for large
  • thug → LOUT a lout is a thug in four letters

Three single letters in a row is a shape worth recognising: 'a large' adds nothing to the picture of a thug, and words that pull no weight in the surface are usually initials.

7 down ANNIE HALL

Film in musical venue (5,4)

Definition: Film · charade

  • musical → ANNIE Annie, the red-haired orphan, is the musical
  • venue → HALL a hall is where a concert is put on

'Musical venue' reads as one phrase, a place gigs happen, and is really two separate clues laid end to end. There is no indicator at all to warn you.

9 down NIGHT

King abandons Lancelot for one dark period (5)

Definition: dark period · deletion

  • Lancelot for one → KNIGHT Lancelot is one of the Round Table's knights, and 'for one' marks him as an example
  • King → K K is the king on a chessboard and in a pack of cards

'King' looks like part of the Arthurian scenery and is really an abbreviation. Deciding which words of a surface are letters is most of the work in a clue like this.

13 down TETRAHEDRON

Pyramid model done rather crudely (11)

Definition: Pyramid · anagram

  • model → T the Model T Ford is the crossword's standard model
  • done rather → DONERATHER ten letters taken exactly as they stand, to be shaken up

'Rather' is fodder rather than an adverb softening 'crudely', and that is the hard part: the sentence reads as plain English right up to the point you count the letters.

14 down CONVALESCE

Rally and unite to secure new victory (10)

Definition: Rally · container + charade

  • unite → COALESCE to coalesce is to come together into one
  • new → N N is the standard abbreviation for new
  • victory → V V is victory, as in the V-sign and VE Day

'Rally' is the medical sense, a patient rallying, which is why it can sit next to 'unite' without either word looking more like the definition than the other.

16 down LEE MARVIN

Western actor playing evil men, including a Republican (3,6)

Definition: Western actor · anagram + container

  • evil men → EVILMEN seven letters offered up to be rearranged
  • a → A the article taken as its own letter
  • Republican → R R is the ballot-paper abbreviation for a Republican

'Playing evil men' is a straight description of the man's career, which is exactly why it reads as biography instead of as an instruction to rearrange.

19 down ARAMAIC

Language master's superseding bachelor's in another language (7)

Definition: Language · substitution

  • another → ARABIC the other tongue named here is Arabic, Semitic and written right to left
  • bachelor → B B is the standard abbreviation for bachelor
  • master → MA MA is the master's degree, and it moves into the B's slot

The word 'language' appears twice and means two different tongues, one the definition and one the raw material. Degrees are a setter's favourite letter store: BA, MA, BSc.

21 down NONET

Nine gross only? (5)

Definition: Nine · charade

  • only? → NO 'only' rules out everything else, and what it rules out here is the other figure
  • gross → NET a sum is quoted gross or net, so naming one denies the other

Nine gross would be 1,296 if the arithmetic were meant seriously. The question mark is there because the answer is a group of nine rather than the number itself.

24 down ACT UP

Revolving set about to cause problems (3,2)

Definition: cause problems · charade + reversal

  • set → PUT to set something down is to put it down
  • about → CA ca. is short for circa, the 'about' in front of a date

'Set about' reads as one phrasal verb, starting on a job, and is really two separate pieces. Splitting it is what makes the turn possible to see.

25 down DOSE

Anonymous person taking small quantity of medication (4)

Definition: quantity of medication · container

  • Anonymous person → DOE John Doe is the name given to a man nobody can name
  • small → S S is the size label for small

The surface is one sentence about a patient with pills, and the split falls between 'small' and 'quantity' — an awkward place, which is why the definition is hard to find.
