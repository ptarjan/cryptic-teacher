Across

1 across MOUTH-WATERING

Attractive German without pants (5-8)

Definition: Attractive · anagram

  • German without → MOUTHWATERING thirteen letters, exactly the thirteen the answer needs, in a different order

'without' looks like a deletion instruction and is really just more fodder — the whole two words go into the mix, which is why nothing has to be taken away.

9 across APRON

Rapper regularly performing somewhere on stage (5)

Definition: somewhere on stage · charade + alternate letters

  • Rapper → APR the second, fourth and sixth letters of rApPeR
  • performing → ON a show that is performing is on

'Rapper regularly performing' paints a gig, and every word of it is machinery: a letter count, and the theatre sense of ON.

10 across THEN AGAIN

On the other hand, the horse is not tailless (4,5)

Definition: On the other hand · charade + deletion

  • the → THE the article is written down as it stands
  • horse → NAG a nag is a worn-out horse
  • is not → AIN is not contracts to AIN'T, which loses its tail

'the horse is not tailless' is a sentence about a horse's tail and an instruction about a word's, and the joke is that the horse keeps its tail while AIN'T does not.

11 across MOSQUITO

Disease vector medical officers leave out at first (8)

Definition: Disease vector · charade + first letter

  • medical officers → MOS a medical officer is an MO, and more than one are MOs
  • leave → QUIT to quit a job is to leave it
  • out → O the opening letter of out

A vector is not a direction here but the creature that carries a disease between hosts, which is the piece of biology the definition is banking on.

12 across BICEPS

Guns from American's murder case in Paris found near Belgium (6)

Definition: Guns · charade + outer letters

  • Belgium → B B is Belgium's international vehicle registration letter
  • American's murder → ICE to ice someone is American slang for killing them
  • Paris → PS the outer letters of Paris

A murder case in Paris is a news story, and not one word of it means what it says: the city contributes only its shell, and the country only its number plate.

14 across RENT

Musical in two parts? (4)

Definition: Musical · double definition

  • Musical → RENT Jonathan Larson's Broadway show about young artists who cannot pay their landlord
  • in two parts → RENT the past participle of rend: a torn garment has been split in two

Four letters, no indicators and a question mark: that shape almost always means the clue splits in two and each half defines the answer on its own.

15 across LEPRECHAUN

Staggering, Herculean power fills legendary Irish figure (10)

Definition: legendary Irish figure · anagram

  • Herculean → HERCULEAN all nine letters go into the mix
  • power → P P is the physicist's symbol for power, and the tenth letter needed

Herculean strength and Irish legend belong to two different mythologies, which is what makes the surface sound like one sentence when it is really a heap of letters.

18 across SUSPICIOUS

Shady assassins essentially promising to eliminate leader (10)

Definition: Shady · charade + middle letter + deletion

  • assassins → S assassins runs to nine letters, and the fifth sits at the dead centre
  • promising → USPICIOUS promising means AUSPICIOUS, which gives up the A at its head

Assassins who are essentially promising is a joke on hitmen with prospects, and the two long words are near-twins: one loses a head, the other lends a heart.

19 across SNAP

I've got the same kitchenware on the counter (4)

Definition: I've got the same · reversal

  • kitchenware → PANS pans are the kitchenware, and they are what gets turned round

A kitchen counter is where pans sit, which is why 'on the counter' reads as furniture and works as an instruction.

22 across ROTATE

Spin doctor who lost California court case, ultimately (6)

Definition: Spin · last letters

  • doctor who lost California court case → ROTATE the closing letters of the six words in turn: doctoR, whO, losT, CaliforniA, courT, casE

A spin doctor losing in a California court is a whole news story, and none of it matters except the letter each word ends on — six words, six letters, in order.

24 across VALHALLA

God can set about creating a heaven (8)

Definition: a heaven · charade + reversal

  • God → ALLAH Allah is God in Islam
  • can → LAV the can is American for the lavatory, the lav

Three religions in eight letters: an Islamic name and a slang toilet, turned round to make a Norse afterlife.

26 across WATER POLO

French defeat almost stopped by wingers in pro team sport (5,4)

Definition: team sport · container + deletion + outer letters

  • French defeat → WATERLO Napoleon's defeat was Waterloo, one letter short of the whole
  • pro → PO the outer letters of pro

The surface is football — wingers, a pro team — and the sport is played in a pool. Waterloo is the setter's favourite French defeat because of what its first five letters spell.

27 across MOTOR

Roadworthiness test for defaced car (5)

Definition: car · charade + deletion

  • Roadworthiness test → MOT the MOT is the annual roadworthiness test for British vehicles
  • for → OR for loses its first letter, its face, and OR is left

An MOT on a defaced car is a plausible garage scene, and 'for' is the word you have to stop reading as joinery before the clue comes apart.

28 across STAFFORDSHIRE

County newscast affords hi-res screens (13)

Definition: County · hidden word

  • newscast affords hi-res → STAFFORDSHIRE run the three words together and the county spans them, from the end of newscast to the middle of hi-res

Thirteen letters is a long thing to hide, so the setter needs a surface that will carry three unusual words in a row — high-definition television does the job.

Down

1 down MYANMAR

Army man reformed country (7)

Definition: country · anagram

  • Army man → MYANMAR the seven letters of the soldier, rearranged

The surface is about a soldier who has mended his ways; the country was itself renamed, which makes 'reformed' do double duty.

2 down UPRISINGS

Using leverage to infiltrate American rebellions (9)

Definition: rebellions · container

  • American → US US is the everyday short form of American, and it splits either side of what enters it
  • Using leverage → PRISING to prise a lid is to lever it, so prising is using leverage

The surface sounds like political meddling abroad, which is exactly the sense the setter wants you stuck on while two letters are being pushed apart.

3 down HANG-UP

Personal issue with dog breed? No, on reflection (4-2)

Definition: Personal issue · charade + reversal

  • dog breed → PUG the pug is a small flat-faced breed
  • No → NAH nah is the spoken form of no

'on reflection' reads as a change of mind in the surface and as a mirror in the wordplay, and the enumeration 4-2 tells you the answer is hyphenated.

4 down ANTITHESIS

Article on parishioner taxes is in opposition (10)

Definition: opposition · charade

  • Article → AN AN is the indefinite article, the one used before a vowel
  • parishioner taxes → TITHES a tithe was the tenth of his produce a parishioner owed the church
  • is → IS the little verb is taken at face value and joins the end

The verb 'is' looks like joinery holding the sentence together and is really two of the letters, which is the trick worth remembering here.

5 down EYED

Fish caught and inspected (4)

Definition: inspected · homophone

  • Fish → EYED the ide is a European freshwater fish, and its name said aloud is spelt this way

Anglers' fish names are a small vocabulary worth collecting — ide, dace, chub, ling — because setters reach for them whenever a short odd-sounding word is needed.

6 down IN A PINCH

I snooze in church if circumstances require it (2,1,5)

Definition: if circumstances require it · charade

  • I → I the pronoun is taken as the single letter it looks like
  • snooze → NAP a nap is a short snooze
  • in → IN this one is written down exactly as it stands
  • church → CH CH is the standard map abbreviation for church

Four pieces and not an indicator among them: when a clue gives you no instruction words at all, it is telling you the parts simply follow one another.

7 down GLADE

Pleased at cheque finally clearing (5)

Definition: clearing · charade + last letter

  • Pleased → GLAD glad and pleased are the same everyday feeling
  • cheque → E the last letter of cheque

A cheque clearing is banking; a clearing in a wood is forestry. The whole surface leans on that one word doing two jobs.

8 down UNISON

Agreement about supporting educational establishments (6)

Definition: Agreement · charade

  • educational establishments → UNIS unis is the familiar short form of universities
  • about → ON a book on birds is a book about birds

The clue reads bottom-up: the piece named first ends up last, because supporting something means sitting beneath it.

13 down TROUBADOUR

Dreadful tour spoiled by this group's singer (10)

Definition: singer · anagram + charade

  • Dreadful → BAD dreadful here means simply bad
  • tour → TOUR the tour goes in whole, four more letters for the mix
  • this group's → OUR someone inside a group calls it ours, so this group's is OUR

'Dreadful tour spoiled' reads as one complaint about a band, and only the first word is a synonym while the third is the instruction.

16 down AGNOLOTTI

Parcels in transit to Long Ashton getting redirected (9)

Definition: Parcels · hidden word + reversal

  • transit to Long Ashton → ITTOLONGA run the words together and this nine-letter string spans them, from the end of transit to the start of Ashton

Long Ashton is a real village outside Bristol, and naming somewhere plausible is how a setter stops you reading the words as a string of letters.

17 down VICTORIA

Underground hub's very graphic stripping (8)

Definition: Underground hub · charade + deletion

  • very → V V is the standard abbreviation for very
  • graphic → ICTORIA graphic means PICTORIAL, which is stripped of its first and last letters

'very graphic stripping' is written to sound like something a censor would object to, and the only thing being undressed is a nine-letter adjective.

18 down SCRAWL

Illegible handwriting beginning from squiggly stroke (6)

Definition: Illegible handwriting · charade + first letter

  • squiggly → S the first letter of squiggly
  • stroke → CRAWL the crawl is the fastest swimming stroke

'stroke' is a pen stroke in the surface and a swimming stroke in the wordplay, and swimming strokes — crawl, fly, back — are worth keeping in mind.

20 down PRAIRIE

Plain couple that is broken up by Romeo (7)

Definition: Plain · container + charade

  • couple → PAIR a couple is a pair
  • that is → IE i.e. is the Latin id est, which translates as that is
  • Romeo → R Romeo is R in the radio alphabet

Romeo breaking up a couple is Shakespeare on the surface; in the wordplay he is one letter from the NATO spelling alphabet, a set worth learning whole.

21 down THAMES

Has Met dealt with one dividing capital between banks? (6)

Definition: one dividing capital between banks · anagram

  • Has Met → THAMES the six letters of Has Met, redealt

The Met dealing with a case is police business, and 'banks' is the pun that has to fall the other way: not the ones holding money but the ones holding water.

23 down TOTES

Bears? Definitely! (5)

Definition: Bears · double definition

  • Bears → TOTES to carry a load, as in 'tote a bag', is the verb meant; here it takes the third-person -s
  • Definitely → TOTES clipped teen slang for totally, used to agree with something wholeheartedly

Two registers a century apart: a word from tote bags and gun-toting, and a word from text messages. The question mark warns you the first reading is the wrong one.

25 down LOAF

Head to lounge (4)

Definition: Head · double definition

  • Head → LOAF Cockney rhyming slang for head shortens a two-word phrase ending in 'bread', since bread rhymes with head
  • lounge → LOAF to idle the day away, doing nothing much, is the verb meant here

'Head to' reads as an instruction to go somewhere, which hides the split: the clue is two definitions with a word of joinery between them.
