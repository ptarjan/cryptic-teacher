Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,895

Across

1 across RISK-AVERSE

Careful, Kelvin insists during climb (4-6)

Definition: Careful · charade + container

  • climb → RISE to rise is to climb, and this is the word that opens up
  • Kelvin → K K is the symbol for the kelvin, the physicist's unit of temperature
  • insists → AVERS to aver something is to insist on it, a favourite of setters and courtrooms

'Careful, Kelvin' sounds like a warning shouted at a climbing partner, and there is no such person: Kelvin is the temperature unit. The comma is where the definition stops.

6 across BURP

Back massage ending in chap's rude emission (4)

Definition: rude emission · reversal + last letter

  • massage → BUR to rub is to massage, and those three letters are read the other way
  • chap → P the last letter of chap

The surface is a spa treatment. The apostrophe-s belongs to the chap, so he only lends a letter and the definition is the two words he owns.

10 across PRATTLE

Silly speech old PM curtailed on electoral system (7)

Definition: Silly speech · charade + deletion

  • electoral system → PR PR is proportional representation, the voting system British reformers campaign for
  • old PM → ATTLE Clement Attlee was the prime minister who beat Churchill in 1945, docked of his last letter

The surface is a politician droning on. A solver outside Britain should file Attlee alongside PR as crossword furniture — both turn up constantly.

11 across UTOPIAN

Perfect work dons distributed at uni (7)

Definition: Perfect · anagram + container

  • at uni → ATUNI five letters of fodder, uni being the British student's word for university
  • work → OP OP is short for opus, a composer's work

The surface pictures gowned academics handing out assignments, and 'dons' is the verb rather than the noun. That is the whole joke of the clue.

12 across DAHLIA

Garden plant had trouble, each part being twisted (6)

Definition: Garden plant · charade + reversal

  • had → DAH the clue's own had, written the other way round
  • trouble → LIA to ail is to be in trouble, and that word turns too

The surface is somebody recovering from an illness among the flowerbeds. Two small words turn rather than one, which is what 'each part' is quietly telling you.

13 across SHRAPNEL

Her plans unexpectedly change (8)

Definition: change · anagram

  • Her plans → HERPLANS anagram fodder, eight letters
  • unexpectedly → anagram indicator

Shrapnel is British slang for the coins weighing down a pocket, which is how a word for shell fragments comes to be defined as change.

15 across THE TWO RONNIES

"No, Os!" went their funny show (3,3,7)

Definition: show · anagram

  • "No, Os!" went their → NOOSWENTTHEIR anagram fodder, thirteen letters, the quotation marks included in the count of nothing
  • funny → anagram indicator

The italics mark the Os as two letters rather than a word. The BBC sketch show of Ronnie Barker and Ronnie Corbett ran for sixteen years and is a household name in Britain.

18 across REINFORCEMENT

Glue on strap to provide extra strength (13)

Definition: extra strength · charade

  • strap → REIN a rein is the strap in a rider's hands
  • to provide → FOR for carries that sense of purpose: money for a new roof is money to provide one
  • Glue → CEMENT cement is the glue that sticks tiles down

Everything in the surface is a physical repair — glue, a strap, added strength. The answer is the abstract word, the kind an army sends rather than a workshop applies.

22 across TIGERISH

Spice Girl shut up after it turned catty (8)

Definition: catty · reversal + charade

  • it → TI the clue's own it, facing the other way
  • Spice Girl → GERI Geri Halliwell was Ginger Spice, so the fragment points at a name rather than a job
  • shut up → SH SH is what you write for the noise that tells somebody to be quiet

'Catty' means feline here, of the big-cat kind, not spiteful in the gossip sense the surface is nudging you towards.

24 across WEIRDO

Eccentric party on wet embankment (6)

Definition: Eccentric · charade

  • wet embankment → WEIR a weir is a low dam built across a river, so the description is loose but fair
  • party → DO a do is a party, as in a works do

The party is a knees-up rather than a political one, and 'Eccentric' is the noun. The clue reads as a riverside gathering and means nothing of the sort.

26 across CAMPARI

Party with dry, mostly red booze (7)

Definition: red booze · charade + deletion

  • Party → CAMP a camp is a faction or side, as in the Remain camp
  • dry → ARI arid means dry, and most of that word is taken

A camp is a faction, not a gathering with drinks, which is where the surface puts you. The comma after 'dry' is doing nothing except holding the sentence together.

27 across EMANATE

Issue from energy our species consumed (7)

Definition: Issue · charade

  • energy → E E is the physicist's symbol for energy, the E of E=mc²
  • our species → MAN man in the collective sense, the species rather than a male
  • consumed → ATE to eat is to consume, in the past tense

'Issue' is the verb, to flow out, and not the noun about magazines or offspring that the surface keeps suggesting.

28 across LIED

Cap worn by European was not what it seemed (4)

Definition: was not what it seemed · container

  • Cap → LID a lid is a cap, on a jar or on a head
  • European → E E is the standard abbreviation for European

The surface dresses a man in a hat that is really a container. Cap for LID and European for E are two of the standbys worth owning early on.

29 across SKEDADDLED

Also note light on desk badly bolted (10)

Definition: bolted · anagram + charade

  • desk → DESK anagram fodder, four letters
  • Also note → ADD to add is to say something further — 'and another thing,' he added
  • light → LED an LED is the small light on every modern gadget

'Bolted' is the horse sense of the word, ran off, rather than the door sense the desk lamp is nudging you towards.

Down

1 down RAPIDS

Power in river helps where the current's strong (6)

Definition: where the current's strong · charade + container

  • river → R R is the map-maker's abbreviation for river
  • helps → AIDS to aid somebody is to help them, and the verb keeps its S
  • Power → P P is the standard abbreviation for power, as on a switch

The surface is a hydroelectric scheme. The definition is the whole of the last five words — no single word inside it would do the job on its own.

2 down SPAGHETTI

Brittle strands break up - it's the cooking binds it (9)

Definition: Brittle strands · anagram + reversal + container

  • it's the → ITSTHE six letters of fodder, taken straight from the clue
  • break → PAG a break in a wall is a gap, and those three letters climb the entry

'break up' wants to be the shuffle instruction; the real one is 'cooking', and 'break' is a noun. The surface is a pan of pasta going in, which is what the wordplay does to it.

3 down ASTRIDE

In riding position since start of that bike trip (7)

Definition: In riding position · charade + first letter

  • since → AS as means since in 'as you're here', both introducing a reason
  • that → T the first letter of that
  • bike trip → RIDE a ride is a trip taken on a bike

The surface has somebody already sitting on the bike. 'Since' as AS is worth filing away: setters use it whenever a clue needs two letters and a reason.

4 down EVEN

Still four at the back for football team? (4)

Definition: Still · last letters

  • football team → EVEN a football side is an eleven, and the closing four letters of that word are what the clue wants

The back four is a real thing in football, the line of defenders, and that is the reading to let go of. What the clue counts is letters at the back of a number.

5 down SOUTHERNER

Texan possibly freaks out her nervous hosts (10)

Definition: Texan possibly · hidden word

  • freaks out her nervous → SOUTHERNER ten letters in a row, beginning on the last letter of the first word and stopping three letters into the last

'freaks out' looks like an instruction to shuffle and is pure surface — nothing moves. The Texan is one example of the wider group, which is what 'possibly' is doing there.

7 down UNION

Short measure of alcohol immediately after wedding (5)

Definition: wedding · charade + deletion

  • measure of alcohol → UNI a unit is the standard measure of alcohol on a health leaflet, here served short
  • immediately after → ON on carries that sense in 'on arrival', meaning at the very moment of

'Short' reads as part of the drink — a short is a spirit — and is really the instruction. The wedding at the end is the definition, not the occasion for the round.

8 down PENALISE

Write a story about saint subject to punishment (8)

Definition: subject to punishment · charade + container

  • Write → PEN to pen a note is to write it
  • a → A the clue's own a, taken across as it stands
  • story → LIE a story in the nursery sense is an untruth, and it opens up here
  • saint → S S is the abbreviation for saint, as in S Paul

The surface is a writer at work on the life of a saint. Story as LIE is a standby worth owning: setters reach for it whenever a tale is being told.

9 down DOMAIN

Complete electricity, gas or water supply for territory? (6)

Definition: territory · charade

  • Complete → DO to do a job is to complete it
  • electricity, gas or water supply → MAIN the mains are the public pipes and cables a house is connected to

Mains is the British word for a utility supply, so the long list is one word rather than three options. The question mark covers the jump from that to land.

14 down SWORDSTICK

Son promises to get teacher's approval for concealed weapon (10)

Definition: concealed weapon · charade

  • Son → S S is the genealogist's abbreviation for son
  • promises → WORDS your word is your promise, so promises are words
  • teacher's approval → TICK a tick is the mark a British teacher puts beside a right answer, what an American would call a check

The surface is a pupil bargaining for a good mark. Nothing is reversed or shuffled: three pieces in clue order, which makes the last one the only thing to spot.

16 down INTERRAIL

Home ground unfinished, left to train abroad? (9)

Definition: train abroad · charade + deletion

  • Home → IN somebody at home is in, the sense of 'is she in?'
  • ground → TERRAI terrain is ground, one letter short of itself here
  • left → L L is the standard abbreviation for left

The surface is a footballer leaving his home ground for a club overseas. An Interrail pass buys a traveller unlimited European rail journeys, and Britons use the name as a verb.

17 down PROTOCOL

Etiquette for returning stolen goods fences start to consider (8)

Definition: Etiquette · charade + reversal + container + first letter

  • for → PRO pro means for, as in the pros and cons of a plan
  • stolen goods → TOOL loot is stolen goods, and these letters are read backwards
  • consider → C the word consider starts with C

A fence is a dealer in stolen goods, which is the surface, and to fence is to enclose, which is the instruction. Both senses are live at once and only one of them moves letters.

19 down FORMAL

Marines in future mount ritual (6)

Definition: ritual · container

  • future mount → FOAL a foal is a young horse, a mount still to come
  • Marines → RM RM is the Royal Marines, Britain's naval infantry

The surface is a military parade. 'Mount' is a horse rather than a verb, and a foal is one too young to be ridden — which is what 'future' is doing there.

20 down EMERALD

Green from mal de mer, miles out at sea (7)

Definition: Green · anagram + deletion

  • mal de mer → MALDEMER French for seasickness, and eight letters to work with
  • miles → M is the abbreviation for miles, and that M is the letter that goes
  • at sea → anagram indicator

Mal de mer is French for seasickness, italicised because it is not English, and 'at sea' does double duty — it keeps the surface afloat and gives the instruction.

21 down TOP-END

High-class warehouse filled with new flip-flops (3-3)

Definition: High-class · container + reversal

  • warehouse → DEPOT a depot is a warehouse or store
  • new → N N is the standard abbreviation for new

The surface is a warehouse stacked with summer sandals, and the footwear is the trap: 'flip-flops' is a verb here. The hyphen in the enumeration is the only clue to the shape.

23 down GIMME

Piece of cake swallowed by pig immediately (5)

Definition: Piece of cake · hidden word

  • pig immediately → GIMME five letters in a row, starting on the last letter of the first word and running across the gap

A gimme is a putt so short the other golfer concedes it, and the word is a squashed 'give me'. Nothing is shuffled here: the letters are already in order.

25 down LEAD

Roofing material guide (4)

Definition: Roofing material · double definition

  • Roofing material → the soft grey metal laid in sheets over church and chapel roofs
  • guide → the verb: to go in front and show somebody the way

Two words with one spelling and two pronunciations — the metal rhymes with bed, the verb with bead. Three words is as short as a clue gets, and the shortness is the difficulty.
