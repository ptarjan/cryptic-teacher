Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,865

Across

1 across CONSOMME

Monsieur enters to have a little soup (8)

Definition: soup · charade + container

  • have → CON to have someone, in the sense of tricking them, is to con them
  • Monsieur → M M is the standard abbreviation for the French courtesy title, as written on an envelope
  • a little → SOME a little of something is some of it, and the Frenchman's letter goes in among these four

'to have' looks like joinery holding the sentence up; it is the first chunk of the wordplay, in the sense of having someone over.

6 across DIGEST

Really enjoy Alien in which ship's beginning to dissolve with acid (6)

Definition: dissolve with acid · charade + container + first letter

  • Really enjoy → DIG to dig something, in sixties slang, is to be very keen on it
  • Alien → ET ET is the crossword's standard alien, short for extraterrestrial
  • ship → S the first letter of ship, the vessel the surface has in mind

Italics mark Alien as the film, which is scene-setting only. The wordplay wants the two-letter alien every crossword uses, and a spacecraft is just a ship.

9 across COURTHOUSE

Trying venue where race might be held that accommodates 1,000 (10)

Definition: Trying venue · container

  • where race might be held → COURSE a race is run over a course, whether the runners are horses or people
  • 1,000 → THOU thou is the everyday short form of a thousand

'Trying' is the pun: not wearisome but conducting a trial. The surface keeps you at the racetrack, where the same word would mean a testing afternoon.

10 across SCAM

Anorak's over fleece (4)

Definition: fleece · reversal

  • Anorak's → SCAM an anorak is a mac, the British word for a raincoat, and one belonging to somebody makes MACS, written in the other way round

Both halves of the clue talk about coats: an anorak in the wordplay and a sheep's fleece in the definition, and only one of them is really clothing.

11 across WITHNAIL AND I

Wii boxing in back of tavern in Thailand, drunken comedy (8,3,1)

Definition: drunken comedy · container + last letter

  • Wii → WII Nintendo's games console, spelled with a double i
  • tavern → N the letter at the back of the word tavern
  • Thailand → THAILAND the country supplies eight letters, opened up early on to take the tavern's letter

'Drunken' looks like an instruction to shuffle and is not: it belongs to the definition, describing a 1987 film about two soaked out-of-work actors.

13 across READY-MIX

Sponge without water perhaps upsetting Mary, die on cross (5-3)

Definition: Sponge without water perhaps · anagram

  • Mary, die → READYMI seven letters tipped out of their printed order
  • cross → X a cross is written as an X, the mark on a ballot paper

The definition is a packet of dry cake mix: add water and you get a sponge. 'Perhaps' is there because concrete is sold the same way.

15 across IMPALE

Stick one's china back on mantelpiece (6)

Definition: Stick · charade + last letter

  • one's → IM a person speaking of themselves turns one is into I'm, two letters once the apostrophe goes
  • china → PAL china plate is Cockney rhyming slang for mate, and a mate is a pal
  • mantelpiece → E the letter this long word ends on

'Stick' is the definition and it is a verb, meaning to run something through on a spike — not the noun the ornaments and the mantelpiece invite.

16 across HONSHU

Japanese island ducks largely avoid (6)

Definition: Japanese island · charade + deletion

  • ducks → HON ducks is a British term of address for someone you are fond of, and hon, short for honey, does the same job
  • avoid → SHU to avoid a person is to shun them, and the word turns up here a letter short

'Ducks' looks like waterfowl, or a batter's score of nothing; it is the friendly Midlands form of address, the sort said across a shop counter.

18 across PINPOINT

Accurately position leg and off-side fielder (8)

Definition: Accurately position · charade

  • leg → PIN pins is old slang for legs, as in being unsteady on them
  • off-side fielder → POINT point is the cricket fielding position square of the batter on the off side

Cricket divides the field into the leg side and the off side, and the clue uses both words — one as a limb, one as a genuine fielding position.

20 across SEVENTY-EIGHT

Old record tied yet broken in prospect (7-5)

Definition: Old record · container + anagram

  • prospect → SIGHT a prospect is a view, and a view is a sight
  • tied → EVEN scores that are tied are level, or even
  • yet → TYE the three letters of yet, put back in another order

'Record ... broken' is the surface's athletics story and has nothing to do with it: the record here is the brittle shellac disc a wind-up gramophone played.

23 across TICK

Beat little pest that won't let go (4)

Definition: Beat · double definition

  • Beat → TICK a clock marks each beat of time with one small sound
  • little pest that won't let go → TICK the parasite that buries its head in a dog's skin and cannot be shaken off

Nothing is anagrammed or hidden here: the clue simply sets two unrelated meanings side by side and lets the surface pretend they are one sentence.

24 across CREDITABLE

Decent plastic ID bracelet (10)

Definition: Decent · anagram

  • ID bracelet → CREDITABLE the ten letters of these two words, pressed into a new shape

'Plastic ID bracelet' is one believable object, which is what hides the instruction inside it. The enumeration is the tell: the phrase holds exactly ten letters.

25 across CHARGE

Bill right to replace note in coins (6)

Definition: Bill · substitution

  • coins → CHANGE the coins rattling in your pocket are your change
  • note → N N is the standard abbreviation for note, and it is the letter that leaves
  • right → R R is the standard abbreviation for right, as on a diagram

'Bill', 'note' and 'coins' are all money on the surface, which is the misdirection: two of them are money and the third is a single letter.

26 across MEDITATE

Consider change to clue, perhaps Mike worried about it? (8)

Definition: Consider · container + charade

  • Mike → M Mike is M in the radio alphabet, the same list that gives uniform for U
  • change to clue, perhaps → EDIT changing a piece of writing is editing it, and a clue is one example of writing
  • worried → ATE what worried you ate at you, in the idiom about something eating a person

The question mark covers 'perhaps': a clue is only one example of the thing being changed. On the surface a setter is fretting over his own puzzle.

Down

2 down OBOE

Bit of wind taking the wings off two Boeings (4)

Definition: Bit of wind · hidden word

  • two Boeings → OBOE four consecutive letters run across the join between these two words once the outer ones are gone

Boeing builds aeroplanes, so 'wings' reads as part of the picture rather than as an instruction. The aircraft never matter; only the spelling does.

3 down SCREWED

Son worked on boat up the creek (7)

Definition: up the creek · charade

  • Son → S S is the standard abbreviation for son, as on a family tree
  • worked on boat → CREWED the hands who work a boat are its crew, and as a verb they crewed her

The surface is a lad helping out on the water. 'Up the creek' is the short form of being up it without a paddle, which is the definition.

4 down MY HAT

Rug on top for Spooner? Surely not (2,3)

Definition: Surely not · spoonerism

  • on top → HIGH whatever sits on top of everything else is high up
  • Rug → MAT a mat is a small rug, the sort you wipe your feet on
  • Rug on top → MY HAT the two pieces stand as HIGH MAT, and trading their opening sounds gives what you write in

A Spoonerism happens in the mouth, not on paper: say the two words with their first sounds exchanged and out comes an old-fashioned cry of disbelief.

5 down EQUINOX

Shortened horsy draught animal's 12 hour day (7)

Definition: 12 hour day · charade + deletion

  • horsy → EQUIN horsy is equine in a vet's vocabulary, and the word arrives with its last letter clipped
  • draught animal → OX an ox is the beast harnessed to pull a plough or a cart

The definition is exact rather than loose: on this date daylight and darkness each last twelve hours, which happens twice a year.

6 down DIETITIAN

Scoffing expert finally said "That is an old master" (9)

Definition: Scoffing expert · charade + last letter

  • said → D the closing letter of said
  • That is → IE i.e. is short for the Latin id est, which translates as that is
  • an old master → TITIAN Titian was the great Venetian painter of the sixteenth century, one of the old masters

The quotation marks make the last five words sound like someone admiring a painting on a wall; the wordplay reads them piece by piece instead.

7 down GAS LAMP

Burning light alarmingly close in space (3,4)

Definition: Burning light · container

  • space → GAP a gap is the space left between two things
  • alarmingly close → SLAM to slam a door is to close it hard enough to make everyone jump

The surface pictures two objects nearly colliding out in the cosmos. 'Close' is not a distance at all but a door being shut, and 'space' is only a gap.

8 down STAND-ALONE

Acting independently troubled Nelson a tad (5-5)

Definition: Acting independently · anagram

  • Nelson a tad → STANDALONE these ten letters, shaken out of the order the clue prints them in

Nelson lends the surface an admiral who stops mattering the moment you count: the name and 'a tad' hold exactly ten letters, which is the enumeration.

12 down BE DONE WITH

Get rid of husband, but shag single comedian first (2,4,4)

Definition: Get rid of · charade

  • shag → BED to bed someone is the printable verb for what the surface says more coarsely
  • single → ONE a single of anything is one of it
  • comedian → WIT a wit is someone whose talk gets laughs, which is a comedian's whole trade
  • husband → H H is the standard abbreviation for husband, as on a form

The order is the trap: the husband is named at the start of the clue and written at the end of the answer, and 'but' only props the sentence up.

14 down MOUSTACHE

You must cut extremely long hair (9)

Definition: hair · container + charade

  • extremely → MOST extremely rich and most rich say the same thing, so the adverb hands over four letters
  • You → U U stands in for you in text-message spelling
  • long → ACHE to long for something is to ache for it

'You must cut' reads as an order barked at the solver, and that is exactly what it is — an instruction about one letter, not about anybody's hair.

17 down SNEAKER

Shoe reeks, case of aeration failing? (7)

Definition: Shoe · anagram + outer letters

  • aeration → AN the two letters that case this word, one at each end
  • reeks → REEKS five letters as printed, waiting to be reordered along with the pair beside them

The question mark flags the whole picture as invention: footwear has no aeration case. It is there so 'case of' reads as a complaint rather than an order.

18 down PRETERM

Half retire in style earlier than expected (7)

Definition: earlier than expected · container + deletion

  • style → PERM a perm is a hairstyle, short for permanent wave
  • retire → RET the first half of retire, three of its six letters

'Half retire' is solver's shorthand for taking half the letters, not for going part-time, and the early retirement in the surface is the joke.

19 down ON TOAST

How to serve cheese and beans? Not with head inside oven (2,5)

Definition: How to serve cheese and beans · container + charade + first letter

  • Not → OT the word Not with its leading letter taken away
  • head → N the head of a word is the letter at its front, here the one just removed
  • oven → OAST an oast is the kiln in a Kentish oast house, built for drying hops

The trick is that the letter taken off the front never leaves the clue: it moves back in one place, which is why the letter count never changes.

21 down GUILD

Put precious coat on to cover uniform for society (5)

Definition: society · container

  • Put precious coat on → GILD to gild is to lay a thin skin of gold over something
  • uniform → U U is uniform in the radio alphabet, where every letter has a spoken name

'Put precious coat on' is one instruction rather than three, and 'precious' is in it only because the metal being laid on has to be gold.

22 down SLOT

Slow mover missing the last scheduled time (4)

Definition: scheduled time · deletion

  • Slow mover → SLOTH the sloth hangs about in South American trees and is a byword for going nowhere fast

The surface grumbles about a dawdler who has missed the last bus. 'The last' is not part of that story: it is an instruction about a letter.
