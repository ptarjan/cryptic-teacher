1 across DAFFODIL

Pallid Offa defiantly holds revolutionary national emblem (8)

Definition: national emblem · hidden word + reversal

The daffodil is the flower Wales adopts as its emblem, so 'national emblem' points at a plant rather than a flag or crest.

  • Pallid Offa defiantly → LIDOFFAD eight consecutive letters start inside the first word and stop inside the third
  • revolutionary → reversal indicator: read that run from its far end
  • holds — What a thing holds is inside it, so these words are keeping a run of letters rather than being shuffled or abbreviated.
  • revolutionary — A revolutionary turns things round, so the run that has been found is read back the other way.

A pale but defiant King Offa clutching some patriotic badge.

Offa was a Mercian king and 'pallid' keeps you in Anglo-Saxon England, which is the wrong country: the daffodil is the national emblem of Wales, worn on St David's Day.

6 across GENTLE

Kind chap starts to lend encouragement (6)

Definition: Kind · charade + first letters

A gentle manner is a kind one, so the adjective swaps straight in: gentle words, kind words.

  • chap → GENT a gent is a chap, short for gentleman
  • lend encouragement → LE the first letters of these two words
  • starts to — A word's start is the letter it begins with, and the plural sense here takes one from each of the two words that follow.

A kindly man beginning to offer a few words of support.

'Kind chap' reads as a single description, which is the trap — only the first word is defining, and the chap belongs to the wordplay.

9 across BREEZE

Piece of cake and some cheeses, we’re told (6)

Definition: Piece of cake · homophone

A breeze is anything achieved without effort, exactly what the idiom 'Piece of cake' means, so the two phrases swap as nouns.

  • some cheeses → BREEZE brie is the soft French cheese, and more than one of them are bries
  • we’re told — Being told something means hearing it, so the cheeses count for the noise they make and not for their spelling.

A cake and a cheeseboard being described to somebody.

'Piece of cake' is a phrase rather than a food, which is what the cheeseboard is there to disguise: both it and the answer mean something that takes no effort at all.

10 across ARMITAGE

Lively Margate embraces independent poet (8)

Definition: poet · anagram

Armitage is Simon Armitage, the Poet Laureate, so 'poet' names him as an instance of the category rather than as a synonym.

  • Margate → MARGATE seven letters to be rearranged; Margate is a seaside town in Kent
  • independent → I I is the standard abbreviation for independent, as beside a candidate's name on a ballot
  • Lively → anagram indicator
  • Lively — Something lively will not stay still, so the town's letters refuse to keep the order they are given in.
  • embraces — To embrace is to take something in, so the extra letter is drawn into the shuffle rather than added on the end.

A jolly seaside town taking in a writer who belongs to no party.

Simon Armitage has been Britain's Poet Laureate since 2019, so 'poet' defines him by example — and with no question mark to warn you that a single person is meant.

11 across EMPHASISE

Stress when preparing pie and mash before suppertime on vacation (9)

Definition: Stress · anagram + outer letters

To emphasise a point is to stress it — the verb of underlining what matters, which both words share exactly.

  • pie and mash → PIEMASH seven letters to be reordered, the 'and' merely joining the dish together
  • suppertime on vacation → SE the two end letters of suppertime, everything between them emptied out
  • when preparing → anagram indicator
  • when preparing — Preparing a dish chops and combines its ingredients, so the food arrives with its letters reordered.
  • before — This one is positional: the reordered food goes in front of what survives of suppertime.
  • on vacation — A house on vacation stands empty, so suppertime loses everything between its two end letters.

Someone cooking pie and mash ahead of an evening meal while away on holiday.

Pie and mash is a London working-class dish, which is what makes the cooking surface hold together. 'Stress' is a verb here, not the noun of pressure the kitchen scene invites.

13 across STORM

Charge in transistor mechanism (5)

Definition: Charge · hidden word

To storm a building is to charge at it in force, so 'Charge' is the verb of assault rather than anything electrical.

  • transistor mechanism → STORM five consecutive letters run across the join between the two words
  • in — Here 'in' is meant literally: the letters sit inside the two words that follow, unbroken and in order.

An electric charge somewhere inside the workings of a transistor.

'Charge' is a verb here — troops storm a position — while the surface keeps your eye on electronics, where charge is a noun.

15 across SLIGHT

Ignore trickery in speech (6)

Definition: Ignore · homophone

To slight a person is to snub them, deliberately taking no notice, which is the pointed sense of 'Ignore'.

  • trickery → SLIGHT sleight, as in sleight of hand, is a conjuror's trickery
  • in speech — What happens in speech is heard rather than written, so the trickery counts only for the sound it makes.

Some deception being practised in the course of a conversation.

The two words differ by one silent letter and sound identical, which is why setters love the pair. 'Ignore' is the verb of the snub: you slight someone by passing them over.

17 across PAROLE

Release standard expression of approval on the continent (6)

Definition: Release · charade

Parole is conditional release from prison, so 'Release' is the noun a prisoner is granted, not the act of freeing.

  • standard → PAR par is the standard score set for a golf hole
  • expression of approval → OLE olé is the shout of approval at a bullfight or a flamenco
  • on the continent — Continental Europe signals a foreign word, and the shout wanted here is the Spanish one.

The usual roar of approval you would hear from a crowd somewhere in Europe.

'Release' is the noun the parole board grants — a prisoner let out on conditions — and PAR for standard is the golfer's word crosswords lean on constantly.

18 across NAPERY

Linen not available for every year (6)

Definition: Linen · charade

Napery is the tablecloths and napkins a household keeps, so 'Linen' is the collective drapery sense rather than the fabric.

  • not available → NA NA is the shorthand a list uses for something not available
  • for every → PER per means for each: fifty pence per person
  • year → Y y is the usual short form of year, as in 3y

A shop reporting that its linen is out of stock for the whole year.

Napery is household table linen — cloths and napkins, from the same root as napkin — and it is built here out of three everyday abbreviations laid end to end.

19 across RED SEA

A reed’s cultivated in area near Egypt (3,3)

Definition: area near Egypt · anagram

The Red Sea runs the length of Egypt's eastern coast, so the definition places the answer geographically instead of describing it.

  • A reed’s → AREEDS six letters, the s of the contraction counting as one of them
  • cultivated → anagram indicator
  • cultivated — Cultivating ground turns it over and breaks it up, and the letters given get the same treatment.

A single reed being grown somewhere close to Egypt.

The apostrophe-s is fodder rather than a verb, which is the hard part: it quietly supplies the sixth letter while reading as 'a reed is'.

21 across CHARY

Fastidious cleaner back from factory (5)

Definition: Fastidious · charade + last letter

Chary means wary and careful about what one will accept, and that choosiness is the 'Fastidious' sense rather than tidiness.

  • cleaner → CHAR a char is a domestic cleaner in Britain, short for charwoman
  • back from factory → Y the last letter of factory
  • back from — The back of a word is the letter it ends with, so factory gives up its final letter only.

A fussy cleaner coming home from a shift at the factory.

'Back' looks like a reversal instruction and is not — it asks for the final letter, and the surface's journey home is what sells the misdirection.

22 across HURRICANE

Reportedly fly staff in old aeroplane (9)

Definition: old aeroplane · homophone + charade

The Hawker Hurricane is a Second World War fighter, so 'old aeroplane' defines the answer by example rather than by synonym.

  • fly → HURRI to fly somewhere is to hurry, and it is the noise of the word that is wanted
  • staff → CANE a cane is a staff you walk with
  • Reportedly — Something reported is passed on by word of mouth, so the next word counts for its sound rather than its spelling.

An urgent instruction to fly some employees out in a vintage plane.

The Hurricane was the RAF fighter that flew alongside the Spitfire in the Battle of Britain, so 'old aeroplane' names one particular machine.

25 across MODERATE

Uncontroversial fashion degree (8)

Definition: Uncontroversial · charade

A moderate view is a middle-of-the-road one that nobody takes offence at, which is what 'Uncontroversial' describes.

  • fashion → MODE mode is fashion, as in à la mode
  • degree → RATE rate is the degree or extent at which something happens

A safe, unremarkable university course in fashion.

'Degree' is the extent sense — the rate at which a thing runs — not a university qualification, which is where the fashion-course surface is pointing you.

26 across STRONG

Powerful ballad absorbs translator (6)

Definition: Powerful · container

A strong wind or a strong argument is a powerful one, so the swap is the everyday adjective sense of force.

  • ballad → SONG a ballad is a song; this is the piece that opens up
  • translator → TR tr. is the standard abbreviation for translator on a title page
  • absorbs — To absorb something is to take it right in, so the ballad closes over the two letters offered.

A stirring ballad drawing in the person who is translating it.

The surface has the ballad absorbing someone emotionally while the wordplay has it swallowing two letters, and tr. is the title-page abbreviation worth keeping.

28 across SEPTUM

Partition badly set up before beginning of meeting (6)

Definition: Partition · anagram + first letter

A septum is the dividing wall between two chambers, in the nose or the heart, so it partitions a space in the body.

  • set up → SETUP five letters waiting to be rearranged
  • beginning of meeting → M the first letter of meeting
  • badly → anagram indicator
  • badly — Something done badly comes out wrong, and letters set down wrong are letters out of order.
  • before — This one is positional: the reordered pair of words goes in front of the single letter that follows.
  • beginning of — The beginning of a word is its first letter, so meeting contributes one letter and no more.

A screen clumsily erected just before a meeting is due to start.

A septum is the wall of tissue dividing two cavities — the one in your nose is the familiar example — so 'Partition' is anatomical rather than a screen in an office.

29 across MISCOUNT

Error when married nobleman loses head (8)

Definition: Error · charade + deletion

A miscount is a specific error, the mistake made in counting — the sort a returning officer orders a recount over.

  • married → M m is the standard abbreviation for married on a form
  • nobleman loses head → ISCOUNT a VISCOUNT is a nobleman, and the V at his front is dropped
  • loses head — Losing your head means losing what sits on top, so the nobleman's first letter is the one that goes.

A husband of noble birth losing his composure and getting something wrong.

The joke is the beheading: a viscount, the rank just below an earl, literally loses his head here. 'Error' is a noun, so the answer has to be one too.

2 down AIR

Broadcast top recipe (3)

Definition: Broadcast · charade

To air a programme is to put it out on radio or television, the verb sense broadcasters use every day.

  • top → AI A1 means top quality, and the one in it is written as the Roman numeral
  • recipe → R R is the R of an old prescription, from Latin recipe, 'take'

A headline offering to share somebody's best recipe.

A1 comes from the shipping register's mark for a first-class vessel, and crosswords spell its one as I. Both halves here are conventions worth owning outright.

3 down FRESH

Cheeky underling returns to hotel (5)

Definition: Cheeky · reversal + charade

To be fresh with someone is to be impertinent to them, so 'Cheeky' is that slangy adjective sense, not newly made.

  • underling → FRES a SERF is a feudal underling, tied to the land, and here he is turned round
  • hotel → H hotel is H in the international radio alphabet
  • returns — Something that returns comes back the way it went, so the underling's letters are entered in the opposite order.

An impertinent junior being sent back to the hotel.

'Cheeky' is the informal adjective — being fresh with someone — while the surface reads the word as an errand. The serf is the medieval labourer, a crossword regular for a lowly worker.

4 down DRESS SHIRT

Part of DJ’s accompaniment? (5,5)

Definition: Part of DJ’s accompaniment? · cryptic definition

A dress shirt is the stiff-fronted white shirt worn with a dinner jacket, so it accompanies the DJ as part of the same outfit.

  • DJ’s accompaniment? → the reading the surface wants: the backing music a disc jockey puts on
  • Part of → the reading the setter meant: one item out of an outfit, because in Britain a DJ is a dinner jacket

Somebody asking which bit of a disc jockey's backing music is meant.

In Britain a DJ is a dinner jacket as often as a disc jockey, and what goes with one is the stiff white dress shirt. There is no separable wordplay: the whole clue is the definition.

5 down LIAISE

Connect electric current in renovated aisle (6)

Definition: Connect · anagram

To liaise is to keep two parties in touch with each other, the go-between sense of 'Connect' rather than plugging anything in.

  • aisle → AISLE five of the six letters, waiting to be reordered
  • electric current → I I is the physicist's symbol for current, and it joins the shuffle
  • renovated → anagram indicator
  • renovated — Renovating a building keeps every brick and rearranges them, which is exactly what happens to these letters.

Wiring being run through the aisle of a church that is being done up.

I for electric current comes up whenever a clue mentions amps or current, and it is worth learning once. 'Connect' here is the committee sense: to act as a go-between.

6 down GAME

It’s part of playing American football, say (4)

Definition: football, say · hidden word

American football is one game among many, so 'say' flags a definition by example rather than a synonym.

  • playing American → GAME four consecutive letters bridge the gap between the two words
  • part of — Being part of something means sitting inside it, so the letters are already present, unbroken, in the words that follow.

American football offered as an example of something you play.

'Say' marks a definition by example: the answer is the whole category and American football is one instance of it. 'Playing' is doing double duty, defining the surface and hiding the letters.

7 down NOTATIONS

Signs from states encircling capitals of Oklahoma and Texas (9)

Definition: Signs · container + first letters

Notations are the systems of written symbols used in music, chess or maths, so 'Signs' means marks on a page.

  • states → NATIONS states in the sense of sovereign countries; this is the piece that opens up
  • capitals of Oklahoma and Texas → OT the initial letter of each name, slipped in after the first letter of the piece above
  • encircling — To encircle is to go round the outside, so the word for states opens up and takes the other piece within it.
  • capitals of — The surface means capital cities, but a capital is also the big letter a name begins with — one from each name here.

Warnings coming out of American states that ring the capital cities of Oklahoma and Texas.

'Capitals' is the trap: the clue wants capital letters, not Oklahoma City and Austin. 'Signs' is the written-symbol sense, the marks used in music or maths.

8 down LEGERDEMAIN

Finesse stage opening in England with finale of Tour tomorrow in France (11)

Definition: Finesse · charade + first letter + last letter

Legerdemain is skilled trickery performed with the hands, and finesse is that same lightness of touch.

  • stage → LEG a leg is a stage of a journey or a relay
  • opening in England → E the letter England opens with
  • finale of Tour → R the last letter of Tour
  • tomorrow in France → DEMAIN demain is French for tomorrow
  • opening — The opening of a word is the letter it begins with, so England contributes just the one.
  • finale of — A finale is the last thing to happen, so what is wanted is the closing letter of Tour.

A cycling stage that starts in England, with the Tour's last day falling tomorrow in France.

Legerdemain is sleight of hand — literally 'light of hand' — so 'Finesse' is the conjuror's dexterity rather than a bridge player's play. The Tour de France really has begun in England, which is what makes the surface sit still.

12 down MISANTHROPE

She distrusts everyone working in metaphors! (11)

Definition: She distrusts everyone · anagram

A misanthrope distrusts and dislikes people in general, so 'everyone' is standing for the whole of humankind.

  • in metaphors → INMETAPHORS eleven letters, exactly the length the grid wants
  • working → anagram indicator
  • working — Something working is being put through its paces, and letters put through their paces come out in a new order.

A suspicious woman who takes against everybody who talks in figures of speech.

The exclamation mark papers over the seam: 'in metaphors' is not part of the description at all but the letters themselves, the little word 'in' included.

14 down CAREERISTS

Those intent on advancement air secrets ill-advisedly (10)

Definition: Those intent on advancement · anagram

Careerists are people who put getting on ahead of everything else, so the plural definition names them by that ambition.

  • air secrets → AIRSECRETS ten letters, matching the enumeration exactly
  • ill-advisedly → anagram indicator
  • ill-advisedly — Doing a thing ill-advisedly is doing it wrongly, and letters set down wrongly are letters out of their proper order.

Ambitious people leaking confidential information when they ought to keep quiet.

'Air' wants to be the instruction — to air something is to broadcast it — but it is fodder, and the word doing the shuffling waits until the very end of the clue.

16 down IMPERFECT

Tense, describing what was happening? (9)

Definition: Tense · double definition

The imperfect is a verb tense, and it is the one used for an action in progress in the past — what was happening.

  • Tense → the grammatical sense: one of the forms a verb can take
  • describing what was happening? → the job that tense does: reporting an action still going on in the past

An anxious person giving an account of events as they unfolded.

'Tense' reads first as nervous, and the second half quietly quotes itself: 'was happening' is written in the very tense the clue is defining, which is the setter's joke.

20 down MUSEUM

Employ in quiet place of study (6)

Definition: place of study · container

A museum keeps objects for scholars as well as visitors, the sense preserved in its Greek root mouseion, the hall of the Muses.

  • quiet → MUM keeping mum is keeping quiet; this is the piece that opens up
  • Employ → USE to use something is to employ it
  • in — 'in' puts one piece within the other: the word for employ goes inside the word for quiet.

Taking staff on in a hushed reading room.

'Quiet place of study' reads as one phrase, and it sounds like a library — but only the last three words are the definition, and the first is wordplay.

23 down CURIO

Regularly scour port city for unusual object (5)

Definition: unusual object · alternate letters + charade

A curio is a small object kept precisely because it is odd or rare, which is the curiosity 'unusual object' describes.

  • Regularly scour → CU every second letter of scour: s-C-o-U-r
  • port city → RIO Rio de Janeiro is a Brazilian port, and its short name is a crossword staple
  • Regularly — Letters taken regularly come at a fixed interval, and the interval here is every second letter of the word that follows.

Someone regularly combing a port city for something odd to buy.

'Scour' is the wrong sort of searching: it is not telling you to hunt, it is handing over two of its own letters. The port city is a named one, not a general description.

24 down CALM

Serenity derived from mathematical musings (4)

Definition: Serenity · hidden word

Calm is the noun for an untroubled state, and serenity is that same settled quiet, so the two swap as nouns.

  • mathematical musings → CALM four consecutive letters straddle the space between the two words
  • derived from — What is derived from something is taken out of it, so the letters come straight out of the words as written.

Peace of mind found in an afternoon of thinking about mathematics.

Hidden words hide best across a word break, and a long adjective like 'mathematical' is chosen to make you read for meaning instead of counting letters.

27 down NUN

On reflection, this sister doesn’t change (3)

Definition: sister · palindrome

Nuns are addressed as Sister, so 'sister' names one by her religious title rather than by any family relation.

  • this sister → the definition end of the clue: a woman in a religious order
  • On reflection → the wordplay end: three letters that read the same in either direction, which is all the help offered
  • On reflection — A reflection is a mirror image, so the word is to be read from its far end as well as its near one.
  • doesn’t change — Read the other way it comes out identical, which is the whole instruction: nothing turns into anything else.

Second thoughts about a sister who never alters her ways.

NUN reads the same backwards, and 'doesn't change' is the setter saying so out loud. Nothing is turned into anything else, so there are no letters to hand over.
