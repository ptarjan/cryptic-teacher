Across

1 across LIMP

Fifty pence 'entertaining' Everyman's spent (4)

Definition: spent · container

spent → LIMP: the sense of a runner at the finish, drained of all stiffness rather than merely tired.

  • Fifty → L L is fifty in Roman numerals
  • Everyman's → IM Everyman is the setter, and a setter speaking of himself says I, so Everyman's is I'm
  • pence → P p is the coin's own abbreviation, as in 50p
  • entertaining — To entertain a guest is to have them inside the house, so what follows is kept within rather than set beside.

Everyman signs his own clues most weeks, and the trick is always the same one: his name is standing in for the word I.

4 across LISP

Condition in which frost becomes frothed? (4)

Definition: Condition in which frost becomes frothed? · cryptic definition

The whole clue is the definition: the speech habit turns every s into a th, so frost leaves the mouth sounding like frothed.

  • frost becomes frothed → read as weather it goes nowhere, since a hard freeze does not turn into foam
  • Condition in which → not a state of the air but of the mouth, and the two words are the same word said two ways

There are no letters to assemble. Say the first word with the speech fault the answer names and what comes out is the second word.

8 across TOOT ONE'S OWN HORN

Swagger, as sax player will do (4,4,3,4)

Definition: Swagger · double definition

Swagger names the phrase's boastful sense; the other half is the literal one, a player blowing an instrument that belongs to him.

  • Swagger → the figurative sense: to boast, to talk yourself up in company
  • as sax player will do → the literal reading, where the horn is the instrument and the tooting is simply playing it

The joke is that a saxophonist does this without boasting at all: the instrument really is his, and blowing it is the job.

11 across ROCKERS

Horses for children who ride motorbikes? (7)

Definition: Horses for children · double definition

Horses for children → ROCKERS as rocking horses; who ride motorbikes → ROCKERS, the sixties bikers who fought mods at seaside resorts.

  • Horses for children → a rocker is a rocking horse, the nursery toy mounted on curved runners
  • who ride motorbikes → the leather-jacketed bikers of the sixties, the mods' opposite number

Both senses are British and both are dated, which is the setter's joke: a nursery toy and a seaside gang, and one word for the pair.

12 across ENDLESS

Like Billy-No-Mates, Friday's gone - going on forever (7)

Definition: going on forever · deletion

going on forever → ENDLESS: having no end to reach, in time or in patience, which is a stronger claim than merely long.

  • Like Billy-No-Mates → ENDLESS Billy No-Mates has no friends at all, so being like him is being FRIENDLESS, and seven of those letters survive
  • Friday's → FRI Fri is the diary's abbreviation for Friday, and those three letters come off the front
  • gone — What has gone is no longer there, so the letters named beside it are simply never written down.

The dash is doing the work of an equals sign: everything to the left of it builds the word and everything to the right defines it.

13 across AMOROUSLY

Morals you declined in licentious manner (9)

Definition: in licentious manner · anagram

in licentious manner → AMOROUSLY: acting out of desire rather than affection, the adverb wanted when the love is the improper kind.

  • Morals you → AMOROUSLY the nine letters of MORALS YOU, rearranged
  • declined — A thing that has declined has slipped from the state it was in, and these letters have slipped out of the order they were given.

The surface says somebody turned down an offer of principles; the wordplay says the principles themselves came apart.

14 across DRAWN

Depicted as haggard (5)

Definition: Depicted · double definition

Depicted → DRAWN in the sense of sketched; haggard → DRAWN as a face is, tightened and gaunt with strain.

  • Depicted → the artist's sense: set down in pencil or ink
  • haggard → the face's sense: pulled tight and hollow by strain or illness

Both come off the same verb to pull — a line pulled across paper, a face pulled thin — which is why one word can carry them.

15 across CADET

Military trainee's dishonourable type - and French (5)

Definition: Military trainee · charade

Military trainee → CADET: a young officer under instruction, still learning the trade rather than holding a commission in it.

  • dishonourable type → CAD a cad is a man who behaves dishonourably, the old word for one
  • and French → ET et is the French for and

A clue in two halves with the join hidden inside an apostrophe. Naming a language after a word is the standard way of asking for its translation.

16 across ANTIPODES

Australian pointed rudely, son - here? (9)

Definition: here? · anagram + charade

here? → ANTIPODES: the region diametrically opposite your own, so which end of the world it names depends on who is asking.

  • Australian → A A is the single-letter abbreviation for Australia
  • pointed → NTIPODE the seven letters of POINTED, shuffled
  • son → S S is the standard abbreviation for son
  • rudely — To do a thing rudely is to do it with no respect for how it ought to be done, and letters handled that way come back out of order.

The dash sets the definition apart, and 'here' is asked from wherever the reader happens to be sitting — each hemisphere is the other's far side.

19 across ORINOCO

Orion arranged company for river to the Atlantic (7)

Definition: river to the Atlantic · anagram + charade

river to the Atlantic → ORINOCO: the great Venezuelan waterway, emptying through a huge delta into the ocean's western edge.

  • Orion → ORINO the five letters of the hunter's name, reordered
  • company → CO Co is the abbreviation on any firm's letterhead
  • arranged — To arrange is to settle things into an order of your own choosing, which is not the order they came in.

A constellation turns into a waterway with one letter moved, and the definition is exact rather than decorative: this one does not feed the Amazon.

21 across ROAD HOG

Primarily 'ravenous', offensively avaricious driver hindering others gallingly? (7)

Definition: 'ravenous', offensively avaricious driver hindering others gallingly? · first letters + &lit

The whole clue reads straight: a driver who takes more than his share of the road and holds up everyone stuck behind.

  • 'ravenous', offensively avaricious driver hindering others gallingly → ROADHOG the initials of the seven words in order: ravenous, offensively, avaricious, driver, hindering, others, gallingly
  • Primarily — What is primary stands first, so what is taken from each word is the letter at its head.

Seven words, seven initials, and the sentence still describes the answer exactly. The quotation marks round the first word are pure surface decoration.

22 across CLEAN AS A WHISTLE

Spotless: all hesitance was resolved (5,2,1,7)

Definition: Spotless · anagram

Spotless → CLEAN AS A WHISTLE: the idiom for something with nothing whatever on it, used of a criminal record as readily as of a kitchen.

  • all hesitance was → CLEANASAWHISTLE the fifteen letters of ALL HESITANCE WAS, rebuilt
  • resolved — To resolve something is to break it down into the parts it is made of, and parts broken out can be put back in any order.

The colon stands in for an equals sign, so the one word before it is the meaning and everything after it is the machinery.

23 across SPUD

Small dish that's sweet potato (4)

Definition: potato · charade

potato → SPUD: the everyday informal name for one, borrowed from the spade-like tool once used to dig them up.

  • Small → S S is the clothing label's abbreviation for small
  • dish that's sweet → PUD pud is short for pudding, the sweet course at the end of a meal

Two abbreviations inside four squares. 'Sweet' as a noun is the British word for dessert, which is what makes the pudding reading fair.

24 across PAYS

After line cut, theatre work is profitable (4)

Definition: is profitable · deletion

is profitable → PAYS: the intransitive sense in 'crime doesn't pay', where the activity itself returns more than it costs.

  • theatre work → PAYS the work put on in a theatre is PLAYS, and with the L cut out these four letters remain
  • line → L l is the standard abbreviation for line, as in a poem's l.4
  • cut — To cut a thing is to take a piece off it, and what is taken off here is the single letter named just before.

An actor's line and a printed line share one abbreviation, and the theatrical surface is built to make you read the wrong one.

2 down IRONED OUT

Organised routine do, and cleared up (6,3)

Definition: cleared up · anagram

cleared up → IRONED OUT: the laundry metaphor, where a wrinkle in a plan is pressed flat until nothing catches on it.

  • routine do → IRONEDOUT the nine letters of ROUTINE DO, set out afresh
  • Organised — To organise is to put things into an order somebody chose, and the order chosen is never the one they arrived in.

The surface is an office party being planned, and the definition is the last two words, which read as more of the same sentence.

3 down PRESSES

Urges drinks (7)

Definition: Urges · double definition

Urges → PRESSES, as pressing a point is urging it; drinks → PRESSES, a pressé being a drink of pressed fruit.

  • Urges → to press somebody is to urge them, as in pressing a guest to stay for one more
  • drinks → the other sense is the pressé, the bottled drink of pressed fruit that turns up as elderflower or ginger

Two words, two dictionaries, and no wordplay to hunt for. The second sense comes from the French for pressed and reached English through a menu.

4 down LOOSELY

In vague sense, how post-diet clothes fit (7)

Definition: In vague sense · double definition

Both halves are one adverb: speaking in a vague sense is speaking without a tight fit to the facts, exactly as slack clothing hangs.

  • In vague sense → the figurative use: an approximate way of speaking, where the words are not meant to be pinned down precisely
  • how post-diet clothes fit → the literal use: slack, with room to spare between the cloth and the wearer

One adverb pulling in two directions — imprecision and slackness — which are the same idea applied to words and to cloth.

5 down SYNOD

Vacuous silly academic getting promotion in ecclesiastical council (5)

Definition: ecclesiastical council · charade + outer letters + reversal

ecclesiastical council → SYNOD: the assembly of clergy that governs a church, that body's own name for its parliament.

  • silly → SY silly hollowed out keeps only its first and last letters
  • academic → NOD an academic is a don, and raised up the column DON reads NOD
  • Vacuous — Something vacuous is hollow, empty through the middle, so the word beside it keeps only the letters at its two ends.
  • getting promotion — A promotion lifts you up the ladder, and in a down clue a word lifted up is read from the bottom.

'Don' is the crossword's word for any academic, Oxbridge or not, and being three letters it nearly always arrives turned round or buried.

6 down STERNA

We hear of harder breastbones (6)

Definition: breastbones · homophone

breastbones → STERNA: the sternum is the flat bone down the front of the chest, and anatomy keeps its Latin plural.

  • harder → STERNA harder is sterner, and said aloud that word lands on the Latin plural of sternum
  • We hear — What we hear is not what is written down, so the match the clue wants is a match of sound alone.

English lets the r at the end of sterner go unsounded, which is the whole reason a comparative adjective and an anatomical plural can share a noise.

7 down UNISON

Agreement accepted by leprechaun is ongoing (6)

Definition: Agreement · hidden word

Agreement → UNISON: singing in unison is everybody on one note, and the word carried that sense across to people who think alike.

  • leprechaun is ongoing → UNISON the run crosses three words: the tail of 'leprechaun', the whole of 'is', and the front of 'ongoing'
  • accepted by — To accept a thing is to take it in and hold it, so the letters are already sitting inside the words that follow.

A hidden answer that spans three words is the hardest kind to spot, because the eye reads whole words before it reads letters.

9 down ON CLOUD NINE

Happy - formerly - to take in brash nephew at home (2,5,4)

Definition: Happy · container + charade

Happy → ON CLOUD NINE: the idiom for elation so complete that it lifts you off the ground, far past merely pleased.

  • formerly → ONCE formerly is once, and it opens up after its third letter to make room
  • brash → LOUD a brash person is a loud one
  • nephew → N nephew is shortened to its own first letter here, the way a family tree writes it
  • at home → IN somebody at home is in, which is the crossword's favourite two letters for it
  • to take in — A household that takes somebody in puts them up within its walls, so everything named next goes inside the word before.

Three separate pieces go into the one gap, which is why the dashes are worth noticing: they fence 'formerly' off as the word being opened.

10 down OPEN-AND-SHUT

Dancing upon the sand? That's easily solved (4-3-4)

Definition: easily solved · anagram

easily solved → OPEN-AND-SHUT: said of a case whose evidence settles it at once, leaving no argument to be had.

  • upon the sand → OPENANDSHUT the eleven letters of UPON THE SAND, re-laid
  • Dancing — Dancers keep swapping places with one another, and letters set dancing do exactly that to their order.

A beach scene turns into a courtroom verdict. The enumeration's hyphens are the clue that the answer is one adjective and not three words.

14 down DEPRAVITY

Sinfulness in drunken VE Day trip (9)

Definition: Sinfulness · anagram

Sinfulness → DEPRAVITY: moral corruption of the settled kind, a character gone bad rather than one lapse.

  • VE Day trip → DEPRAVITY the nine letters of VE DAY TRIP, reordered
  • drunken — Somebody drunken cannot walk a straight line, and letters in that state will not hold the line they were given either.

VE Day is the eighth of May 1945, so the surface is a street party outing — exactly the innocent picture the definition is not describing.

15 down CHOICE

Delicious serving of gazpacho I celebrated (6)

Definition: Delicious · hidden word

Delicious → CHOICE: the grocer's sense of picked out as the best of the crop, which is how a choice cut earns its name.

  • gazpacho I celebrated → CHOICE the run crosses three words: the tail of 'gazpacho', the whole of 'I', and the front of 'celebrated'
  • serving of — A serving is a helping taken out of something larger, so only part of what follows is wanted.

A one-letter word in the middle is the best cover a hidden answer can have, because the eye treats a lone 'I' almost as punctuation.

16 down AROUSED

Revelled topless, titillated (7)

Definition: titillated · deletion

titillated → AROUSED: a plain synonym at the milder end, to arouse being to stir feeling, with titillate naming the kind stirred.

  • Revelled → AROUSED revelled is CAROUSED, and with the C at its head gone these seven letters remain
  • topless — The top of a down entry is the letter standing at its head, and topless says that letter is missing.

Carouse for revel is a word crosswords keep alive almost single-handed, and here the surface leans on the racier sense of all three words at once.

17 down THROW UP

Reveal ultimately stylish line of stitching included in source of wool (5,2)

Definition: Reveal · container + charade + last letter

Reveal → THROW UP: the sense in 'the search threw up new evidence' — to bring something to the surface where it can be seen.

  • source of wool → TUP a tup is a ram, and the fleece comes off him
  • stylish → H the letter stylish finishes on
  • line of stitching → ROW stitches are worked in rows
  • ultimately — What comes ultimately comes at the very end, so the letter wanted is the one the word finishes on.
  • included in — Whatever is included in a thing sits within its boundaries, so the letters before it go inside the word after it.

'Tup' for a ram is worth banking: setters reach for it whenever they want a T and a P with a gap open between them.

18 down SAGGED

Good to visit Impressionist retrospective that's hung slackly (6)

Definition: hung slackly · container + reversal

hung slackly → SAGGED: what a rope or a hemline does when nothing holds it taut, sinking in the middle under its own weight.

  • Impressionist → SAGED the Impressionist is Degas, and his name looked back over gives these five letters
  • Good → G G is the standard abbreviation for good, as on a school report
  • to visit — A visitor comes inside the house, so the letter doing the visiting goes within the word it calls on.
  • retrospective — A retrospective looks back over what came before, and a word looked back over is read the other way round.

A painter's name is raw material as often as it is a person, and 'retrospective' is doing gallery duty on the surface and letter duty underneath.

20 down OWN UP

Confess painful response as joke backfires (3,2)

Definition: Confess · charade + reversal

Confess → OWN UP: the phrasal verb for admitting a fault, where own means acknowledge as yours rather than possess.

  • painful response → OW the noise you make when something hurts
  • joke → NUP a joke made on words is a pun, turned round here
  • backfires — Something that backfires comes out of the wrong end, and a word sent that way is read from its last letter first.

The surface is a gag falling flat; the wordplay has the same word doing it literally, which is about as neat as a five-letter clue gets.
