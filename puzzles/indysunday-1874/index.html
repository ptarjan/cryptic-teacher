Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,874

Setter: Coot · Published: Sunday 25 January 2026 · Difficulty: Gentle (harder than 1% of the puzzles here) · Grid: 15×15

Across

1 across CRUMPET

Cake and stiff drink fed to cold animal (7)

Definition: Cake · container

  • cold → C c is the standard abbreviation for cold, the letter on the tap
  • stiff drink → RUM rum is a strong spirit, the stiff drink of the sailor's tot
  • animal → PET a pet is an animal kept at home

The surface lays out a tea table, so 'cold animal' reads as a shivering creature. It is two separate pieces of wordplay that happen to sit side by side.

5 across TEA TREE

Oil producer's area of operation? Hour away east (3,4)

Definition: Oil producer · deletion + charade

  • area of operation → THEATRE a theatre of operations is the whole region a campaign is fought over, and the question mark nods at the surgical sort too
  • Hour → H h is the standard abbreviation for hour
  • east → E e is the compass abbreviation for east

The oil comes from an Australian shrub of the myrtle family, nothing to do with the tea in your cup: only the name overlaps.

9 across CORKSCREW

Irish city's workers take indirect route? (9)

Definition: take indirect route · charade

  • Irish city → CORK Cork is a city and county in the south of Ireland
  • 's → S the possessive apostrophe-s hands over its own letter
  • workers → CREW a crew is a body of workers, on a ship or a film set

The definition is a verb, which is easy to miss when the answer is such a familiar noun. The question mark marks the stretch.

10 across LEGIT

Reasonable to run away (5)

Definition: Reasonable · double definition

  • Reasonable → the slang clipping of legitimate: above board, within the rules, nothing to answer for
  • to run away → split the five letters after the third and you have the British phrase for taking to your heels

The enumeration is the disguise: (5) hides the fact that the second definition wants the letters read as two words rather than one.

11 across BABOON

Monkey bars cut down, abuse received (6)

Definition: Monkey · deletion + container

  • bars → BANS to bar something is to ban it
  • abuse → BOO a boo is the noise a crowd throws at a performer it wants to abuse

'Monkey bars' is the trap: it reads as the playground climbing frame, but the definition stops after one word and 'bars' starts the wordplay.

12 across ORIENT

Football team without wingers got grief and, ultimately, lost (6)

Definition: Football team · charade + middle letters + last letter

  • got → O the middle letter, once the outer pair is dropped
  • grief → RIE the middle three, on the same instruction
  • and → N the middle letter again, from a three-letter word
  • lost → T the last letter of that word

'Without wingers' looks like a note on the team's formation. It is the instruction, and it is applied to three ordinary words in a row.

14 across LASH

Leather eye protector (4)

Definition: Leather · double definition

  • Leather → the verb sense: to leather someone is to thrash them with a strap
  • eye protector → the noun sense, and only half a word of it: the fringe of hair on the lid that keeps dust out

Four words, no wordplay, and the two definitions are different parts of speech: a verb for beating and a noun for hair. Both readings are ordinary English.

16 across ROSE

Some revolting eyesore that's attached to the ceiling (4)

Definition: attached to the ceiling · hidden word + reversal

  • eyesore → ESOR four consecutive letters sitting in the middle of that word, printed the wrong way round

'Some revolting eyesore' reads as one phrase about something ugly. It is two separate instructions and a word to take the letters from.

18 across MOSEL

Writer left to tour huge wine region (5)

Definition: wine region · container

  • Writer → ME the person writing the clue is the setter, and setters refer to themselves this way
  • left → L l is the standard abbreviation for left
  • huge → OS os is outsize, the clothing label for the largest size

'Writer' as the setter talking about himself is a convention worth memorising: it turns up whenever a clue seems to need an author nobody can name.

20 across AORTA

Schedule has first couple returning on American vessel (5)

Definition: vessel · reversal + charade

  • American → A a is the standard abbreviation for American
  • Schedule → ROTA a rota is the schedule saying who is on duty and when

'American vessel' promises a ship and the surface sails on. The vessel is anatomical, and 'American' has quietly been detached to give a single letter.

23 across AIRY

Fresh drink supplier opening late (4)

Definition: Fresh · deletion

  • drink supplier → DAIRY a dairy supplies milk, and milk is a drink

'Fresh drink' wants to be read as one phrase, a cold beer or a new round. The definition is the single word, and the wordplay starts at 'drink supplier'.

25 across THEE

You once learnt he 'entertains' nurses (4)

Definition: You once · hidden word

  • learnt he 'entertains' → THEE four consecutive letters running from the tail of the first word, through the second and into the third

'Learnt he' has the ring of Bible English, matching the archaic answer so neatly that you stop seeing the letters running straight through it.

27 across QUANGO

First half of specified number leave semi-public body (6)

Definition: semi-public body · charade + deletion

  • specified number → QUANTITY a quantity is a specified number or amount of something
  • leave → GO to go is to leave

The word is an acronym — quasi-autonomous non-governmental organisation — and has been a staple of British political grumbling since the 1970s.

28 across WANGLE

Manipulate old laundry equipment, overturning the front (6)

Definition: Manipulate · substitution

  • old laundry equipment → MANGLE a mangle is the hand-cranked pair of rollers that squeezed the water out of the washing
  • overturning the front → W an M stood on its head is a W

The instruction turns a letter over rather than a word, and only a few letters survive that treatment. M and W are the classic pair.

32 across AMISS

Novelist's out of order (5)

Definition: out of order · charade

  • Novelist → AMIS Kingsley and his son Martin were both novelists of the first rank
  • 's → S the possessive apostrophe-s gives up its own letter

The apostrophe-s reads as 'is' in the surface — the novelist is out of order — while the wordplay quietly takes the letter itself.

33 across SAILBOARD

Rower adlibs desperately going around in unfamiliar craft? (9)

Definition: unfamiliar craft · anagram + container

  • adlibs → ADLIBS six letters handed over as printed, ready to be rearranged
  • Rower → OAR an oar is the blade a rower pulls, so the word stands in for the person by naming their tool

'Rower' looks like the person the surface is about. It is three letters standing for the thing they pull, which is the commonest way a setter hides a short word.

34 across FENLAND

Space to tuck into pudding, the 3rd for Jodie Marsh (7)

Definition: Marsh · container + charade + middle letter

  • pudding → FLAN a flan is a baked open tart, served as a pudding
  • Space → EN an en is a printer's space, the width of the letter n
  • Jodie → D the third of that name's five letters

Jodie Marsh is a British media personality, so the two names read as one woman and hide the fact that only the first of them is wordplay.

35 across RIBBONS

Strips of meat, good French cuts (7)

Definition: Strips · container

  • meat → RIBS ribs are a cut of meat, taken off the animal's side
  • good French → BON bon is the French for good

'Cuts' works twice over: in the surface it is a butcher's word sitting beside the meat, and in the wordplay it is the knife going in.

Down

1 down COCK

Male rugby player initially doubled in value? (4)

Definition: Male · substitution

  • rugby player → LOCK a lock is a second-row forward, the tall one who packs down behind the front row
  • initially doubled in value → C that leading L is fifty in Roman numerals, and a hundred is written as one letter

'Doubled in value' sounds like money, and the surface invites you to price a player. The sum is arithmetic done on a Roman numeral instead.

2 down UPROARS

Expert breaking suspect exposed bursar's rackets (7)

Definition: rackets · anagram + container + middle letters

  • bursar's → URSA the four letters left in the middle once the outer pair of that word goes
  • Expert → PRO a pro is a professional, expert enough to be paid for it

The surface is an interrogation, so 'rackets' reads as criminal schemes. The definition wants the other sense of the word entirely: plain noise.

3 down PESTO

Here we find Basil and Coot asleep occasionally, having retired (5)

Definition: Here we find Basil · alternate letters + reversal

  • Coot asleep → OTSEP the second, fourth, sixth, eighth and tenth letters of those two words, in that order

Two capital letters that look like two men: Basil is the herb, not Fawlty, and Coot is the setter signing his own clue and lending it letters.

4 down TORONTO

City suffering torn parts excessively (7)

Definition: City · anagram

  • torn → TORN four letters handed over exactly as printed
  • excessively → TOO too much is more than enough, so this is the everyday word for excessively

'Torn' reads as the instruction to tear the letters up. It is the fodder, and the words on either side of it are what do the tearing.

5 down TOW ROPE

What one lacking drive might need to move forwards? (3,4)

Definition: What one lacking drive might need to move forwards · cryptic definition

  • one lacking drive → the surface offers a person with no motivation, someone who cannot get going; read it of a vehicle and 'drive' is the engine's power reaching the wheels
  • might need to move forwards → for the idle person this would be a pep talk; for the dead car it has to be a physical link to something in front that is still moving

There is no wordplay to find here. Both halves of the sentence read twice over, and the answer is the object that makes the second reading true.

6 down ABLEISM

Discrimination - is blame misdirected? (7)

Definition: Discrimination · anagram

  • is blame → ISBLAME seven letters handed straight over from the clue

The surface asks whether fault is being aimed at the wrong people, which sits very close to what the answer itself describes. That is neat setting, not coincidence.

7 down REGENTS

They take temporary charge of toilets (7)

Definition: They take temporary charge · charade

  • of → RE re means about or concerning, the two letters a memo heading uses for 'of'
  • toilets → GENTS in Britain the men's public lavatory is signed the Gents

'Of' is not joinery here but a piece of the build, which is what lets the definition stop dead at 'charge' with the sentence still reading smoothly.

8 down EATS

Maybe hoovers furniture top to bottom (4)

Definition: Maybe hoovers · cycling

  • furniture → SEAT a seat is a piece of furniture

In Britain any vacuum cleaner is a hoover, and to hoover food is to clear it fast and greedily. That slang sense is what the definition leans on.

13 down BLUE

Conservative affected by wind when speaking (4)

Definition: Conservative · homophone

  • affected by wind → BLUE what the wind has done to something is blown it, and the past tense of blow is the word you say aloud

A homophone this short lives on its surface, and 'affected by wind' reads as a sail, a tree or a bad dinner long before it reads as a past tense.

14 down LEAR

He wrote "ladlecum", "enkoopia", "attery" and "runcible" originally (4)

Definition: He wrote · first letters

  • "ladlecum" → L the opening letter of the first invented word
  • "enkoopia" → E the opening letter of the second
  • "attery" → A the opening letter of the third, which describes a squash in one of the nonsense poems
  • "runcible" → R the opening letter of the fourth, the best known of them, as in the runcible spoon

All four are Edward Lear's own coinages, and several of the creatures from his poem The Quangle Wangle's Hat are hiding elsewhere in this grid.

15 down HAT

Covers removed from that end for fast bowler? (3)

Definition: bowler · charade + middle letters + last letter

  • that → HA the two letters in the middle, once the outer pair goes
  • fast → T the final letter of that word

The surface is a cricket ground with the covers coming off before a quick bowler runs in. A bowler is also headgear, and this puzzle is built round a Lear poem with one in the title.

17 down SKI

Sports equipment? Inside - ask Ingrid (3)

Definition: Sports equipment · hidden word

  • ask Ingrid → SKI three consecutive letters straddling the gap between those two words

The question mark admits this is one piece of equipment out of thousands, and the dash is punctuation the wordplay has no use for at all.

19 down OUT

Solicitor covering face in public (3)

Definition: in public · deletion

  • Solicitor → TOUT a tout solicits custom in the street, selling tickets at inflated prices near the ground

The surface puts a lawyer in a mask in a crowd. A tout is the British street seller who solicits, and the definition is the sense of open knowledge.

21 down REUNION

District using country club for grand social gathering (7)

Definition: social gathering · substitution

  • District → REGION a region is a district, an area marked off within a country
  • country club → UN the United Nations is the club almost every country belongs to
  • grand → G g is the standard abbreviation for grand, a thousand pounds

The surface hires a country club for a big do. The phrase is really the club that countries join, and 'grand' is money rather than scale.

22 down AMNESIA

Agitated international seaman recalling difficulty (7)

Definition: recalling difficulty · anagram

  • international → I i is the standard abbreviation for international
  • seaman → SEAMAN six letters handed over as printed

'Recalling' looks like an instruction to turn something round, sitting where an indicator would sit. Both words are the definition, and the only instruction is at the front.

23 down AROUSED

Revel topless, getting director excited (7)

Definition: excited · deletion + charade

  • Revel → CAROUSE to carouse is to revel, drinking noisily and at length
  • director → D d is the standard abbreviation for director

The surface has a stripped-off party-goer and a delighted boss, and that picture is exactly what stops 'topless' looking like the instruction it is.

24 down ROWDIER

Worried Barking is relatively rough (7)

Definition: relatively rough · anagram

  • Worried → WORRIED seven letters handed over as printed

Barking is also a district of east London, which is what makes the capital letter look innocent. 'Relatively' is the quiet signal that the answer is a comparative.

26 down HALF A MO

Brief time in hospital by Texan mission, holding strong (4,1,2)

Definition: Brief time · container + charade

  • hospital → H h is the standard abbreviation for hospital, the letter on the road sign
  • Texan mission → ALAMO the Alamo is the old Spanish mission at San Antonio, besieged in 1836
  • strong → F f is forte in music, the instruction to play strongly

The answer is British for 'wait a second', a spoken phrase rather than a word, which is why the enumeration comes in three odd little pieces.

29 down NABOB

'Moneybags' Nick admits personal hygiene problem (5)

Definition: Moneybags · container

  • Nick → NAB to nab is to nick, in the sense of grabbing or pinching something
  • personal hygiene problem → BO BO is body odour, the polite two letters for it

The word came home from India with the officials who made fortunes there, and it has meant conspicuous wealth ever since. The quotation marks flag the nickname.

30 down CALF

Tip from Chris Ramsey for youngster (4)

Definition: youngster · charade + first letter

  • Chris → C the first letter of that name
  • Ramsey → ALF Alf Ramsey managed England to the World Cup in 1966

'Chris Ramsey' reads as one person. The clue takes a letter from the first name and a famous football manager from the second.

31 down ODES

Poems most peculiar, but not every 3rd (4)

Definition: Poems · deletion

  • most peculiar → ODDEST the superlative of odd, six letters to count through

The trap is reading 'most peculiar' as an anagram signal. It is the fodder, and the only instruction in the clue comes after the comma.
