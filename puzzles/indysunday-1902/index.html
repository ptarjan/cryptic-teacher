Independent Sunday Cryptic Crossword No 1,902

Across

1 across HEN NIGHT

Wedding party so nearly having start delayed (3,5)

Definition: Wedding party · charade + cycling

  • so → THEN 'so' in the sense of consequently - do this, so that follows - is THEN
  • nearly → NIGH nigh is the old word for nearly, as in the end is nigh

Write the two words out and the opening T is simply sent to the back of the queue. Nothing in the wedding surface suggests a letter is running late.

6 across BECOME

Turn odd bits of bomber jacket green (6)

Definition: Turn · container + alternate letters

  • bomber → BME the first, third and fifth letters of bomber
  • green → ECO eco is the everyday shorthand for green in the environmental sense

'Bomber jacket' is one garment on the surface and two separate instructions underneath, and green here is not a colour but a cause.

9 across SIR WALTER SCOTT

19C novelist amends chapter in Curious case of Oliver Twist (3,6,5)

Definition: 19C novelist · anagram + outer letters

  • amends → ALTERS to amend a text is to alter it
  • chapter → C c. is the abbreviation for chapter in a legal or biblical reference
  • case of Oliver → OR the outside of Oliver, the letter at each end
  • Twist → TWIST the second half of the title joins the pot as five plain letters

The italics dress the last four words up as a Dickens title, and that is the misdirection: Oliver Twist has nothing to do with the answer beyond lending letters.

10 across CONSPIRE

Short cone and tall cone share an end (8)

Definition: share an end · charade + deletion

  • Short cone → CON cone with its tail clipped
  • tall cone → SPIRE the tall cone standing on a church tower is its spire

Two cones, two different jobs: the first is a word to trim, the second a picture to name. And 'end' in the definition means aim, not tail.

12 across COOLLY

Company outing abandoned by Jack with indifference (6)

Definition: with indifference · charade + deletion

  • Company → CO Co is the abbreviation tacked onto a firm's name
  • outing → JOLLY a works outing at someone else's expense is a jolly
  • Jack → J J is the jack in a pack of cards, and it is the letter that deserts

'Jolly' as a noun for a free day out is a very British sense, and the card-school jack is what leaves it. The surface reads like office gossip throughout.

13 across MEGA

Bumper possibly clipped by old lady (4)

Definition: Bumper · container

  • old lady → MA ma is the old lady of the family
  • possibly → EG eg introduces an example, which is the job 'possibly' is doing

Bumper as an adjective - a bumper crop, a bumper issue - is the sense wanted, and the car the surface parks in front of you is there to keep you off it.

14 across ADDITIONAL

Bill wandering into Aldi further (10)

Definition: further · charade + anagram

  • Bill → AD a bill pasted on a wall is an ad
  • into Aldi → INTOALDI the eight letters that get shuffled

'Bill' is the trap: it looks like a person, or the tab at the end of a meal, and it is the poster sense that pays. The supermarket keeps the surface shopping.

16 across HAVE IT MADE

Enjoy security? Commission bespoke tech (4,2,4)

Definition: Enjoy security? · container

  • Commission bespoke → HAVEMADE to commission a bespoke thing is to have it made to your own order, and those two words open up to take whatever is being ordered
  • tech → IT IT, information technology, is what an office calls its tech

The wordplay is the answer's own three words in their own order, which is the joke: one reading is a phrase about comfort, the other a plain instruction to a tailor.

19 across I SAY

Jesus lives forever (1,3)

Definition: Jesus · charade

  • lives → IS he lives, he is - the verb in its plainest form
  • forever → AY ay is the old word for ever, kept alive in the phrase 'for ay'

Both halves are exclamations rather than statements, which is the seam: a startled 'Jesus!' and a very English 'I say!' do the same job in a sentence.

21 across FABRIC

Grand, opulent, almost plush? (6)

Definition: plush? · charade + deletion

  • Grand → FAB grand and fab are both slang for wonderful
  • opulent → RIC opulent is rich, with the final letter left off

Three adjectives in a row read as one breathless description of a room, so nothing marks where the definition starts. Plush is velvet's shaggier cousin.

23 across CROSSBAR

Snappy save that stops high shot? (8)

Definition: that stops high shot? · charade

  • Snappy → CROSS a snappy reply is a cross one, given with a bite
  • save → BAR save in the sense of except - all save one, all bar one

Football runs right through the surface, and 'save' is the word doing the misleading: it is the preposition meaning except, not the goalkeeper's.

24 across DIG ONE'S HEELS IN

Refuse to move bowl containing dead fish from outside (3,4,5,2)

Definition: Refuse to move · container + charade

  • bowl → DISH a bowl on the table is a dish
  • dead → GONE someone who is gone is dead
  • fish → EELS eels are the fish, in the plural
  • from outside → IN you come in from outside, so the direction being named is in

None of the pieces line up with the words of the answer: the bowl is split in half and the phrase's own gaps fall in the middle of the chunks.

26 across ONE-TWO

Punches landed excessively on your ear (3-3)

Definition: Punches · charade + homophone

  • landed → WON a fight landed is a fight won
  • excessively → TOO too, as in too much, is excessively
  • landed excessively → ONE TWO said aloud, WON TOO is heard as the pair of numbers

Boxing on the surface, counting underneath. The hyphenated enumeration helps: it promises two short words, which numbers supply and a match report does not.

27 across ROYALIST

Suspect solitary enemy of Republican (8)

Definition: enemy of Republican · anagram

  • solitary → SOLITARY the eight letters of solitary are the whole of the raw material

The capital R makes it look like American politics; the quarrel being named is the much older one between crowns and republics.

2 down EPISODE

Notable experience swimming so deep around island (7)

Definition: Notable experience · anagram + container

  • so deep → SODEEP the two words hand over six letters to be swum about
  • island → I I is the map abbreviation for island

'Notable' is doing quiet work: not every experience qualifies, only one that stands out far enough to be told afterwards as a story.

3 down NOW

Pig's head turning 180 degrees unlike before (3)

Definition: unlike before · substitution

  • Pig → SOW the pig in the sty is a sow
  • head → N the head of SOW is S, which is south on any compass, and the bearing facing it is north

Degrees of the compass, not degrees of heat - 180 is due south, and the setter wants that letter spun right round. The pig keeps the rest of herself.

4 down GALLIVANT

Four brave nurses go out for a good time (9)

Definition: go out for a good time · container

  • brave → GALLANT a gallant soldier is a brave one
  • Four → IV IV is four in Roman numerals

The surface puts four brave nurses on a night out; the moment you read 'nurses' as an instruction the sentence stops being about hospital staff at all.

5 down TEEHEED

Did 25 fully pay attention to getting shirt on? (7)

Definition: Did 25 · charade

  • getting shirt → TEE a tee is the shirt you pull on over your head
  • fully pay attention to → HEED to heed something is to give it your whole attention

The definition is a cross-reference: 25 down is LOL, so what is wanted is the past tense of a giggle, spelt the way comics write it.

6 down BASIC

No-frills airline defective but let's go with it (5)

Definition: No-frills · charade

  • airline → BA BA is British Airways, the crossword's default airline
  • defective but let's go with it → SIC sic is what an editor writes beside a mistake to say it is being printed exactly as it stood

'Sic' is worth banking: Latin for thus, and it always means the error you have just read was in the original and has been left alone.

7 down CHOCOHOLICS

Addicts who frequent bars never weary? (11)

Definition: Addicts who frequent bars never weary? · cryptic definition

  • Addicts who frequent bars → the surface sends you to the pub, where the addicts propping up the bar are drinkers - and the word for those is the one this coinage is modelled on
  • never weary? → the bars the setter means are the ones that come wrapped, and the sugar in them is why this particular habit never flags

There is no wordplay to take apart here, only a pun on 'bars': move from the saloon to the confectionery aisle and the addicts change with it.

8 down MATILDA

Original guy bored by literature mounted musical (7)

Definition: musical · container + reversal

  • Original guy → ADAM the first man in Genesis is the original guy
  • literature → LIT lit is the standard short form of literature, as in Eng Lit

'Mounted' does double duty on the surface, where a show is mounted rather than climbed - and Roald Dahl's schoolgirl has been a West End musical since 2011.

11 down STAGE FRIGHT

One put off from playing gig after this? (5,6)

Definition: One put off from playing gig after this? · anagram + deletion + &lit

  • gig after this → GIGAFTERTHIS the three words supply the letters, including the 'this' that points at the answer
  • One → I one is the single stroke I, and that is the letter shut out

The clue defines the answer and spells it out at the same time, which is why every word has to work twice. The question mark is the setter flagging that double duty.

15 down THE BOWERY

Bum drained extremely hot brew in NY's skid row (3,6)

Definition: NY's skid row · anagram + outer letters

  • drained extremely → EY the two letters left at the ends of 'extremely' once the middle has run out
  • hot brew → HOTBREW the drink itself supplies seven letters to the mixture

'Bum' is the first word of the clue and also its instruction, which is why it hides so well: on the surface it is simply the down-and-out doing the drinking.

17 down ALADDIN

Nearly everyone puttin' on show at Christmas (7)

Definition: show at Christmas · charade + deletion

  • Nearly everyone → AL everyone is ALL, and nearly all of ALL is AL
  • puttin' on → ADDIN putting on is adding, and the dropped g in puttin' says to drop the g here too

The apostrophe is the whole instruction: the clue writes 'puttin'' with its g missing so that 'adding' has to lose one as well. Panto season does the rest.

18 down ACCUSER

Copper section in Dell rival's charger (7)

Definition: charger · container + charade

  • Dell rival → ACER Acer is the rival computer maker, and it splits to take the insertion
  • Copper → CU Cu is copper's symbol on the periodic table
  • section → S s. is the abbreviation for section in a statute

Two traps in one line: the copper is the metal rather than the policeman, and the charger is a person bringing a charge rather than the thing a laptop plugs into.

20 down ADAGIOS

Spaniard's so long collecting silver pieces (7)

Definition: pieces · container

  • Spaniard's so long → ADIOS adios is how a Spanish speaker says goodbye
  • silver → AG Ag is silver's chemical symbol, from the Latin argentum

'So long' is a farewell, not a length of time. The definition means music: a slow movement takes its name from the tempo written at the top of the score.

22 down CAN-DO

Eager to help both sides in Congo (3-2)

Definition: Eager to help · container + outer letters

  • in Congo → CO the letters at the two edges of Congo
  • both → AND both this and that - the little word that couples a pair, written out in full and dropped between them

Read the wordplay aloud and it is just 'C and O'. Taking the joining word literally is the step that makes the clue click, and nothing here looks like an instruction.

25 down LOL

See face of lunatic that slays me (3)

Definition: that slays me · charade + first letter

  • See → LO lo! is the old imperative for look or see, as in lo and behold
  • face of lunatic → L the letter at the front of lunatic

Texting shorthand in a cryptic is newer than it looks, and the definition is the same joke a century older: being killed by something funny.
